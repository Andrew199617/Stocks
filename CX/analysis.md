## What it is
Cemex is one of the largest building-materials groups in the world: cement, ready-mix concrete, aggregates and urban solutions, sold mainly in Mexico, the United States, Europe / Middle East / Africa and South-Central America and the Caribbean. It files a 20-F and reports under IFRS. Since its FY2019 20-F the issuer has presented its statements in U.S. dollars. The engine's FX record names MXN as the reporting currency because the earlier filings were in pesos. One NYSE ADS represents ten CPOs, and each CPO represents three ordinary shares, so one ADS is 30 ordinary shares (judgment.json `ads_ratio`, 20-F Note 1). In 2024–25 it sold the Philippines, the Dominican Republic and Guatemala businesses and its remaining Neoris stake, and these are reported as discontinued operations. Its "Project Cutting Edge" cost programme is running, and management raised its 2026 EBITDA guidance in July 2026 (see the guidance check under DCF).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification (judgment.json): local cement and aggregates density, scarce quarries and plants, and high transport costs give Cemex pricing power in its core local markets. But the product is a commodity, ROIC has stayed below 10% through the cycle (judgment.json words it as about 4–10%, but the series below falls to 3.5% in FY2022 and −2.1% in FY2020), and earnings ride construction cycles, energy and carbon costs, and FX.
- **Evidence: ROIC proxy (fundamentals.json `roic_proxy`)**, FY2019 → FY2024: 3.9%, −2.1%, 8.2%, 3.5%, 7.8%, 9.9%. **Gross margin**, same years: 32.8%, 32.2%, 32.2%, 30.5%, 33.7%, 33.6%. Returns have not stayed well above the cost of capital through a cycle. They reached the high single digits only in FY2021 (8.2%) and FY2023–FY2024 (7.8%, 9.9%), with FY2022 back at 3.5% between them. A score of 7 or above would need a reason this history cannot support.
- **FOR:**
  - local-market density: cement is transport-cost bound, roughly a 200–300 km radius, so plant and quarry positions create local oligopolies;
  - scarce permitted quarry reserves and capital-intensive plants deter entry;
  - proven local pricing power, with Mexico cement prices up in local currency in the Q2 2026 prints while volumes grew;
  - scale across Mexico, the US and EMEA, plus a ready-mix and aggregates network that pulls through cement volumes.
- **AGAINST:**
  - ROIC of only 3.5–9.9% over 2022–2024 and negative in 2020;
  - a commodity product whose economics depend on utilisation (high operating leverage);
  - gross margin flat-to-down over the decade, with no expanding pricing-power trend;
  - energy, carbon (EU ETS / CBAM) and decarbonisation capex as structural cost threats;
  - MXN and multi-currency exposure under a USD-priced ADS, plus recurring tax and legal overhangs (the Spanish tax fine).
- A 5 gives **CAP 8.5 years**: growth holds 2 years, then fades to the 2.50% terminal rate.
