## What it is
Independent investment-banking firm founded 1995: strategic advisory (M&A, restructuring, activism defense, private funds), underwriting and Evercore ISI equities/research, plus a small wealth-management arm. Investment Banking & Equities is ~98% of revenue (Q1 2026 segment split from the earnings release: $1.369B of $1.392B); Investment Management is ~2% (AUM $16.2B at Q2 2026, +12% YoY). No lending book, no trading inventory of consequence — the product is senior-banker judgment, sold conflict-free because there is no balance sheet to push. The firm advised on 5 of the 15 largest globally announced transactions in 2025 and ranks #1 among independent advisors by advisory revenue; it has grown its senior managing director base ~39% since end-2021 (188 SMDs at Q2 2026, +19 hired year-to-date) and added UK boutique Robey Warshaw.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, people-based
Justification: the strongest independent advisory franchise in the world — brand, board access and a decade of share gains, with returns on equity of 16.2%–56.1% over the last five fiscal years (fundamentals.json ROE series: 2021 56.1%, 2022 31.0%, 2023 16.2%, 2024 22.2%, 2025 29.1%) — but the asset walks out the door every night, clients owe it nothing recurring, and the compensation ratio (~65% of net revenue, 65.0% in Q1 2026 and 64.8% GAAP in Q2 2026 per the releases) shows the talent, not the shareholder, captures most of the franchise rent. Note on evidence: fundamentals.json carries no roic_proxy series for EVR (EDGAR provides no operating-income tag the engine reads) and no gross margin (a people business has no COGS line) — the ROE series above and the compensation ratio are the return/margin evidence available.
- FOR: Reputation-and-talent flywheel — senior bankers win board access, execution reinforces the brand, the brand recruits the next bankers; advised on 5 of the 15 largest global deals in 2025, #1 in independent advisory revenue; independence is itself the product — conflict-free advice bulge brackets cannot credibly sell; diversification beyond M&A (restructuring, activism, private funds, underwriting) — over 40% of LTM adjusted revenue from non-M&A businesses per the Q2 2026 call summary, and underwriting hit a record in Q2 2026; Evercore ISI's top-ranked research feeds corporate access; ROE stayed ≥16% even in the 2023 trough year.
- AGAINST: The moat is people, not property — SMDs are poachable and their client relationships travel with them; no contracts, no switching costs, no recurring revenue of scale (wealth management is ~2% of revenue); compensation ratio ~65% means shareholders keep only the residual after talent is paid; earnings are the deal cycle — OE/share went $21.58 → −$1.93 in two years (2021→2023); bulge brackets (GS/MS/JPM) bundle financing Evercore cannot offer, and Centerview/Lazard/PJT contest the same independent mandates.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY2025 ended 9.6 months ago): resolved by computing the TTM — ttm.py legs quoted in full above (TTM OE $864,800,000, $20.84/share through Q2 2026). The TTM is disclosed and carried as the method-range run but not used as the base, for the cycle-peak reason stated there; the base is the normalized median.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real — it is the deal cycle itself ($21.58 → $1.00 → −$1.93 → $9.32 → $12.04). Handled by the normalized median base plus bear-widened probabilities (15/30/25/20/10; 45% bear-or-worse), exactly as the flag directs.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (NI vs OCF differ >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): the driver is the SBC addback plus bonus-compensation timing, not a one-off gain. OCF adds back SBC that net income has already expensed — SBC was $515,381,000 (2023), $569,734,000 (2024), $675,100,000 (2025) in financials_history.csv — which alone explains the gap's size. On top of that, annual bonuses are paid in Q1, so quarterly OCF is deeply negative every Q1 (−$549,653,000 in Q1 2025, −$225,863,000 in Q1 2026, fundamentals.json quarterly) and catches up by Q4. The conservative OE subtracts SBC right back out, so the divergence does not contaminate the base: full-year conservative OE vs net income is $388,316,000 vs $378,279,000 (2024) and $507,362,000 vs $591,922,000 (2025), the 2025 gap being working-capital absorption in a +29.5% revenue year. The one genuinely weak year, 2023 (OE −$77,475,000 vs NI $255,479,000), is drought-year revenue decline against sticky compensation plus a working-capital swing (ΔNWC +$125,485,000) — cyclicality, already handled under OE_VOLATILE.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance sector): confidence is graded **low**, as the flag directs. Substantively, the misfit rationale is weaker here than for the names the policy was written for: Evercore holds no deposit, loan or policyholder float, so its operating cash flow is advisory fees collected minus cash compensation and tax — the OE construction (OCF − capex − SBC) measures a real fee business, and capex is trivial ($74.0M in FY2025). No tier_override is applied: the insurer/bank float-sizing policy does not transfer (no float is capitalized in this OCF), and the numbers tier is already D at the conservative end — an override to F would misstate a company with positive normalized OE in 4 of the last 5 years.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.46x super-bear IV): real — the deal cycle produces exactly this range (2021 OE/share $21.58 vs 2023 −$1.93). Confidence is low, and the verdict is sized to the bear end.
- **BIG_NET_CASH** (net cash 2.1x normalized OE): noted in the verdict — net cash is $837,796,000 (net debt −$837,796,000 in dcf_numbers.json), a real cushion that the engine's IV bridge already credits; it does not rescue the tier because the operating business at the normalized base is what falls short of the hurdle, and part of that cash pile is the working float of a compensation cycle that pays out every Q1.
- **TIER_BAND** (bear D 6.47% vs bull C 10.03%): the verdict is sized to the bear end — final tier D, and the one-liner quotes the normalized base, not the bull case.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Quality is not the debate. This is the best independent advisory franchise in the world, with ROE of 16–56% across a full cycle, a net-cash balance sheet, a 16-year unbroken dividend record (DPS TTM $3.41, 5-yr CAGR 7.16% per fundamentals.json) and genuine share gains. The debate is purely what a share of peak-adjacent, people-based, fully cyclical earnings is worth.
- The price tells you which base the market is using: at $256.51 the stock trades almost exactly on the TTM-base weighted IV@15% ($258.56). The market is capitalizing the record H1 2026 as the new normal. If the deal cycle merely normalizes — not crashes — the normalized-base math (IV@15% $109.39) says the downside is severe; the super-bull IV@15% ($219.93) on the normalized base still sits below today's price, so even the boom-continues case needs the TTM base to justify $256.51.
- SBC is the silent term: TTM SBC of $761,106,000 exceeds TTM net income attributable to the firm — the OE definition here already charges it in full, which is why this DCF is harsher than a P/E read (trailing adjusted EPS annualizes to a mid-teens multiple that treats much of that comp as add-back-able).
- Method range: Tier D (normalized base, headline) to Tier B (TTM peak base). Anyone quoting one number for EVR without its base is hiding the ball.
