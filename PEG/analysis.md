## What it is
Newark-based holding company for PSE&G, New Jersey's largest regulated electric and gas utility (~2.4M electric, ~1.9M gas customers per search results), PSEG Power (merchant generation dominated by nuclear — Salem and Hope Creek in NJ plus a stake in Peach Bottom, PA, ~3,758 MW in total), and PSEG Long Island (operations provider for LIPA). The regulated business is the earnings anchor: a $22.5–$25.5B regulated capex plan through 2030, rate base guided to a 6–7.5% CAGR, and a planned base-rate filing by year-end 2026. Management reaffirmed 2026 non-GAAP operating EPS guidance of $4.28–$4.40 with Q2 2026 results and a long-term operating-EPS growth target of 6–8% through 2030 (search results).

## Moat: 6/10 — durable legal monopoly plus a scarce nuclear fleet, capped economics
Justification: Legal monopoly NJ transmission and distribution franchise (PSE&G, ~2.4M electric and ~1.9M gas customers) with essential, inelastic demand and a $22.5–$25.5B regulated capex plan through 2030 guided to 6–7.5% rate-base CAGR, plus NJ's only nuclear fleet (~3,758 MW, scarce, with PJM capacity upside) and a plan management says is fundable with no new equity. Capped at 6 because the NJ BPU sets allowed returns (no pricing power, affordability-exposed base-rate filing due by year-end 2026), merchant nuclear lost ZEC support in May 2025 and is hedged not contracted long-term, growth is debt-funded (net debt $22,413M), and the gas network faces long-run electrification risk.
- FOR: Legal monopoly NJ T&D franchise — PSE&G serves ~2.4M electric / ~1.9M gas customers, essential inelastic demand; regulated capex $22.5–25.5B through 2030 guided to 6–7.5% rate-base CAGR, largely clause-recovered; scarce nuclear fleet ~3,758 MW — NJ's only nuclear operator, PJM capacity and data-center load optionality; 5-yr plan fundable with no new equity per management, dividend raised with 2026 indicative $2.68/share (search results).
- AGAINST: BPU caps allowed returns — no pricing power, affordability-exposed base-rate filing planned by year-end 2026; merchant nuclear unsubsidized after ZECs ended May 2025 — hedged (~95% for 2026 per search results), not contracted long-term, GAAP swings with mark-to-market; debt-funded growth — net debt $22.413B at YE2025, equity absorbs any disallowance or rate lag first; long-run gas-network electrification / decarbonization risk, data-center / nuclear premium discussed not contracted.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade (key_numbers.md records CAP as None on this F run).

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is the model's known blind spot, flagged as such: PEG is a profitable, dividend-growing regulated monopoly whose conservative owner's earnings are negative in 4 of the last 5 years because utility capex runs at or near operating cash flow every year. The dividend is funded the utility way — rate recovery plus continuous debt issuance — which an OE screen reads as ~zero distributable cash.
- On numbers a utility investor actually uses (from search results, not the engine): 2026 operating EPS guided $4.28–$4.40 at a $70.47 price, long-term operating-EPS growth guided 6–8% through 2030, rate base guided 6–7.5%. That is a bond-like mid-to-high-single-digit equity return profile before any multiple change — nowhere near Andrew's 15% hurdle on any defensible reading, so the F here happens to agree with the hurdle conclusion even though the model's mechanism (negative OE) is a sector artifact. No method range spanning tiers is claimed: on the v5 method there is one result, F, and the prior v4 D (9.9% on a hand-built operating-earnings base) is superseded, not averaged in.
- Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (dividend/rate-base), which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the v5 method cannot support.
- Real risks for a holder (outside the model): NJ affordability politics in the planned year-end 2026 base-rate filing, merchant nuclear earnings after ZEC expiry (hedged, not contracted), and leverage (net debt $22.413B) refinanced at current rates.
