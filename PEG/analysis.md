# PEG — Public Service Enterprise Group Incorporated
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Utilities / Regulated Electric & Gas + Nuclear Generation. Price $70.35 (Nasdaq quote API, final engine run). Market cap ~$35.2B — in scope under $40B.

## What it is
Newark-based holding company for PSE&G, New Jersey's largest regulated electric and gas utility (~2.4M electric, ~1.9M gas customers), PSEG Power (merchant generation dominated by nuclear: Salem and Hope Creek in NJ, plus a stake in Peach Bottom, PA — about 3,758 MW of nuclear in total, roughly 40% of NJ's electricity and most of its carbon-free power), and PSEG Long Island (operations provider for LIPA, contract extended through 2030). The regulated business is the earnings anchor: a $22.5–$25.5B regulated capex plan through 2030, a ~$36B year-end 2025 rate base guided to a 6–7.5% CAGR, and a planned base-rate filing by year-end 2026. Management reaffirmed 2026 non-GAAP operating EPS guidance of $4.28–$4.40 with Q2 2026 results (Q2 operating EPS $0.86 vs $0.77 a year earlier; GAAP $0.67 vs $1.17 on mark-to-market reconciling items) and raised its long-term operating-EPS growth target to 6–8% through 2030. Indicative 2026 dividend $2.68/share, up ~6% — a ~3.8% yield at $70.35.

## Owner's earnings — conservative OE is structurally near zero; the usable base is TTM operating earnings
- **Conservative OE (OCF − total capex − SBC; SBC is nil — no SBC tag, utility comp is cash):** FY2025 $3,298M − $3,272M = **$26M = $0.05/share**. Positive in only 2 of the last 5 fiscal years (FY2023 $481M, FY2025 $26M; FY2024 −$1,247M, FY2022 −$1,385M, FY2021 −$983M). This is structural, not a slump: a utility growing rate base at 6–7.5%/yr spends roughly depreciation-plus-growth on plant every year, funded by debt. OCF − total capex treats growth capex that enters rate base and earns an allowed return as if it were maintenance — the wrong yardstick here, exactly why the maintenance definition exists.
- **Maintenance OE (engine formula NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC):** the engine leaves it blank from 2019 onward because PEG's `DepreciationDepletionAndAmortization` tag carries no annual YTD totals in EDGAR companyfacts. On the years where the formula can be completed by hand it tracks reported operating earnings closely (FY2025: NI $2,111M − ΔNWC −$156M ≈ $2,267M ≈ $4.53/share if capex > D&A, vs non-GAAP operating earnings $4.05/share) — close enough to use operating earnings as the maintenance proxy, as was done for ED.
- **TTM conservative OE, filing-derived from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (for the STALE_FY resolution):** OCF TTM = FY25 $3,298M − H1'25 $1,527M + H1'26 $1,821M = **$3,592M**; capex TTM = $3,272M − $1,415M + $1,459M = **$3,316M**; conservative OE TTM = $3,592M − $3,316M = **$276M = ~$0.55/share** on ~499M diluted shares. Still near zero — the TTM update confirms the FY picture, it does not rescue the conservative definition for a rate-base grower.
- **Base used — TTM non-GAAP operating earnings through Q2 2026 = $4.26/share:** FY2025 $4.05 − H1 2025 $2.20 + H1 2026 $2.41 = **$4.26**. Cross-checks: TTM GAAP EPS = $4.22 − $2.35 + $2.15 = $4.02 (GAAP TTM net income $2,111M − $1,174M + $1,075M = $2,012M); the gap to $4.26 is the reconciling items management excludes (mainly mark-to-market on Power hedges / NDT funds — H1 2026 reconciling items were +$0.26/share). Price = **16.5× TTM base OE**; OE yield 6.1% on the final run.

## Moat score: 6/10 — durable legal monopoly plus a scarce nuclear fleet, capped economics
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Legal monopoly T&D franchise in northern/central NJ — no switching, essential and inelastic demand, ~1%/yr customer growth; (2) multi-year visibility: clause/clause-recovered programs (gas modernization, energy efficiency, transmission) plus the $22.5–$25.5B regulated plan compound rate base at a guided 6–7.5%; (3) the nuclear fleet is genuinely scarce — NJ's only nuclear operator, an early site permit for new nuclear at Salem, high-90s/92% capacity factors, and PJM capacity clearing at elevated prices (nuclear cleared ~3,600 MW at $325/MW-day for the 2028 delivery year in results reported with Q2); interconnection requests for large loads at PSE&G jumped to ~4.7 GW, a real data-center option; (4) management says the 5-yr plan is fundable with no new equity or asset sales, and the dividend has been raised consistently ($2.68 indicative for 2026).
- **AGAINST:** (1) The NJ BPU caps the prize — allowed returns are regulator-set, earned returns cannot be wide-moat high, and a base-rate case filed into an affordability-focused political environment is a two-sided event; (2) merchant nuclear is no longer subsidized the old way — Zero Emission Certificates ended in May 2025, output is hedged (~95% for 2026) rather than contracted long-term, and GAAP Power earnings swing with mark-to-market (Q2 2026 Power was a GAAP loss despite higher operating earnings); (3) the gas distribution network faces long-run NJ electrification / decarbonization policy — stranded-asset risk the 2.5% terminal rate does not punish; (4) the data-center / new-nuclear upside is discussed, not contracted — no multiyear nuclear contract was in Q2 results; (5) growth is debt-funded at the margin (see HIGH_NET_DEBT), so rate-base growth translates to EPS growth only while credit stays cheap and allowed.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (final run; FY ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override. Full filing arithmetic is in the OE section: TTM OCF $3,592M, TTM capex $3,316M, TTM conservative OE $276M (~$0.55/share), and the base actually used — TTM non-GAAP operating earnings $4.26/share ($4.05 − $2.20 + $2.41). H1 2026 operating EPS grew +9.5% YoY ($2.41 vs $2.20), so the TTM base is current, not stale.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (final run: 10.5× normalized OE on the engine's basis):** Engine net debt at Dec 31, 2025 = debt current $875M + long-term debt $21,670M − cash $132M = **$22,413M**, ~10.5× the TTM base OE total (~$2,130M) and rising — long-term debt including current portion was $23.59B at June 30, 2026 vs $22.55B at Dec 31, 2025. Resolution: for a regulated utility this leverage is structural and regulator-sanctioned — debt is serviced out of allowed rate-base returns, not free cash flow, and management's no-equity funding plan for the $24–$28B program means the debt load keeps growing by design. It is folded into the verdict via the defensive scenario weights and low confidence: in a disallowance or rate-lag bear case the equity, sitting behind ~$23.6B of debt, absorbs it first.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD (preliminary run only: 0.1% on the $0.05 conservative base):** Resolved — it was a definition artifact, not a valuation signal. On the corrected TTM operating base the OE yield is 6.1% and the flag no longer fires. The honest statement is the reverse of the prelim read: PEG's earnings yield is respectable; its growth ceiling is what fails the hurdle.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (preliminary run only: 4.5×):** Resolved — the prelim spread was default ±25% multipliers applied to a near-zero base. On the corrected base with utility-appropriate multipliers the final spread is 2.15× and the flag no longer fires. Confidence is still capped at **low** by the sector-model mismatch below, not by the spread.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (agent-identified, in substance — regulated utility, the same class the audit names for banks/insurers/REITs):** Conservative owner's earnings scores a growing regulated utility at ~$0 forever, which would make every rate-base compounder a Tier F. The verdict therefore rests on the maintenance-proxy (TTM operating earnings) base, with confidence **low** and the tier treated as soft — though the direction is robust, since every defensible earnings definition (GAAP $4.02, operating $4.26, hand-built maintenance ~$4.53 for FY2025) at guided 6–8% growth lands far below a 15% IRR at $70.35.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** mechanical engine run on the conservative base: Tier D, weighted IRR −7.6%, IV@15% $0.53 — reject as a definition artifact (it prices a $2.1B-earning utility at fifty cents). Literal conservative-OE reading: Tier F — also wrong for a rate-base grower, for the reason stated in the flags. Corrected operating-earnings base with guided growth: **9.9%, Tier D** — the defensible answer, corroborated by the yield-plus-growth cross-check (~10.8%).
- Quality is not the debate: PEG is a better-than-average regulated compounder — 6–8% guided EPS growth is top-half for utilities, the nuclear fleet gives it capacity-market and data-center optionality Con Ed does not have, and the balance-sheet plan needs no equity. The debate is purely price and ceiling: a regulator-capped ~9–10%-return-on-rate-base business cannot deliver a 15% equity IRR from a 16.5× starting multiple without a re-rating or growth it has never guided to.
- What would change the answer: a price near the IV, a contracted long-term nuclear/data-center premium that lifts Power earnings structurally, or an allowed-ROE step-up. NJ affordability politics and the end of ZECs point the other way.
- **Hurdle math:** the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$41** (weighted IV@15%); with a 20% margin of safety, ≈ **$33**.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (= tier by numbers on the corrected base; the preliminary −7.6% D is also rejected as an artifact — same tier, completely different reason, written above). Weighted IRR 9.9%.
