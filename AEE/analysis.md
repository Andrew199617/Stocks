## What it is
St. Louis-based regulated utility holding company: Ameren Missouri (integrated electric and gas), Ameren Illinois (electric distribution and gas), and Ameren Transmission / ATXI (FERC-regulated transmission, including MISO long-range projects). Essentially all earnings are regulated. Management's plan is rate-base compounding: a $31.8B regulated infrastructure plan guided to ~10.6% rate-base CAGR from 2025 through 2030, a >$70B investment pipeline through 2035, EPS growth guided at 6–8% CAGR from 2026 through 2030 (expected near the upper end), and 2.8 GW of signed large-load (data-center) electric service agreements in Missouri by 2030 under a large-load rate plan approved in November 2025 (search results).

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Exclusive regulated electric and natural-gas franchises in Missouri and Illinois (Ameren Missouri, Ameren Illinois) plus FERC-regulated transmission, with essential inelastic demand and a $31.8B infrastructure plan guided to ~10.6% rate-base CAGR through 2030 and 2.8 GW of signed large-load agreements — a durable, visible compounding machine. Capped at 6 because regulators (MoPSC, ICC, FERC) set the allowed return so there is no pricing power beyond rate cases, Illinois recovery is capped / more restrictive, the plan is funded with debt (net debt $19,174M) plus recurring equity issuance (share count +1.61%/yr), and coal-retirement and gas-transition costs sit with the equity if recovery is denied.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated electric and gas franchises in Missouri and Illinois — captive, essential-service customers; $31.8B regulated infrastructure plan guided to ~10.6% rate-base CAGR 2025-2030, largely recovered through rates / riders; FERC-regulated transmission (Ameren Transmission / ATXI, MISO long-range projects) earns formula-rate returns; 2.8 GW of signed large-load electric service agreements by 2030 under an approved Missouri large-load rate plan.
- AGAINST: Regulators cap returns — MoPSC, ICC and FERC set allowed ROE and recovery; no pricing power beyond rate cases; Illinois is the tougher jurisdiction: MYRP reconciliation cap and ICC restrictiveness create disallowance / lag risk; growth is debt- plus equity-funded: net debt $19,174M and share count +1.61%/yr dilutes rate-base growth per share; coal retirement / Rush Island remediation and gas-network transition risk; large-load data-center demand could face recovery pushback.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade (key_numbers.md records CAP as None on this F run).
