## What it is
Pure-play North American nitrogen fertilizer producer: ammonia, granular urea, UAN, ammonium nitrate and diesel exhaust fluid, sold primarily into the U.S. Corn Belt from Gulf Coast and inland plants. The business is an energy-spread trade — natural gas is the feedstock and most of the cash cost, nitrogen products are global commodities — with a low-carbon angle in construction: the Blue Point blue-ammonia joint venture in Louisiana (CF 40%, partners JERA and Mitsui) started construction in 2026, targets 2029 operations, and CF's share of the spend is roughly $2B (via search of Q2 2026 reporting).

Current state (via search): tight global nitrogen supply-demand through 2026 into 2027 on management's outlook; H1 2026 net earnings rose 92.3% YoY and adjusted EBITDA rose 54.8% on higher average selling prices; Q2 2026 revenue was $2.22B (+17.6% YoY) but sales volume fell 15.3% YoY, in part because the Yazoo City complex has been idled since a November 2025 incident, with restart now expected in H1 2027, not late 2026. Gross ammonia production in 2026 is guided to ~9.5M tons including that outage. The quarterly dividend was raised 20% to $0.60 in July 2026, and ~2M shares were repurchased for $225M in Q2 2026.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow; a cost moat, not a franchise
Justification: Structural low-cost position from North American Henry Hub / AECO natural gas (70-90% of nitrogen production cost) versus European/Asian rivals on imported LNG, plus scale, 98% ammonia capacity utilization in H1 2026 vs an ~86% North American peer benchmark, and a Mississippi River / pipeline logistics network into the Corn Belt — against pure commodity price-taker economics: undifferentiated nitrogen products, no pricing power, earnings set by the global nitrogen price and the gas spread.
- FOR: Low-cost North American natural gas feedstock (Henry Hub/AECO) vs European/Asian rivals on high-cost imported LNG — gas is 70-90% of nitrogen cash cost; scale and operating excellence (~98% of available ammonia capacity utilized in H1 2026 vs ~86% North American peer benchmark); Mississippi River, pipeline and rail logistics network from Gulf Coast plants (Donaldsonville) into the U.S. Corn Belt; ROIC proxy above 20% in each of FY2023-FY2025 and positive through the FY2020 trough (9.4%); disciplined capital return — diluted shares fell from 216.2M (FY2021) to 162.2M (FY2025) to 154.2M current-YTD (ttm.py), dividend raised 20% to $0.60/qtr in July 2026.
- AGAINST: Commodity price-taker — ammonia, urea, UAN and AN are undifferentiated and priced globally; earnings hostage to the nitrogen price / gas spread (revenue went $11.186B in FY2022 to $5.936B in FY2024 in two years, financials_history.csv); the cost advantage narrows if U.S. gas rises or overseas gas falls, and new capacity — including CF's own Blue Point (2029) — adds supply; concentration/outage risk (Yazoo City, up to 1.3M tons, idled since Nov 2025, restart pushed to H1 2027; Q2 2026 sales volume −15.3% YoY, via search); capex intensity rising (TTM capex $1.067B vs $0.453B-$0.518B/yr in FY2022-FY2024, ttm.json / financials_history.csv).
- ROIC / margin evidence (fundamentals.json, roic_proxy and gross margin): FY2020 ROIC 9.4% at a 19.4% gross margin; FY2021 28.0% / 36.5%; FY2022 73.3% / 52.4%; FY2023 27.4% / 38.4%; FY2024 23.1% / 34.6%; FY2025 30.4% / 38.5%; TTM gross margin 42.5%. Returns well above the cost of capital in the last five years are real, but they are spread-driven — the FY2022 spike and the FY2020 trough are the same business at different gas/fertilizer prices — so a moat above 6 would need pricing power or switching costs CF does not have.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.
