# YUM — Yum! Brands, Inc.
Run 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: $39.52B at universe build ($144.82); engine price $146.75 × 281M diluted shares = ~$41.2B — it has drifted just over the $40B line since the universe was built, noted, not re-scoped.

## What it is
The world's largest restaurant franchisor: over 64,000 restaurants in 157 countries, 97% franchisee-owned and operated. KFC (chicken category leader, ~34,700 units), Taco Bell (Mexican-inspired leader), Pizza Hut (pizza leader, being sold — see below), and the small Habit Burger & Grill. YUM mostly collects royalties, franchise fees, and rent on franchisee sales — an asset-light royalty toll on other people's capex, plus a minority of company-owned stores. Q2 2026 prints: revenue +12.2% YoY to $2.17B, worldwide system sales +5% ex-FX, same-store sales +3% (Taco Bell +7%, KFC +2%, Pizza Hut negative), units +5%, digital >60% of system sales ex-Pizza Hut, adjusted EPS $1.62 (+12.5%).

## Owner's earnings
History (SEC EDGAR 10-Ks, conservative = OCF − capex − SBC): OE positive 5 of the last 5 years — $1,401M (2021) → $1,064M (2022) → $1,223M (2023) → $1,363M (2024) → $1,569M (2025). OE/share: $4.64 → $3.67 → $4.29 → $4.78 → $5.58 (the 2022 dip is the trough the engine's 14.1% OE/share CAGR is measured off — that blend component flatters forward growth and is one reason for the recalibration below).
- FY2025 conservative OE: OCF $2,010M − capex $371M − SBC $70M = **$1,569M = $5.58/share**. SBC is trivial here (3.5% of OCF) and charged in full.
- Maintenance version FY2025 (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): $1,719M — ~10% above the conservative figure, which supports OE quality; the conservative number stays the base for screen consistency.
- **Base used = TTM owner's earnings through Q2 2026, built from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (STALE_FY resolution):**
  - OCF TTM = FY25 $2,010M − H1'25 $850M + H1'26 $923M = $2,083M
  - Capex TTM = $371M − $142M + $175M = $404M
  - SBC TTM = $70M − $37M + $35M = $68M
  - **Conservative OE TTM = $2,083M − $404M − $68M = $1,611M = $5.80/share** on 278M H1'26 diluted shares (10-Q EPS note). Used via `--base-oe-ps 5.795`.
- The TTM base is +21% above the 3-yr median ($4.78) and +3.8% above the latest FY ($5.58). I use the TTM: the median is anchored on the 2022–23 trough years and is 9+ months stale; the TTM is filing-derived and current. The verdict fails on either base (see method range), so the choice is not load-bearing.
- One-offs inside the TTM OCF (checked, left unadjusted — they roughly offset): +$44M credit-card interchange litigation settlement income (H1'26, cash, in OE) vs −$81M YTD Pizza Hut strategic-review charges in G&A (cash costs, in OE). Net drag ≈ $37M ≈ 2% of OE.
- Q2 2026 GAAP net income ($853M, +128% YoY) is NOT OE and is heavily distorted: it includes a $359M non-cash deferred tax benefit triggered by signing the Pizza Hut sale plus $113M YTD tax benefits from the Pizza Hut IP reorganization (adjusted EPS $1.62 vs GAAP $3.08). The OCF-based OE is clean of all of it — the cash-flow statement backs the deferred taxes out (−$411M H1) before OCF. This is the NI/OCF-divergence story for 2026, resolved in OE's favor.

## Moat score: 7/10 — strong franchisor moat
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 7 = **11.1 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Two global category leaders in one portfolio — KFC and Taco Bell are the default chicken and Mexican-inspired brands across 157 countries; brand mindshare built over decades is the asset franchisees pay royalties for. (2) The franchise toll model itself: 97% franchised, so growth is funded by franchisee capex while YUM clips high-margin royalties — returns on capital routinely north of 40% and OE positive through COVID and the 2022 margin squeeze. (3) Scale advantages: 64,000 units of purchasing, advertising-fund, and development leverage no regional rival matches. (4) Byte by Yum! proprietary tech platform and >60% digital mix ex-Pizza Hut — data and ordering infrastructure that raises franchisee switching friction (and keeps being sold to Pizza Hut's buyer post-sale). (5) Proven pricing power: repeated menu price takes with system sales still compounding ~5% ex-FX.
- **AGAINST:** (1) End consumers have zero switching costs — the moat runs through franchisees and habit, not lock-in, and QSR is in an open value war. (2) Brand decay is demonstrably possible inside this portfolio: Pizza Hut, the #3 brand, shrank to negative comps and is being sold (below). (3) Franchisee health is the real balance sheet — Turkey showed it in Jan 2025 when the IS Gida termination closed 283 KFC and 254 Pizza Hut units at once. (4) Secular/regulatory pressure: GLP-1 appetite suppression, labor and commodity inflation (Q2 operating margin fell to 30.7% from 32.2%), and food-safety headline risk (an Argus downgrade in Sept 2026 cited exactly that). (5) The leverage (below) limits how much buyback support the equity gets in a downturn.

## DCF — FINAL v4 run

The prints that mean something: H1'26 OE/share grew **+7.8%** YoY, core operating profit +5% in Q2 (+8% ex-Pizza Hut), system sales +5% ex-FX, adjusted EPS +12.5% (flattered by tax items). Scenario starts are set directly: super bear −3%, bear +3%, **base +7.5%**, bull +11%, super bull +15%. Probabilities are the defaults (10/25/30/25/10) — a 97%-franchised royalty stream is steadier than most of this universe, and the leverage/Pizza Hut transition risks are carried in the growth starts and the bear OE multiplier rather than a probability skew. ## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY2025 ended 9.4 months before the run):** Resolved with the TTM override above — OCF/capex/SBC rebuilt line-by-line from 10-Q YTD figures through Jun 30, 2026, base $5.80/share vs the $4.78 median / $5.58 latest-FY. The TTM base *raises* value and the tier still fails by a wide margin, so staleness was hiding nothing.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (fired on the preliminary run at 4.5×):** The final recalibrated run's spread is 3.9× and the flag no longer fires, but 3.9× is still wide in absolute terms — it reflects a genuine fork (levered franchisor compounding vs value-war/GLP-1 erosion), so confidence is capped at medium regardless.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (manual — the engine MISSED this, and its printed net-debt figure is wrong):** The engine reports net debt of **−$671M (net cash)** because EDGAR's `LongTermDebtNoncurrent` series comes back empty for YUM in the merged history (debt_lt is blank in financials_history.csv for every year). The filing balance sheet says the opposite: at Jun 30, 2026 total debt was **$12,275M** (short-term borrowings $2,813M including revolver drawings + long-term debt $9,462M) against $674M cash = **net debt ≈ $11,601M = 7.2× TTM conservative OE**, with shareholders' deficit of −$7,107M and junk ratings (BB+ / Ba2). Mitigants, honestly stated: ~96% of the debt is fixed at ~4.5% effective, maturities are laddered, interest coverage is ~5×, management targets ~4.0× EBITDA leverage, and OE here is measured *after* cash interest (OCF-based), so the DCF is not double-counting the debt — the leverage instead caps the buyback in a downturn and is a core reason the bear book carries real weight. It does not change the tier, which is set by the weighted IRR, but any reader trusting the engine's diagnostics alone would invert this company's balance-sheet risk. Do not.
- **BASIS_BREAK — forward, manual (the engine's historical test cannot see a signed-but-unclosed sale):** On Jun 16, 2026 YUM signed definitive agreements to sell Pizza Hut in two transactions (ex-China to LongRange Capital; Mainland China to Yum China), expected to close in Q3 2026 at significant book gains, with ~$2.3B of expected net proceeds earmarked to repay revolver drawings and fund buybacks under a new $4B authorization (through Jun 2028; $4,389M total capacity at Jun 30). The TTM base therefore still contains Pizza Hut — the weakest brand (≈12% of Q2 revenue, negative comps) — while the forward company will not. The per-share math roughly self-corrects: losing Pizza Hut's OE while retiring ~10% of the share count at current prices is close to per-share neutral, which is why the scenario starts are set on KFC/Taco Bell-led system growth (+5–8% core profit prints ex-Pizza Hut) rather than on consolidated prints that straddle the break. The Q3 2026 gain on sale will inflate GAAP earnings, not OE.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2026, manual — post-FY, so the engine's FY test didn't fire):** Resolved in the OE section above — $359M deferred tax benefit + $113M IP-reorg tax benefits sit in net income, are backed out of OCF, and never touch the base. Adjusted (ex-items) EPS is the honest earnings print: $1.62 in Q2, +12.5%.
- Red flags checked, none found that change the run: dilution — none (SBC 3.5% of OCF; diluted count fell 282M → 278M; $674M repurchased in H1'26 alone); customer concentration — none (64k-unit franchise system); going concern — none; litigation — disclosed India regulatory enforcement action against a KFC/Pizza Hut subsidiary (YRIPL), not quantified as material; the $44M interchange settlement was a plaintiff *win*. Food-safety/brand headlines and GLP-1 exposure are carried in the moat AGAINST section, not as separate flags.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is narrow and entirely in D.** The preliminary run (3-yr-median base $4.78, engine-default growth/probabilities) gave weighted IRR 7.6% and IV@15% $55.67; the final run (TTM base, recalibrated growth, moat 7) gives 8.1% and $62.15. Every defensible input set lands at **7.5–8.5% weighted IRR — Tier D on all of them.** Even the super-bull scenario (15% growth start, OE ×1.25, 10% weight) only reaches a 12.1% IRR / $107.63 IV — below today's price at a 15% discount rate. There is no honest version of this model where $146.75 clears 15%.
- The business quality is not the debate — a 7/10-moat franchisor with 40%+ returns on capital, 5/5 positive OE years, and a self-funding buyback is a genuinely good asset. The debate is purely price: at 25.3× TTM owner's earnings and a 3.9% OE yield, the market is paying today for a decade of clean compounding from a levered (7.2× net debt/OE, junk-rated) balance sheet, with a brand sale mid-flight. OE yield (3.9%) + realistic per-share growth (~7%) ≈ 11% gross expected return before the leverage and fade haircuts the model applies — the model's 8.1% is what survives them.
- The Pizza Hut exit is strategically right (removes the decaying brand, funds the buyback) and roughly per-share neutral — it is a quality improvement, not a valuation rescue. Watch: closing terms/gain in Q3 2026 results, pace of the $4B buyback, Taco Bell comp durability against the value war, and any food-safety escalation.
- **Hurdle math:** the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at the weighted IV@15% of **~$62**; with a 25% margin of safety, **~$47**. That is 58–68% below the current price. This is a watchlist-at-a-very-different-price name, not a near-miss — the same conclusion as the market-cap peers run so far, but here even the bull book doesn't rescue it.
