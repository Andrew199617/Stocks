## What it is
The world's largest restaurant franchisor: KFC, Taco Bell, Pizza Hut, and the small Habit Burger Grill, overwhelmingly franchisee-owned and operated (search results put the system at 63,778 restaurants at Q2 2026, up 5% year over year). YUM mostly collects royalties, franchise fees, and rent on franchisee sales — an asset-light royalty toll on other people's capex, plus a minority of company-owned stores. Q2 2026 prints (search results): revenue $2.17B, up 12.2% year over year; worldwide same-store sales +3% (Taco Bell +7%, KFC +2%); worldwide system sales +5% excluding foreign currency; excluding Pizza Hut, system sales +7% ex-FX, unit count +6%, core operating profit +8%, and digital was more than 60% of system sales; adjusted EPS $1.62, up 12.5%.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong franchisor moat
Justification: Two global category leaders (KFC, Taco Bell) run as a ~97%-franchised royalty toll with proven pricing power and returns on capital held through cycles — against zero consumer switching costs, demonstrated brand decay inside this same portfolio (Pizza Hut), franchisee-health dependence, and high leverage that limits downturn flexibility.
- FOR: KFC and Taco Bell are the default global brands in chicken and Mexican-inspired QSR — brand mindshare built over decades is what franchisees pay royalties for; the franchise toll model itself (growth funded by franchisee capex while YUM clips high-margin royalties, OE positive 5/5 years including through COVID and the 2022 margin squeeze); scale in purchasing, advertising funds, and development across ~64k units no regional rival matches; the Byte by Yum! tech platform and >60% digital mix ex-Pizza Hut, raising franchisee switching friction; proven pricing power — repeated menu price takes with system sales still compounding ~5% ex-FX.
- AGAINST: End consumers have zero switching costs — the moat runs through franchisees and habit, not lock-in, and QSR is in an open value war; brand decay is demonstrably possible inside this portfolio — Pizza Hut shrank to negative comps and is being sold (below); franchisee health is the real balance sheet (a single master-franchisee termination can close hundreds of units at once); secular/regulatory pressure — GLP-1 appetite suppression, labor and commodity inflation, food-safety headline risk; high leverage (see FLAGS) limits buyback support in a downturn.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 9.43 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, quoted above and applied via `--ttm` (base_source: ttm_json). This is the only flag on the final run.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (fired on the preliminary run at 4.47x): the final recalibrated run's spread is 3.90x and the flag no longer fires, but 3.90x is still wide in absolute terms — it reflects a genuine fork (levered franchisor compounding vs value-war/GLP-1 erosion), so confidence is held at medium regardless.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT — manual flag, the engine MISSED this and its printed net-debt figure is wrong:** dcf_numbers.json reports net debt of −$671,000,000 (net cash) because EDGAR's LongTermDebtNoncurrent series comes back empty for YUM (debt_lt is blank in financials_history.csv). Search results on the actual filings say the opposite: total borrowings were about $12.28B at June 30, 2026 (short-term borrowings $2.81B), up from about $11.91B at the end of 2025. OE here is measured after cash interest (OCF-based), so the DCF is not double-counting the debt — the leverage instead caps buyback flexibility in a downturn and is a core reason the bear book carries real weight. It does not change the tier, which is set by the weighted IRR, but no reader should trust the engine's net-debt diagnostic for this name.
- **BASIS_BREAK — forward, manual (the engine's historical test cannot see a signed-but-unclosed sale):** search results report YUM agreed in June 2026 to sell Pizza Hut to LongRange Capital for $2.7B, with closing expected in Q3 2026, proceeds earmarked for revolver repayment and buybacks, and transition services phasing out during 2027. The TTM base therefore still contains Pizza Hut — the weakest brand (negative U.S. comps in 2025, per search results) — while the forward company will not. The per-share math roughly self-corrects (losing Pizza Hut's OE while retiring share count with the proceeds), which is why scenario starts are set on KFC/Taco Bell-led prints rather than consolidated prints that straddle the break. Any Q3 2026 gain on sale will inflate GAAP earnings, not OE.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE — 2026, manual (post-FY, so the engine's FY test did not fire):** resolved in the Owner's earnings section — Pizza Hut sale-related deferred tax benefits sit in GAAP net income, are backed out of OCF, and never touch the base. Adjusted EPS ($1.62 in Q2, +12.5%) is the honest earnings print.
- Red flags checked, none found that change the run: dilution — none (TTM SBC $68,000,000 on TTM OCF of $2,083,000,000; diluted count fell from 282,000,000 prior-YTD to 278,000,000 current-YTD per ttm.json); customer concentration — none (a ~64k-unit franchise system); going concern — none. Food-safety/brand headlines and GLP-1 exposure are carried in the moat AGAINST section, not as separate flags.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is narrow and entirely in D.** The preliminary run (3-yr-median base $4.78, engine-default growth and probabilities) gave weighted IRR 7.54% and IV@15% $55.67; the final run (TTM base, recalibrated growth, moat 7) gives 8.08% and $62.06. Even the super-bull scenario (15% growth start, OE ×1.25, 10% weight) only reaches a 12.04% IRR / $107.48 IV@15% — below today's price at a 15% discount rate. There is no honest version of this model where $147.06 clears 15%.
- The business quality is not the debate — a 7/10-moat franchisor with 5/5 positive OE years and a self-funding buyback is a genuinely good asset. The debate is purely price: at $147.06 against TTM OE/share of $5.79, the OE yield is 3.94%, and the market is paying today for a decade of clean compounding from a levered balance sheet with a brand sale mid-flight.
- The Pizza Hut exit is strategically right (removes the decaying brand, funds debt repayment and buybacks) and roughly per-share neutral — a quality improvement, not a valuation rescue. Watch: closing terms in Q3 2026 results, pace of buybacks, Taco Bell comp durability against the value war, and any food-safety escalation.
- **Hurdle math (from key_numbers.md / dcf_numbers.json, not recomputed):** the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at the weighted IV@15% of $62.06 — the weighted IV sits 57.80% below the price (upside_at_15 is −57.80%). A buyer wanting a margin of safety below the weighted IV needs a materially lower price still. This is a watchlist-at-a-very-different-price name, not a near-miss.
