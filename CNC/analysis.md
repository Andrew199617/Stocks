## What it is
The largest US Medicaid managed-care organization: government-sponsored health insurance across three books — Medicaid (the core, ~29-state footprint), ACA Marketplace plans under the Ambetter brand, and Medicare (Medicare Advantage plus a fast-growing standalone prescription-drug-plan business, 8.8M PDP members in Q2 2026). Q2 2026 (reported Jul 28, 2026): total revenue $53.6B (+~10% YoY), premium and service revenue $44.4B (+4% YoY), adjusted diluted EPS $2.51 vs a $(0.16) loss a year earlier, consolidated health benefits ratio (HBR) 89.6% vs 93.0% a year earlier. Membership is shrinking even as margins repair: Medicaid 12.1M members (−5.5% YoY, full-year 2026 guided down 8–9%), Marketplace 3.5M vs roughly 5.9M a year earlier after enhanced-subsidy expiry.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak
Justification: the largest US Medicaid managed-care organization with incumbency across a ~29-state footprint, the leading ACA Marketplace franchise (Ambetter), and growing Medicare PDP scale (8.8M members in Q2 2026) at a lean 7.4% adjusted SG&A ratio — real scale advantages in government-program procurement. Against that: the customer is the government, rates are set politically and contracts are rebid, so there is no pricing power; Medicaid federal funding cuts and redeterminations are shrinking the rolls (FY2026 Medicaid membership guided down 8–9%); ACA enhanced-subsidy expiry already cut Marketplace membership from ~5.9M to 3.5M YoY; and insurer margins are razor-thin (FY2025 HBR 91.9%), leaving earnings hostage to medical-cost inflation in behavioral health, home health and high-cost drugs. Morningstar assigns managed-care names at most a narrow moat and Centene none — consistent with a weak 4/10.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 9.4 months ago): ttm.py was run and succeeded; its full inputs are quoted here, and the TTM base was then **rejected as the pin, with the reason stated**: OCF FY $5,088,000,000 − prior YTD $3,295,000,000 + current YTD $7,956,000,000 = TTM $9,749,000,000; capex FY $767,000,000 − prior YTD $343,000,000 + current YTD $374,000,000 = TTM $798,000,000; SBC FY $204,000,000 − prior YTD $94,000,000 + current YTD $126,000,000 = TTM $236,000,000; shares (current-YTD weighted diluted) 496,605,000; TTM OE total $8,715,000,000 → TTM OE/share $17.55 (ttm.py output: 17.549158788171685). That TTM figure is more than double the $8.35 median base, and the gap is managed-care cash timing, not earnings power: the TTM window ends June 30 and captures an H1-weighted premium/claims and settlement cycle (current YTD OCF alone is $7,956,000,000), while FY2024 proved a full year of this company's OCF can print $154,000,000. Cross-checks agree the TTM OE is not a distributable-earnings base: FY2025 adjusted EPS was $2.08 and FY2026 adjusted EPS is guided to greater than $4.80 (earnings releases via search) — both far below even the median OE base. The engine's median base is therefore used, per the BASE_DIVERGENCE logic of deciding which base is distorted: here the TTM is the distorted one. If a future run's TTM window shows the H1 OCF surge annualizing into a full-year print, this decision should be revisited.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 5.4%/yr): real historically — share count CAGR −5.38% with the +5.00% growth adjustment already credited in g_base (key_numbers.md), and not double-counted in the verdict. Current evidence says it is fading: ttm.py's share legs show current-YTD weighted diluted shares of 496,605,000 vs the FY2025 figure of 493,116,000 — the buyback did not shrink the count in H1 2026 — which is one reason scenario growth starts were recalibrated downward rather than letting the full historical tailwind compound.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): structural insurer working capital plus named one-offs, different each year — from financials_history.csv: 2023 net income $2,702,000,000 vs OCF $8,053,000,000 (favorable claims/reserve and premium timing); 2024 net income $3,305,000,000 vs OCF $154,000,000 (the timing reversed — a profitable year on paper produced almost no operating cash); 2025 net income −$6,674,000,000 vs OCF $5,088,000,000 (the GAAP loss was driven largely by non-cash goodwill and divestiture-related impairment charges taken in 2025, per FY2025 earnings coverage, while cash generation recovered and HBR hit 91.9%). Because the sign of the divergence flips year to year, neither net income nor a single year's OCF is a trustworthy base on its own — the 3-year median OE base is the least-distorted of the engine's options, and the residual model risk is handled via low confidence and the tier softening in the verdict.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (flagged on the preliminary run; final-run diagnostics show a 4.00x spread, key_numbers.md): real — a government-rate-dependent insurer's 15-year range genuinely runs from a renewed medical-cost spiral (super-bear IV@15% $38.35, well below the $69.29 price) to full margin repair ($153.21). Consequence: confidence is low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the headline.** On the engine's OCF-based numbers this is a Tier B (weighted IRR 16.42%, only ~1.4pp above the 15% hurdle, on a base that is itself an upper bound for an insurer). On any earnings-based reading — FY2025 adjusted EPS $2.08, FY2026 guided above $4.80 against an $8.35 OE base — the same cash flows clear 15% only at a materially lower price, in words; the TTM OE of $17.55/share would print the opposite extreme. The defensible range therefore spans C to B, and the verdict takes the conservative end. As with HIG, this is the insurer artifact: premiums collected are not owner cash until claims and reserves settle.
- Quality/recovery evidence is genuinely improving: Q2 2026 HBR 89.6% vs 93.0% a year earlier, commercial HBR 79.2% in Q2 2026 vs 95.4% in Q4 2025, Medicaid HBR stabilizing (93.0% in Q4 2025, a 40bp sequential improvement per the FY2025 release), guidance raised twice during 2026, and net cash on the engine's balance-sheet tags. The stock has already re-rated hard on this (roughly doubled off its 2025 lows per Q2 coverage) — the recovery is real but substantially priced.
- Red flags to monitor: federal Medicaid funding policy (the structural shrink in the largest segment), Medicaid rate decisions vs post-redetermination acuity, ACA Marketplace viability if subsidies stay expired, medical-cost trend in behavioral health / home health / high-cost drugs, No Surprises Act dispute costs (management flagged these in Q4 2025 and is litigating), and how much of the 2026 guidance raise is the ~$0.50 of non-recurring items.
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine's numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at $77.51 (the weighted IV@15%) and trades 11.9% below that figure at $69.29 — but that clearing price inherits the OCF-inflated base, so it overstates the true margin of safety; a margin-of-safety buy price would need to sit materially below the weighted IV@15%, in words, and no hand-computed figure is quoted for it.
