## What it is

Attorney-in-fact for the Erie Insurance Exchange, a Pennsylvania reciprocal insurer owned by its subscribers (policyholders), since 1925. Indemnity performs policy issuance/renewal, claims handling and investment management for the Exchange and administrative services for the Exchange's insurance subsidiaries, and retains a management fee set as a percentage — capped at 25% — of the Exchange's direct and affiliated assumed premiums written. The fee rate was set at 25% for 2025 and 2024, and the Board set it at 25% again for 2026 (FY2025 10-K). The Exchange is not consolidated: the consolidated statements cover Indemnity and its wholly owned subsidiaries only, and Indemnity's balance sheet carries no insurance reserves or unearned premiums — it bears no underwriting risk. SIC is 6411, "Insurance Agents, Brokers & Service," which is the accurate description: this is a fee manager, not a risk carrier. The Exchange's direct and affiliated assumed premiums written grew 8.9% to $13.0 billion in 2025 (10-K MD&A), but 2026 has decelerated hard: H1 2026 management fee revenue +4.5% year over year, Exchange direct written premiums +3.4% H1 (+3.3% in Q2), policies in force down ~2% and retention easing to 87.5% (Q2 2026 report/call coverage).

## Moat: 7/10 — strong, capped

Justification: century-old attorney-in-fact lock-in on the Erie Insurance Exchange (since 1925) with the management fee held at the 25% contractual maximum, and ROIC proxy of 20–31% in every year 2020–2025 — but the fee rate is capped with no pricing headroom, and the entire revenue base is one counterparty in a concentrated regional footprint whose policies in force are now shrinking.

- **Evidence first (fundamentals.json):** `roic_proxy` (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) ran 23.9% (2020), 20.1% (2021), 22.6% (2022), 27.0% (2023), 31.3% (2024), 28.7% (2025) — well above any plausible cost of capital in every year, through the 2022 market drawdown and the Exchange's weak-underwriting years. The filer reports no COGS/gross profit (services model; gross margin is null in fundamentals.json), so the margin evidence is operating margin: 15.9% (2023), 17.8% (2024), 17.6% (2025), 17.9% TTM — a mid-teens spread on the fee, expanding as premium volume grew. A 7 sits on that ROIC record; it is not an 8+ because of the AGAINST list.
- FOR: attorney-in-fact structure since 1925 — subscribers appoint Indemnity under the subscriber's agreement, no contract bidding, effectively perpetual; management fee held at the 25% contractual maximum (2024, 2025, set again for 2026) — proven durable pricing on the Exchange's premium base; ROIC proxy 20–31% for 2020–2025 with no debt on the balance sheet; exclusive independent-agent network and top-ranked customer satisfaction (J.D. Power #1 among large auto insurers, third straight year in 2026) supporting 87.5% retention.
- AGAINST: single-counterparty concentration — substantially all revenue is the management fee on one reciprocal Exchange's premiums; fee rate capped at 25% and already there — zero pricing headroom, the Board can cut it, and all growth must come from Exchange premium volume; concentrated Pennsylvania-weighted footprint, with policies in force down ~2% in H1 2026 and retention easing to 87.5% — the premium base is currently shrinking on a unit basis; cost linkage to the Exchange's underwriting cycle — agent incentive compensation rises when the Exchange's combined ratio improves (H1 2026 commissions +8.1% vs management fee revenue +4.5%), squeezing Indemnity's spread exactly when the Exchange does well.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay

- Quality is not the debate; price is. A debt-free, 20–31% ROIC fee franchise with a century-old structural lock-in is being priced at $242.68 — roughly 23x the TTM base OE/share of $10.57 and a 4.36% OE yield — as if the 2023–25 premium-rate growth regime were permanent. The prints say it already ended: growth is 3–5% and policies in force are shrinking. At a 4.5% base growth start there is no path to a 15% IRR; the stock clears 15% only below the weighted IV@15% of **$98.16**, 20% below **$69.05**, 25% below **$53.07** (key_numbers.md hurdle prices).
- Method range: the engine-native run before growth recalibration (default offsets on the 11.31% blend) printed weighted IRR 10.15%, Tier C — still nowhere near the hurdle, and it rests on growth starts (base 11.31%) that the company's own current prints contradict. Across defensible methods the name spans C to D; the verdict is sized to D, the conservative end, per the recalibrated run.
- Share-basis note: the score's market cap ($12.693B) is price × the if-converted diluted count (52,305,424), which charges Class A holders with 6,100,800 Class A equivalents for the Class B shares even though the two-class method allocates Class B only 150x a Class A share's income (Note 5: Class B basic EPS $1,802 vs Class A $12.01). The diluted denominator is the filer's own diluted-EPS basis and is the conservative choice; on the filer's two-class income allocation the per-Class-A-share OE claim is higher. The verdict is unchanged either way — the gap to the hurdle is ~60%, not ~12%.
- The dividend is real support but not a rescue: TTM dividends paid were $263,355,000 (fundamentals.json), funded comfortably by TTM OE of $553,083,000 — the payout does not impair the franchise; it just cannot close a valuation gap of this size.
- Watch items: the Board's annual fee-rate decision (any cut from 25% goes straight to the super-bear case), policies-in-force trend, and the Q4 2026 print, when the charitable contribution rolls out of the TTM window and the base mechanically steps up — that step-up is arithmetic, not improvement, and should not be misread.
