## What it is
Precision power conversion, measurement and control: plasma/RF power for semiconductor wafer-fab equipment (eVerest, eVoS platforms), rack power for hyperscaler data centers, plus industrial and medical power. Q2 2026 (search results): revenue $574.1M, +30% YoY; Semiconductor Equipment a record $278.3M (+32.8% YoY); Data Center Computing $191.5M (+35.2% YoY); non-GAAP EPS $2.74. Management guided H2 2026 semiconductor revenue up ~50% YoY, full-year data-center revenue growth of at least 50%, Q4 gross margin in the 42% range, and 2026 capex of $180–195M (raised, to support growth). Capacity is being added in Malaysia, the Philippines and Mexico, plus a new 500,000 sq ft Thailand facility (initial production late 2026 / early 2027) that lifts revenue capacity from ~$2.5B toward ~$3.5B once built out.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: real qualification switching costs in plasma power for leading-edge fabs (eVerest/eVoS design wins validated on yield/throughput) and in hyperscaler rack power, against through-cycle ROIC that collapsed in the trough and gross margin well below the level of a decade ago. Evidence (fundamentals.json): roic_proxy 19.1% (FY2021), 19.9% (FY2022), 8.8% (FY2023), 2.8% (FY2024), 13.1% (FY2025); gross margin 36.6% / 36.6% / 35.8% / 35.7% / 37.7% over FY2021–25, 39.3% TTM — recovering, with the ~42% Q4 level guided rather than delivered, and structurally below the 50%+ earned in 2016–18 after the mix shift toward data-center power and acquisitions. That record — above the cost of capital in upcycles, below it in troughs — supports a 5, not a 7+.
- FOR: leading plasma power position (eVerest/eVoS) with customer-validated yield/throughput gains at leading-edge logic and memory nodes — high qualification switching costs in fab tools; design wins in hyperscaler data-center rack power incl. next-generation and 800V programs, plus second-wave customers ramping 2027; broad precision-power portfolio across semiconductor, data center, industrial and medical with global engineering and manufacturing scale; net cash balance sheet (net debt −$223.7M) funds the capacity build without leverage.
- AGAINST: through-cycle ROIC mediocre and volatile (19.9% FY2022 → 2.8% FY2024 → 13.1% FY2025); gross margin 35.7–37.7% over the last five fiscal years, structurally below 2016–18's 50%+; deep cyclicality (revenue −10% in both FY2023 and FY2024, OE/share $3.09 → $0.74); customer/end-market concentration — semi capex and a handful of hyperscalers set demand, competition (e.g. MKS) is program-by-program; growth now requires heavy capex ($180–195M guided for 2026 vs ~$62M D&A), so OE lags reported earnings through the build.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is robustness, not a point estimate: every defensible method lands in Tier D. The preliminary engine run (blend growth 1.26%, default probabilities) gave weighted IRR 3.31% and IV@15% $15.20 (dcf_numbers.json, preliminary run). The TTM base ($0.43/share) would print lower still. This final run deliberately recalibrates growth *up* to starts anchored on the current +25–30% prints and the ~50% H2/data-center guidance — and still produces IV@15% of $26.25 against a $238.05 price. The gap is not a growth-assumption dispute; it is that the price capitalizes non-GAAP earnings power (~$11/share annualized at the Q2 print, per search results) while owner's earnings — after SBC ($68,600,000 TTM) and the capex the growth requires ($151,500,000 TTM, $180–195M guided) — are $0.43 TTM and $1.82 on the normalized base.
- Quality is real but cyclical: record semiconductor revenue, validated plasma platforms, hyperscaler design wins, net cash. None of that is in dispute. What is in dispute is paying ~131x normalized OE/share (price $238.05 vs base $1.82, both from key_numbers.md) for a business whose revenue fell 10% in each of the last two down years and whose ROIC fell to 2.8% in the trough.
- Risks to this verdict (i.e. what would make it wrong): the data-center power business becoming a durable, less cyclical franchise (800V/second-wave ramps in 2027–28) at structurally higher margins, converting today's growth capex into OE at high incremental returns — the super-bull case gestures at this and still reaches only $69.05 at a 15% discount rate. A buyer at $238.05 needs substantially more than the super-bull case as modeled.
