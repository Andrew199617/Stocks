## What it is
Cincinnati-based insurance holding company (roots 1872) operating through Great American Insurance Group: a diversified specialty property & casualty platform of roughly 36 niche commercial businesses (property, transportation, casualty, specialty financial, crop), plus a large investment portfolio ($17.1B at Q2 2026 per company reporting). The annuity business was sold in 2021; this is now a pure specialty P&C story with disciplined, opportunistic capital return (special dividends are a recurring feature).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: a diversified specialty P&C franchise (~36 niche businesses via Great American) with a combined ratio better than the industry for more than two decades and sustained high returns, but insurance capacity is close to a commodity — no network effects, modest switching costs, price set by the cycle — and social inflation / catastrophe / alternative-investment returns can erase a year of underwriting profit.
- Evidence first (fundamentals.json): the `roic_proxy` series (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) is 17.3% (FY2025), 19.6% (FY2024) and 18.8% (FY2023) — well above a cost of capital through this stretch — and ROE is 17.5% / 19.9% / 20.0% over the same years. Gross margin is not computable or meaningful for an insurer (no COGS is filed); the underwriting substitute is the combined ratio: 91.5% in Q2 2026 and better than the industry average for more than two decades per company/Zacks reporting. Caveat stated plainly: both ROIC-proxy and ROE are float-levered for an insurer, which is why this earns a 5, not a 7+.
- FOR: diversified specialty P&C platform (~36 niche businesses) with granular underwriting expertise in hard-to-insure commercial niches; combined ratio better than the industry average for more than two decades, 91.5% in Q2 2026; sustained returns (roic_proxy 17.3-19.6% and ROE 17.5-20.0% in FY2023-25, fundamentals.json); pricing power in the current cycle — renewal rate increases ~5% ex workers' comp for four consecutive quarters through Q2 2026; disciplined capital allocation — $707M returned in FY2025 including $334M of special dividends, debt/total capital 27.5%.
- AGAINST: insurance capacity is close to a commodity — no network effects, low switching costs, pricing set by a softening industry cycle; social inflation on U.S. casualty lines and catastrophe exposure can produce reserve charges that erase underwriting profit; earnings depend materially on investment returns including alternatives (7.1% annualized in Q2 2026 vs a 10%+ long-term target) — volatile and market-driven; crop seasonality concentrates profitability in Q4 and workers' comp pricing was down ~2% in Q2 2026.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: on the OCF-based bases (TTM $18.63 and median $18.14 — they agree) this is B by numbers at 16.11% weighted IRR; on the earnings-based maintenance definition ($928M FY2025 total) the base would be roughly 40% lower and the name would not clear 15% at $149.13 at all. - Quality is not the debate: two decades of better-than-industry combined ratios, ~36 diversified niche businesses, renewal pricing +5% ex workers' comp, ROE 17.5-20.0% for three straight years, $707M returned in FY2025 including special dividends, and Q2 2026 printed a record P&C pretax operating income with core EPS +32% YoY. This is one of the better underwriters in the sweep universe.
- The debate is what a dollar of insurer OCF is worth: at $149.13 the buyer pays for the OCF definition. If premiums stop growing, float stops contributing and OE/share converges toward the earnings-based figure — the bear case (-2.6% start, IV@15% $110.90) is that convergence, not a disaster scenario.
- Margin of safety on the final numbers: the weighted IV@15% sits only 9.1% above the price, and the bear IV@15% ($110.90) sits ~26% below it. A buyer here is paid if the base case holds, not insured against float normalization.
