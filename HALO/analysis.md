## What it is
Asset-light drug-delivery platform. Its ENHANZE technology (recombinant human hyaluronidase PH20) lets partners convert IV biologics into subcutaneous injections; Halozyme collects royalties on commercialized partner products — DARZALEX SC (Janssen), VYVGART Hytrulo (argenx), Phesgo, Ocrevus Zunovo, Opdivo Qvantig, Rybrevant Faspro — plus product sales and collaboration/milestone revenue. Q2 2026: total revenue $481M (+48% YoY), royalty revenue $307.7M (+50% YoY); management raised FY2026 guidance to total revenue $1.835B–$1.91B and royalty revenue $1.22B–$1.245B. In late 2025 it bought Elektrofi (Hypercon) and Surf Bio to build a second, longer-dated royalty platform.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong, with a dated cliff
Justification: contractual royalties on already-approved blockbusters with clinical/regulatory/commercial switching costs once a co-formulated SC version wins share (DARZALEX SC at ~96% US conversion), gross margins of 76.8–84.3% over the last five fiscal years (fundamentals.json: 2021 81.6%, 2022 78.9%, 2023 76.8%, 2024 84.3%, 2025 83.6%), and roic_proxy above the cost of capital through the cycle (2021 22.8%, 2022 15.1%, 2023 18.6%, 2024 25.1%, 2025 15.5%). The ROIC proxy's denominator is flattered/distorted by convertible-funded buybacks and acquisitions that shrank book equity to $48,814,000, so the level should be read as "comfortably above WACC," not as precise. Capped at 7, not higher, because the core ENHANZE composition patent expires around 2027–2029, a large share of ENHANZE royalties rolls off by 2035, and Alteogen's competing hyaluronidase already powers Merck's Keytruda SC.
- FOR: royalties are contractual and run past the core patent date on the big products (e.g. DARZALEX SC royalties contractually through ~2032); switching a co-formulated, approved SC product back out is a clinical, regulatory and commercial cost, not just a technical one; six material royalty contributors and broadening — five new collaboration agreements signed through July 2026, including first deals in ADCs and nucleic acids; near-zero capex royalty economics (TTM capex $12,371,000 on TTM OCF $818,595,000); Hypercon/SurfBio add a second platform with IP guided into the mid-2040s.
- AGAINST: single-platform concentration — ENHANZE is the business until Hypercon produces royalties in the 2030s; core patent expiry 2027–2029 weakens pricing power on NEW deals even where existing contracts hold; Alteogen/Merck competition is live (Keytruda Qlex approved in the US and Europe) and Halozyme is spending on multi-country Mdase patent litigation against Merck; partner concentration in a handful of blockbusters whose own patent cycles and competition Halozyme does not control; acquisition appetite (Elektrofi $750M upfront, Surf Bio) is converting a pure royalty stream into balance-sheet and integration risk.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Acquired IPR&D — the Surf Bio charge (engine 5.6 one-time value deduction)
- **Amount:** $284,887,000, FY2025 (2025-12-31), expense basis, tag `ResearchAndDevelopmentAssetAcquiredOtherThanThroughBusinessCombinationWrittenOff` (key_numbers.md). That is the expense-tag figure; the 10-K's investing line shows $287,771,000 "Acquisition of in-process research and development, net of cash acquired" under a company-specific `halo_` tag that companyfacts does not carry, so the engine has no us-gaap cash figure and uses the expense. FY2023 and FY2024: $0. Current YTD (2026-01-01..2026-06-30): no 10-Q acquired-IPR&D fact, counted as 0 (dcf_numbers.json `iprd_adjustment.notes`).
- **Why owner's earnings never saw it:** the FY2025 cash-flow statement (10-K accession 0001159036-26-000024, R7.htm) adds back "Acquired in-process research and development expense 284,887" (thousands) in operating activities as non-cash, and pays the deal in investing ("Acquisition of in-process research and development, net of cash acquired (287,771)"), on a line outside every capex tag. So OCF − capex − SBC is clean of it, while the same money spent as internal R&D would have come out of OCF. judgment.json `iprd_cash_flow_check` records that presentation (`added_back_paid_in_investing`) with these lines; it is what lets the engine deduct an expense-basis charge.
- **The deal:** Surf Bio, Inc., a preclinical drug-delivery company (polymer-enabled hyperconcentration of antibodies and biologics). It was acquired on December 22, 2025 (merger agreement dated December 18, 2025), so the charge sits in Q4 FY2025 and inside the TTM window. 10-K Business Combination note (R11.htm / R46.htm): Surf Bio did not meet the ASC 805-10 definition of a business, so it was accounted for as an ASC 805-50 asset acquisition. Of the $294.2 million allocated cost, $284.9 million went to IPR&D with no alternative future use and was expensed.
- **Consideration mix: cash.** The total transaction cost was $305.0 million: $294.8 million of consideration paid to the sellers plus $10.2 million of transaction expenses (the 10-K's "total purchase consideration ... inclusive of $10.2 million in transaction expenses"). The allocated $294.2 million is that $294.8 million plus the $10.2 million of transaction expenses, less $10.8 million of accelerated stock-based compensation. $6.4 million of cash and restricted cash was acquired, which is why the investing outflow ($287,771,000) is net of cash acquired. No shares were issued, so no part of the charge is already in the diluted share count. A further **$100.0 million of contingent milestone payments** is not recognized. Under ASC 805-50 it is booked when paid or payable, and, because the IPR&D has no alternative future use, it would most likely come through as another acquired-IPR&D expense in the period it is paid. The engine reads that period's charge when it is filed. It is a deferred tail of this deal, not a new one.
- **Treatment (engine, mechanical):** $284,887,000 ÷ 122,092,000 shares (ttm.json `ttm_shares_diluted`, the base's own denominator) = **$2.33/share**, 38.33% of the base OE total, so `ACQUIRED_IPRD_CHARGE` fires (≥ 5%). It is a year-0 outflow in every scenario: sunk and known, so it is the same in every scenario. Every IV is lower by exactly $2.3334/share, and every IRR is solved at price + charge. The **base is unchanged at $6.0869/share**, as are the paths. Cutting the base instead would treat a one-off purchase as a permanent drop in royalty cash. The treatment is `one_time_value_deduction`.
- **Before → after at $122.50 (key_numbers.md):** weighted IV@15% $243.61 → $241.27 · @20% $150.17 → $147.83 · @25% $103.09 → $100.76; weighted IRR 22.57% → 22.32%; tier A → A.
- **Recurrence: one-time.** Only one fiscal year of the last three carries a charge (`fy_with_charge`: FY2025), so `ACQUIRED_IPRD_RECURRENT` does not fire and no amortized base is declared. Halozyme's other platform deals were **business combinations**, capitalized rather than expensed as IPR&D: Antares (May 2022, outside the FY2023–25 window) and Elektrofi (November 18, 2025; "Acquisition of business, net of cash acquired (725,965)" in investing, with $705.0 million of identifiable intangible assets plus goodwill recognized). Neither produced an acquired-IPR&D expense, and both are out of scope for this adjustment. Surf Bio, a preclinical company, was the only one of the three that failed the business test. The pattern to watch is the platform M&A itself (see My thoughts), not a habit of expensed IPR&D.
- **Impairment excluded by design:** the $48.7M FY2025 "Impairment of intangible asset" (the full write-down of the ATRS-1902 IPR&D asset from the Antares deal, after Halozyme discontinued its development) is also added back in OCF. It is not deducted. It writes down an asset bought in 2022 and no cash left in FY2025, so deducting it would charge the old purchase a second time (engine 5.6 non-goal).

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range. Re-measured under engine 5.6 at $122.50 (2026-10-19, `--measure`, not saved), the preliminary run on the 3-yr median base ($3.2836) prints Tier C: weighted IRR 13.49%, weighted IV@15% $103.65, after its own Surf Bio deduction on the latest FY share count. The TTM-base final prints Tier A at 22.32% / $241.27. The TTM base is the right one (the median demonstrably averages a smaller, pre-launch-cohort company), but the spread between them is the finding: this verdict is base-sensitive, and pass 2 should attack the TTM base first — in particular how much of TTM OCF is recurring royalty cash vs collaboration upfronts and product sales.
- Quality is high: asset-light royalty economics, gross margin ~84%, SBC $63,066,000 TTM is a real but modest cost against $818,595,000 TTM OCF, buybacks shrinking the count ~4.1%/yr (already credited).
- The dated risk is not demand — it is duration. Core ENHANZE patent expiry 2027–2029, royalty roll-off into 2035, and a credible competitor (Alteogen) already inside the biggest SC conversion in the industry (Keytruda). The 15-year projection with an 11.1-year fade assumes the contractual tail plus Hypercon bridge that duration; the super-bear case (IV@15% $96.68, below the $122.50 price) is the one where it doesn't.
- The acquired-IPR&D deduction is small against this valuation ($2.33/share; weighted IRR 22.57% → 22.32%) because the TTM base carries a full year of royalty cash. The larger M&A point is the one the adjustment does not cover. Elektrofi's $725,965,000 (net of cash acquired) was a capitalized business combination, which also never touches an OE leg. With Surf Bio, the 10-K's MD&A puts FY2025 platform buying at $1.0 billion, against $818,595,000 of TTM OCF. Platform buying is part of how Halozyme plans to get past the patent cliff. If it becomes a regular habit, the TTM base overstates the cash an owner can take out. Pass 2 should treat further platform deals as a cost of the duration bet.
- Margin of safety on the final numbers: weighted IV@15% sits 97.0% above price, but the bear-case IV@15% ($162.92) is the more honest anchor — still above price — and the super-bear sits below it. A buyer is paid on the bear case, not insured against the super bear.
