## What it is
Global power company: regulated US utilities (AES Ohio, AES Indiana), US renewables development under long-term PPAs, and competitive generation across Latin America. SIC 4991 (cogeneration / small power producers). **The standalone story is currently moot: a consortium led by Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP) and EQT Infrastructure (with CalPERS and QIA) agreed in March 2026 to acquire AES for $15.00/share in cash — $10.7B equity value, ~$33.4B enterprise value. Stockholders approved on June 26, 2026; CFIUS cleared in August, PUCO (Ohio) on September 17, and EU antitrust on October 16, 2026. FERC approval is still pending (a bipartisan group of lawmakers asked FERC on September 28, 2026 to block it). Expected close: late 2026 or early 2027.** At $14.95 the stock trades as a merger-arb stub against the $15.00 cash price.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate
Justification: Regulated utility franchises and a 10.9 GW contracted PPA backlog give contracted, visible cash flows, but returns are regulator-capped where they are safe and commodity/FX-exposed where they are not, and growth capex has consumed more than operating cash flow for four straight years.
- FOR: Regulated US utility franchises (AES Ohio, AES Indiana) — local monopolies with rate-base recovery; long-term contracted PPAs (10.9 GW backlog at Q2 2026, 3.8 GW under construction); scale platform with data-center demand pull (Indiana approval, Oct 2026, to power a Google data center).
- AGAINST: Gross margin compressed from 27.9% (2020) to 18.1% (2025) (fundamentals.json) — no demonstrated pricing power; chronic asset impairments ($1,079M in 2023, $337M in 2025, 10-K cash-flow statement); ~$29B of debt plus tax-equity/NCI capital funds the growth; FERC now decides whether the take-private itself proceeds.
- ROIC evidence: the engine could not compute an ROIC proxy (operating-income and debt tags are blank in EDGAR for AES), so there is no filing-direct evidence of returns above the cost of capital — another reason the score stays at 4. A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years (key_numbers.md).

## Thoughts / method range
- The OE DCF's answer is unambiguous on its own terms: a company whose growth capex exceeds operating cash flow every year produces no owner's earnings to capitalize, at any moat or growth assumption. That is a statement about the model's fit to a build-phase infrastructure developer as much as about AES — the regulated-utility peers in this sweep land the same way.
- The economically live question is not the DCF but the deal: $15.00 cash vs the $14.95 price is a ~0.3% gross spread to a late-2026/early-2027 close, i.e. the return is the arb spread and the risk is FERC. If FERC blocks it, the fallback is a levered standalone whose equity story rests on adjusted (non-GAAP) EPS, not owner's earnings.
- Growth blend is -2.61% and share count CAGR is +2.24%/yr (key_numbers.md) — even the revenue engine has stalled on the filed basis while the share count creeps up.
