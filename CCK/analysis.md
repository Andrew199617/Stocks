## What it is
Global metal-packaging maker: the second-largest beverage-can producer (Americas Beverage, European Beverage, Asia Pacific), plus food cans, transit packaging (Signode) and closures. SIC 3411 Metal Cans (fundamentals.json). Revenue is mostly contractual pass-through of aluminum costs on top of a conversion margin — volume, not metal price, is the real driver. Q2 2026 (release, July 20, 2026): net sales $3,668 million, global beverage can volumes +5% (also +5% in Q1), adjusted diluted EPS $2.49 vs $2.15 a year earlier (+16%), share repurchases of $305 million in the quarter (almost 7% of shares repurchased over the previous twelve months), adjusted net leverage ~2.5x, and full-year 2026 guidance raised to adjusted EPS $8.30 to $8.50 with adjusted free cash flow of at least $900 million and capital spending of approximately $550 million.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: second-largest player in a global can-making oligopoly with contractual pass-through and plant-to-filling-line proximity, but a commodity product sold to powerful dual-sourcing customers, and returns only modestly above the cost of capital.
- FOR: Global oligopoly scale: second-largest beverage-can maker, plant network close to customer filling lines is hard to replicate; Long-term supply contracts with major beverage companies; aluminum cost pass-through limits commodity margin risk; Aluminum recyclability / sustainability tailwind as brands shift off plastic and glass; ROIC proxy 10.7-14.4% in 2023-2025 and positive operating income through the cycle; adjusted net leverage down to ~2.5x at Q2 2026.
- AGAINST: Powerful concentrated beverage customers dual-source with Ball and Ardagh — pricing is largely pass-through, not structural; Commodity can: proven, replicable technology, no proprietary lock-in or owned consumer surface; Cyclical, capex-heavy OE: capex spiked to $816M-$839M in 2021-2022 and OE/share fell to $0.43 / -$0.54 despite $1.3B+ operating income; High absolute leverage: net debt $5.20B, 4.3x normalized OE on the final (TTM-base) run; Latin America softness and aluminum / inflation cost pressure noted in Q2 2026 reporting.
- Evidence (fundamentals.json): roic_proxy was 8.9% (2019), 8.3% (2020), 14.1% (2021), 12.4% (2022), 10.7% (2023), 13.3% (2024), 14.4% (2025) — above the cost of capital in most years but never wide-moat territory. Operating margin ran 10.3-12.6% over the last five fiscal years. No gross-margin series is available: gross_margin is null in fundamentals.json for every annual year, so no gross-margin claim is made. This is the same moat score the sweep gave Ball Corporation, the closest peer, on the same oligopoly logic.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, quoted above with all four legs.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): real and explained — it is the 2021-2022 capacity-build capex spike ($816M / $839M) against $403M-$527M since, not demand collapse; operating income never fell below $1,269,000,000 in any of those years. Handled by widening bear weight (super bear 15% vs the 10% default, bull cut to 20%) and by sizing the verdict to the bear end.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): explained by non-cash reconciliation items, not by aggressive revenue recognition. The FY2025 cash-flow statement in the company's results release reconciles total net income of $883 million to OCF of $1,530 million with depreciation and amortization of $456 million, restructuring and other of $83 million, pension and postretirement expense of $33 million and stock-based compensation of $48 million, partly offset by a $275 million gain on sale of an equity method investment; in FY2024 the same reconciliation carries pension and postretirement expense of $568 million against net income of $560 million, which is the single largest driver that year. The OCF-based conservative OE used throughout already excludes those non-cash pension/D&A effects, so the divergence does not contaminate the base.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $9.24 vs 3-yr median $6.25): the median is the distorted one — see Owner's earnings. It is the FY2024 print from the capex wash-through window; the latest FY ($9.24) and the TTM base ($10.39, on TTM capex of $527,000,000, near the ~$550 million 2026 guide) are the current economics. The median is not discarded: it defines the method-range low end below.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV is 4.5x super-bear IV): real — the outcome range for a levered, capex-cyclical can maker IS wide. Confidence is set to low, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: the preliminary run on the engine's normalized (3-yr median) base of $6.25/share printed weighted IRR 9.90%, weighted IV@15% $65.60 and Tier D; the final TTM-base run prints weighted IRR 13.99%, IV@15% $104.38 and Tier C. Both bases are filing-derived; the TTM base is preferred for the reasons above, but anyone quoting CCK's tier without its base is hiding the ball. The range is D to C.
- Quality is middling-good, not elite: ROIC proxy has improved to 14.4%, volumes are growing +5%, buybacks took out almost 7% of shares in twelve months, and leverage is at a fifteen-year low on management's adjusted measure — against a commodity product, powerful customers, and an OE history that went negative within the last five years when capex peaked.
- Revenue growth overstates volume growth: in Q2 2026 Americas Beverage revenue advanced 21%, which the earnings call said was almost entirely due to the pass-through of higher aluminum costs, and the release attributes the consolidated increase to 5% volume growth, higher material-cost pass-through and favorable currency translation. The DCF's OE/share growth start of 6.79% is therefore a volume-plus-buyback claim, not a metal-price claim — if aluminum falls, reported revenue falls with no OE harm, and vice versa.
- Margin of safety: there is none at $114.15 on the weighted case — the price sits 8.6% above weighted IV@15%, and the median-base IV@15% from the preliminary run sits 42.5% below the price. A buyer here is paying for the bull case (IV@15% $137.56, IRR 17.32%) to materialize.
