# EME — EMCOR Group, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: market cap ~$35.0B < $40B. Price at final run: **$776.05** (Nasdaq quote API).

## What it is
The largest US specialty contractor: electrical and mechanical construction and facilities services, run as ~80 local operating companies. Segments: US Electrical Construction, US Mechanical Construction (together ~77% of Q2 2026 revenue), US Building Services (maintenance/retrofit — the steadier, recurring base), and US Industrial Services (refinery/petrochemical). It sold its UK operations, so reported organic growth adjusts for that exit. The growth engine right now is Network & Communications (data-center) work: $1.77B of Q2 2026 revenue, 34.4% of the total vs 24.6% a year earlier, inside both construction segments. Peers/competitors: Quanta Services, Comfort Systems, MasTec, Sterling Infrastructure, APi Group, Dycom.

Current prints (SEC filings via EDGAR + Q2 2026 earnings coverage):
- Q1 2026 revenue $4.628B (+19.7% YoY); Q2 2026 revenue $5.155B (+19.8% YoY, 19.6% organic), operating income $547.3M at a 10.6% margin, diluted EPS $9.06 (+35% YoY).
- FY2026 guidance raised twice in 2026: revenue $20.0–20.5B (vs $16.99B in FY2025, +17.8% to +20.7%), diluted EPS $32.00–33.25 (vs $28.19 in FY2025, +13.5% to +18.0%), operating margin guided 9.5–9.8% — management explicitly cautioned that Q2's 10.6% margin was a peak, not a run rate.
- Backlog (remaining performance obligations) $17.14B at 2026-06-30, +43.9% YoY, ~95% of the growth organic, ~76% expected to convert within 12 months.
- Balance sheet: $924M cash at 2026-06-30, essentially no funded debt, >$1B of unused credit. ~$700M of acquisitions were slated to close in Q3 2026. Dividend $0.40/qtr (declared 2026-10-02).

## Moat score: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: FOR — (1) Scale and breadth no US rival matches across both electrical *and* mechanical trades nationally, which is what qualifies EMCOR for bundled, complex mission-critical programs (data centers, fabs, hospitals) that smaller contractors cannot bond, staff, or execute; (2) qualification and reputation barriers on hyperscaler work — safety record, prefabrication/VDC capability, and incumbent relationships with repeat data-center buyers; (3) a skilled-workforce pipeline (training, apprenticeships) in an industry where labor, not demand, is the binding constraint; (4) best-in-class execution economics — ~10% operating margin vs a ~7.2% peer median, and a debt-free balance sheet that lets it bid aggressively and do bolt-on M&A through cycles; (5) the Building Services base adds recurring, less cyclical revenue under the construction cycle. AGAINST — (1) Construction is won bid-by-bid: there is no contract lock-in beyond the current backlog, ~76% of which burns off within 12 months, and no intellectual property; (2) the workforce is the asset and it can walk — wage inflation and poaching hit EMCOR like everyone else; (3) customer concentration is rising fast (data-center/Network & Communications work is 34.4% of revenue and climbing), and hyperscalers are powerful buyers who can pause capex on a quarter's notice; (4) capable peers (Quanta, Comfort Systems, MasTec, Sterling) are scaling into the same data-center work, so today's scarcity pricing for skilled labor is cyclical, not structural; (5) fixed-price execution risk on mega-projects is a permanent tail. Scale + execution + qualification is a real moat, but it is narrow and must be re-won on every bid — a 5, giving CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 5) = **8.5 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection).

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY2025 ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override above — TTM OE/share $26.25 from 10-Q YTD math, within 0.3% of the normalized median. The staleness was not hiding deterioration or a jump: TTM revenue grew to $18.60B and TTM net income to $1,439M, while TTM *cash* OE is flat vs FY2024's level (see OE quality caveat) — which is exactly why the override matters and why it was used.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 4.4× super-bear):** Real, not a modeling artifact — a contractor at a cyclical peak with a 29.6× OE multiple genuinely has this outcome range. Consequence applied: confidence capped at medium (never high with this flag), and the verdict below is sized to the weighted/conservative end, not the bull tail.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND (share count shrinking 3.4%/yr):** Verified and material: diluted shares fell from 61.2M (2016) to 45.1M (FY2025) to 44.6M (H1 2026 YTD); FY2025 alone retired ~1.7M shares. The +3.4pp is already inside the engine's 19.4% blended growth — it is *not* added again anywhere in the calibration (the −2pp offset shift partly guards against double-counting it). Buybacks at ~$776 (≈30× OE) are a much weaker use of cash than they were at lower prices; that is a capital-allocation watch item, not a model input.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share 5-yr range >3×):** Resolved by decomposition, not waved off. OE/share went $5.20 → $8.95 → $17.27 → $28.48 → $26.34. Most of the span is a genuine earnings ramp off a COVID-depressed base (FY2020 net income was $133M; operating margin rebuilt from 5.4% in FY2021 to 10.1% in FY2025) plus buybacks — not accounting noise. But two honest cautions follow: (1) margins at ~10% are peak-level for specialty contracting, so the *level* can give back (handled by the bear/super-bear multipliers and the 30% bear weight); (2) cash OE has been flat for two years ($28.48 → $26.34 → TTM $26.25) while EPS compounded — the volatility is now expressing itself as conversion lag. The normalized base helps precisely because all three base candidates (median, latest, TTM) land within $0.10 of each other; confidence in the *base* is high even though confidence in the *path* is not.
- **No HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag fired — checked manually because the engine's net_debt is null:** the null is a data artifact (EMCOR files no current/long-term debt tags in recent periods because there is essentially nothing to tag). Verified from 10-Q data and earnings coverage: $924M cash at 2026-06-30, no funded borrowings, >$1B unused revolver. Net cash ≈ $0.92B ≈ 0.79× TTM owner's earnings — a fortress balance sheet that de-risks the bear cases (no refinancing or covenant risk in a downturn) but is not large enough relative to a $35B market cap to move the valuation. No adjustment made; noted so the null is not misread as missing risk.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** defensible inputs span a narrow band here — the preliminary default run (normalized base $26.34, default probs/offsets) gave weighted IRR 10.5%, IV@15% $461 (Tier C); the calibrated final run gives 9.6%, IV@15% $412 (Tier D). The range straddles the C/D boundary, and both ends sit ~5pp below Andrew's 15% hurdle. Unlike APP, this verdict does not flip on method choice: at $776 the stock fails the hurdle under every defensible input set, because the binding constraint is the 29.6× OE multiple, not the growth debate.
- **Quality is not the debate.** This is arguably the best-run specialty contractor in the US: record backlog with 95% organic growth, twice-raised guidance, ~10% operating margins vs ~7% peers, no debt, disciplined buybacks, and a services base under the construction cycle. The debate is purely price: the market has already paid for the data-center supercycle (stock +20%+ YTD even after an ~18% pullback from the high on AI-capex-pause fears).
- **What could make this wrong on the upside:** if hyperscaler capex compounds for 3+ more years and EMCOR's labor scarcity converts into durable pricing power, OE/share could compound at bull rates for longer than an 8.5-year CAP fade assumes — the super-bull path. Even granting that path a full 10% weight, the weighted IRR only reaches ~10-11%. An investor who believes super-bull is the *base* case still only clears ~15%.
- **Hurdle math:** the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is the weighted IV@15% itself — **~$412**. With a 20% margin of safety against that weighted value, ~**$330**. Even the *base-case-only* IV@15% ($397) is roughly half the current price; the stock would need to fall ~47% before the weighted case clears the hurdle.
- **Watch items if revisited cheaper:** Q3 2026 close/integration of the ~$700M acquisition slate (announced as near-term EPS-neutral), H2 margin delivery vs the 9.5–9.8% guide, RPO conversion pace, and whether OCF conversion recovers as growth normalizes (FY2026 OCF print is the tell for OE quality).
