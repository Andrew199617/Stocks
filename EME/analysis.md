## What it is
The largest US specialty contractor: electrical and mechanical construction and facilities services, run as ~80 local operating companies. Segments (Q2 2026 Form 10-Q, via the Q2 2026 earnings release): US Electrical Construction, US Mechanical Construction, US Building Services (maintenance/retrofit — the steadier, recurring base), and US Industrial Services (refinery/petrochemical). It sold its UK operations in 2025 (the UK segment is gone from Q1 2026 reporting), so reported growth is now US-only. The growth engine right now is Network & Communications (data-center) work inside both construction segments. Peers/competitors: Quanta Services, Comfort Systems, MasTec, Sterling Infrastructure, APi Group, Dycom.

Current prints (SEC filings via EDGAR + Q2 2026 earnings coverage):
- Q1 2026 revenue $4.628B (+19.7% YoY); Q2 2026 revenue $5.155B (+19.8% YoY), operating income $547.3M at a 10.6% margin, diluted EPS $9.06 (+35% YoY).
- FY2026 guidance raised twice in 2026: revenue $20.0–20.5B (vs $16.986B in FY2025), diluted EPS $32.00–33.25, operating margin guided 9.5–9.8% — management explicitly cautioned that Q2's 10.6% margin was a peak, not a run rate.
- Backlog (remaining performance obligations) $17.14B at 2026-06-30, +43.9% YoY, ~95% of the growth organic, ~76% expected to convert within 12 months.
- Balance sheet: $924.411M cash at 2026-06-30 (Q2 2026 10-Q cash-flow statement), essentially no funded debt. ~$700M of acquisitions were slated to close in Q3 2026. Dividend $0.40/qtr (declared 2026-10-02).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: Scale + execution + qualification is a real moat, but it is narrow and must be re-won on every bid.
- FOR: Scale and breadth no US rival matches across both electrical *and* mechanical trades nationally, which qualifies EMCOR for bundled, complex mission-critical programs (data centers, fabs, hospitals) that smaller contractors cannot bond, staff, or execute; qualification and reputation barriers on hyperscaler work — safety record, prefabrication/VDC capability, and incumbent relationships with repeat data-center buyers; a skilled-workforce pipeline (training, apprenticeships) in an industry where labor, not demand, is the binding constraint; best-in-class execution economics — ~10% operating margin vs a ~7.2% peer median (v4 pass), and a debt-free balance sheet that lets it bid aggressively and do bolt-on M&A through cycles; the Building Services base adds recurring, less cyclical revenue under the construction cycle.
- AGAINST: Construction is won bid-by-bid — no contract lock-in beyond the current backlog, ~76% of which burns off within 12 months, and no intellectual property; the workforce is the asset and it can walk — wage inflation and poaching hit EMCOR like everyone else; customer concentration is rising fast (data-center/Network & Communications work is roughly a third of revenue and climbing), and hyperscalers are powerful buyers who can pause capex on a quarter's notice; capable peers (Quanta, Comfort Systems, MasTec, Sterling) are scaling into the same data-center work, so today's scarcity pricing for skilled labor is cyclical, not structural; fixed-price execution risk on mega-projects is a permanent tail.
- A 5 gives CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 5) = **8.5 years**: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:sbc** (SBC missing for FY2023–2025 of the last 3): resolved by the filing-sourced override — see OVERRIDE above for the gap, the source, the out-of-band acknowledgement, and the measured impact.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc**: same section — gap, decision, and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **STALE_FY** (FY2025 ended 9.4 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, quoted above, within 0.4% of the normalized median. The staleness was not hiding deterioration or a jump: TTM revenue and net income grew, while TTM *cash* OE is flat vs FY2024's level — which is exactly why the TTM base (not a momentum extrapolation) was used.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 3.4%/yr): verified and material: diluted shares fell from 61,206,792 (2016) to 45,145,767 (FY2025) to 44,619,442 (H1 2026 YTD). The +3.43pp is already inside the engine's 19.40% blended growth — it is *not* added again anywhere in the calibration (the −2pp offset shift partly guards against double-counting it). Buybacks at ~$750 (≈29× OE) are a much weaker use of cash than they were at lower prices; that is a capital-allocation watch item, not a model input.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 yrs spans >3x): resolved by decomposition, not waved off. Conservative OE totals went $262.0M → $428.0M → $800.7M → $1,312.3M → $1,168.7M (FY2021–FY2025, with the override). Most of the span is a genuine earnings ramp off a COVID-depressed base (FY2020 net income was $132,943,000) plus buybacks — not accounting noise. But two honest cautions follow: (1) margins at ~10% are peak-level for specialty contracting, so the *level* can give back (handled by the bear/super-bear multipliers and the 30% bear weight); (2) cash OE has been flat-to-down for two years while EPS compounded — the volatility is now expressing itself as conversion lag. The normalized base helps precisely because all three base candidates (median, latest, TTM) land within $0.10 of each other; confidence in the *base* is high even though confidence in the *path* is not.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.40× super-bear): real, not a modeling artifact — a contractor at a cyclical peak with a ~29× OE multiple genuinely has this outcome range. Consequence applied: confidence capped at medium (never high with this flag), and the verdict below is sized to the weighted/conservative end, not the bull tail.
- **No HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag fired — checked manually because the engine's net_debt is null:** the null is a data artifact (EMCOR files no current/long-term debt tags in recent periods because there is essentially nothing to tag). Verified from filing data: $1,111,968,000 cash at FY2025 end and $924,411,000 at 2026-06-30, a $525,000,000 revolver draw in 2025 that was fully repaid the same year, and no revolver flows in H1 2026. Net cash is a fortress balance sheet that de-risks the bear cases (no refinancing or covenant risk in a downturn) but is not large enough relative to a $33.871B market cap to move the valuation. No adjustment made; noted so the null is not misread as missing risk.
- **No NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE flag fired:** checked anyway — the one named one-off is the $144,876,000 UK-sale gain inside FY2025 net income; it is reconciled out of OCF, so the OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is unaffected. The maintenance definition (which starts from net income) is correspondingly the less clean lens for FY2025, and it still lands close to the conservative figure.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** defensible inputs span a narrow band here — the v5 zero-fill measurement (override toggled off, same final configuration, TTM basis) gives weighted IRR 9.87%; the calibrated final run gives 9.75% (Tier D); the v4 pass a day earlier spanned 9.6% (calibrated, D) to 10.5% (defaults, C). The range straddles the C/D boundary, and both ends sit ~5pp below Andrew's 15% hurdle. Unlike APP, this verdict does not flip on method choice: at $750 the stock fails the hurdle under every defensible input set, because the binding constraint is the ~29× OE multiple, not the growth debate.
- **Quality is not the debate.** This is arguably the best-run specialty contractor in the US: record backlog with 95% organic growth, twice-raised guidance, ~10% operating margins vs ~7% peers, no debt, disciplined buybacks, and a services base under the construction cycle. The debate is purely price: the market has already paid for the data-center supercycle.
- **What could make this wrong on the upside:** if hyperscaler capex compounds for 3+ more years and EMCOR's labor scarcity converts into durable pricing power, OE/share could compound at bull rates for longer than an 8.5-year CAP fade assumes — the super-bull path. Even granting that path a full 10% weight, the weighted IRR only reaches ~9.8%. An investor who believes super-bull is the *base* case still only clears ~15.5%.
- **Hurdle math:** the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is the weighted IV@15% itself — **~$405**. With a 20% margin of safety against that weighted value, ~**$324**. Even the *base-case-only* IV@15% ($390.28) is roughly half the current price; the stock would need to fall ~46% before the weighted case clears the hurdle.
- **Watch items if revisited cheaper:** Q3 2026 close/integration of the ~$700M acquisition slate (announced as near-term EPS-neutral), H2 margin delivery vs the 9.5–9.8% guide, RPO conversion pace, and whether OCF conversion recovers as growth normalizes (FY2026 OCF print is the tell for OE quality).
