## What it is
Canadian gold miner (SIC 1040, Gold and Silver Ores; Form 40-F, IFRS, USD reporting). Operating mines: the Island Gold District in Ontario (Island Gold underground plus the Magino open pit and mill, combined after the Argonaut Gold acquisition closed July 12, 2024), Young-Davidson in Ontario, and the Mulatos District in Mexico (La Yaqui Grande). Development pipeline: Lynn Lake in Manitoba (a $920M build per Q2 2026 reporting) and the Island Gold Phase 3+ expansion, with management guiding the portfolio toward ~1M oz/yr by 2030. The shares are the same Class A Common Shares on the TSX and the NYSE — see the ADS flag below. Market cap $13.576B at $32.12 (nasdaq_quote_api) — in scope under $40B.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-narrow cost moat, no franchise
Justification: a gold miner is a price taker — the only durable advantages are cost-curve position, reserve life and jurisdiction. Alamos has real ones, but no pricing power, and its return history proves the point.
- Evidence (fundamentals.json): **ROIC proxy** was 0.46% (FY2021), 3.40% (FY2022), 8.43% (FY2023), 9.07% (FY2024) and 22.18% (FY2025) — below the cost of capital through most of the cycle; the FY2025 spike is peak-gold and is flattered by a $218.8M impairment reversal sitting inside operating income. **Gross margin** over the same five years: 35.2%, 25.9%, 37.7%, 44.2%, 55.2% — the expansion is the gold price (FY2025 realized $3,372/oz on 531k oz sold, per the year-end release), not pricing power the company controls.
- FOR: low-cost Island Gold District — high-grade underground ore processed through the larger Magino mill, with the expansion targeted to lower AISC further; long reserve life and an organic pipeline (Island Gold District, Lynn Lake, PDA); tier-1 jurisdictions (Ontario, Manitoba, Mexico); net-cash balance sheet; free-cash-flow positive while funding growth (FY2025 free cash flow $352M per the year-end release).
- AGAINST: pure gold price taker; ROIC below the cost of capital through most of the cycle; reserves deplete — every ounce must be replaced via capex or M&A (FY2025 capex $507.1M, ~28% of revenue); few-asset concentration — a June 2026 seismic event at Young-Davidson cut FY2026 production guidance to 510,000–560,000 oz and raised AISC guidance to $1,775–$1,875/oz (Q2 2026 release).
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

That gap is disclosed here and drives the low confidence; it is not used to restate the base, because no computed TTM output exists to build one from within the override authority.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): operating cash flow ran far above net income in both years (FY2023: OCF $472.7M vs NI $210.0M; FY2024: OCF $661.1M vs NI $284.3M, both from financials_history.csv). This is the structural miner pattern — non-cash depletion and depreciation on a multi-billion-dollar PPE base ($4,618.0M at FY2024) plus deferred-tax effects; the D&A leg itself is not separately tagged in the IFRS facts (da tag null), so the engine cannot split it out, but the OE base is built from OCF directly and is not contaminated by it. FY2024 also carried Argonaut acquisition and integration costs ($9.3M per the 40-F). The pattern inverted in FY2025 (NI $885.8M vs OCF $795.3M) for identified one-offs in NI only: a $218.8M impairment reversal and a $231.0M gain on the sale of the Turkish projects and Quartz Mountain, partly offset by $230.5M of losses on commodity derivatives from the Argonaut legacy hedges (year-end 2025 release; adjusted net earnings were $587.1M, $1.40/share). None of the NI one-offs inflate the OCF-based base.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (x1.0): resolved in judgment.json — Alamos is a direct cross-listing, not an ADS program: the F-10 share-capital description states the Common Shares are listed on the TSX and the NYSE under the symbol AGI, so one NYSE share is one Class A Common Share and per-share figures compare to the price with no scaling (source and reason recorded in judgment.json and score.json).
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (base OE yield 1.61%): real and conceded — at $32.12 the market is capitalizing gold-price persistence and future volume growth, not current owner's earnings. The verdict is sized to the conservative end; even so the tier is D in every scenario, so the fragility cuts against the price, not against the tier.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is that this D is a **gold-price verdict, not an operations verdict**. The FY2025 base was earned at a $3,372/oz realized price; Q2 2026 printed $4,504/oz. If ~$4,500 gold persists and Island Gold / Lynn Lake deliver toward ~1M oz/yr by 2030, run-rate owner's earnings are a multiple of the $0.52 base and this tier is too harsh. If gold mean-reverts toward the levels at which FY2021–22 OE was negative, even the bear case here is generous — AISC guidance was just raised to $1,775–$1,875/oz and FY2026 production guidance was cut to 510,000–560,000 oz after the Young-Davidson seismic event.
- Method range, stated plainly: (1) the engine deducts *all* capex, including the $318M FY2025 growth capex — a maintenance-only base would be higher; (2) the base is FY2025 because no IFRS TTM exists in the computed outputs — a 2026 run-rate base at current gold would also be higher. Both push the same way, which is why confidence is low even though the filed-numbers tier is unambiguous: on the sweep's own rules, no scenario — not even super bull at a 41.9% growth start — reaches the $32.12 price.
- Quality notes in the company's favor: net cash ($-423.1M net debt), FY2025 free cash flow $352M while building, dividend raised and an NCIB in place, and the Argonaut legacy hedges being eliminated (50,000 oz settled early in Q4 2025 at a $113.5M cost) increases unhedged exposure to the gold price — which is exactly the exposure the buyer at $32.12 is paying for.
- Hurdle-clearing prices (weighted path, key_numbers.md): 15% at $12.24, 20% at $8.11, 25% at $5.91.
