# SSL — Sasol Limited
v5 run (engine release 5.5.0), 2026-10-20. South African integrated energy and chemicals company, listed on the JSE (SOL) and on NYSE as ADSs (SSL) at a 1:1 ADR-to-ordinary-share ratio. Market cap $10.073B at $15.69 (nasdaq_quote_api) — in scope under $40B.

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, fundamentals.json or a cited company result — none are recomputed.

## What it is
An integrated fuels-and-chemicals group built on proprietary Fischer-Tropsch coal/gas-to-liquids technology: the Secunda complex in South Africa, the Natref refinery, its own coal mining, Mozambique natural gas, and chemicals businesses in Africa, America and Eurasia. Earnings are set by Brent, refining margins and chemical basket prices — Sasol is a price taker in every one of its markets.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: hard-to-replicate assets (Fischer-Tropsch technology, the integrated Secunda complex, own coal feedstock, low-cost Mozambique gas), but a commodity price taker whose returns record is the verdict — the roic_proxy series in fundamentals.json runs 7.3% (FY2021), 18.1% (FY2022), 5.1% (FY2023), -9.8% (FY2024), 5.4% (FY2025), below a plausible cost of capital in most years. Gross margin is not filed in a form fundamentals.json can read (null), so ROIC carries the evidence.
- FOR: proprietary Fischer-Tropsch technology and the integrated Secunda complex — a coal/gas-to-liquids asset that cannot be replicated at economic cost today; own coal mining feedstock and low-cost Mozambique natural gas give an integrated cost position in Southern African fuels; scale and incumbency in South African liquid fuels and a diversified chemicals portfolio across Africa, America and Eurasia; balance sheet repairing (net debt down to US$3.3B at FY2026 per company results, cash fixed costs held flat for three years).
- AGAINST: pure commodity price taker — Brent, refining margins and chemical basket prices set earnings; weak, volatile ROIC (above); recurring mega-impairments — R76.0B (FY2024), R21.8B (FY2025), R17.0B (FY2026) per the company's annual financial statements, with the Secunda and Sasolburg refinery CGUs still fully impaired; Secunda's structural carbon/regulatory exposure; Mozambique gas in natural decline (FY2026 production -7%) and South African chemicals swung to an EBIT loss in FY2026; dividend suspended until net debt is sustainably below US$3B.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 15.7 months ago per diagnostics): the FY2026 Form 20-F was filed 2026-09-01 (fundamentals.json profile), but EDGAR companyfacts has not ingested it — fundamentals.json states its annual rows stop at 2025-06-30 — so no engine-usable FY2026 or TTM series exists, and ttm.py cannot build one for an IFRS filer (it reads US-GAAP facts only and exits non-zero). The median base is used and the staleness is priced into confidence (low). The published FY2026 results (company announcement, 1 September 2026) show what the next engine pass will see: revenue R272.118B, EBIT R25.690B, adjusted EBITDA R61B (+17%), impairments of R16.8B, capex down 18%, net debt US$3.3B and again no final dividend.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real cyclicality, not an artefact — FY2024 OE was negative while FY2025 printed $1.05/share. Handled with the normalized median base and bear-shifted probabilities (45% on bear-or-worse).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): the one-off is identified — non-cash impairments sit in net income and never touch operating cash flow. Impairment of assets was R76,035M in FY2024 and R21,836M in FY2025 (annual financial statements, remeasurement items note), and FY2024 also carried the R15.3B derecognition of a Chemicals America deferred tax asset (FY2025 results announcement). Net income is therefore far below OCF in all three years. The OE base is OCF-based, so the divergence does not contaminate it; what it does signal — repeated destruction of carrying value — is in the moat score.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: reporting currency ZAR converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (FY2025 rate 0.05624, recorded in dcf_numbers.json fx_conversion). Flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average — a measurement caveat on every USD figure here, and one reason confidence is low.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: ratio 1.0 declared from the filer's own documents (see OVERRIDES) — no per-share figure changes.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:shares**: see OVERRIDES — filing-sourced, measured impact zero on base and IRR, unblocks the tier.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $1.05 vs median $0.28): the latest FY is the distorted one. FY2025 operating cash flow includes the Transnet SOC Limited net cash settlement of R4.3B (before tax) and a R2.9B reduction in the asset rehabilitation provision (FY2025 results announcement) — one-offs that lift FY2025 OE well above run-rate, while the median year FY2023 carried peak capex of $1.629B. The median is retained as the normalized base; the sensitivity run on the latest-FY base is printed above and is still Tier D.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (1.80%): stated plainly — at $15.69 the normalized base yields 1.8%, so the price rests almost entirely on cyclical recovery and growth the model cannot credit; even the super-bull case earns only 6.03%.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 19.34x normalized OE): balance sheet from the filings — net debt $3.502B (key_numbers.md), debt on a TOTAL:Borrowings basis, plus lease liabilities of $976,326,400 in dcf_numbers.json. On the latest-FY base the same debt is 5.20x (sensitivity run) — still heavy for a cyclical, and the reason the dividend stays suspended until net debt is sustainably below US$3B. The bear cases are the stress: in FY2024 leverage on this order coincided with negative OE.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The gap between price and value here is not a modelling quirk: it survives the friendliest defensible base (latest FY, 8.71% weighted IRR) and the friendliest scenario (super bull, 6.03% on the normalized base). At $15.69 the market is paying for the FY2026 upcycle — refining margins, a stronger final quarter, EBITDA +17% — as if it were the run-rate. Sasol's own history (OE/share of $0.28, -$0.11, $1.05 across FY2023-25) says the run-rate is far lower and the capex bill never leaves.
- What would change the answer: the FY2026 20-F landing in companyfacts with a full year of stronger OE, sustained net debt below US$3B restoring the dividend, and an end to the impairment cycle at Secunda. None of those is in the filed data the engine can use yet.
- Data caveat for the next pass: this score is built on FY2023-25 because FY2026 is filed but not yet ingested by companyfacts; expect a refresh trigger when it lands.
