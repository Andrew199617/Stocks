## What it is
Merchant payment processing and commerce software. On January 9, 2026 it closed a portfolio swap that defines the current company: it acquired 100% of Worldpay (enterprise value $24.25B; consideration included ~$6.0B cash to GTCR and 42,779,788 newly issued GPN shares) and simultaneously sold its Issuer Solutions business (the former TSYS issuer-processing unit) to FIS for an enterprise value of $13.5B (~$7.7B cash plus FIS's Worldpay interest). GPN is now a pure-play merchant/commerce provider, realigned into Enterprise, Platforms and SMB segments, serving over 6 million merchant locations. Q2 2026 (first full post-deal quarter): GAAP revenue $3.32B, adjusted net revenue $3.16B — up ~4% year over year on a normalized basis (Worldpay included as if always owned, divested businesses excluded), normalized adjusted operating margin 42.0% (+70bp), adjusted EPS $3.46 (+12%). FY2026 guidance: normalized constant-currency adjusted net revenue growth of ~4–5%, adjusted EPS $13.60–$13.80, >$2B of capital returns planned for 2026.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
Justification: Real scale and switching costs in merchant acquiring, now larger with Worldpay, but acquiring is a contested, price-pressured business with low differentiation at the SMB end and powerful rivals (Adyen, Stripe, Fiserv, Chase) — scale earns a 6, not a 7+.
- FOR: Combined scale — over 6 million merchant locations, ~$3.7T volume / ~94B transactions a year across 175+ countries (company statements at deal close)
- FOR: Switching friction for enterprise and integrated merchants — Genius point-of-sale / integrated software embeds payments into the merchant's operations
- FOR: Global distribution and bank/partner referral channels from both legacy GPN and Worldpay
- FOR: Proven margin structure — 42.0% normalized adjusted operating margin in Q2 2026, expanding
- AGAINST: Merchant acquiring is price-competitive; SMB merchants churn and multi-home, and Stripe/Adyen win on developer experience and e-commerce
- AGAINST: No owned consumer surface or network — GPN rents the card networks' rails and is exposed to interchange / regulatory pressure on them
- AGAINST: Growth is bought, not organic — the equity story depends on integrating Worldpay without attrition, against a long history of deal indigestion in payments
- AGAINST: Leverage (net debt 7.0x normalized OE) limits how much buyback / investment flexibility backs the moat
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.46 months ago): ttm.py was run and its full output saved to ttm.json. Its inputs — OCF: FY $2,656,592,000 − prior YTD $1,372,649,000 + current YTD $373,798,000 = TTM $1,657,741,000; capex: FY $617,769,000 − prior YTD $279,747,000 + current YTD $497,000,000 = TTM $835,022,000; SBC: FY $153,647,000 − prior YTD $79,550,000 + current YTD $57,193,000 = TTM $131,290,000; shares (current-YTD weighted diluted) 271,564,000 — give **TTM OE total $691,429,000 → $2.55/share**. That pin was NOT used, for a specific reason: the TTM window straddles the January 9, 2026 closing. The FY and prior-YTD legs are the pre-deal company; the current-YTD leg is the post-deal combined company carrying the sale's discontinued-operations loss of $1,688,190,000 for H1 2026 (including ~$1.7B of tax expense, primarily from derecognizing non-deductible goodwill on the sale, per the Q2 2026 10-Q reporting), deal and integration costs, a step-up in capex to $497,000,000 for the half, and a share count inflated by the 42,779,788 shares issued for Worldpay before any offsetting buybacks. A sensitivity run on that pin (same moat / probs) gave weighted IRR 6.31%, IV@15% $24.57, Tier D — that is a measure of the deal noise in the window, not of the go-forward merchant business, whose Q2 2026 adjusted EPS was $3.46 in a single quarter. The audited median base was used instead, and the TTM result is disclosed here as the low end of the method range.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 4.24%/yr): credited in the blend via the +4.24pp adjustment and not double-counted. Caveat, stated plainly: the trend reversed in H1 2026 — current-YTD weighted diluted shares in ttm.json are 271,564,000 vs 242,008,000 for FY2025 — because of the shares issued for Worldpay. Management plans >$2B of capital returns in 2026 ($1.2B returned year-to-date at Q2), so buybacks resume, but the historical shrink rate should not be assumed to repeat immediately; the base growth start is anchored on the revenue guide instead (see DCF section).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (NI vs OCF differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): resolved — this is structural, not a one-off. From financials_history.csv: net income was $986,233,000 vs OCF $2,549,805,000 in 2023, $1,570,365,000 vs $3,057,583,000 in 2024, and $1,400,107,000 vs $2,656,592,000 in 2025. The gap is dominated by D&A — $1,141,894,000 / $1,217,288,000 / $1,229,208,000 in those years — i.e. amortization of intangibles from GPN's acquisition history, a non-cash charge that depresses GAAP net income every year. No tax-deposit or impairment one-off is needed to explain it. The OE base is built on OCF, so the divergence does not contaminate the base; it does mean GAAP EPS will keep understating cash earnings, and the maintenance-OE definition (which adds D&A back) will keep printing above the conservative one.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.84x super-bear IV): real — the outcome range genuinely is this wide one year into a transformational deal with 7x leverage. Confidence is set to low, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 7.01x normalized OE): folded into the verdict explicitly. Net debt is $13,214,799,000 on the engine's FY2025 balance sheet, and total long-term debt was $22,418,326,000 at June 30, 2026 per the Q2 2026 10-Q reporting — the deal was substantially debt- and stock-funded. Levered OE can vanish fast: the bear IV@15% ($50.12) and super-bear IV@15% ($30.62) sit far below the $84.33 price, and 35% of the probability weight is on those two cases. This is the main reason the stock does not get the benefit of the doubt on its bull cases.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, and unusually both ends are deal artifacts: Tier C (weighted IRR 13.70%, IV@15% $75.16) on the clean audited median base of the pre-deal company, vs Tier D (weighted IRR 6.31%, IV@15% $24.57) on a TTM pin whose window contains the closing itself — the discontinued-operations tax hit, integration costs, and the pre-buyback share issuance. Neither base is the go-forward combined company; no filing-direct base for it exists yet. The median-base result is used as the headline because its distortion is understood and conservative in direction, but confidence is low until a clean post-deal fiscal year files.
- Quality is middling-good, not elite: real scale, 42% adjusted operating margins, SBC a modest $153,647,000 in FY2025 against OCF of $2,656,592,000 — but growth is only guided at ~4–5%, the moat is contested, and the balance sheet (7.0x net debt / normalized OE) does the equity no favors if integration slips.
- Price discipline: at $84.33 the stock is priced above the weighted IV@15% of $75.16 — the market is already paying for part of the bull case. It clears Andrew's 15% hurdle at or below $75.16 on the weighted case; with the spread this wide and leverage this high, a margin of safety means demanding a price below that, closer to the base-case IV@15% of $69.56, before it is interesting.
