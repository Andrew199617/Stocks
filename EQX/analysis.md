## What it is
Growth-by-acquisition gold producer operating entirely in the Americas (Canada, USA, Brazil, Nicaragua). The portfolio was reshaped on June 17, 2025, when Equinox closed its acquisition of Calibre Mining Corp. (search-cited, company Q2 2025 release), adding the Nicaragua operations and the Pan Mine to its two Canadian cornerstone assets — Greenstone (Ontario, ramping) and Valentine (Newfoundland & Labrador, commissioning through 2025). It sells a commodity at a price it does not set.

## Moat: 2/10 — none-to-weak
Justification: a gold miner is a pure price taker — no pricing power, no switching costs, no network effects. Equinox sits on the weak end even for the sector: the `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json runs 7.3% / 5.4% / 4.1% / 0.3% / 1.2% / 2.7% / 4.9% for FY2019–FY2025 — never above ~7% in any year and near zero in FY2022–23, i.e. no evidence of returns above the cost of capital through a cycle — and consolidated AISC was $1,959/oz in Q2 2025 (search-cited), high for a producer of this scale. Gross margin is not separately tagged in its IFRS filings (fundamentals.json carries it as null), so the ROIC series and the filed operating-income line are the margin evidence available.
- FOR: Scale — 663,921 oz produced in 2025, guided 700,000–800,000 oz for 2026 (search-cited company presentation); Two long-life Canadian cornerstone mines (Greenstone, Valentine) now ramping; Americas-only jurisdictions (Canada, USA, Brazil, Nicaragua) — no single-country concentration at the asset level
- AGAINST: Pure commodity price taker — no pricing power; earnings are a leveraged function of the gold price; ROIC proxy never above ~7% in FY2019–FY2025 and near zero in FY2022–23 (fundamentals.json) — no evidence of returns above the cost of capital through a cycle; High cost position: consolidated AISC $1,959/oz in Q2 2025 (search-cited); Reserves deplete every year and must be replaced with heavy capex — FY2025 capex was $692.3M against $818.3M of OCF; Serial dilution: diluted share count compounding at 27.5%/yr (engine), driven by equity-financed acquisitions (Calibre closed June 17, 2025)
- A 2 gives CAP = 4.6 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 4.6 years, 15-year projection. Moot here — with no positive base there is nothing to fade.

## DCF — engine 5.4 final run (2026-10-17)
**No scenario table exists, by design.** The engine's data gate refuses to project a DCF off a non-positive normalized base: tier_by_numbers is F, f_subtype `no_cash_generation` (dcf_numbers.json basis: the sum of the last 3 fiscal years — the median window — is not positive), implied_irr_weighted and IV are null. Probabilities passed were the defaults (10/25/30/25/10); they change nothing when the base is negative. Inventing scenario IVs off the single positive year would be exactly the fake-precision failure the gate exists to prevent.
- Growth sanity check (mandatory): the engine's growth blend of 19.03% (components: rev CAGR 5y 16.54% · rev CAGR 3y 24.03% · latest rev YoY 99.07%, key_numbers.md) is basis-break arithmetic, not a growth rate — the +99.07% latest YoY is the Calibre consolidation (see BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT), and the CAGRs straddle it. The blend was not used; under Tier F no growth input can change the verdict.
- The honest quantitative statement the numbers support: at $11.15, the market is paying for the current gold price and a successful Greenstone/Valentine ramp. On the normalized base there is no price at which a DCF can be run at all; on the single best filed year (FY2025, OE/share $0.17), the earnings power is a rounding error against the price. A buyer is long gold and execution, not long owner's earnings.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): RESOLVED WITH THE CAVEAT STATED. ttm.py was run and failed as the spec predicts for an IFRS filer: "cannot compute TTM for EQX (CIK 1756607): ocf: no annual (FY) fact; sbc: no annual (FY) fact; shares_diluted: no annual (FY) fact; capex: no capex facts in companyfacts under any candidate tag" — ttm.py reads us-gaap facts only, EQX files ifrs-full, so the median base was used, as instructed. Interim context the engine cannot score (search-cited): Q2 2026 revenue was $769.8M on 177,959 oz sold at a realized $4,256/oz, with net income of $230.6M, and Q2 2026 production was 176,836 oz with Canadian production up 11% quarter-over-quarter — the current year is running far above the FY2023–25 median window on both price and volume. The F verdict is therefore a verdict on normalized, filed annual numbers; it is not a claim that the trailing twelve months are loss-making at $4,000+ gold. A base_override built from interim filings is outside a worker's bounded authority (it could not be built exclusively from this run's code outputs, which contain no interim OE legs) — if a current-base read is wanted, that is a main-agent decision.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 27.5%/yr): REAL, and acquisition-driven, not SBC-driven. FY2025 SBC was $19.0M (financials_history.csv) — immaterial. The count growth is equity consideration: the Calibre acquisition closed June 17, 2025 for shares (search-cited), following earlier equity-financed build-out (Greenstone). Diluted weighted shares went 473,546,710 (FY2024) → 630,306,219 (FY2025), and shares outstanding at Dec 31, 2025 were already 785,632,450 (dcf_numbers.json) — the weighted average still lags the post-merger count, so per-share figures face a further mechanical headwind as the full count annualizes. The February 2026 company presentation (search-cited) puts basic shares at 788.3M and fully diluted at 834.5M including convertible notes. The −5.00% growth adjustment (key_numbers.md) is already deducted in the blend; the flag stands as a quality finding regardless.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump in the last 5 yrs): NAMED — the Calibre Mining acquisition, closed June 17, 2025 (search-cited, company Q2 2025 release: full-period Q2 contributions of 72,823 oz from the Nicaragua operations and Pan Mine). ## My thoughts / overlay
- The F is the method working as intended on a hard case: five filed years of owner's earnings contain exactly one positive year, and it is positive by $0.17/share in a year when the gold price did the heavy lifting. Normalizing that away is the point of the median base.
- What the F does NOT capture, stated plainly: (1) FY2025 is only a part-year for Calibre (consolidated from June 17, 2025), so the latest filed year understates the current asset base's full-year cash generation; (2) 2026 interim prints (search-cited above) show revenue and net income running at multiples of the median window on a ~$4,256/oz realized price; (3) the balance sheet has deleveraged since the FY2025 filing — the February 2026 company presentation (search-cited) shows net debt of ~$75M at January 31, 2026 after the term-loan repayment, vs the engine's $1.125B at FY2025 year-end. Anyone re-underwriting this name on current numbers will get a higher base — but building that base requires interim OE legs this run's tools do not produce for an IFRS filer, so it is flagged here rather than faked.
- Watch items: the announced Equinox–Orla Mining combination (search-cited July 2026 release, expected to close Q3 2026) would be another all-share deal — more scale, more dilution, same question. AISC discipline at Greenstone/Valentine is the operational test: consolidated AISC of $1,959/oz (Q2 2025, search-cited) leaves thin margin if gold retraces, and the flagship mines have a record of ramping slower than guided.
- Method range: there isn't one to quote — every defensible base this run can construct (3-yr median −$0.10; latest FY +$0.17) fails to support a DCF at $11.15. The verdict is robust to the base choice even though the business's current run-rate is not.
