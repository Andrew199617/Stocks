## What it is

The world's largest ice cream company, demerged from Unilever on 6 December 2025 and listed 8 December 2025 on Euronext Amsterdam (primary), the London Stock Exchange and the NYSE — in every market the security is the ordinary share itself (612,259,739 issued at admission; Unilever retained ~19.9%, to be sold within five years). Brands: Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto and the Heartbrand (Wall's) — ~21% of the global retail ice cream market, 30 factories, ~16,500 employees. FY2025 revenue €7,910M as filed (fundamentals.json; $9,294,250,000 after the engine's period-end FX conversion), organic sales growth +4.2% (volume +1.5%, price +2.6%). SIC 2024 — Ice Cream & Frozen Desserts.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate

Justification: the world's largest ice cream company (~21% global retail share) with four power brands, a proprietary freezer-cabinet estate and cold-chain scale. Evidence first: gross margin (fundamentals.json, as filed) held 34.08% / 34.91% / 34.63% across FY2023–25 through cocoa and dairy inflation, and FY2025 combined +2.6% price growth with +1.5% volume growth — proven pricing power. The roic_proxy series reads 20.92% (FY2023) and 22.28% (FY2024), but those are carve-out years on an allocated, nearly debt-free capital base and overstate the standalone franchise; FY2025's 214.27% is a demerger artifact (equity fell to €633M as the €2,967M net debt was pushed down) and is given no weight. Standalone through-cycle ROIC is unproven, and the category grows only 3–4%/yr — that caps the score at 6 rather than 7+.

- FOR: ~21% global retail share, #1 position, 30 factories — procurement and cold-chain cost advantage; power-brand portfolio (Magnum, Ben & Jerry's, Cornetto, Heartbrand/Wall's) with demonstrated pricing power; proprietary freezer-cabinet estate and impulse route-to-market rivals must rent or replicate; innovation cadence (150 launches in 2025; Magnum and Cornetto brands grew high-single-digit organically).
- AGAINST: category growth of only 3–4%/yr plus a structural GLP-1 appetite headwind; private label and Froneri competition; standalone economics unproven and levered (€2,967M net debt, 2.4× adjusted EBITDA); Unilever's 19.9% stake is a committed-seller overhang for up to five years; Ben & Jerry's governance conflict (independent board removed, co-founder resigned September 2025, federal litigation live); commodity (cocoa/dairy) and EM FX exposure (Türkiye hyperinflation loss €31M in FY2025).
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay

- The honest headline is the method range, and unusually it does not change the tier: on the latest year ($0.1737/share) the weighted IRR is 3.56% (the override-impact zero-fill world); on the 6-K-bridged TTM ($0.6165/share; run recorded in dcf_numbers_ttm_bridge.json) it is 6.24%; on the pro-forma median ($1.1057/share) it is 9.14%. All three are Tier D. What the base choice changes is the hurdle price, not the verdict at $18.78 — the market is paying roughly 2× the median-base IV@15% ($9.84) for a business whose demerger year earned $0.17/share of owner's earnings.
- The bull case for the stock is a normalization story: separation costs roll off, the guided 40–60bps margin improvement lands, and OE/share recovers toward the €-carve-out prints. Even granting that in full (super bull: 1.25× base, +10.07% start) the IRR is 13.21% — still short of 15%. At $18.78 there is no scenario in the set that clears Andrew's hurdle.
- What could make this interesting later: a price near the IV@15% ($9.84), or the first clean standalone annual print (FY2026, with a full year of standalone interest and no separation costs) proving OE/share back above ~$1.30. The Unilever 19.9% sell-down (registered April 2026) is a known, dated overhang that could supply the price.
- Data quality caveats: carve-out bases, FX-at-period-end conversion, blank debt tags, and a roic_proxy series that is unusable across the demerger (20.92% / 22.28% carve-out, 214.27% artifact). This score is a screening verdict, not a precise valuation.
