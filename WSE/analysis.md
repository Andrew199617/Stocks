## What it is
Cross-border payments and multi-currency account infrastructure: personal and business transfers, the Wise Account and card, and Wise Platform (rails sold to banks/fintechs). FY2026 (company release, research): cross-border volume $243.5B (+31%), active customers 18.9M (+21%), customer holdings $39.0B (+40%), card spend $43.6B (+37%), net revenue $2,502.8M (+19%). Note the engine's revenue tag captures only transaction revenue ($1,893,600,000 in FY2026); fundamentals.json, on the filer's own total basis, shows net revenue of $2,502,800,000 — the difference is principally net interest income on customer balances ($609M in FY2026, per the same release).

## Owner's earnings — the engine's definitions are both unusable, and why
**FY2026 (20-F via EDGAR, from financials_history.csv):** operating cash flow $7,553,900,000; net income $498,700,000; SBC $95,500,000; capex $19,600,000; D&A $14,400,000.
- **Conservative owner's earnings FY2026 (OCF − capex − SBC): $7,438,800,000 = $7.22/share** (key_numbers.md: latest FY OE/share $7.22; normalized 3-yr median $5.36/share). This is customer-balance plumbing, not distributable earnings. The filer's own 20-F states the FY2026 OCF increase "was primarily due to a 36% increase in our customer accounts balances," and its balance sheet carries **funds payable and amounts due to customers of $30,254.2M** at FY2026 ($22,279.9M at FY2025) — an increase roughly equal to the entire OCF figure. The cash side is the mirror image: cash of $27,802,200,000 is mostly safeguarded customer funds (the FY2026 release, research: $14,824.1M in segregated safeguarding accounts plus $7,592.1M in money-market funds and $4,582.7M in liquid securities), against total equity of only $1,925,200,000.
- **Maintenance version FY2026: $9,714,000,000** (financials_history.csv) — also meaningless here: its ΔNWC term (−$9,215,300,000) is the same customer-balance flow with the sign flipped through the netting of customer cash against customer liabilities. Both engine definitions run through the safeguarded-balance balance sheet; neither is used.
- **Base used = median net-income-based owner's earnings per share, declared as a `base_override` in judgment.json (bounded authority (c) — built exclusively from this run's financials_history.csv):** per year, (net income + D&A − min(capex, D&A)) ÷ diluted shares, ΔNWC excluded for the reason above: FY2024 ($501,500,000 + $14,300,000 − $13,400,000) / 1,048,900,000 = $0.4790; FY2025 ($550,300,000 + $9,700,000 − $9,700,000) / 1,045,700,000 = $0.5263; FY2026 ($498,700,000 + $14,400,000 − $14,400,000) / 1,029,700,000 = $0.4843; **median = $0.48/share** (key_numbers.md: base OE/share $0.48). For a payments firm holding customer balances, net income is the honest owner's-earnings proxy (the FUTU/LPLA broker precedent in this sweep): SBC is already expensed inside it, and D&A and capex are trivial and roughly offset. The net-income-only series gives the same median, and the median equals the latest-FY value, so the base does not rest on picking a flattering year — FY2026 net income in fact *fell* 9.4% (fundamentals.json) as operating expenses rose 39% on listing one-offs and investment (company release, research).
- No TTM pin: ttm.py was run and exits non-zero for this filer (no annual FY facts on its companyfacts path for the new holding-company CIK), so no ttm.json exists and no TTM figure was fabricated. Directional corroboration only (research): Q2 FY2027 net revenue +21% YoY to $739.1M with FY2027 guidance raised to the upper half of the 15–20% net-revenue growth range and an income-before-tax margin around the top of the 20–25% range — the FY2026 annual base is not stale-flattering on current prints.
- Balance sheet (FY2026, financials_history.csv): total assets $33,259,800,000, total liabilities $31,334,600,000, equity $1,925,200,000, corporate debt $334,700,000, lease liabilities $149,000,000. The bulk of both sides is customer balances (above), not corporate leverage.

## BASE_OVERRIDE — net-income base (the declared decision)
- **The problem:** the preliminary engine run printed a fake **Tier S** on the OCF definition: normalized base $5.36/share, weighted IRR 78.98%, weighted IV@15% $103.89 (preliminary run, this session, default scenarios). Re-run with this analysis's calibrated scenarios the same OCF normalized base still prints Tier S (weighted IRR 75.29%, IV@15% $96.95, exploratory run). That is the safeguarded-float artifact in full — customer deposit inflows capitalized as owner's earnings — and it is **rejected in full**.
- **The decision (bounded authority (c), Andrew 2026-10-14):** judgment.json carries `base_override = {value: 0.48431582014178887, source: "financials_history.csv construction quoted under Owner's earnings above", reason: <the customer-balance rejection above>}`, and the final engine run was pinned with `--base-oe-ps 0.48431582014178887`. make_score.py verified the pin against the declared override. Note the score's `base_source` label reads "agent_supplied_ttm_override" — that is the engine's generic label for a `--base-oe-ps` pin; this is a declared base_override, not a ttm.py TTM (ttm.py produced no output for this filer).
- **Measured impact:** same calibrated scenarios, three bases — OCF normalized base ($5.36/share): Tier S, weighted IRR 75.29%, IV@15% $96.95 (rejected artifact). FY2025 net-income base ($0.53/share, the best NI year): Tier C, weighted IRR 13.60%, IV@15% $9.53 (exploratory run). ## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
Justification: direct connections to domestic payment systems (Brazil and Japan added in FY2026, Malaysia its ninth in Q2 FY2027, per company releases), a broad licensing footprint (new approvals in South Africa, the UAE and Thailand in FY2026), local netting at scale (cross-border volume $243.5B in FY2026, +31%; 78% of transfers instant in Q2 FY2027) and 85+ Wise Platform partners (Morgan Stanley, Standard Chartered, Nubank, Google Pay, UniCredit) let Wise cut its cross-border take rate to 0.49–0.52% and still earn a 26% pre-tax margin in FY2026 (company release figures, research). Returns on the small own-equity base are high (fundamentals.json ROE: 43.0% FY2024, 31.7% FY2025, 25.9% FY2026). Two evidence caveats: the `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json is negative in all three years and is not meaningful here — its denominator subtracts $27.8B of cash that is mostly safeguarded customer funds; and fundamentals.json presents no gross-margin series for this filer (no COGS/gross-profit tags), so the moat rests on ROE, operating margin (36.6% / 34.7% / 23.6% across FY2024–26, fundamentals.json) and the cost/take-rate record instead. Capped at 6 because pricing power is deliberately given back, float earnings are rate-sensitive, and every corridor is contested.
- FOR: Direct connections to domestic payment systems plus a broad global licensing footprint — infrastructure rivals cannot replicate quickly; structural cost advantage from local netting at scale (volume $243.5B FY2026, take rate cut to 0.49% in Q2 FY2027 while staying profitable); Wise Platform distribution (85+ partners embedding Wise rails); high ROE on a debt-light own balance sheet funding a ~$540M buyback programme (research, 6-K buyback notices).
- AGAINST: No pricing power by design — cross-border take rate fell 0.64% (Q1 FY2025) to 0.52% (FY2026) to 0.49% (Q2 FY2027), so volume must outrun price cuts forever; float-dependent profit — FY2026 net interest income of $609M against income before tax of $660.4M (company release, research), so rate cuts or paying customers more interest compress earnings directly; Revolut, Airwallex, banks and stablecoin rails contest every corridor and customers multi-home; regulatory tail — US national trust bank application denied in July 2026 (company intends to reapply, per Reuters, research), and safeguarding/AML compliance is a permanent cost across dozens of licences.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2024, 2025, 2026):** not a one-off — it is structural, in all three years, and it is the reason both engine OE definitions were removed rather than adjusted (full figures under Owner's earnings). The filer's own 20-F attributes the FY2026 OCF increase primarily to a 36% increase in customer account balances; funds payable and amounts due to customers rose from $22,279.9M to $30,254.2M in the same year. The genuine earnings one-off in the period sits in the NI series itself: FY2026 operating expenses rose 39% on Nasdaq-listing one-off costs and investment (company release, research), which is why FY2026 net income fell despite +19% net revenue — handled by using the 3-yr median NI base rather than the depressed single year alone (the median and the FY2026 figure coincide at $0.48/share).
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED (x1.0):** there is no ADS programme. The 20-F cover registers "Class A ordinary shares, nominal value $0.01 per share" (WSE, Nasdaq Global Select Market) under Section 12(b), and Item 12.D states American Depositary Shares are "Not applicable" — the Nasdaq security is the ordinary share itself, the same share that trades on the LSE and that the company repurchases on both venues (6-K buyback notices, research). judgment.json carries the filing-sourced ads_ratio of 1.0; all per-share figures are per ordinary share, directly comparable to the $10.98 price.
- **THIN_HISTORY (only 3 fiscal years of data):** real — EDGAR history under the new holding-company CIK (2099039) is FY2024–FY2026 only. The predecessor Wise plc traded on the LSE from 2021, but no longer filing history exists under this CIK in the engine's pull, and no figure from outside the filed history was used. - **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 4.2x super-bear IV):** real — the 15-year outcome genuinely ranges from float-income compression to continued 20%+ compounding. Confidence is low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *method range*: on earnings-based bases the result is Tier C on both the median NI base (12.85%, IV@15% $8.77, scored) and the best NI year (13.60%, $9.53); on the engine's OCF base it is a Tier S artifact (75.29%, $96.95) that capitalizes other people's money. Anyone quoting a Wise DCF without stating which side of that line the base sits on is hiding the ball.
- Quality is not the debate: a genuinely advantaged cost/licence network, 25.9% ROE in a down-earnings year, volume +31% and customers +21% in FY2026, and a buyback running. The debate is (a) how much of the $609M net interest income survives lower rates and the guided policy of paying more of it to customers — in FY2026 that one line was nearly equal to total pre-tax profit of $660.4M; and (b) whether volume growth keeps outrunning a take rate that has fallen 15bp in two years by design.
- Regulatory watch item: the July 2026 denial of the US national trust bank application (reapplication intended) does not change current numbers, but the US is Wise's largest and fastest-growing market (Q2 FY2027, research), so a second denial would matter to the bull case, not the base case.
- Margin of safety on the scored numbers: none against the weighted case — the price sits 25.3% above the weighted IV@15% ($8.77). The stock only clears Andrew's 15% hurdle below ~$8.77, and only the bull/super-bull cases (35% combined weight) clear it at the current price.
