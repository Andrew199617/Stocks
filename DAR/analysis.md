## What it is
The world's largest renderer: it collects animal by-products and used cooking oil at 260+ facilities in 15+ countries and converts them into Feed, Food (collagen/gelatin via Rousselot and Gelnex) and Fuel ingredients. It also holds 50% of Diamond Green Diesel (DGD), the 50/50 renewable-diesel JV with Valero (~1.2B gallons/year capacity), equity-method accounted — Darling's cash from DGD arrives as distributions inside operating cash flow, not as consolidated revenue. Latest FY (ended 2026-01-03) revenue was $6,135,877,000.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: scale and route-density in rendering are real barriers, but the output is commodity-priced and a large earnings leg (DGD) is policy-priced. Evidence first: the `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json runs 15.1% → 12.2% → 9.8% → 4.4% → 2.5% over the last five fiscal years — falling through the cycle, not holding above the cost of capital — while gross margin stayed in a narrow 22.4–26.2% band (24.0% latest FY; 26.3% on fundamentals' TTM through 2026-07-04). Stable margin share on commodity volume with declining returns on a growing, acquisition-built asset base is a scale moat, not a pricing-power moat.
- FOR: largest global renderer — ~15% of the world's animal by-products, 260+ facilities; collection route density and rendering-plant permitting are hard to replicate; integrated captive feedstock (animal fats, used cooking oil) makes DGD a structurally low-cost renewable diesel producer; collagen/gelatin scale (Rousselot, Gelnex) at roughly 30% of global collagen supply with growing food/health demand; flexibility to route fats between feed, food and fuel markets captures value across cycles.
- AGAINST: commodity price-taker — fat, protein and renewable diesel prices are set by markets, not by Darling; DGD earnings are policy-dependent (RIN values, LCFS, 45Z production credits, small-refinery exemptions); ROIC proxy fell 15.1% → 2.5% over the last five fiscal years; leverage — net debt $3,848,789,000 is 8.0x normalized OE; acquisition-built growth (Valley Proteins, Gelnex, FASA) carries goodwill/integration risk.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the honest headline:** on the normalized median base the weighted result is Tier D (9.21%, IV@15% $31.34); re-running the identical scenarios on the latest-FY base of $4.10/share (--measure sensitivity, same moat/probs/offsets) gives Tier C — weighted IRR 11.51%, IV@15% $42.74, scenario IRRs 6.27% / 8.97% / 11.09% / 14.01% / 18.97%. The full generated output of that run is committed as dcf_numbers_sensitivity_latest_fy.json (a --measure run at the same $60.52 price with identical moat/probs/offsets and --base-oe-ps pinned to the latest-FY figure); it reproduces every figure in this bullet. Both methods sit below the $60.52 price at a 15% hurdle; the disagreement is about how overvalued, not whether.
- The market is pricing the bull leg: the stock has roughly doubled from its October 2025 level (~$30) on the DGD recovery, and at $60.52 a buyer needs Q2 2026's $2.23/gallon DGD economics — struck at peak RIN/diesel spreads and including a ~$51M tariff recovery — to be the new normal rather than a policy-cycle peak. DGD has printed $0.34/gallon within the last year; that range is the whole investment question.
- The core ingredients business is the ballast: TTM gross margin 26.3% (fundamentals.json), collagen demand growing, and Q2 2026 core execution strong enough that management guided Q3 core ingredients EBITDA of $325–340M (earnings call, as reported). If DGD merely normalizes instead of collapsing, the bear cases here are too harsh — but "normalizes" at $1.00/gallon vs the $2.23 print is still a large earnings step-down from what the price embeds.
- Deleveraging is the fastest self-help lever: at the Q2 2026 pace ($223M of net-debt reduction in one quarter, $73M of buybacks), net debt/OE compresses quickly and per-share OE compounds faster than total OE — the bull cases credit this; a policy reversal at DGD would stop it, which is why leverage and DGD policy are the same risk, not two risks.
- Minority/equity note: DGD is a 50% equity-method JV, so consolidated OE captures only Darling's share via distributions — no minority-interest haircut applies to the OE series, but DGD's own capex is also outside Darling's capex line; DGD distributions are net of JV-level reinvestment, which is the correct owner's-earnings treatment.
