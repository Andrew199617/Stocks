## What it is
The largest publicly traded US regulated water and wastewater utility: exclusive state-granted franchises across 14 states, providing an essential service with inelastic demand. Earnings do not come from selling more water — they come from the rate-base loop: capital invested in mains, plants, lead-line and PFAS work enters rate base, and state commissions set rates to recover costs plus an authorized return on that base. Management's long-term targets affirmed with Q2 2026 results (search results): EPS and dividend growth 7-9%, 2026 adjusted EPS guidance $6.02 to $6.12 affirmed after Q2 2026 EPS of $1.61 on revenue of $1.36 billion (+6.2% YoY), a 2026 capital plan of $3.7 billion ($1.8 billion invested in H1 2026), and $19-$20 billion of investment planned through 2030 supporting 8-9% rate-base growth. The proposed merger with Essential Utilities is progressing through state approvals and is expected to close by the end of Q1 2027 per Q2 reporting.

## Moat: 8/10 — strong, regulated and durable, but return-capped
Justification: Exclusive regulated water/wastewater franchises across 14 states, inelastic essential demand, and irreplaceable long-lived infrastructure give a strong durable moat, but regulators cap allowed returns, affordability and rate-case lag limit pricing power, and the model requires continuous debt and equity funding for rate-base capex.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated franchises — no competing water provider in served territories; essential, inelastic demand for water and wastewater service; irreplaceable infrastructure — mains and plants last generations, replacement cost is prohibitive; 14-state diversification spreads single-state rate-case risk; rate-base model: authorized returns on invested capital drive 7-9% long-term EPS guidance.
- AGAINST: Regulators cap allowed returns and impose rate-case lag — pricing power is granted, not owned; affordability and political pressure as bills rise with PFAS, lead-line and main-replacement capex; capex permanently exceeds operating cash flow — funded by rising debt and share issuance (share count +2.33%/yr); rising interest expense on the debt-funded capital program (Q2 2026 interest expense rose year over year per search results); Essential Utilities merger needs remaining state approvals, expected close by end of Q1 2027.
- A moat of 8 would give CAP = 12.4 years under the sweep formula (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP, 15-year projection) — but the engine runs no projection here at all, because the normalized base is negative (key_numbers.md shows CAP as — for this run). The moat is recorded for comparability; it does not rescue a negative base.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range here is binary, not a spread of tiers: on the conservative definition there is no tier at all (F, no IV, no IRR at any scenario weighting); on the maintenance definition the FY2025 OE total is positive and large. That definitional flip — not growth, not the moat — is the whole finding for AWK, and it is exactly what SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH warns about.
- Quality is not the question the F answers. This is a regulated monopoly compounding EPS at a guided 7-9%, growing its dividend, with revenue up 10.06% in the latest fiscal year (key_numbers.md) and rate authorizations continuing in 2026 per search results. None of that is capturable by an OCF-minus-all-capex screen while the company is in a multi-decade, regulator-approved investment supercycle ($19-$20 billion planned through 2030 per search results).
- The real risks are funding and regulation, not demand: capex exceeds OCF every year, the shortfall is funded with debt and new shares (share count CAGR 2.33%/yr — dilution is a real, ongoing cost of the model), interest expense is rising with the debt-funded program, allowed returns are set by 14 state commissions with lag, and the Essential Utilities merger still needs remaining state approvals.
- Price that clears 15%: none can be stated. With a negative normalized base the engine produces no IV@15% at any price input, so there is no hurdle-clearing price to quote from the computed outputs — saying so in words rather than calculating one, per the hard rule. At $131.71 the stock plainly does not clear a 15% owner's-earnings hurdle on this method; whether it clears a regulated-utility return hurdle is a question this sweep's model cannot answer.
