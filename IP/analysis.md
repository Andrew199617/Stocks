## What it is
Global fiber-based packaging: Packaging Solutions North America, Packaging Solutions EMEA, and (until its sale, completed January 2026) Global Cellulose Fibers. On January 31, 2025 IP completed the all-stock acquisition of DS Smith, which roughly doubled the revenue base — FY2025 net sales $23,634,000,000 vs $15,835,000,000 in FY2024 (financials_history.csv) — and the diluted share count rose from 354,200,000 (FY2024) to 505,700,000 (FY2025). In January 2026 the company announced a plan to separate into two independent regional packaging companies (North America and EMEA); in October 2026 press reports (Financial Times, via search) put private-equity bids for the EMEA business at under $6B, vs the ~$9.9B paid for DS Smith, and the company confirmed early-stage talks while keeping the spin-off on track. Q2 2026 (release, via search): net sales $6.00B, loss from continuing operations $12M, adjusted EBITDA $587M, operating cash flow $526M, free cash flow -$7M; full-year 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance reaffirmed at $3.20–$3.40B, with Q3 guided at $780–$830M including an ~$85M Pine Hill mill closure hit. That guidance step-up is the bull case's fuel; it is not yet in the filed cash flows.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak / narrow
Justification: scale and network density in North American containerboard/corrugated packaging plus integrated recycled fiber give a narrow cost advantage, but the product is a commodity priced off indices, gross margin has stayed at 28.0–29.6% for five years, returns on capital are low to negative, and the EMEA acquisition destroyed value within a year — no durable pricing power.
- FOR: largest North American containerboard/corrugated scale — mill and box-plant network density lowers delivered cost and lead times regionally; integrated recycled-fiber collection and mill system (a real, if modest, input-cost and supply-security advantage); long-standing relationships with large national food, beverage and e-commerce shippers, with multi-plant supply agreements sticky at the contract level if not the price level.
- AGAINST: commodity product priced off published indices — customers can dual-source from Smurfit Westrock, Packaging Corp and regional mills; gross margin stuck at 28.0–29.6% over the last five fiscal years (fundamentals.json annual: 28.56% 2021, 28.44% 2022, 28.20% 2023, 28.03% 2024, 29.61% 2025) — no pricing-power expansion through a cycle; the DS Smith EMEA business took a ~$2.5B goodwill impairment within a year of acquisition and is now reported saleable for under $6B vs ~$9.9B paid; returns on capital are low/negative — FY2025 net loss $3,516,000,000 and TTM net income -$3,438,000,000 (fundamentals.json) against a $37,964,000,000 asset base (financials_history.csv); cyclical end demand plus OCC/energy/transport cost exposure that cannot be passed through reliably. The roic_proxy series in fundamentals.json is null for this filer (operating income is not carried in its annual rows), so the ROIC evidence is taken from the filed net-income and margin history above — which is exactly why a moat above 3 would need a written excuse, and there isn't one.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the honest headline, and it does not rescue the stock:** on the rejected median base ($1.76/share, same moat/probs, run this session before the TTM pin), the weighted IRR was 7.31% with IV@15% $13.18 — Tier D. On the TTM base actually used: 4.63% / $5.84 — Tier D. Even that median run's super-bull alone reached only a 12.18% IRR. There is no defensible base in the filed record on which $34.05 clears a 15% hurdle; it does not clear 10% either.
- What the market is paying for at $34.05 is not current owner's earnings (OE yield 2.29%) — it is the separation/sale story: a North-America-only IP, delevered by an EMEA exit and the $1.5B GCF proceeds, with the guided H2 2026 EBITDA step-up delivered. That may be a fine event trade, but it is a sum-of-the-parts bet on a sale price the reporting currently puts *below* what was paid, not an earnings-yield investment.
- The dividend ($1.85/share TTM DPS, fundamentals.json) is not covered by TTM conservative OE ($0.78/share) — it is being paid out of the balance sheet while capex runs at $2,155,000,000 TTM. That is sustainable only if the guided EBITDA step-up is real.
- Watch items for a re-run: the EMEA sale/spin terms when filed, Q3 2026 (guided EBITDA $780–$830M despite the Pine Hill hit — the first hard test of the step-up story), and whether TTM capex falls back toward the FY2024 level of $921,000,000 for the old perimeter (scaled for the combined footprint) once transformation spend rolls off.
