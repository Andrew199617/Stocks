## What it is
Liberty Media is now a pure-play motorsport owner. After the split-off of Liberty Live Holdings (Live Nation stake + QuintEvents) completed December 15, 2025, the remaining company is the Formula One Group: Formula 1, MotoGP (Dorna — acquisition closed July 3, 2025), and minority investments. FWONK is the Series C tracking stock of that group (Series A FWONA / B / C FWONK are the same economic group). FY2025: consolidated revenue $4,482M (Formula 1 $3,873M + MotoGP $325M in the segment table), operating income $577M. F1 standalone 2025: revenue up 14% to $3.9B, operating income up 28% to $632M, Adjusted OIBDA up 20% to $946M, attendance 6.75M, live viewership up 21% (Q4/FY2025 press release).

## Moat: 7/10 — strong
Justification: Liberty owns the exclusive commercial rights to the world's two premier motorsport properties — Formula 1 (Concorde Agreement with all teams and the FIA signed through 2030) and MotoGP (acquired July 2025) — with contracted, escalating media-rights, sponsorship and race-promotion fees, proven pricing power (F1 revenue +14% and Adjusted OIBDA +20% in 2025), and an asset-light model in which the teams fund the cars and R&D while Liberty collects the fees. Against that: the rights stack resets at renewals, team payments absorb roughly half of F1 revenue and the teams bargain collectively, the calendar is exposed to geopolitics and host-country politics, and the post-MotoGP balance sheet is levered.
- FOR: exclusive long-term commercial rights to F1 and MotoGP — scarce, irreplaceable global sports IP under one owner; contracted escalating fees across media rights / sponsorship / race promotion with proven pricing power (F1 revenue +14%, OIBDA +20% in 2025; Las Vegas GP extended 10 years through 2037); asset-light — teams fund cars, drivers and R&D, Liberty collects fees, so incremental margins are high and capex is minimal; audience momentum (attendance 6.75M, live viewership +21% in 2025) plus a second property (MotoGP) to apply the same commercial playbook to.
- AGAINST: economics reset at rights renewals (Concorde/FIA, broadcasters, promoters) — the 2030 Concorde expiry is the fade horizon; team payments consume roughly half of F1 revenue and teams negotiate collectively; calendar/geopolitics risk is live, not theoretical (the 2026 Bahrain GP was rescheduled to Malaysia; Wells Fargo cut its target $105 → $96 on September 29, 2026 citing Middle East risk); post-MotoGP leverage (net debt 5.27x normalized OE) thins the equity cushion; popularity is concentrated in one sport's stars and competitive storyline.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:shares / OVERRIDE_APPLIED:shares**: resolved by the filing-sourced override — see OVERRIDE. The gap is a companyfacts dimension artifact, not missing disclosure: the diluted WASO is printed in every 10-K EPS note.
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.46 months ago): ttm.py was run and its full output is saved to ttm.json. The engine's median base was used instead, per the stale-FY fallback, with the TTM OE *total* ($799M, ~4% above FY2025) used as the growth sanity check.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: identified — three breaks. (1) SiriusXM: consolidated until its September 2024 split-off, then discontinued; the engine's 2023 OCF ($2,464M) is the pre-recast total including $1,813M of what the FY2025 10-K now shows as discontinued operating cash flow, while its 2024/2025 OCF legs are continuing-operations — the 2023 OE is therefore overstated (see Owner's earnings). (2) Braves split-off (July 2023) and (3) Liberty Live split-off (December 15, 2025) with Live Nation presented as discontinued. Pre-2023 revenue in the history ($9–11B years) is the old multi-group consolidation and no growth input was taken from it: the 5-yr revenue CAGR was explicitly discarded in calibration.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range >3x over 5 years): real and mostly structural, not cyclical — the range is manufactured by the basis breaks above plus the depressed FY2024 continuing OE ($462M). Handled with the bear-tilted probabilities (45% on bear-or-worse) and by using the median rather than any single year.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): the one-offs are in discontinued operations and in the attribution, not in continuing OCF. 2024: attributable net income −$2,063M contains a $2,819M non-cash goodwill impairment of the Sirius XM reporting unit (FY2024 10-K critical audit matter; discontinued net loss −$2,431M in the FY2025 10-K presentation) recorded around the SiriusXM split-off — it never touches continuing OCF ($567M). 2023: attributable net income $761M (continuing $73M + discontinued $889M, FY2025 10-K) vs total-basis OCF $2,464M that still consolidated SiriusXM's cash flows. - **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (5.47x): real — the range between a levered rights-reset bear case and a two-property compounder is genuinely wide. Confidence is held at low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note the spread does not straddle the hurdle — even the super-bull IRR (10.69%) is below 15%.

The MotoGP acquisition (€4.2B, closed July 3, 2025) was debt-funded: attributed Formula One Group debt was $5,056M at September 30, 2025 (Q3 2025 release), including $1,172M of MotoGP credit facilities at 5.6x MotoGP leverage. ## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range here is unusually well-behaved in one respect: every defensible base lands in Tier D. The engine median used ($2.9767) is the *highest* defensible base (flattered by the 2023 basis break); a continuing-basis median would be lower, the (unusable) TTM pin prints $2.3709, and even the super-bull scenario on the highest base returns 10.69%. There is no honest calibration of this model that gets FWONK near a 15% hurdle at $91.43 — the market is pricing F1/MotoGP as scarce trophy IP on OIBDA multiples, not on owner's earnings, and at a 3.26% OE yield with 5.27x leverage the DCF simply cannot follow it there.
- Quality is not the issue: the moat is real (7/10), the model is asset-light, SBC is trivial, and F1's contracted escalators give unusual revenue visibility through the 2030 Concorde term. The issue is purely price versus cash conversion: team payments, interest on the MotoGP debt, and taxes stand between OIBDA and owner's earnings.
- Price that clears 15%: the weighted IV@15% is **$23.45** — i.e. the stock would need to fall roughly three-quarters from $91.43 before this DCF clears Andrew's hurdle, and even the super-bull IV@15% ($56.05) sits well below the current price. That gap is the finding: FWONK is a quality compounder priced far beyond what an owner's-earnings screen can underwrite.
- Residual risks to the (already bearish) numbers: a 2030 Concorde renegotiation that shifts economics to the teams, further calendar disruption in the Middle East, and MotoGP integration underperformance against a 5.6x-levered acquisition.
