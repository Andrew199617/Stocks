## What it is
Global marketplace for used commercial assets: Ritchie Bros. (heavy equipment, trucks, agriculture — live/online auctions, IronPlanet, Marketplace-E, GovPlanet), IAA (salvage-vehicle auctions for insurers), plus data/services (Rouse, SmartEquip, VeriTread). Ritchie Bros. acquired IAA in a stock-and-cash deal closed March 2023 (~$7B, search result) and renamed itself RB Global in May 2023. Revenue is a mix of service revenue (auction commissions/fees) and inventory sales revenue. Price $84.30 (nasdaq_quote_api), market cap $15.76B — in scope under $40B.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: Real marketplace liquidity and scale in two niches, but measured returns on the post-IAA capital base are mediocre, the strongest salvage rival is a better operator, and current pricing evidence (take rate) points the wrong way. ROIC proxy (fundamentals.json): 25.4% in FY2022 (pre-IAA, small capital base), then 4.6% (2023), 7.8% (2024), 7.6% (2025) — since the IAA deal the franchise has earned *below* a plausible cost of capital on its goodwill/intangible-heavy base, which caps the moat at 5 under the rubric. Gross margin is not available in fundamentals.json for the recent fiscal years (null), so the margin evidence is operating margin: 12.8% (2023) → 17.8% (2024) → 15.5% (2025), 16.0% TTM — mid-teens, not the profile of a wide-moat marketplace.
- FOR: Liquidity network effects — the largest buyer/seller pool for used heavy equipment and transportation assets globally, 40+ auction sites (search result); IAA's entrenched insurer relationships in salvage vehicles, a two-player market; physical yard/site network and local permits that take years to replicate; ancillary data/services (Rouse valuations, SmartEquip, VeriTread) that deepen seller lock-in; trusted brands (Ritchie Bros. since 1958, IAA).
- AGAINST: Copart is the entrenched, higher-margin rival in salvage and sets the competitive bar IAA has not cleared on returns; ROIC proxy only 7.6–7.8% in 2024–25 post-IAA — the deal's capital is not yet earning its keep; service revenue take rate fell 110bp to 20.0% in Q2 2026 (search result) — pricing/mix pressure, not demonstrated pricing power; GTV is cyclical (construction, transport, auto volumes) and salvage supply depends on insurer total-loss decisions; growth is partly bought (BigIron, May 2026, $316,600,000; Blackmon, April 2026, $17,000,000 — search results), which keeps adding goodwill.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.5 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, legs quoted above. TTM OE/share $2.96 vs latest FY / median $3.49: the TTM deterioration is disclosed and adopted, not avoided.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 18.6%/yr): an IAA artifact. The CAGR straddles the deal — diluted shares went 111,886,025 (FY2022) → 168,200,000 (FY2023, part-year weighted after the March 2023 stock issuance) → 185,300,000 (FY2024, first full year) → 186,900,000 (FY2025) → 187,200,000 TTM. The recent pace is ~0.5%/yr, and the direction has flipped: the company repurchased ~1.5M shares for $150,000,000 in Q2 2026 and raised the dividend from $0.31 to $0.33 (search results). SBC is modest and already deducted in full: $66,000,000 in FY2025 (1.4% of revenue), $57,900,000 TTM. The engine's −5pp share-count growth adjustment is therefore too harsh for the forward path — but it is *not* corrected in the inputs, because the blend it adjusts is itself inflated by the same deal (see BASIS_BREAK); the growth recalibration to observed post-IAA prints supersedes both errors, and leaving the adjustment uncorrected keeps the result conservative.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: named — the March 2023 IAA acquisition (revenue $1,733,800,000 FY2022 → $3,679,600,000 FY2023; debt, capex, D&A and share count all step up the same year). Growth inputs were chosen only from post-IAA signals (latest FY YoY, TTM YoY, Q2 2026 YoY, FY2026 GTV guidance); no CAGR that straddles 2023 is used anywhere in the final run.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024, 2025): structural, not a one-off. FY2025 net income $428,400,000 vs OCF $978,200,000; FY2024 $413,100,000 vs $932,000,000. The gap is D&A of $483,400,000 (2025) / $444,400,000 (2024) — dominated by amortization of the IAA acquired-intangible base — which depresses net income without touching cash flow. The OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is unaffected by the divergence; no tax deposit, impairment, or one-off gain drives it.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 3.5x normalized OE): balance sheet from the filings (financials_history.csv, FY2025): cash $531,500,000, total debt $2,471,500,000, net debt $1,940,000,000, plus operating lease liabilities $1,585,000,000 the engine's net debt excludes. Against adjusted EBITDA the picture is milder — adjusted net debt was $2,379,200,000 = 1.6x adjusted EBITDA at June 30, 2026 (search result) — but on owner's earnings the leverage is real, and it is why the bear side carries 40% combined probability and the super-bear OE multiplier (0.75x) is paired with a 0% growth start.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the gap between the naive engine output and the recalibrated one: the preliminary run (median base, unadjusted 27.78% blended growth) printed weighted IRR 15.11% / IV@15% $85.25 — a fake Tier B manufactured entirely by CAGRs straddling the IAA acquisition. Recalibrated to post-IAA prints, the method range is narrow and unanimous: median base → weighted IRR 8.44%, IV@15% $38.21, Tier D; TTM base (final) → 7.56%, $32.32, Tier D. Every scenario on the final run, including super bull (11.73%), is below the 15% hurdle.
- The business itself is fine — TTM revenue +9.7%, net income +15.9%, raised GTV guidance, buybacks begun, dividend growing. The problem is purely price and cash conversion: at $84.30 the market is paying for the artifact growth rate, while TTM owner's earnings actually fell 15.1% YoY (fundamentals.json) on the inventory/WC build.
- What would change the verdict: a sustained OCF recovery back to FY2024–25 conversion (OE/share back above ~$3.50 and growing), or a much lower price. The weighted IV@15% is $32.32; even the median-base IV@15% is $38.21. The stock clears a 15% hurdle only in the low-to-mid $30s on these inputs.
- Capex note: the engine's capex is PP&E only ($249,500,000 TTM); management's FY2026 capex guidance of $350–$400M (search result) uses a broader definition including intangible additions — if the broader figure is the true maintenance-plus-growth spend, owner's earnings are overstated here and the verdict is, if anything, generous.
