# UAL — United Airlines Holdings, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Consumer Discretionary / Airlines (passenger). Price $103.45 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap $34.88B in universe.csv (engine intraday estimate $33.98B on 328.5M diluted shares — in scope either way, under $40B).

## What it is
The largest U.S. airline by mainline fleet (~1,066 aircraft at year-end 2025, plus 424 United Express regional aircraft) and the largest U.S. carrier across both the Atlantic and Pacific. Seven hubs — Chicago O'Hare, Denver, Houston Intercontinental, Newark Liberty, San Francisco, Washington Dulles, Los Angeles — a founding Star Alliance membership, and the MileagePlus loyalty program. The strategy ("United Next") is upgauging to larger mainline aircraft, premium-heavy interiors (Polaris, premium economy, Starlink Wi-Fi rolling out fleetwide), and international expansion, funded by the largest aircraft order book in the industry (250+ deliveries expected by April 2028). FY2025 revenue was a company-record $59.07B (+3.5%), net income $3.35B. H1 2026 (EDGAR 10-Q): revenue $32.28B (+13.5% YoY), net income $1.50B. Q2 2026: revenue $17.67B (+16.4% YoY), adjusted EPS $1.99, premium revenue +16.4%, loyalty +11.3%, cargo +22.6% — but jet fuel expense surged ~84% YoY on U.S.–Iran tensions, and management warned higher fuel could add ~$6B to 2026 costs vs its start-of-year assumptions. Full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance was nonetheless raised to $9–11; Q3 guided to $2.50–3.50. Q3 2026 reports Oct 20, 2026.

## Owner's earnings
- **Engine conservative OE (OCF − capex − SBC):** FY2025 $2,557M = $7.78/share; the 3-yr median is also $7.78/share (the last-3 median lands on FY2025 itself: FY2023 OE was −$260M / −$0.78/share on a $7.17B capex delivery spike, FY2024 was $3,830M / $11.49). OE positive only 3 of the last 5 years (2021: −$40M; 2023: −$260M) — this is a cyclical, capex-lumpy series, not a stable earnings stream.
- **SBC tag trap (same failure as HIG):** the engine's SBC line reads $0 because United stopped tagging `ShareBasedCompensation` after 2013. Its real stock comp is tagged `AllocatedShareBasedCompensationExpense`: $80M (FY2023), $142M (FY2024), **$148M (FY2025)** — ~$0.45/share the engine's base does not subtract. Per the standing rule (SBC is always a real cost), the final base corrects for it.
- **TTM build (EDGAR 10-Q YTD, filed 2026-07-16):** TTM OCF = FY2025 $8,431M − H1 2025 $5,927M + H1 2026 $6,409M = **$8,913M**; TTM capex (`PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets` — aircraft/property) = FY $5,874M − H1'25 $2,520M + H1'26 $3,015M = **$6,369M**; TTM OE pre-SBC = **$2,544M**. Note what the TTM says: H1 2026 OE was $3,394M vs $3,407M in H1 2025 — *flat* despite revenue +13.5%, because the Q2 fuel shock ate the entire revenue gain. TTM revenue $62,902M is +6.5% vs FY2025; TTM net income $3,496M.
- **Base used — TTM OE less real SBC:** ($2,544M − $148M FY2025 Allocated SBC run-rate; no H1 YTD SBC tag exists in the 10-Q) ÷ 326.7M diluted shares (H1 2026 YTD) = **$7.33/share**. Price = 14.1× that base; OE yield 7.1%.
- **Maintenance version (for the range, not the base):** FY2025 maintenance OE (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC) = $4,248M = $12.93/share — far above the conservative figure because capex ($5.87B) runs at 2.0× D&A ($2.94B) in the United Next fleet build. Treating all of that capex as growth would flatter the base: over a 15-year horizon a fleet must be replaced, and deliveries are lumpy, not optional. The conservative all-capex definition stays the base; the gap is the bull debate, documented in the overlay.
- Balance sheet (folded in explicitly): engine net debt **$15,324M** at FY2025 (debt current $4,096M + long-term $17,170M − cash $5,942M) = **6.4× normalized OE** on the adjusted base. The company's own leverage framing is kinder (TTM net leverage 2.0× at Q1 2026, on an EBITDA denominator; $17.2B of available liquidity; $3.1B of debt paid down in Q1 2026 alone, partly refinanced with $2B of unsecured bonds) — but on distributable owner's earnings this is a heavily levered equity, and levered OE is what this DCF capitalizes.

## Moat score: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate; the best network in a bad industry
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 4) = **7.2 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Hub geography that cannot be replicated — fortress positions at Newark (the New York gateway), San Francisco (the Pacific gateway), Denver, Houston and O'Hare, with slot/gate constraints at those airports protecting incumbents; United is the largest U.S. carrier on both the Atlantic and Pacific. (2) MileagePlus — a high-margin, cash-up-front loyalty economy (co-brand cash sales arrive before the travel obligation) that also proved itself as financeable collateral in 2020; loyalty revenue grew +11–13% in H1 2026. (3) Scale and premium mix: the world's largest mainline fleet, upgauging that structurally lowers unit cost per seat, and premium revenue compounding at +14–16% — United and Delta form the industry's premium tier. (4) Star Alliance feed and corporate contracts that fill the international network.
- **AGAINST:** (1) The core product is a price-transparent commodity seat — any fare premium must be re-won daily against Delta, American and the ULCCs, and basic economy exists precisely because the marginal customer buys on price. (2) The industry's economics leak to its inputs: unionized labor captures productivity gains at each contract cycle (a tentative flight-attendant agreement for ~30,000 employees is a current cost item; CASM-ex rose ~5.9% YoY in Q1 2026), and fuel is an exogenous tax — Q2 2026's ~84% fuel spike erased a +16% revenue quarter's profit growth. (3) Returns through the cycle are poor: OE was negative in 2 of the last 5 years and the equity carries 6.4× net debt/OE. (4) The moat's own assets are capped — regulators, not United, set the capacity ceilings now binding at Newark and O'Hare. That is a 4: real, durable network advantages *relative to other airlines*, inside an industry structure that has never let any airline keep wide-moat economics.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the full TTM build above (EDGAR 10-Q YTD): TTM OE pre-SBC $2,544M = $7.79/share on H1 2026 diluted shares — essentially identical to FY2025's $7.78, so staleness does not distort the level; the correction that matters is SBC (below), giving the used base of $7.33. Latest quarter Q2 2026 (10-Q filed 2026-07-16); Q3 reports Oct 20, 2026.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (6.4× normalized OE):** Real and central, not a tagging artifact: $21.27B gross debt vs $5.94B cash at FY2025. Mitigants are genuine — $17.2B liquidity at Q1 2026, $3.1B paid down in Q1, maturities termed out, 2.0× leverage on the company's EBITDA definition — but the DCF capitalizes post-interest OE, and at 6.4× OE a single bad year (2023 printed *negative* OE with far less drama than 2020) puts the equity on the wrong side of its debt load. This is why the bear weight is 45% and why no scenario gets a heroic multiplier. The verdict already stresses the bear cases; it does not forgive them.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT (>60% revenue jump/drop in last 5 yrs):** Identified — it is the pandemic, not a divestiture, M&A or restatement: revenue fell −64.5% in 2020 ($43.26B → $15.36B), then rebounded +60.4% (2021) and +82.5% (2022) on the recovery. Consequence handled: the 5-yr revenue CAGR (+30.9%) is a recovery artifact and is given no weight anywhere in this analysis; growth inputs were set from FY2025/TTM/H1 prints, not from any CAGR that straddles 2020–22.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs):** Real and structural: OE/share ran −$0.12 (2021), +$3.78 (2022), −$0.78 (2023), +$11.49 (2024), +$7.78 (2025) — driven jointly by demand cyclicality and delivery-lumpy capex ($7.17B in 2023 vs $5.62B in 2024). The 3-yr median base helps (it happens to equal FY2025), but the honest response is distributional, done via probabilities: 45% combined bear weight and a super-bear OE multiplier of 0.75 on a *negative* growth start. Confidence capped at low.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2023, 2024, 2025 — gaps >40%):** Structural airline mechanics, not a KO-style one-off: OCF exceeded net income by $4.29B (2023), $6.30B (2024) and $5.08B (2025), and D&A ($2.67–2.94B/yr) explains only part of it. The rest is cash collected before it is earned — advance ticket sales (air traffic liability) and MileagePlus co-brand cash sales, both of which grow the balance of travel/loyalty obligations owed later — plus working-capital rebuild after the pandemic. Consequence: OCF flatters distributable earnings in growth years for an airline; that is precisely why the base charges *full* capex against it and why the maintenance definition (which would add back most of the gap) is rejected as the base. No single-year one-off was found and none is needed to explain the flag.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (preliminary run: 4.5×):** The final run's spread compresses to 3.6×, but only because the recalibrated growth starts are deliberately narrower — the underlying uncertainty (fuel, cycle, 6.4× leverage) has not shrunk. Treated as still live: confidence is low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- No BASE_DIVERGENCE flag (median = latest FY by construction here — the distortion is *inside* the 3-yr window, 2023's negative year, and is handled under OE_VOLATILE), no SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (airlines fit the OE/capex model mechanically; the model's weakness here is cyclicality and leverage, both flagged), no LOW_OE_YIELD / HIGH_TV_SHARE (yield 7.1%, TV 18.2%).

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** the preliminary engine run (median base $7.78 pre-SBC, default probabilities/offsets, moat 5) printed a 13.7% weighted IRR and IV@15% of $91.95 — upper Tier C. The final run (TTM SBC-adjusted base, print-checked growth, bear-widened probabilities) prints 10.1% / $62.35 — bottom of Tier C. Both defensible methods land in C; the argument is only about where in C. The verdict is sized to the conservative end, per the spread and leverage.
- The rejected upside lens: if most United Next capex were truly growth (maintenance OE $12.93/share), the same model would screen far higher. It is rejected as the base because aircraft capex over a 15-year horizon is substantially replacement — but it is the honest bull debate, and it is why the super-bull case still only reaches a 16.4% IRR at this price: even *that* scenario keeps the conservative OE definition and merely grows it faster.
- Quality vs price: United is executing well — record revenue, premium/loyalty compounding, guidance raised into a fuel shock, debt being paid down, double-digit pre-tax margins targeted for 2027. None of that is the debate. The debate is that at $103.45 the market already pays for most of it: the price sits above even the bull case's IV@15% ($84.67) and only ~11% below the super bull's ($116.04). A buyer here needs the fuel recovery, the 2027 margin delivery, *and* no recession, all three, just to earn a mid-teens return on conservative OE.
- Red flags to monitor: fuel (a further spike overwhelms the guided recovery path and forces deeper capacity cuts), the flight-attendant contract cost landing, CASM-ex running ~+6%, OEM delivery slippage against the 250+ aircraft plan, Newark/O'Hare regulatory capacity caps, and any demand crack in premium — the mix the whole thesis leans on.
- **Hurdle math:** on the final numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$62** (weighted IV@15%); with a 20% margin of safety the buy price is ≈ **$50**. On the kinder preliminary method the clearing price is ~$92 — still below the current $103.45.
