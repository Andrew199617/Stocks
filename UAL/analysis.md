## What it is
The largest U.S. airline by mainline fleet and the largest U.S. carrier across both the Atlantic and Pacific. Seven hubs — Chicago O'Hare, Denver, Houston Intercontinental, Newark Liberty, San Francisco, Washington Dulles, Los Angeles — a founding Star Alliance membership, and the MileagePlus loyalty program. The strategy ("United Next") is upgauging to larger mainline aircraft, premium-heavy interiors (Polaris, premium economy, Starlink Wi-Fi), and international expansion, funded by the largest aircraft order book in the industry. FY2025 revenue was a company-record $59,070M (+3.5% YoY per the engine's latest revenue YoY component), net income $3,353M. Latest reported quarter Q2 2026 (web research): revenue $17.67B (+16.4% YoY), adjusted EPS $1.99, premium revenue +16.4%, loyalty revenue +11%, cargo revenue +23% — but jet fuel expense surged ~84% YoY on Middle East tensions, and management warned higher fuel could add ~$6B to 2026 costs vs its start-of-year assumptions. Full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance was nonetheless raised to $9–11, with management expecting to recover 80–90% of the fuel increase through pricing in Q3 and 100% by Q4. Q3 2026 reports Oct 20, 2026.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate; the best network in a bad industry
Justification: Irreplicable hub geography (Newark, San Francisco, Denver, Houston, O'Hare fortresses; largest U.S. carrier across Atlantic and Pacific), MileagePlus loyalty economics with cash-up-front co-brand sales, scale/upgauging unit-cost advantage and a premium mix compounding at double digits — against a price-transparent commodity seat product, union labor and fuel capturing the economics each cycle, negative OE in 2 of the last 5 years, and 6.4× net debt / normalized OE.
- FOR: Hub geography that cannot be replicated — fortress positions at Newark (the New York gateway), San Francisco (the Pacific gateway), Denver, Houston and O'Hare, with slot/gate constraints protecting incumbents; largest U.S. carrier on both the Atlantic and Pacific.
- FOR: MileagePlus — a high-margin, cash-up-front loyalty economy (co-brand cash sales arrive before the travel obligation) that also proved itself as financeable collateral in 2020; loyalty revenue grew +11% in Q2 2026.
- FOR: Scale and premium mix — the world's largest mainline fleet, upgauging that structurally lowers unit cost per seat, and premium revenue compounding at +16.4% in Q2 2026; United and Delta form the industry's premium tier.
- FOR: Star Alliance feed and corporate contracts that fill the international network (contracted business revenue +27% in Q2 2026 per web research).
- AGAINST: The core product is a price-transparent commodity seat — any fare premium must be re-won daily against Delta, American and the ULCCs, and basic economy exists precisely because the marginal customer buys on price.
- AGAINST: The industry's economics leak to its inputs — unionized labor captures productivity gains at each contract cycle (the flight-attendant contract ratified in 2026 carries an average ~31% base-pay rise and a $740M retro-pay/bonus pool per web research), and fuel is an exogenous, unhedged tax — Q2 2026's ~84% fuel spike erased a +16% revenue quarter's profit growth.
- AGAINST: Returns through the cycle are poor — OE was negative in 2 of the last 5 years and the equity carries 6.36× net debt / normalized OE.
- AGAINST: The moat's own assets are capped — regulators, not United, set the capacity ceilings now binding at Newark and O'Hare.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## DCF — v5 final run (2026-10-14)

Against actual prints: FY2025 revenue +3.52% (engine's latest YoY component), Q2 2026 revenue +16.4% YoY flattered by fare pass-through of the fuel shock, while Q2 profit *fell* YoY despite that revenue growth because fuel expense rose ~84%. A 9.7% OE growth *start* would assume instant, sustained margin expansion the prints do not show. Scenario starts were therefore recalibrated down via offsets (−0.157 / −0.097 / −0.057 / −0.007 / +0.043 vs g_base), with reasons: base +4.0% (above the weak current OE print only because management guides 80–90% fuel-cost recovery in Q3 and full recovery by Q4, capacity was cut to defend yields, and premium/loyalty mix is compounding double digits — but well below revenue prints because the fleet-capex cycle absorbs cash); bear 0.0% (fuel stays high, recovery incomplete); super bear −6.0% (recession or a second fuel leg — mild by UAL's own history, where OE was negative as recently as 2023); bull +9.0% (revenue growth with the guided margin recovery arriving); super bull +14.0% (the Q2 revenue print sustained with margin delivery — given only 8% weight). ## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 9.43 months ago): attempted resolution via ttm.py — it exited non-zero ("sbc: no post-FY YTD fact (no 10-Q since the 2025-12-31 fiscal year end)"), so no TTM pin exists and the engine's median base is used, per the v5 rules, with the failure recorded here. Mitigant: the median and latest-FY bases coincide at $7.33, and the latest reported quarter (Q2 2026, web research) shows the business running at roughly the base's level — record revenue offset by the fuel shock — so staleness is unlikely to flatter the base. Q3 2026 reports Oct 20, 2026.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs): identified — it is the pandemic, not a divestiture, M&A or restatement: revenue fell from $43,259M (2019) to $15,355M (2020), then recovered through $24,634M (2021) and $44,955M (2022) (financials_history.csv). Consequence handled: the 5-yr revenue CAGR (+30.93%) is a recovery artifact and is given no weight anywhere in this analysis; growth inputs were set from FY2025 / latest-quarter prints, not from any CAGR that straddles 2020–22.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 yrs spans >3×): real and structural — conservative OE totals ran −$278M (2021), +$1,158M (2022), −$340M (2023), +$3,688M (2024), +$2,409M (2025), driven jointly by demand cyclicality and delivery-lumpy capex ($7,171M in 2023 vs $5,874M in 2025). The 3-yr median base helps, but the honest response is distributional, done via probabilities: 45% combined bear weight and a super-bear OE multiplier of 0.75 on a negative growth start. Confidence capped at low.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): structural airline mechanics, not a KO-style one-off. From financials_history.csv: OCF vs net income was $6,911M vs $2,618M (2023), $9,445M vs $3,149M (2024), $8,431M vs $3,353M (2025), and D&A ($2,671M / $2,928M / $2,939M) explains only part of each gap. The rest is cash collected before it is earned — advance ticket sales (air traffic liability) and MileagePlus co-brand cash sales, both of which grow the balance of travel/loyalty obligations owed later — plus post-pandemic working-capital rebuild. Consequence: OCF flatters distributable earnings in growth years for an airline; that is precisely why the base charges *full* capex against it and why the maintenance definition (which would add back most of the gap) is rejected as the base. No single-year one-off was found and none is needed to explain the flag.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 6.36× normalized OE): real and central, not a tagging artifact — net debt $15,324M at FY2025. Mitigants are genuine (substantial liquidity, debt being paid down, maturities termed out, and a much kinder leverage ratio on the company's EBITDA definition), but the DCF capitalizes post-interest OE, and at 6.36× OE a single bad year (2023 printed *negative* OE with far less drama than 2020) puts the equity on the wrong side of its debt load. This is why the bear weight is 45% and why no scenario gets a heroic multiplier. The verdict already stresses the bear cases; it does not forgive them.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (preliminary run: super-bull IV 4.47× super-bear IV): the final run's spread compresses to 3.58×, but only because the recalibrated growth starts are deliberately narrower — the underlying uncertainty (fuel, cycle, 6.36× leverage) has not shrunk. Treated as still live: confidence is low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- No SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (airlines fit the OE/capex model mechanically; the model's weakness here is cyclicality and leverage, both flagged), no LOW_OE_YIELD / HIGH_TV_SHARE (yield 7.09%, TV share 18.24%), no BASE_DIVERGENCE (median = latest FY here).

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** the preliminary v5 engine run (median base $7.33, default probabilities/offsets, moat 5) printed a 13.17% weighted IRR and IV@15% of $86.63 — upper Tier C. The final run (print-checked growth, bear-widened probabilities, moat 4) prints 10.09% / $62.38 — bottom of Tier C, 0.09pp above the C/D line. The v4 run a day earlier (hand-built TTM SBC-adjusted base of $7.33/share, same scenario design) printed 10.1% / $62.35 — the v5 mechanics reproduce it almost exactly, now with the SBC correction done by the engine instead of by hand. Every defensible method lands in C; the argument is only about where in C. The verdict is sized to the conservative end, per the spread and leverage.
- The rejected upside lens: if most United Next capex were truly growth (maintenance OE FY2025 $4,248M vs conservative $2,409M), the same model would screen far higher. It is rejected as the base because aircraft capex over a 15-year horizon is substantially replacement — but it is the honest bull debate, and it is why the super-bull case still only reaches a 16.38% IRR at this price: even *that* scenario keeps the conservative OE definition and merely grows it faster.
- Quality vs price: United is executing well — record revenue, premium/loyalty compounding, guidance raised into a fuel shock, debt being paid down. None of that is the debate. The debate is that at $103.50 the market already pays for most of it: the price sits above even the bull case's IV@15% ($84.71) and close to the super bull's ($116.09) — a buyer here needs the fuel recovery, the margin delivery, *and* no recession, all three, just to earn a low-teens return on conservative OE.
- Red flags to monitor: fuel (a further spike overwhelms the guided recovery path and forces deeper capacity cuts — December 2026 schedule trims were already reported in September), the ratified flight-attendant contract's cost landing in CASM, OEM delivery slippage against the order book (737 MAX 10 certification delays already forced schedule changes), Newark/O'Hare regulatory capacity caps, and any demand crack in premium — the mix the whole thesis leans on.
- **Hurdle math:** on the final numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$62** (the weighted IV@15% of $62.38); a buyer wanting a margin of safety needs a price meaningfully below that. On the kinder preliminary method the clearing price is $86.63 — still below the current $103.50.
