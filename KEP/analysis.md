## What it is
South Korea's state-controlled electric utility: the sole nationwide transmission and distribution operator, buying and generating power through its generation subsidiaries (including KHNP for nuclear) and selling to effectively every household, business and factory in the country under government-set tariffs. Listed on the Korea Exchange (common stock) and on NYSE as ADSs — one ADS represents one-half of one common share (FY2025 20-F cover and Item 9.A). The Korean government and Korea Development Bank are the controlling holders, and KEPCO doubles as a price-stabilization policy tool — that dual role, not competition, is what drives its economics.

## Moat: 4/10 — durable national franchise, confiscated economics
Justification: Exclusive national T&D franchise with captive essential-service demand and generation scale, but the regulator/government — also the controlling holder — sets tariffs politically and froze them through the 2021-2023 fuel spike, so the franchise has no pricing power and its measured ROIC through the cycle is deeply negative (roic_proxy -47.01% in 2022, -6.36% in 2023, 9.78% in 2024). A durable monopoly whose economics are capped and periodically confiscated is a weak moat, not a wide one.
- FOR: Sole nationwide transmission and distribution operator for South Korea, with generation concentrated in its genco subsidiaries and KHNP (nuclear) — an exclusive, essential-service national franchise; Captive demand: effectively every Korean household, business and factory buys delivered power through KEPCO; share count has been flat at 641,964,077 for a decade — no dilution; Scale and state backing: FY2024 revenue $62,953,220,200 (engine, FX-converted) and continued access to bond funding through the crisis years despite the losses.
- AGAINST: No pricing power: tariffs are set politically and the fuel-cost adjustment is capped — for Q4 2026 the formula indicated KRW 7.30/kWh and the charge was frozen at the KRW 5.00 ceiling for the 18th consecutive quarter (search results); Measured returns through a cycle are value-destructive: fundamentals.json roic_proxy 22.73% (2015) collapsed to -47.01% (2022) and -6.36% (2023) before recovering to 9.78% (2024); gross margin was -43.0% in 2022 and -2.5% in 2023; The state is the controlling holder and uses KEPCO as a price-stabilization policy tool — the 2021-2023 tariff freeze through the fuel spike is what produced the crisis losses in the base window; Heavy leverage funds the grid build: engine net debt $12,427,953,360 with a further $2,515,630,080 of lease liabilities, and consolidated liabilities of KRW 210.7 trillion at H1 2026 (search results).
