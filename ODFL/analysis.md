# ODFL — Old Dominion Freight Line, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. In scope: market cap ~$37.7B (universe) / ~$38.4B at the engine's $181.30 price — under the $40B cutoff.

## What it is
The largest U.S. less-than-truckload (LTL) carrier by revenue: ~11-12% share of the LTL market, a nationwide network of ~260 service centers, single-network model running regional, inter-regional and national LTL plus smaller container-drayage/logistics offerings. A pure freight-cycle play on U.S. industrial/retail volumes (roughly 40% manufacturing / 30% retail exposure), run with uncommon discipline: it invests through the cycle, holds ~35% spare capacity, and competes on service — 99% on-time, claims ratio 0.1%, multi-year #1 in LTL customer satisfaction — rather than price.

TTM OE is +17% vs FY2025 almost entirely on the capex trough: management guides FY2026 capex to ~$380M (H2-weighted, incl. pulled-forward tractor/trailer buys), so a normalized-capex TTM base is ~$4.79/share (OCF $1,394.1M − $380M − $12.9M = $1,001.3M ÷ 209.0M). That variant is carried in the method range below.

## Moat score: 7/10 — strong
**FOR:**
- Structural cost advantage proven through cycles: operating ratio 70.1% in Q2 2026 (72.9% H1) vs an LTL industry norm in the mid-80s — a ~15-point structural gap no rival has closed in two decades. It comes from network density, a non-union, low-turnover workforce, and owning (not leasing) its service-center real estate.
- Service quality that converts to pricing power: 99% on-time, 0.1% claims, #1 satisfaction rankings → a 4.9% general rate increase effective Oct 5, 2026 and Q2 2026 LTL revenue/cwt +5.5% ex-fuel while volumes fell — customers pay the premium and stay.
- Network + capacity as a share-taking weapon: ~260 service centers with ~35% spare capacity, built by investing through downturns (including capacity absorbed after Yellow's 2023 collapse). When freight recovers, ODFL can say yes while constrained rivals can't — it has been the industry's biggest share winner over the last 10 years (to ~11-12%).
- Fortress balance sheet (below) funds that through-cycle investment without dilution or distress risk.
**AGAINST:**
- LTL is ultimately a service commodity sold on annual bids: switching costs are low, shippers multi-source, and there are no network effects — the moat is execution, re-earned daily, not lock-in.
- Capable, well-funded rivals are closing the service gap: XPO, Saia, FedEx Freight and Estes are all investing in terminals, technology and service quality, and Yellow's exit removed the weakest competitor, not the strongest.
- Deep cyclicality the moat cannot repeal: revenue fell −6.3% in 2023 and −5.5% in 2025; tons/day were still −4.1% in Q2 2026 and −0.9% in Aug 2026. Pricing held, volumes did not.
- Cost inflation is structural (driver wages, equipment prices, real estate) and the GRI mechanism only passes it through with a lag; fuel is a pass-through that flatters revenue in both directions.
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 7 = **11.1 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to 2.5% terminal over the CAP, 15-yr projection).

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override above — TTM OE $5.27/share from Q2 2026 10-Q YTD OCF/capex, replacing the $4.05 median. Staleness here was hiding a real inflection (yield + capex trough), and the TTM base is what the final run uses. Caveat carried openly: the same TTM window contains a capex trough (TTM capex $279M vs ~$380M guided for FY2026 and $757-771M in 2023-24); the normalized-capex variant ($4.79/share) is in the method range and changes nothing.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 5.2× super-bear):** Real but mechanical at a 2.9% OE yield — small base cash flow vs a large price fans any growth range out in IV terms. It caps confidence at medium. Decisively, the spread does not straddle the hurdle: even the super-bull IV ($99.20) is 45% below the price, so there is no tier ambiguity to size around.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND (share count −2.2%/yr):** Credited once, in the engine's growth adjustment (+2.19pp) — not double-counted in the scenario stories. H1 2026 actuals confirm the pace ($239.7M of repurchases, diluted shares 209.0M vs 212.8M a year earlier, $1.31B of authorization left). Note the overlay point: buybacks at 34× owner's earnings destroy value per dollar vs buybacks at the hurdle price — the tailwind is real but expensively bought at $181.
- **Balance sheet (no HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag fired — resolved explicitly):** Engine FY2025 net debt −$100.1M (cash $120.1M, debt $20.0M). Updated at Q2 2026: cash $283.9M, only $20.0M of senior notes outstanding, nothing drawn on the $400M revolver → **net cash ~$264M**. The OE stream is an equity cash flow (OCF is after the trivial interest bill), so net cash is not added to IV; it is downside protection and the reason ODFL can invest through the cycle. It does not rescue the valuation — $264M is $1.26/share against a $132/share gap to weighted IV.

## Thoughts / overlay
- This is one of the best-run industrial companies in the U.S. at a price that pays for a decade of flawless compounding: 34.4× TTM owner's earnings (which are themselves flattered by a capex trough), a 2.9% OE yield, for a business whose revenue shrank in two of the last three years and whose volumes are still negative.
- **Method range:** preliminary run (median base $4.05, moat 5) → IV@15% $36.79, IRR 5.1%. Final (TTM base $5.27, moat 7) → $48.95, 6.0%. Normalized-capex TTM variant ($4.79) → ~$44.50, still D. Every defensible input set lands deep in Tier D; even the 10% super-bull tail (9.9% IRR) doesn't clear 10%, let alone 15%.
- Red flags, honestly sized: none are about the company's integrity or balance sheet. The risks are (1) valuation — the entire question; (2) freight-cycle duration — the "recovery" so far is yield and fuel surcharges, not tons; if tons keep falling, OR leverage runs in reverse; (3) Q2 2026 quality — $17.2M of disposal gains flattered overhead and the OR print; (4) capex normalization is guided, not hypothetical (~$380M in 2026, with $115M pulled forward/added mid-year) — forward OE will not repeat the TTM capex trough; (5) buybacks and a raised capex plan at peak multiples are procyclical capital allocation, even if affordable.
- SBC/dilution: clean — SBC ~$13M/yr, share count shrinking ~2%/yr.
- **Hurdle math:** the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is **$48.95**; with a 20% margin of safety, **~$39**. The market price is 3.7× the hurdle-clearing price.
