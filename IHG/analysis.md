# IHG — InterContinental Hotels Group PLC
v5 run, 2026-10-15. In scope (market cap $25.59B at $164.25, nasdaq_quote_api). UK hotel franchisor filing on Form 20-F under IFRS; ADS ratio 1.0 declared in judgment.json (see ADS flag below), so per-share figures and the NYSE price are on the same basis.

All figures below are copied from dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, or the cited H1 2026 results reporting — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Asset-light global hotel franchisor/manager: 20 brands (InterContinental, Holiday Inn and Holiday Inn Express, Crowne Plaza, Kimpton, Six Senses, voco, Garner and others) across 100+ countries. IHG predominantly franchises and manages hotels owned by third parties and collects royalty and management fees, keeping only a small owned/leased estate. H1 2026 reporting put the system at 7,109 hotels and 1,049k rooms, with a pipeline of 348k rooms (2,385 hotels) equal to 33% of current system size. Growth drivers are RevPAR (price × occupancy) and net room growth at a high fee margin — not property appreciation.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong, not the widest
Justification: 20 brands, a 145m+ member loyalty programme and global distribution create real owner switching costs and pricing power, proven by a 65.9% fee margin and an asset-light model needing almost no capital. It sits behind Marriott/Hilton on scale and loyalty depth, owners can reflag at contract end, demand is cyclical (FY2020 collapse), and rivals are pressuring owner fees — durable, but contestable.
- FOR: 20-brand portfolio (InterContinental, Holiday Inn family, Crowne Plaza, Kimpton, Six Senses) across 100+ countries — distribution owners pay to access; IHG One Rewards loyalty programme (145m+ members) driving direct bookings and repeat stays; asset-light fee model with fee margin 65.9% in H1 2026 and only $28M capex in FY2025 — high returns on capital and strong cash conversion; development pipeline of 348k rooms, 33% of current system size, giving visible multi-year fee growth; proven pricing power — fee margin up 1.2 points in H1 2026 with ADR +2.5% alongside occupancy gains.
- AGAINST: third in scale behind Marriott and Hilton, with smaller loyalty ecosystems and less owner leverage in contested markets; owners can reflag at contract end and conversion brands lower switching frictions across the industry; cyclical RevPAR (FY2020 revenue $2,394M vs $4,627M in FY2019) — demand tracks the consumer/business cycle and geopolitics; owner fee pressure as rivals cut fees and grant concessions to hold owners (2026 earnings-season reporting); buyback-funded leverage — net debt 3.4x normalized owner's earnings.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.46 months ago): `ttm.py IHG` was run and exited 3 — it cannot compute a TTM for this IFRS filer because it reads us-gaap facts only (no us-gaap annual facts for OCF/SBC/shares/capex under its candidate tags). That is the expected IFRS limitation documented in the sweep spec, so the engine's median base is used, with the reason stated here. The gap is bridged qualitatively with the H1 2026 release (six months to 30 June 2026): RevPAR +4.1%, fee business revenue +7%, operating profit from reportable segments up 10%, adjusted EPS +13% — current trading supports the base rather than undermining it, and no TTM base within any reasonable range closes a valuation gap of this size.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 5.05%/yr): already credited via the +5.00pp growth adjustment in g_base — and deliberately not taken at face value in the final scenarios: the recalibrated offsets pull the base start to 8.0% because the forward buyback (~$950M on a $25.59B market cap) supports a smaller shrink rate, and printed 2-yr OE/share CAGR is only 4.08%. No double-count in the verdict.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (20-F filer): resolved by declaration under bounded authority — judgment.json carries ads_ratio {ratio: 1.0} sourced from the filer's own Annual Report 2025 / Form 20-F, whose shareholder information states each ADR represents one ordinary share. Per-share figures needed no scaling; the flag confirms comparability with the $164.25 ADS price.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 3.41x normalized OE): real and folded into the verdict. The leverage is buyback-funded, not operating-distress leverage: H1 2026 reporting put net debt at 2.63x trailing adjusted EBITDA with the fee business generating cash at a 65.9% margin and minimal capex. It still caps the verdict — in the bear/super-bear cases levered OE is what gets squeezed first, which is why those scenarios carry OE multipliers of 0.90/0.75 and 35% combined probability. Negative book equity (−$2,736M) is a buyback artifact, noted, not treated as insolvency.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (fired on the preliminary pass at 4.44x): after the growth recalibration the spread is 3.28x and the flag no longer fires. Confidence is still held at medium, not high, given the leverage and cyclicality above.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a quality-versus-price result, not a quality dispute. The business prints a 65.9% fee margin, needs $28M of capex against $898M of operating cash flow, grows the system ~5% net with a pipeline at 33% of the system, and returns cash aggressively ($950M buyback for 2026, 42% complete by 30 June; interim dividend up 10%). None of that is in question. What is in question is paying $164.25 for $4.88 of normalized owner's earnings — a 2.97% starting yield. At that yield, even the super-bull path (15% growth start, 1.25x OE) returns only 9.94%.
- Method range: the verdict is unusually robust to method. The preliminary pass on the engine's unadjusted defaults (base growth 12.73%, super-bull start 24.73%) printed a weighted IRR of 7.87% and weighted IV@15% of $65.22 — also Tier D. My recalibrated pass is lower (6.91% / $53.81) because it refuses the COVID-recovery CAGR and the full buyback credit, but the tier does not move: every defensible calibration lands in D, and no scenario in either pass clears even a 10% hurdle.
- The price that clears 15% on the weighted case is the weighted IV@15% itself: **$53.81**. Sizing to the conservative end (bear IV@15% $39.99) is the appropriate anchor given the leverage — a buyer at ~$40 is paid even if the bear case lands. Between here and there, IHG is a good business at roughly 3x a hurdle-clearing price.
- Watch items that could change the picture: a RevPAR recession would hit the levered OE the flags warn about; owner-fee concessions spreading from rivals to IHG would compress the fee margin that carries the moat score; buybacks executed at these prices destroy per-share value relative to buying at or below IV — capital allocation discipline at $164 is itself a (mild) moat-against signal.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (weighted IRR 6.91% on the v5 final inputs; no tier override — the numbers tier and the judged tier agree)
