## What it is
Jackson, Michigan regulated utility holding company. Its principal subsidiary, Consumers Energy, is Michigan's regulated electric and natural-gas utility (monopoly service territories); the smaller NorthStar Clean Energy segment owns independent power generation (Dearborn Industrial Generation and renewables). In July 2026 management completed a strategic review of NorthStar and is exiting non-utility renewables development, retaining Michigan-based assets, to concentrate on the regulated business (search results). The regulated plan is rate-base compounding: the FY2025 10-K presents Consumers' planned capital expenditures of $24.1 billion through 2030, and CMS projects $4.4 billion of capital expenditures in 2026 alone. Management guides long-term adjusted EPS growth of 6–8%, reaffirmed 2026 adjusted EPS guidance of $3.83–$3.90 and introduced 2027 guidance of $4.08–$4.17 after Q2 2026 (search results).

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Exclusive Michigan regulated franchise (Consumers Energy electric + gas monopolies) with captive essential-service demand and a $24.1B capex program through 2030 that compounds rate base — a durable, visible earnings machine, and management guides 6–8% long-term EPS growth. Capped at 6 because the regulator, not the company, sets returns: the MPSC granted 9.8% ROE and $139M of a $240M request in the Oct 2026 gas case, the Attorney General intervenes against increases, and the build is funded with $18,248M of net debt plus recurring equity issuance (share count +1.25%/yr) — durability is high, pricing power is nil.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated Michigan franchise — Consumers Energy holds monopoly electric and gas service territories with captive, essential-service demand; Rate-base compounding: the $24.1B planned capital program through 2030 (10-K) enters rate base and earns a regulator-allowed return; Michigan's 2023 clean-energy law locks in a multi-decade investment runway (renewables, storage, grid, gas safety); Regulatory recovery is recurring in practice: a $277M electric increase took effect May 2026 and a $139M gas increase was approved Oct 2026 (search results).
- AGAINST: No pricing power: the MPSC sets the allowed ROE (9.8% granted in the Oct 2026 gas case) and granted $139M of a $240M gas request — returns are capped and political, with the Michigan AG intervening in rate cases; Growth is externally funded: net debt $18,248M and a share count growing 1.25%/yr from recurring equity issuance dilute per-share compounding; Capex structurally exceeds operating cash flow, so the build depends on continuous capital-market access; Single-state concentration: Michigan weather (Q2 2026 earnings fell on mild weather), affordability politics, and storm costs all sit with the equity.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.
