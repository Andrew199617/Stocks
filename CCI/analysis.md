# CCI — Crown Castle Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: market cap ~$34.8B < $40B.

## What it is
The largest US tower REIT, and — since May 1, 2026 — a pure-play one: ~40,000 US towers leased to the big wireless carriers under long-term contracts with ~3% annual escalators (weighted-average remaining tenant-contract term ~6 years; $23.7B of contracted amounts at Dec 31, 2025). On May 1, 2026 it closed the sale of its Fiber Solutions business (to Zayo) and Small Cell business (to an EQT fund company, Arium Networks) for $8.5B gross / ~$8.4B net, then repurchased $1.0B of shares and repaid more than $7B of debt. What remains is a slow-growth, high-payout rental business: Q2 2026 site rental revenues $967M (−4% YoY), Adjusted EBITDA $675M (−4%), AFFO $488M / $1.13 per share (+11%, flattered by lower interest). FY2026 outlook (continuing operations only): site rental revenues $3,855M (−5% vs 2025), Adjusted EBITDA $2,690M (−6%), AFFO $1,975M / $4.59 per share (+4% / +5%). Dividend reset to $1.0625/quarter = $4.25/yr (was $6.26 in 2024, $4.75 declared in 2025) — a 5.34% yield at $79.56 and ~93% of guided AFFO.

## Owner's earnings (SEC EDGAR, filing-direct) — the base had to be rebuilt on a continuing basis
The engine's raw output is not usable as printed for CCI, for a structural reason: the May 2026 fiber/small-cell exit is a full basis break, and the engine's history mixes bases.
- Engine FY2025 conservative OE: OCF $3,057M − capex $182M − SBC $73M = **$2,802M = $6.41/share**; normalised base (median of last 3 FY) **$6.41/share**. Both are distorted in opposite directions at once: consolidated OCF includes **$1,185M of FY2025 operating cash flow from the discontinued Fiber Business** (separately tagged in EDGAR), while the capex tag the engine reads (`PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets`) is continuing-basis in the restated filings — so fiber's ~$1B/yr of build capex (FY2025 discontinued investing cash flow was −$980M) is *not* charged. The $6.41 base capitalises a business Crown Castle no longer owns, at a capex figure that never paid for it.
- Maintenance version FY2025: $1,019M ($2.33/share) — anchored on GAAP net income, which is polluted by the fiber impairments/disposal losses below; not used as the anchor.
- OE positive 5 of the last 5 years on the engine's (mixed-basis) definition; on a continuing basis it is also positive every year (continuing income from operations was $1,103M in FY2025).

**STALE_FY resolution — TTM base through Q2 2026, continuing operations, from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (final engine pass used `--base-oe-ps 3.6927`):**
- TTM consolidated OCF = FY25 $3,057M − H1'25 $1,473M + H1'26 $1,040M = **$2,624M**
- TTM discontinued-operations OCF = FY25 $1,185M − H1'25 $581M + H1'26 $108M = **$712M** (fiber ran until the May 1, 2026 close; Q2'26 discontinued OCF was actually −$105M on separation/sale costs)
- **TTM continuing OCF = $2,624M − $712M = $1,912M**
- TTM capex (continuing-basis tag) = $182M − $80M + $116M = **$218M** (charged in full, including the rising land-purchase capex: Q2'26 capex $59M, +48% YoY)
- TTM SBC = $73M − $36M + $47M = **$84M** (total-company SBC charged entirely to continuing — conservative; the fiber share is immaterial at this scale)
- **TTM continuing conservative OE = $1,912M − $218M − $84M = $1,610M = $3.69/share** on H1'26 diluted shares of 436M.
- Known conservatism in this base, quantified as a sensitivity rather than baked in: the TTM window carries the *pre-sale* debt stack's interest for ~10 of 12 months (US GAAP OCF is after cash interest). The debt is already repaid — Q2 2026 total debt was $18,239M vs $27,120M at FY2025 — and the company's May 1 outlook update quantified the earlier-than-expected repayment as $40M less 2026 interest for ~2 months, implying roughly **$240M/yr of run-rate interest savings** not yet in the TTM base. Adding it back gives $1,850M = **$4.24/share** — corroborated from the other direction by guided FY2026 AFFO of $4.59/share (AFFO adds back SBC and straight-line items the conservative OE charges). Sensitivity run on $4.24 below.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong: scarce contracted sites, concentrated tenants
FOR — (1) Scarcity and permitting: ~40,000 towers in zoned, permitted locations that are slow and often impossible to replicate; a carrier's network topology is built around specific sites, so relocation is expensive and disruptive — real switching costs. (2) Contract structure: ~6-year weighted-average remaining term, $23.7B contracted, ~3% fixed escalators, and high incremental margins on added tenants/equipment (Q2 2026 organic contribution to site rental billings was +3.9% excluding DISH terminations and Sprint cancellations). (3) Scale: the only large US-listed pure-play tower company after the fiber exit, with an investment-grade balance sheet, 100% fixed-rate debt and ~7-year weighted maturity.
AGAINST — (1) Extreme tenant concentration: AT&T alone is ~28% of site rental revenues and the top three carriers are the overwhelming majority; the "customers" can merge (Sprint cancellations are still rolling through revenue) or fail (DISH terminations cost $49M of site rental revenue in Q2 2026 alone). (2) No pricing power beyond the contractual escalator — growth is whatever carrier capex cycles deliver, and US carrier spending is ex-growth; site rental revenue is currently *shrinking* (−4% Q2, −5% guided FY2026). (3) Demonstrated capital-allocation drag: a decade of fiber investment ended in a ~$5.0B goodwill impairment (2024) and a sale at a held-for-sale valuation loss — the tower moat did not protect shareholders from the strategy built on top of it. (4) The dividend consumes ~93% of guided AFFO, leaving almost nothing retained — the equity cannot compound internally the way a wide-moat compounder's does.
CAP from moat 6: clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP, 15-yr projection).

## FLAGS — resolving every fragility flag
Final run flags: STALE_FY, HIGH_NET_DEBT, NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE. Two further flags fire in substance and are resolved here: BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT and SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH.
- **STALE_FY** (FY2025 ended ~9.4 months before this run) — Resolved with the filing-derived continuing TTM base above ($3.69/share), built line-by-line from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures including the separately tagged discontinued-operations OCF. The TTM is *lower* than both FY bases because it strips out the sold fiber business — that is the point of it.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** — The engine's 16.7× uses the stale pre-sale balance sheet (net debt $27,021M at FY2025). Post-sale reality (Q2 2026 10-Q): total debt $18,239M, cash $1,042M → **net debt $17,197M = 10.7× continuing TTM OE and ~6.4× guided FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA**. The >$7B repayment is real deleveraging, but 10.7× OE / ~6.4× EBITDA is still a levered equity: a 10% OE disappointment is a much larger equity event, and the dividend already takes ~93% of AFFO. Mitigants are genuine: 100% fixed-rate, ~7-yr weighted maturity, ~$4.5B revolver availability. Fitch was reported to have moved its outlook to negative in 2026 (secondary reporting) — consistent with leverage remaining the binding constraint. The bear/super-bear OE multipliers (0.90/0.75) are where this stress is priced.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025) — Found and named: (1) **2024: the ~$5.0B non-cash goodwill impairment of the Fiber reporting unit** drove GAAP net income to −$3,903M while OCF was $2,943M; the year also carried ~$100M of restructuring charges and a $106M small-cell asset write-down (both flagged in the FY2024 release). (2) 2025: net income of $444M includes held-for-sale valuation losses on the Fiber Business (~$1.5B valuation allowance recorded in 2025, disposal loss net of tax −$1,575M for the year) against OCF of $3,057M. (3) Structural REIT wedge in all three years: ~$690-790M/yr of depreciation & amortisation is non-cash. Direction matters: GAAP income *understates* cash generation here, and none of it is a KO-style tax-deposit distortion — the OCF-based conservative OE is the right series, provided it is put on the continuing basis (done above). Book equity fell from $15,666M (2023) to $6,273M (2025) through these charges.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (manual — the engine did not fire it because the mixed-basis OE/share series happens to look flat at ~$6.4) — This is the central fact of the run: the Fiber/Small Cell businesses (FY2025 discontinued revenues $2,173M, OCF $1,185M) were sold on May 1, 2026. Every historical input straddles the break: consolidated OCF includes fiber, the capex tag is continuing-only, the revenue tag is services-only (site rental is lease income — see growth recalibration), and the balance sheet was transformed by $8.4B of proceeds. Resolution: headline base rebuilt on continuing operations only (above); growth inputs taken from continuing prints and continuing guidance only; net debt taken from the Q2 2026 post-sale balance sheet; the method range across bases reported in the DCF section.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (manual — universe sector Real Estate / REIT; the engine did not emit it) — Fires by rule: owner's-earnings/capex DCF fits REITs poorly (real-estate depreciation depresses the NI-anchored maintenance definition; AFFO is the sector's native measure). Per the sweep rule: **confidence low, tier soft.** Mitigation done, not waved: the AFFO cross-check (17.3× guided AFFO; dividend-yield-plus-growth ≈ 8%) independently lands on the same Tier D answer as both OE bases, so the mismatch is not what's keeping CCI below the hurdle — the price is.
- **Flags checked and not firing:** WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (2.7× — wide in dollars, below the flag threshold; confidence is low anyway via SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH), DILUTION (share count essentially flat: 437M FY2025 diluted → 436M H1 2026; the $1.0B post-sale buyback was completed in Q2 2026 and shows up in the AFFO/share guide, not yet in weighted counts), BIG_NET_CASH (no — net debt, above), THIN_HISTORY / OE_VOLATILE (10 clean fiscal years; continuing OE is stable, the volatility in the filed series is the fiber exit, resolved under BASIS_BREAK).

## My thoughts / overlay
- Crown Castle did the right thing — exited a fiber business it had impaired by ~$5B, repaid >$7B of debt, reset the dividend to a (barely) covered $4.25 — and the stock *still* doesn't clear a 15% hurdle, or a 10% one, at $79.56. The remaining business is a ~3%-growth contracted-rent stream with a 5.3% dividend yield, ~93% payout, and ~6.4× leverage. That arithmetic is a high-single-digit return at this price under every base tried, including the company's own AFFO lens.
- The Oct 9 spectrum spike is doing real damage to the entry price: satellite/spectrum tenancy is a plausible *option*, but no lease exists, and the DCF above already pays for a version of it in the super bull (10% weight, +8% start) — which alone reaches only 10.3%.
- **Hurdle-clearing price:** the weighted IV@15% is **$31.30** on the headline base and $35.97 on the interest-adjusted sensitivity — i.e. the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only roughly 55-60% below the current price, and a margin of safety against the headline case means the mid-$20s. At anything near $79.56 the buyer is underwriting multiple expansion or a leasing re-acceleration that has not appeared in a filing.
- What would change the tier: two consecutive filings of positive *reported* site rental revenue growth with AFFO/share compounding ≥8%, or the price falling into the $30s. Neither is close today.
