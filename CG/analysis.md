## What it is
One of the world's largest diversified alternative asset managers, in three segments: Global Private Equity, Global Credit, and Carlyle AlpInvest (secondaries / co-investment / portfolio finance). Total AUM was a record $485.5B at June 30, 2026, with fee-earning AUM of $334.4B (segment AUM: Global Credit $211.1B, Global Private Equity $162.7B, AlpInvest $111.7B). The manager's own economics are management fees, fee-related performance revenues and realized carried interest on other people's capital — reported by the company as segment fee-related earnings (FRE) and distributable earnings — while its GAAP statements consolidate a slice of the funds (including CLO vehicles) whose assets it manages. (Fortitude Group, the reinsurance platform Carlyle advises, is not one of the consolidated entities — it appears in the 10-K only in AUM contexts, its general-account assets sitting inside fee-earning AUM under the strategic advisory services agreement.) That consolidation gap is the whole story of this run. Q2 2026 was a record quarter on the manager's own basis: FRE $358M (+11% YoY, 47% FRE margin), distributable earnings $472M ($1.07/share, the highest in nearly four years), $16.8B of inflows in the quarter and $56B over twelve months, dividend held at $0.35/share.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range (the real headline):** on the standardized consolidated-OCF definition every variant is Tier F — normalized median base -$3.55/share, latest-FY base -$10.11/share, TTM base -$12.33/share. On the maintenance definition (NI-based, $901,400,000 in FY2025) the earnings sign flips positive but the figure inherits GAAP marks and is not an owner's-earnings base this screen can capitalize. On the manager's own segment definition — FRE of $1.2B in FY2025 growing +12%, distributable earnings of $472M in Q2 2026 alone — the business is plainly cash-generative, pays a $1.40/share annual dividend and bought back $915,300,000 of stock TTM (fundamentals.json); a DCF on that definition is a different model, not a variant of this one, and building its base is a main-agent decision, not a worker override. Anyone quoting this Tier F without the misfit attached is hiding the ball in the opposite direction from the usual one.
- Quality on the manager's own terms is genuinely improving: record AUM ($485.5B) and FRE, Global Credit and AlpInvest at record distributable earnings in Q2 2026, $56B of LTM inflows, pending fee-earning AUM of $28B (+57% YoY) as a visible future fee pipeline, and a fundraising super-cycle claimed by management into 2027–28 as flagship funds return to market. None of that is measurable in OE terms on this screen, and none of it is priced here.
- Risks to monitor on the manager's own terms: a private-credit default wave hitting Global Credit (now the largest segment at $211.1B) and consolidated-fund marks at once; fundraising cyclicality if allocators pause; performance-revenue lumpiness if exit markets close (Q1 2026 GAAP revenue missed heavily on unrealized marks before Q2 reversed it); SBC scale ($414,100,000 TTM and rising with headcount in wealth / AI initiatives); and the stock's own de-rating — at $36.38 it sits near its 52-week low after trading near $69.85 within the last year, which says the market is pricing the cycle risk, not the FRE record.
- **Hurdle math:** none exists on this screen — a negative base computes no IV and no hurdle-clearing price (score.json: iv_at_15 null, implied_irr null). The name does not clear 15% at any price *on current standardized OE numbers*, which is a statement about the definition, and it should be re-scored on the standardized screen only when a filed year prints positive consolidated OCF − capex − SBC, or sooner if a main-agent FRE-based base is authorized.
