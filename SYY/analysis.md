## What it is
The largest foodservice distributor in the US (~17% share, vs US Foods and Performance Food Group as the other members of the "Big Three"; the rest is ~15,000 regional/local distributors). Sysco delivers food and related products to restaurants (local independents and national chains), healthcare, education, and lodging customers, with US Foodservice Operations the dominant segment plus International and SYGMA (customized chain distribution). This is a scale-logistics business earning thin spreads on FY2026 revenue of $84,553,000,000 (financials_history.csv).

Latest earnings: Q4 / full-year FY2026 (fiscal year ended 2026-06-27, reported August 4, 2026). FY2026 sales increased 3.9% (engine rev_yoy_latest 3.91%); Q4 sales rose 4.7% year over year, with U.S. Foodservice volume up 2.5% and U.S. local volume up 2.6% (earnings release, via search). FY2026 GAAP net income was $1,757,000,000 and adjusted EPS was $4.61 for the full year (release via search). FY2027 guidance (standalone, reaffirmed September 9, 2026) is sales growth of 6–7% and adjusted EPS growth of 9–11% to $5.02–$5.12 on a 53-week basis, including approximately $100 million of AI-enabled efficiency savings; management also introduced a target of at least $500 million of AI-powered efficiency by fiscal 2029 and raised mid-term (FY2028–29) targets to 4–7% sales growth and 9–11% adjusted EPS growth (8-K/release via search).

The dominant forward event is not in the historicals: Sysco has agreed to acquire Jetro Restaurant Depot (cash-and-carry wholesale) — per the prior run's research, an enterprise value of ~$29.1B, funded with ~$21B of new/hybrid debt plus 91.5M new shares, close targeted by Q3 FY2027 subject to regulatory approval, with $250M of cost synergies guided (also referenced in this run's search results). Buybacks are paused in the meantime per the prior run's research. Other red flags: labor cost pressure / strike authorizations at several distribution centers during 2026 (prior run), restaurant-traffic softness, and gross-margin compression in Q4 (gross margin contracted on elevated fuel costs, per search coverage of the release).

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate, scale-based (narrow)
Justification: genuine scale cost advantage as the largest US foodservice distributor, but locally competitive distribution with low switching costs, decade-flat gross margins, and scaled rivals cap it at moderate. Morningstar independently classifies Sysco as narrow-moat (via search), consistent with this score.
- FOR: Largest purchasing volume in the industry gets better terms from producers than any rival — a structural scale cost advantage (~17% US share, largest of the Big Three)
- FOR: Route/warehouse density lowers per-case delivery cost versus regional distributors
- FOR: Sysco Brand private label builds customer lock-in and mix margin
- FOR: Broadline one-stop breadth (food + supplies + specialty) is hard for regionals to match
- FOR: Franchise has compounded through cycles with 50+ years of dividend growth and historically double-digit ROIC (prior run's research)
- AGAINST: Low customer switching costs — a restaurant can split orders between Sysco, US Foods, PFG, and a local distributor, so pricing power is limited (gross margin has sat at ~18–19% for a decade, prior run)
- AGAINST: US Foods and Performance Food Group are scaled, aggressive consolidators; a USFD/PFG combination was explored in 2025 and would have created a near-equal rival (prior run; also in this run's search results)
- AGAINST: Labor- and fuel-intensive model (Teamsters exposure, driver/warehouse wages) on thin margins — small cost or volume misses move earnings a lot; Q4 FY2026 gross margin compressed on fuel costs
- AGAINST: Cash-and-carry formats are structurally taking the price-sensitive independent customer — which is precisely why Sysco is paying up to buy Jetro Restaurant Depot
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range: narrow, and it never reaches the hurdle.** Preliminary v5 run (default moat 5, default probs/offsets/mults): weighted IRR 8.69%, IV@15% $37.89. Final recalibrated run: 8.14%, $34.64. Both are Tier D. There is no defensible standalone input set that gets Sysco near 15% at $79.03 — even the super-bull scenario alone (Jetro success, +14.0% start) yields a 12.22% IRR and an IV@15% of $59.51, still below today's price.
- The Jetro deal is the one thing that could rewrite this — in both directions. Success (close on time, synergies, delevering) plausibly lifts per-share earning power by low-teens percentages by FY2029, which is what the super-bull case prices. Failure modes are equally concrete: a blocked or conditioned deal after a year of distraction, or a closed deal that leaves a 4.5×-levered, thin-margin distributor integrating a different format into soft restaurant traffic. A screening DCF on standalone historicals cannot underwrite either branch; the honest treatment is the one used here — standalone base, deal only in the super-bull, defensive probabilities.
- Quality is not the debate: Sysco will be delivering food in 15 years and the AI cost-out program is a real lever. The debate is purely price: a 4.79% OE yield for ~4–6% underlying per-share growth plus a binary mega-deal.
- **Hurdle math:** the weighted path clears 15% at the weighted IV@15% of **$34.64**; a margin of safety requires a price materially below that figure (stated in words — no MoS price is computed here). At $79.03 the weighted upside at 15% is −56.17% (dcf_numbers.json).
