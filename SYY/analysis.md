# SYY — Sysco Corporation
Run date: 2026-10-13. Engine v4. Price $78.26 (Nasdaq quote API, final run), market cap ~$37.6B — in scope (<$40B).

## What it is
The largest foodservice distributor in the US (~17% share of a ~$300B+ domestic market, vs US Foods ~10% and Performance Food Group third; the rest is ~15,000 regional/local distributors). Sysco delivers food and related products from 333 distribution centers to ~670,000–730,000 customer locations — restaurants (local independents and national chains), healthcare, education, lodging — in 10 countries, with US Foodservice Operations the dominant segment plus International and SYGMA (customized chain distribution). This is a scale-logistics business earning thin spreads: FY2026 gross margin 18.5%, operating margin 3.7%, net margin 2.1% on $84.55B of revenue.

Latest earnings: Q4 / full-year FY2026 (fiscal year ended 2026-06-27, reported early August 2026). FY2026 sales $84.55B, +3.9%; Q4 sales $22.12B, +4.7%, with US local case volume +2.6% and International local volume +4.5%. FY2026 GAAP net income fell 3.9% to $1.757B (EPS $3.66) while adjusted EPS rose 3.4% to $4.61; OCF rose to $2.638B and FCF (OCF − capex) to $1.938B. FY2027 guidance (standalone, reaffirmed 2026-09-09) is sales +6–7% to ~$90B and adjusted EPS $5.02–$5.12 (+9–11%) — both including a 53rd week; excluding it, the EPS midpoint sits at the high end of the long-term algorithm. Management also raised FY2028–29 targets to 4–7% annualized sales growth and 9–11% adjusted EPS growth, anchored on ≥$500M of AI-powered efficiency savings by FY2029 ($100M in FY2027).

The dominant forward event is not in the historicals: on 2026-03-30 Sysco agreed to acquire Jetro Restaurant Depot (cash-and-carry wholesale, 166 warehouse stores, ~725,000 customers; CY2025 revenue ~$16B, EBITDA ~$2.1B) for an enterprise value of ~$29.1B — $21.6B cash (funded with ~$21B of new/hybrid debt plus cash on hand) plus 91.5M new Sysco shares (~19% more shares; sellers end up owning ~16% of the combined company). Close is targeted by Q3 FY2027, subject to regulatory approval. Management guides mid-to-high single-digit EPS accretion in year 1 post-close, low-to-mid teens in year 2, $250M of cost synergies fully ramped by year 3, and delevering from 4.5× to 3.5× net debt/EBITDA within 24 months of close. Buybacks are paused in the meantime. Other red flags: Teamsters strike authorizations at several distribution centers during 2026 (labor cost pressure, historically resolved without major disruption), restaurant-traffic softness (national chain volumes down in Q3 FY2026), and food-cost inflation running ~2.8% at enterprise level in Q4.

## Owner's earnings
FY2026 (SEC 10-K via EDGAR): OCF $2.638B − capex $0.700B − SBC $0.118B = **conservative owner's earnings $1.820B = $3.79/share**. Maintenance version (NI $1.757B + D&A $0.976B − min(capex, D&A) $0.700B − ΔNWC $0.147B): $1.886B = $3.92/share. The two definitions agree within ~4%, which raises confidence in the base — this is a business whose reported capex is mostly real maintenance/growth in warehouses and fleet, with no giant gap between the definitions.

OE/share history (conservative): 2022 $2.02, 2023 $3.88, 2024 $4.08, 2025 $3.08, 2026 $3.79 — positive 5 of the last 5 years (and 10/10 in the CSV). The 2025 dip is capex/OCF timing, not a broken business: FY2025 capex peaked at $906M while OCF dipped to $2.510B; FY2026 capex normalized to $700M and OCF recovered.

**Base used: the engine's normalized 3-yr median, $3.79/share** (median of FY2024 $4.08, FY2025 $3.08, FY2026 $3.79 — the median happens to equal the FY2026 figure, so normalized and latest agree; no override needed). No TTM override: the fiscal year ended only 3.5 months ago (no STALE_FY), and Q1 FY2027 (ending late September 2026) had not been reported as of this run, so there is no newer filing-derived base. The base is deliberately standalone Sysco: Jetro's earnings are not in it, because Jetro also brings ~$21B of new debt, 91.5M new shares, and regulatory risk that a per-share OE base cannot silently absorb (see overlay for the method range).

Balance sheet — the engine's `net_debt_latest` is null because Sysco's EDGAR debt tags came back blank in the CSV (debt_current/debt_lt empty for FY2026), **not** because Sysco is unlevered. From external balance-sheet data for the period ending 2026-06-27: total debt ~$13.52B against EDGAR cash of $1.786B = **net debt ~$11.73B ≈ 6.4× normalized owner's earnings** (~2.9× FY2026 EBITDA of ~$4.1B), BBB-rated, with FY2026 dividends of $2.17/share (~$1.0B) and $1.2B total returned via dividends + buybacks. That is material leverage before the deal; pro forma for Jetro it rises to management's stated 4.5× net debt/EBITDA starting point.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate, scale-based (narrow)
FOR — Genuine scale cost advantage: the largest purchasing volume in the industry gets better terms from producers than any rival; route/warehouse density (333 centers) lowers per-case delivery cost; Sysco Brand private label builds customer lock-in and mix margin; broadline one-stop breadth (food + supplies + specialty companies) is hard for regionals to match; the franchise has compounded through cycles, raised its dividend for 50+ years, and historically earned double-digit ROIC. Morningstar independently classifies Sysco as narrow-moat, consistent with this score.
AGAINST — Distribution is locally competitive and customers can multi-source: a restaurant can split orders between Sysco, US Foods, PFG, and a local distributor with low switching friction, so pricing power is limited to passing through inflation plus small mix gains (gross margin has sat at ~18–19% for a decade). US Foods and PFG are scaled, aggressive consolidators — a USFD/PFG combination was explored in 2025 and would have created a near-equal rival. The model is labor- and fuel-intensive (Teamsters exposure, driver/warehouse wages), and margins are thin enough (3.7% operating) that small cost or volume misses move earnings a lot. Cash-and-carry (Restaurant Depot, Costco-style formats) is structurally taking the price-sensitive independent customer — which is precisely why Sysco is paying $29.1B to buy into it.
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 6 = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-yr projection).

## DCF — final v4 run
Base OE $3.79/share (normalized median = FY2026, above). Price $78.26 = 20.7× base owner's earnings, a 4.8% OE yield — a full price for a low-single-digit revenue grower, before any deal risk.

**Growth sanity check and recalibration (written reasons, per the standing rule).** The engine's blended base is 5.65% (blend 3.70% — median of rev CAGR 5y 10.5% [a COVID-rebound artifact: FY2022 revenue +33.8% off the trough], rev CAGR 3y 3.5%, latest YoY 3.9%, OE/share CAGR 3y −3.7% — plus a +1.95pp buyback adjustment). The engine's *default* scenario starts (−6.4% / +0.7% / +5.7% / +10.7% / +17.7%) fail the sanity check at the top end: a 17.7% super-bull start sits far above everything Sysco has printed or guided (FY2026 adj EPS +3.4%; FY2027 guide +9–11% *including* a 53rd week; FY28–29 algorithm 9–11%). Starts were recalibrated via offsets to −2% / +2% / +5.65% / +10% / +14%:
- Base +5.65% (engine base, kept): between underlying sales growth (~4–5% ex-53rd week, per the guide and FY28–29 algorithm) and guided adjusted EPS growth — OE/share should outgrow sales on AI cost-out, but grow slower than adjusted EPS because the buyback tailwind credited in the engine's blend is now paused and adjusted EPS adds back items owner's earnings charges.
- Bull +10%: the top of the FY2027/FY28–29 adjusted-EPS algorithm sustained — requires the $500M AI savings program to land on schedule.
- Super bull +14%: above every standalone guide — justified only as the Jetro-success case (management's own low-to-mid-teens year-2 accretion guide, plus $250M synergies). Its OE multiplier was cut from the 1.25 default to 1.15 for the same reason: Jetro lifts combined FCF ~55% per management, but on ~19% more shares and ~$21B more debt, so the *per-share level* uplift in the early years is guided at only mid-to-high single digits.
- Super bear −2% with OE ×0.75: a traffic recession plus deal distraction/integration costs; FY2020–21 showed OE/share can fall far harder ($1.67 in FY2020), which the 0.75 level multiplier is there to represent.

Probabilities skewed more defensive than the 10/25/30/25/10 defaults → **15/30/30/17/8**: a $29.1B deal at 4.5× pro-forma leverage, pending regulatory approval (Sysco's 2015 US Foods deal was blocked by the FTC — a different, more overlapping deal, but the precedent is real), on a thin-margin business argues for more left-tail weight; the base case stays modal because the standalone guide is credible and the business is defensive.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (fired on the preliminary run at 4.5×): resolved by the recalibration above — the preliminary super-bull start (17.7%) was an artifact of applying default offsets to a defensive distributor, not a real outcome range. Final spread is 3.1× ($59.51 / $19.06), the flag no longer fires, and confidence is still capped at medium because a pending $29.1B acquisition makes the true outcome range genuinely wide.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT — engine-missed, raised here**: the engine emitted no leverage flag because its EDGAR debt tags for Sysco returned blank and `net_debt_latest` is null. That is a data gap, not low leverage: total debt ~$13.52B, cash $1.786B, net debt ~$11.73B ≈ 6.4× normalized OE, rising to a guided 4.5× net debt/EBITDA pro forma for Jetro. Consequences folded into the verdict: probabilities skewed defensive (15/30 on the bear side), the super-bull OE multiplier cut to 1.15, and no tier upgrade for deal upside — levered OE is exactly the flow this DCF discounts, so the leverage is a reason for caution, not an adjustment to the base.
- All other v4 flags checked and **not fired** on the final run, with reasons: STALE_FY (FY ended 3.5 months ago; Q1 FY2027 unreported, so no TTM base exists to use), BASE_DIVERGENCE (median $3.79 = latest $3.79), NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (FY2026 NI $1.757B vs OCF $2.638B gap is explained by $0.976B of D&A — structural for a fleet/warehouse business), BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT (no divestiture/restatement in the window; the FY2022 revenue jump is the COVID recovery, handled by using the blend's median rather than the 5-yr CAGR — and Jetro is a *forward* basis break, handled in the overlay, not the historicals), SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (food distribution is an operating business; the OE/capex model fits).

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range: narrow, and it never reaches the hurdle.** Preliminary run (default moat 5, default probs/offsets/mults): weighted IRR 8.7%, IV@15% $37.89. Final recalibrated run: 8.2%, $34.64. Both are Tier D. There is no defensible standalone input set that gets Sysco near 15% at $78.26 — even the super-bull scenario alone (Jetro success, +14% start) yields a 12.3% IRR and an IV@15% of $59.51, still 24% below today's price.
- The Jetro deal is the one thing that could rewrite this — in both directions. Success (close on time, synergies, delever to 3.5×) plausibly lifts per-share earning power by low-teens percentages by FY2029, which is what the super-bull case prices. Failure modes are equally concrete: a blocked or conditioned deal after a year of distraction, or a closed deal that leaves a 4.5×-levered, thin-margin distributor integrating a different format into soft restaurant traffic. A screening DCF on standalone historicals cannot underwrite either branch; the honest treatment is the one used here — standalone base, deal only in the super-bull, defensive probabilities.
- Quality is not the debate: Sysco will be delivering food in 15 years, the dividend ($2.17/share, ~2.8% yield) is covered by owner's earnings, and the AI cost-out program is a real, management-incentivized lever. The debate is purely price: 20.7× owner's earnings for ~4–6% underlying per-share growth plus a binary mega-deal.
- **Hurdle math:** the weighted path clears 15% at **~$35** (the weighted IV@15%); with a 20% margin of safety, ~$28. At $78.26 you are paying ~2.3× the hurdle-clearing price.
