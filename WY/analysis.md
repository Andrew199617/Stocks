## What it is
One of the world's largest private owners of timberlands: owns or controls more than 10 million acres of U.S. timberlands, manages long-term licenses covering more than 14 million acres in Canada, and operates 33 wood-products manufacturing facilities (lumber, OSB, engineered wood) in North America (FY2025 10-K, "Our Business"). Revenue $6,905M in FY2025, down from $10,184M in FY2022 (financials_history.csv) as lumber prices and housing demand normalized. Returns 75–80% of Adjusted FAD to shareholders under its cash-return framework: base dividend $0.21/quarter ($0.84/yr) plus supplemental dividends / buybacks.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-narrow, asset-backed, not a franchise
Justification: an irreplicable asset (more than 10M acres of U.S. timberlands owned/controlled, the largest private holding) with scale cost advantages, but a pure commodity price taker — no pricing power, no switching costs — and the return history proves the earnings moat is thin: roic_proxy (fundamentals.json) was 20.48% / 17.67% in the 2021–22 spike but 7.51% / 4.50% / 3.97% in 2023–25 and 3.63–10.12% across 2016–20, at or below the cost of capital in most non-spike years; gross margin compressed 40.17% (2021) → 35.55% → 21.92% → 18.43% → 14.84% (2025) as lumber prices normalized (fundamentals.json annual).
- FOR: irreplicable >10M-acre timberland base — land and standing timber cannot be recreated; peer-leading scale in Southern/Western forestry, logistics and export (Japan) access; biological growth accretes value on the stump and harvest can be deferred into better prices; Strategic Land Solutions / higher-and-better-use optionality (real estate, energy, carbon, land sales — ~$450M of guided 2026 Adjusted EBITDA); investment-grade balance sheet and a base dividend framed to survive low commodity prices.
- AGAINST: prices for logs, lumber and OSB are set by housing starts and industry capacity — zero pricing power; revenue fell $10.18B (2022) → $6.91B (2025) on the housing downturn alone; ROIC at/below the cost of capital outside spike years and gross margin halved from peak; Canadian softwood duties/trade policy and wildfire/pest/climate risk sit on the core asset; the REIT payout framework (75–80% of Adjusted FAD) leaves little retained cash to compound.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: on the zero-filled (wrong) history the screen printed a $1.32 base and 9.09% weighted IRR; on the filing-corrected median base it prints $0.71 and 6.04% (Tier D); on the TTM trough base ($0.08) it would print near-F; on a 5-year median OE base ($1.30/share, taking in the 2021–22 spike years) it would print materially higher but still short of 15% at $19.61. Every defensible OE base leaves the stock below the 15% hurdle at this price — the conclusion is robust even though the point estimate is not.
- What the screen cannot price, and the market clearly does: 10M+ acres of timberland carried at historical cost (book equity $9,426,000,000 = ~$13.03/share against the $19.61 price), land-sale and higher-and-better-use gains (a $266M gain on sale of timberlands in FY2025 and a $71M pretax gain in Q2 2026, per the filings/search), the Strategic Land Solutions / climate platform, and a housing-supply deficit that makes a lumber recovery a matter of timing for many holders. The dividend is real cash support meanwhile: $0.84/yr base = ~4.3% yield at $19.61.
- Quality is not the debate — SBC is trivial ($43M), the asset is irreplicable, and management is recycling the portfolio ($459M of timberland acquisitions in Q3 2025 against ~$410M of planned divestitures, via search). The debate is price versus through-cycle cash earnings: at $19.61 a buyer pays ~27.6x the median OE and ~55x the ex-pension trough OE, i.e. pays today for the recovery.
- Hurdle math: the stock clears a 15% expected return on this model only at or below the weighted IV@15% of $5.55 — a ~72% decline — and clears 10% only below $9.24. Those numbers say less about where WY "should" trade (asset-backed REITs rarely trade on trough OE) than about how much of the price is recovery/land optionality rather than current owner's earnings.
