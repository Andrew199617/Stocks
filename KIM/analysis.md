## What it is
The largest US owner/operator of open-air, grocery-anchored shopping centers: 564 centers and mixed-use assets, ~100 million sq ft at June 30, 2026 (search results). A REIT whose rent roll is necessity-based — ~86% of annualized base rent from grocery, off-price and services tenants (Q1 2026 investor presentation, search results). Q2 2026 (search results from the company's release and its coverage): FFO $0.46/share, +4.5% YoY; revenue $550.8M, +4.9% YoY; same-property NOI +3.5%; pro-rata occupancy 96.4% matching the all-time high, small-shop occupancy a record 92.9%; 461 leases signed with comparable new-lease cash rent spreads of +40.4% and a blended spread of +13.1%; management raised the low end of FY2026 FFO guidance to $1.83/share (range $1.83–$1.84) and raised the quarterly dividend 12% to $0.28/share. Fundamentals.json TTM through Q2 2026: revenue $2,187,134,000 (+4.41% YoY), operating income $788,443,000, OCF $1,179,106,000. DPS TTM is $1.03 (fundamentals.json).

## OVERRIDE — capex (filing)
- **The gap:** the preliminary engine run exited 4 with `DATA_GAP:capex` — capex missing for FY2023 and FY2024 of the last 3 (dcf_numbers.json data_gap: ["capex"]). Kimco files its real-estate capex — tenant improvements, re-tenanting and redevelopment of operating properties — only under its own extension tag kim:PaymentsForImprovementsToOperatingRealEstate, which EDGAR companyfacts does not carry under any us-gaap candidate tag the engine reads (the same dead end as DTE/TKO in the system README). The one us-gaap PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment fact in the file, $18.4M for FY2025, is not that line.
- **The decision (bounded authority, kind "filing"):** judgment.json carries `capex_override = {value: 324465000, kind: "filing"}` — the filed FY2024 improvements figure, which is also the median of the three filed years ($264,395,000 FY2023 / $324,465,000 FY2024 / $347,616,000 FY2025), from the FY2025 10-K Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows (filed 2026-02-20, accession 0001193125-26-060760). The engine applies the single value to every missing period (2016–2024 in the history; only 2023–2024 are in the base window). Against the true per-year figures it overstates FY2023 capex by $60,070,000 (~5.6% of FY2023 OCF, lowering that year's OE — conservative direction) and is exact for FY2024.
- **Out-of-band, acknowledged:** the override is 17.6x the last filed value under an engine-read tag ($18.4M), so it flags out-of-band; judgment.json carries the ack_reason — the band's anchor is a fact that is not the filer's capex line at all, and the override value is the filer's own filed figure, not an estimate.
- **Excluded, deliberately:** "Acquisition of operating real estate and other related net assets" ($277,308,000 / $152,943,000 / $218,377,000 for FY2023/24/25, same statement) is property M&A, presented separately by the filer; charging it as capex would double-count growth the share count already paid for (RPT Realty was a stock merger, closed January 2024).
- **Measured impact (dcf_numbers.json override_impact, same scenario inputs both ways):** zero-fill base $1.5821/share → final base $1.1551/share; zero-fill weighted IRR 8.41% → final 7.99%. The override lowers the base and the IRR — it does not manufacture the tier, and without it there is no tier at all.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: scarce first-ring suburban grocery-anchored locations that zoning and land constraints make hard to replicate, the largest US open-air platform, a necessity-based rent roll and demonstrated pricing power — against measured returns that do not corroborate a wide moat, powerful national tenants, and leverage on top of the rent stream.
- FOR: irreplaceable first-ring suburban grocery-anchored locations — zoning/land scarcity blocks replication and new shopping-center supply is limited (management, Q2 2026); scale — 564 centers, ~100M sq ft, national-tenant relationships sub-scale landlords cannot match; necessity-based rent roll (~86% of ABR from grocery, off-price and services); proven pricing power in this cycle — Q2 2026 new-lease cash spreads +40.4%, blended +13.1%, occupancy 96.4% matching the all-time high, small-shop occupancy a record 92.9%; signed-not-open pipeline (~$95M of ABR per Q2 2026 coverage) converts signed leases into rent without new leasing risk.
- AGAINST: measured returns are thin — fundamentals.json roic_proxy ran 3.21% / 2.54% / 2.77% / 3.61% / 3.25% / 4.30% across FY2020–FY2025, at or below a REIT cost of capital, so the filed record does not show a franchise earning well above its capital cost (the spec's test for a 7+ moat fails, and no gross-margin series is filed — fundamentals.json gross margin is null for every year, the REIT rental model files no COGS/gross-profit tags); no tenant lock-in — powerful national anchors negotiate from strength and leases roll; tenant credit is a recurring leak (the Painted Tree bankruptcy rejections cost 16bp of Q2 2026 occupancy, per call coverage in search results); $7.507B of net debt with low-coupon maturities refinancing at higher rates; Regency, Federal Realty, Brixmor and private landlords compete for the same tenants, and e-commerce keeps pressuring discretionary categories.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:capex** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing override in the OVERRIDE section above — gap, source, per-year accuracy and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied / override_impact), not just here. data_gap_uncovered is empty in the final run.
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): ttm.py was run and its legs are quoted here — OCF: FY $1,120,015,000 − prior YTD $529,216,000 + current YTD $588,307,000 = **TTM $1,179,106,000**; SBC: FY $33,225,000 − prior YTD $18,349,000 + current YTD $18,856,000 = **TTM $33,732,000**; shares (current-YTD weighted diluted) **672,282,000**; capex: FY $18,400,000 − prior YTD $18,400,000 + current YTD $160,899,082 (override, prorated) = **TTM $160,899,082**; **TTM OE total $984,474,918 → $1.4644/share** (ttm.json). The TTM base was **rejected as the pin**: its capex leg is broken by construction — the FY and prior-YTD legs both carry the spurious $18.4M PP&E fact and cancel, leaving only a half-year prorated override fill, so TTM capex of $160.9M is roughly half the filer's actual annual improvements run-rate ($324–348M filed, FY2024–25) and the TTM OE/share is flattered by that shortfall. The median-of-3 base charges a full year of improvements capex in each of its years and is the conservative, filing-consistent choice. The --ttm variant was still computed for the method range (Tier D, above), so the rejection costs the verdict nothing it was hiding.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $1.58 vs 3-yr median $1.16): the **latest FY is the distorted one** — its capex leg is the $18.4M spurious tag against a true $347.616M improvements line (FY2025 10-K, same statement as the override source). The median year (FY2023, $1.1551/share) carries the override capex fill, which if anything overstates that year's true capex. Median base used; latest FY reported but not used.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 3.0%/yr): merger issuance, not SBC dilution. The count stepped up on the Weingarten (2021) and RPT Realty (January 2024) stock mergers — shares diluted went from 421,799,000 in FY2019 to 675,279,000 in FY2025 (financials_history.csv) while the rent roll those shares bought came with them. SBC itself is flat and small: $33,054,000 / $34,900,000 / $33,225,000 in FY2023–25 and $33,732,000 TTM (ttm.json) — ~3% of OCF, already deducted in full in OE. The engine's −3.01pp share-count adjustment to the revenue growth components is retained as filed; scenario starts were set from per-share operating prints, not from the adjusted blend, so the adjustment is not double-counted in the verdict.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 9.62x normalized OE): balance sheet stated from the filing — debt total $7,718,700,000 (tag LongTermDebt, which includes current maturities), cash $211,648,000, net debt $7,507,052,000 (financials_history.csv / dcf_numbers.json); book equity $10,391,836,000 at FY2025 (financials_history.csv). On the REIT lens, Q2 2026 coverage put consolidated net debt/EBITDA at ~5.2x — investment-grade REIT leverage, not distress — but on this sweep's OE lens the leverage is exactly why the bear cases are stressed: bear and super-bear carry 0.90 / 0.75 OE multipliers with −1.0% / −5.8% growth starts, producing IRRs of 6.39% / 4.98%, and the final tier is sized to that end (the whole band is D, so no sizing-down was needed). Refinancing low-coupon maturities at current rates is a real, ongoing drag on OE/share growth and is one reason the base start is 3.5%, not the FFO growth rate.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:capex**: see OVERRIDE — filing gap filled for 2016–2024, never replacing a filed value; the FY2025 filed value it could not replace is itself the subject of BASE_DIVERGENCE.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Real Estate): accepted in full — the OE/capex DCF fits REITs poorly in both directions here: it charges all improvements capex (including redevelopment that creates new rent) against OE, and it gives no credit for the asset value of the land bank or for FFO, the industry's own metric (FY2026 FFO guided at $1.83–$1.84/share, search results). Confidence is graded **low** and the tier is read as "fails the standardized conservative OE screen," not as "the rental business loses cash." The mismatch does not rescue the hurdle math: even on the kindest computed variant (TTM base, IV@15% $12.63) and even in the super-bull scenario (IV@15% $15.82), the stock does not reach a 15% owner's-earnings return at $22.91 — a buyer at this price is underwriting the FFO stream, the $1.03 DPS TTM and cap-rate value, none of which this model prices.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the gap between the two lenses. On the REIT lens this is a healthy, improving credit: record occupancy, +40.4% new-lease spreads, same-property NOI +3.5%, FFO guidance raised, dividend raised 12%, ~5.2x net debt/EBITDA. On the owner's-earnings lens the same company converts $1,179,106,000 of TTM OCF into roughly $1.16/share of median OE because keeping grocery-anchored centers leasable costs $264–348M a year of improvements capex, forever — that spend is not optional growth capex, it is the rent roll's maintenance bill, and the sweep is right to charge it.
- Growth quality is the quiet issue: the filed revenue CAGRs (15.14% over 5y) are merger arithmetic. Organic growth is the 3.5% NOI print. A buyer paying $22.91 — a price at which even the super-bull OE case is worth $15.82 at a 15% discount — needs cap-rate compression or FFO-multiple expansion to make 15%, i.e. needs the next buyer, not the cash flows.
- Method range: Tier D on both computed bases (median $1.1551/share → weighted IRR 7.99%, IV@15% $9.96; TTM $1.4644/share → 9.45%, $12.63). No tier override: the numbers tier D is retained, with the sector mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the method cannot support.
- What would change the tier: the price, mostly — the 15% hurdle clears below ~$10 on the median base and below ~$12.63 on the TTM base. On fundamentals, a sustained step-up in same-property NOI growth toward the bull start (6.5%) with improvements capex flat would have to file first; Q3 2026 results are scheduled for October 29, 2026 (search results).

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (tier by numbers D — weighted IRR 7.99% on the median base; TTM-base variant also D at 9.45%)
- Confidence: **Low** (SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH for a REIT, a capex series that exists only under the filer's extension tag, and a latest-FY OE figure distorted by a spurious capex fact — though every computed variant lands in D, so the tier itself is robust to the data problems)
- One-liner: Quality grocery-anchored open-air REIT, moat 5/10 — record 96.4% occupancy and +40.4% new-lease spreads are real, but measured ROIC runs only ~3–4% and at $22.91 the buyer pays for a $1.16/share median owner's-earnings base (after charging real-estate improvements capex) growing ~3.5% against $7.51B of net debt; weighted IRR 8.0% (IV@15% $9.96), so it clears the 15% hurdle only below ~$10 — the REIT lens (FY2026 FFO guided $1.83–$1.84/share) is kinder than the OE lens, but neither supports 15% at this price.
