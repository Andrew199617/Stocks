## What it is
Europe's largest low-cost airline: a single-type Boeing 737 fleet, high-density seating, direct online distribution and a large ancillary-revenue engine (baggage, priority, food, third-party products). 208.4m passengers in FY26 (year ended March 2026, per published results). The traded security is a Nasdaq ADS; since September 2024 one ADS equals two ordinary shares (see ADS_RATIO_APPLIED below).

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, capped by a commodity product
Justification: Europe's structural low-cost carrier: a genuine, cycle-tested unit-cost advantage (single-type 737 fleet, high-density Gamechanger aircraft, direct distribution, ancillary revenue engine, disciplined fuel hedging) that has kept it profitable through shocks that bankrupted rivals, plus the scale of Europe's largest passenger airline and a debt-free, net-cash balance sheet after the May 2026 bond repayment. Capped at 6 because the product is a commodity seat bought purely on price — there are no switching costs, no loyalty lock-in and no network-effect moat; fares fell 6% in Q1 FY27 despite the cost lead, and the economics remain hostage to fuel prices, EU environmental taxes, ATC disruption, Boeing deliveries and geopolitics.
- FOR: Structural unit-cost advantage vs EU rivals (single-type fleet, high-density B737-8200 Gamechangers); scale — Europe's largest airline by passengers (208.4m in FY26); ancillary revenue engine (~€24 per passenger in FY26); debt-free / net-cash balance sheet after May 2026 bond repayment; disciplined fuel hedging (80% of FY27 needs at ~$67/bbl); profitable through cycles that bankrupted less efficient rivals.
- AGAINST: Commodity seat bought on price alone — zero switching costs, no loyalty lock-in; fuel exposure (20% unhedged FY27 volume spiked above $150/bbl in Q1 FY27); rising EU environmental taxes (~€300m additional expected in FY27); Boeing delivery delays have repeatedly capped traffic growth; regulatory risk — Italian AGCM €256m fine (appealed; €85m provided in FY26); ATC strikes, geopolitics and fare cyclicality hit earnings directly.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 18.5 months ago): ttm.py cannot resolve this for an IFRS filer — it reads us-gaap facts only and exited with: "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for RYAAY (CIK 1038683): ocf: no annual (FY) fact; sbc: no annual (FY) fact; shares_diluted: no annual (FY) fact; capex: no capex facts in companyfacts under any candidate tag and no capex_override in judgment.json — refusing to zero-fill (same DATA_GAP standard as the engine)". Per the v5 spec for IFRS names, the engine's median base is used. Direction of the staleness: published FY26 results (revenue +11%, pre-exceptional PAT up strongly on +10% fares and +4% traffic to 208.4m) say the FY23–25 median base is more likely conservative than flattering; Q1 FY27 (PAT −34%, fares −6%, costs +11% on unhedged fuel) cuts the other way for FY27 specifically. One operational note: a first preliminary engine pull this run transiently returned a different, FY26-inclusive companyfacts payload (and failed safe with a DATA_GAP on SBC); two repeat pulls reproduce the FY25-latest history above exactly, and the run was completed on the reproducible data.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: the >60% revenue swing in the last 5 filed years is the COVID collapse and rebound (FY2021 revenue fell to a fraction of FY2020's, then recovered through FY2023) — not a divestiture, M&A or restatement. Consequence: the 41.45% 3-yr revenue CAGR is a rebound artifact; the blend's median structure already tempers it, and scenario starts were sanity-checked against traffic/fare prints rather than that CAGR.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2025): in both years OCF far exceeds net income (FY2025: NI $1,742,945,400 vs OCF $3,694,079,550; FY2023: NI $1,428,757,500 vs OCF $4,231,462,500 — financials_history.csv). This is the airline cash model: fares are collected in cash before the flight (advance bookings / working-capital inflow, rebuilding after COVID in FY23), plus non-cash charges. The divergence is OCF *above* earnings, and the DCF base is built on the OCF-based conservative OE, so the gap does not flatter the base beyond the airline's genuine cash-collection advantage; it is a timing feature of the model, not a one-off gain.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: EUR reporting converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (frankfurter.app); flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average. Rates are recorded in dcf_numbers.json (FY2025: 1.0815). All EUR-denominated conclusions carry that simplification.
- **USD_TRANSLATIONS_USED**: where the issuer files its own USD convenience translations, the engine used the filed USD figures as-is.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: resolved by judgment.json ads_ratio {ratio: 2.0} under the bounded filing authority — Ryanair's FY2025 Form 20-F (Item 12) states that during FY25 the ADS ratio changed so one ADS equals two ordinary shares (2:1, previously 5:1; effected September 2024). Per-share figures are scaled ×2 to the ADS price. Using the old 5:1 ratio would overstate per-ADS OE 2.5x; the unscaled preliminary run (weighted IRR 7.54%, Tier D) was a units mismatch, not a defensible method.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.83x): real — airline earnings genuinely swing this much on fuel and fares (Q1 FY27 PAT −34% after a record FY26). Confidence is held at medium and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Quality vs price is the whole story. The business clears every quality test this screen has — real cash earnings, trivial SBC, net cash, buybacks (a €750m programme ~90% complete per Q1 FY27 reporting), a structural cost gap rivals have failed to close for two decades. None of that matters at the wrong price: at $51.33 the weighted path returns 12.49%, below the 15% hurdle, and even the bull case (14.96%) doesn't clear it — only super bull does.
- The method range is narrower than most names in this sweep once the ADS units are fixed, but the stale base cuts in the investor's favour: the FY23–25 median predates a record FY26. A base built on FY26 actuals would likely lift the weighted IRR toward the B/C border. That base is not in this run's reproducible engine data, so it is noted, not used — and FY27's fuel-driven profit decline would claw part of it back.
- Hurdle arithmetic (from the final run's own figures): the price that clears 15% on the probability-weighted path is the weighted IV@15%, **$40.23**. A buyer who wants the *base case alone* to clear 15% with a margin of safety needs ≤ $37.25 (base-case IV@15%). At $51.33 you are paying today for the bull case.
