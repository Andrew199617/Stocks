## What it is
Building a space-based cellular broadband network: large BlueBird satellites in low Earth orbit with the largest phased arrays ever deployed in LEO, designed to deliver full broadband directly to standard, unmodified smartphones. Distribution is wholesale through mobile network operators — 60+ MNO partnerships covering over 3 billion subscribers — plus U.S. government contracts. Still pre-commercial: 13 spacecraft in orbit, ~45 targeted by early 2027, beta service with select partners planned before full commercial launch. Q2 2026 revenue was $31.5M (gateway deliveries and government milestones); management reaffirmed FY2026 revenue guidance of $150–200M, weighted to Q4, and cites a ~$1.3B revenue backlog.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, technology lead without a built network
Justification: the engineering lead is real (full broadband to unmodified phones, where the live competitor is still text-first) and the MNO distribution web is a genuine asset, but there is no network effect until the constellation exists, the strongest competitor is already live, owns the launch stack AST now depends on, and has effectively unlimited capital. Nothing here has yet survived contact with a paying mass market.
- FOR: Largest phased arrays in LEO — full broadband to unmodified smartphones, not a text-only service; 60+ MNO partnerships covering 3B+ subscribers — distribution without consumer acquisition cost; patented satellite architecture plus spectrum/regulatory position and a first-mover window before Amazon Leo deploys (from 2028); ~$1.3B contracted backlog including U.S. government awards above $100M.
- AGAINST: SpaceX Starlink direct-to-cell is already live with 650+ dedicated satellites, its own launch vehicles, and deep capital — and is AST's practical launch provider since Blue Origin's New Glenn grounding; only 13 satellites in orbit vs ~45 needed, target already slipped to early 2027, so the network effect is unbuilt; Amazon Leo filed for 5,105 direct-to-device satellites in July 2026; the model is funded by continuous dilution (below), so per-share economics lag any company-level success.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years — moot here, since there is no positive base OE to project.

- **DILUTION** (share count +76.81%/yr): real and structural. Diluted shares per the filed history: 81,824,122 (FY2023) → 154,501,344 (FY2024) → 255,982,592 (FY2025) → 294,898,761 (current YTD 2026, ttm.py). Any future positive OE must be divided by a much larger share count than today's.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in last 5 yrs): not a divestiture, M&A, or restatement — it is emergence from pre-revenue. Revenue was $5,967,000 (FY2020) and $13,825,000 (FY2022) on test/gateway activity, then $70,918,000 in FY2025 as the first BlueBirds entered service and gateway/government work began; Q2 2026 printed $31.5M in a single quarter. All revenue CAGRs in dcf_numbers.json are therefore artifacts and were not used for any growth input.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): explained by non-cash charges in net income that never touch OCF, per the FY2025 results release cash-flow reconciliation: loss on remeasurement of warrant liabilities $268,627,000 in FY2024 and $68,154,000 in FY2025, plus an induced conversion expense on convertible notes of $99,681,000 in FY2025, alongside D&A ($63,340,000 FY2024; $51,111,000 FY2025) and SBC. That is why net income (−$300,083,000 FY2024; −$341,940,000 FY2025, engine figures) runs far below OCF (−$126,143,000; −$71,517,000) without either being wrong. The conservative OE base is built from OCF and deducts SBC separately, so the divergence does not flatter or contaminate the base — the F rests on capex, not on this gap.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a venture-stage infrastructure bet trading at $11.381B. The OE-DCF has nothing to grip: five straight years of negative owner's earnings on both definitions, a TTM base of −$6.06/share, and a buildout whose TTM capex is $1,493,334,000 against FY2025 revenue of $70,918,000.
- The bull case is not a DCF case: if ~45 satellites reach orbit by early 2027 and MNO/gov revenue ramps toward management's ~$1B first-full-commercial-year ambition, today's revenue base is irrelevant. That outcome is binary on launch cadence — now dependent on SpaceX, the direct competitor whose direct-to-cell service is already live — and on continued access to capital on tolerable terms.
- There is no hurdle-clearing price to quote: with negative owner's earnings at every base, no positive price produces a 15% OE-implied return. The 15% question only becomes askable after the company prints its first positive-OE year; until then any entry price is paying for the option, not the cash flows.
- Sizing: the F is a statement about the screen, not a short thesis — pre-revenue compounders fail this screen by construction. But on Andrew's hurdles there is nothing here to underwrite today.
