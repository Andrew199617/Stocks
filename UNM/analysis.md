## What it is
Workplace-benefits insurer (SIC 6321, Accident & Health Insurance): the #1 U.S. group disability income provider and #2 in voluntary benefits, selling group long/short-term disability, group life, accident, critical illness, dental and vision through employers, plus Colonial Life voluntary benefits (agent force), Unum International (UK and Poland), and a Closed Block segment holding legacy long-term care (LTC) and individual disability in runoff. Revenue is premium income plus investment income on the float — the reason the OE/capex model fits poorly (see SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: the leading U.S. group disability franchise plus Colonial Life voluntary benefits, embedded in employer HR systems through HR Connect/Total Leave with high persistency and a deep broker network — real switching friction and specialist scale — against price-competed, broker-re-bid products with no network effects, claims/rate exposure outside management control, and a legacy LTC block that has repeatedly produced reserve charges.
- FOR: #1 U.S. group disability income position and #2 in voluntary benefits (Colonial Life) — specialist scale in workplace benefits built over decades; switching costs via employer HR integration — HR Connect / Total Leave platforms embed claims, leave and benefits administration into the employer's HR stack, making carrier changes disruptive; high persistency (UK 91.8% cited in Q3 2025 reporting); broker and employer distribution — deep, loyal broker network across Unum US, Unum International (UK/Poland) and Colonial Life's agent force; through-cycle core profitability — OE positive 5 of the last 5 fiscal years, core adjusted operating returns strong (consolidated adjusted operating ROE ~15.9% in Q2 2026 per company reporting; 21.4% in Q3 2025), diluted shares shrinking 5.1%/yr on buybacks.
- AGAINST: price-competed, broker-intermediated products — employers and brokers re-bid group coverage; no network effects and no structural pricing power beyond actuarially justified rate filings; legacy Closed Block long-term care — 2025 reserve assumption updates added a $643.1M pre-tax LTC charge (Closed Block statistical supplement, cited search result) and the block still ran an adjusted operating loss in Q2 2026; reserve risk is not fully retired despite the Fortitude Re cession ($3.4B of individual LTC reserves ceded in 2025, ~19% of the total LTC block); claims and rate hostage — benefit ratios ride disability incidence/recovery and morbidity, investment income rides the yield curve; capital intensity and regulation — insurance capital rules and ratings gate growth and capital return, and much larger rivals (MetLife, Prudential, Lincoln, Aflac) carry bigger scale and technology budgets.
- **Return evidence (spec step 5):** fundamentals.json carries **no roic_proxy series for UNM** (null in every year — the filer's tagging does not produce one) and **no gross margin** (insurers file no COGS/gross profit), so neither can be quoted; the return anchor available in the computed outputs is ROE (fundamentals.json): 16.3% (2021), 16.1% (2022), 13.3% (2023), 16.2% (2024), 6.6% (2025, charge-hit), with net margin 13.81% in 2024 falling to 5.65% in 2025. Mid-teens ROE in normal years supports a moderate moat, not a 7+ — the through-cycle record is repeatedly interrupted by the legacy block.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py was run and cannot produce a TTM OE for UNM — it exits non-zero because no post-FY SBC fact is filed under the tags it reads, and fundamentals.json's TTM block confirms sbc and oe_conservative are null (TTM revenue $13,347,700,000, net income $702,700,000, OCF $830,400,000 quoted above). The median-of-3-FY base is therefore used, as the spec directs when TTM is unavailable; the TTM GAAP window still contains the Q3 2025 charge, so it would not have raised the base in any case.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 5.1%/yr): already credited via the +5.00% growth adjustment in the blend (share count CAGR −5.07%); not double-counted in the verdict. Fundamentals.json corroborates the pace: buybacks TTM $1,118,100,000 and dividends paid TTM $307,500,000.
- **CASH_INCLUDES_RESTRICTED**: the only cash tag filed for the latest fiscal year is the combined cash + restricted-cash total (CashCashEquivalentsRestrictedCashAndRestrictedCashEquivalents) with no separate restricted-cash tag, so the restricted amount cannot be separated in the computed outputs — stated in words, as required. The engine's net debt basis is LongTermDebtNoncurrent+LongTermDebtCurrent ($3.609B); to the extent the combined cash figure contains restricted balances, true net debt is higher than a net-of-cash figure would show, which only reinforces HIGH_NET_DEBT below.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance sector): accepted in full. Unum is an accident & health insurer; premiums are largely float/reserve flows, capex is not the maintenance investment of an operating business, and the balance sheet is dominated by investment assets and policy reserves that this model does not value. Confidence is graded **low**, the ranking lists the name under sector misfits, and alert_eligible is false for this reason. Unlike the PFG pattern, no tier softening was needed: the numbers here do not flatter the name (TTM/float effects are not inflating the base — FY2025 OCF of $687,700,000 ran *below* net income of $738,500,000), so tier-by-numbers D stands unadjusted.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $2.90 vs 3-yr median $5.08): the **latest FY is the distorted one**. FY2025 absorbed the annual LTC reserve assumption update — a $643.1M pre-tax "Reserve Assumption Updates — Long-term Care" charge in the Closed Block (statistical supplement, cited search result; after-tax net reserve increase of $377.8M reported for Q3 2025), which removed morbidity/mortality improvement assumptions (~$850M reserve increase) and discontinued new employee coverage on existing group LTC cases (~$200M), partly offset by an expanded premium rate-increase program (~$525M reserve reduction). GAAP net income fell from $1,779,100,000 (2024) to $738,500,000 (2025) and OCF from $1,513,200,000 to $687,700,000 in the same year the Fortitude Re risk transfer closed. Core adjusted operating EPS ran at roughly double the GAAP figure (Q3 2025 adjusted $2.09 vs GAAP $0.23). The median ($5.08, the 2023 year of the last three) is the cleaner normalized base; if anything it is conservative against core adjusted earnings power, which is noted as the main method caveat below.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 4.11x normalized OE): the $3.609B is holding-company/corporate debt (LongTermDebtNoncurrent+LongTermDebtCurrent), not policyholder reserves — for an insurer this ratio overstates operating leverage, since the debt is serviced from subsidiary dividends and holding-company liquidity (reported at ~$2.0B in Q3 2025 with a ~455% weighted-average RBC ratio, cited search results). It is nonetheless real leverage on the equity: bear cases stress it via the 0.75/0.90 OE multipliers, and the verdict is sized to the bear end.
- **TIER_BAND** (bear tier D at 7.14% vs bull tier C at 10.71%): the verdict spans tiers and is sized to the bear end — the final tier is D under either reading. Note the band is narrow because every scenario, including super bull (13.38%), falls short of the 15% hurdle at this price.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *gap between the two earnings languages* for this name: on the standardized OCF-based model the stock is a clear D (weighted IRR 8.84%, IV@15% $45.93 vs $92.23) — but the model's base ($5.08/share) is GAAP OE through a reserve-charge cycle, while core adjusted operating EPS is running at roughly $8–9/share annualized (Q2 2026 adjusted $2.16; Street expects ~$8.66 growing to ~$9.49, cited search results). A bespoke insurer model on adjusted operating earnings and book value would print materially higher than this screen; that is exactly why confidence is low and the name sits in the sector-misfit bucket rather than being presented as a precise D.
- What the screen does establish robustly: at $92.23 the price already capitalizes the core franchise generously (book value per share $64.30 at FY2025 from fundamentals.json; TTM revenue growth only +3.42%), the Closed Block tail is not finished (Q2 2026 adjusted operating loss $75.4M and widening), and even this model's super-bull case (13.38% IRR) does not clear 15%. There is no scenario in the computed set where the current price clears Andrew's hurdle.
- Capital return is the real per-share engine: buybacks TTM $1,118,100,000, dividends paid TTM $307,500,000, DPS TTM $1.84 (fundamentals.json) with an 8-year growth streak — that supports the per-share growth blend but is already credited in it.
- Method range: latest-FY base ($2.90) would print far below D; median base prints D (8.84%); an adjusted-operating-earnings base would print higher, plausibly C territory — the range itself is the finding, and under sector mismatch none of these should be quoted without the method attached.
