## What it is
Akron, Ohio-based regulated electric utility holding company: ten electric utility subsidiaries serving more than six million customers in Ohio, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, Maryland, New Jersey and New York, plus a stand-alone transmission business (over 24,000 miles of transmission lines, search results) regulated by FERC. Essentially all earnings are regulated. The investment story is rate-base compounding under the Energize365 program: $36B of capital investment planned for 2026–2030 (about $6B in 2026), supporting about 10% annual rate-base growth; 2026 core EPS guidance of $2.62–$2.82 was reaffirmed after Q1 2026 (search results). Full-year 2025 core earnings were $2.55/share, close to 8% above 2024, on a consolidated return on equity of 9.8% on rate base of $27.8B, versus 9.4% on $25.6B in 2024 (Q4 2025 earnings call, search results).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Regulated franchise monopolies — ten electric utilities serving more than six million customers across Ohio, Pennsylvania, West Virginia, Maryland, New Jersey and New York, plus one of the largest US transmission systems (over 24,000 miles of lines) — give durable, regulator-granted exclusivity and a visible rate-base growth runway (Energize365: $36B of capital investment planned 2026-2030, ~10% annual rate-base growth and 9.8% consolidated ROE cited in Q1 2026 reporting). Capped at 5 because earned returns are set by regulators at allowed ROEs with no pricing power beyond rate cases, the Ohio regulatory history includes the HB6 scandal and ESP V order drag, and the growth plan is funded with heavy leverage (long-term debt $26,390M per the FY2025 10-K contractual-obligations table) while shareholders were diluted by the 30% FET stake sale to Brookfield in March 2024.
- FOR: Regulated franchise monopolies: ten electric utilities serving 6M+ customers in OH, PA, WV, MD, NJ and NY — captive, essential-service demand; visible rate-base growth: Energize365 plans $36B of investment 2026-2030 (~$6B in 2026), supporting ~10% annual rate-base growth; formula-rate transmission investment (FERC-regulated, ~50% of 2025 investment) recovers capital faster than traditional rate cases; constructive recent regulatory outcomes: base rate cases settled/filed across PA, NJ, WV and MD, Ohio base rate case resolved Nov 2025, S&P upgrade to BBB.
- AGAINST: Returns are capped at regulator-allowed ROEs — no pricing power; Ohio's ESP V order already cost earnings and Ohio regulatory risk has a scandal history (HB6); heavy leverage: long-term debt $26,390M (FY2025 10-K) against equity of $12,510M (financials_history.csv) — funding costs and rate sensitivity are structural; growth is bought with dilutive capital: the 30% FET stake sale to Brookfield (closed March 2024) diluted transmission earnings to fund the plan; storm costs and recovery lag are recurring earnings hits across a six-state footprint.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is the model's known blind spot, flagged as such — the same shape as DTE and FTS in this sweep: a profitable, dividend-paying regulated monopoly (dividends declared were $1.78/share during 2025, per the FY2025 10-K equity statement) whose conservative owner's earnings are negative in 4 of the last 5 years, and on TTM, because utility capex runs above operating cash flow every year of a multi-year rate-base build (Energize365: $36B planned 2026–2030, ~$6B in 2026, search results). The dividend and the build are funded the utility way — rate recovery plus continuous debt issuance — which an OE screen reads as negative distributable cash.
- On numbers a utility investor actually uses (from search results, not the engine): 2026 core EPS guided $2.62–$2.82 at a $45.41 price, rate base guided to grow about 10% a year, consolidated ROE 9.8% in 2025. That is a bond-like high-single-digit total-return profile before any multiple change — nowhere near Andrew's 15% hurdle on any defensible reading, so the F here agrees with the hurdle conclusion even though the model's mechanism (negative OE) is a sector artifact. No method range spanning tiers is claimed: on the v5 method, with the filing's own SBC figure applied, every base (latest FY −$1.81, normalized −$2.05, TTM −$3.08) is negative and there is one result, F.
- Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (dividend / rate-base), which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the v5 method cannot support.
- Real risks for a holder (outside the model): rate-case outcomes across six states (Maryland and West Virginia filings planned for 2026, per the Q4 2025 call, search results), Ohio regulatory politics after HB6, storm-cost recovery lag, and refinancing a $26,390M long-term debt stack (FY2025 10-K) at current rates while funding a $36B five-year plan.
