## What it is
Copper-focused critical-minerals miner with three operating mines — Constancia (Peru), Snow Lake / Manitoba (Canada) and Copper Mountain (British Columbia) — producing copper with gold, silver and zinc by-products, plus a US growth pipeline: Copper World (Arizona, DFS ~95% complete and FID expected later in 2026 with first production targeted H2 2029, per the Q2 2026 release summary), Cactus (acquired with Arizona Sonoran, closed June 24, 2026) and Mason (Nevada, pre-feasibility started). Sector: Materials / Copper and gold mining. In scope: market cap (est) $9.369B at the $23.62 price (nasdaq_quote_api).

## Moat: 3/10 — weak; cost position only, no pricing power
Justification: a copper/gold miner is a pure commodity price taker — no pricing power, no switching costs, and reserves deplete and must be replaced with heavy capex. The only moat-like traits are cost-curve position, long asset life and jurisdiction. Measured returns confirm the weakness: roic_proxy (fundamentals.json) was 0.6% in FY2021, 6.2% in FY2022, 7.2% in FY2023 and 9.8% in FY2024 — below the cost of capital through most of the cycle — reaching 15.4% only in FY2025 at strong metal prices; gross margin (fundamentals.json) swung from 8.7% in FY2021 to 18.9%, 23.2%, 27.4% and 33.6% in FY2025, tracking metal prices, not any pricing power. A moat of 7+ on that ROIC history would be indefensible.
- FOR: low cost-curve position — gold by-product credits drove Q2 2026 consolidated cash cost to -$0.40/lb Cu and sustaining cash cost to $1.39/lb (company Q2 2026 release); long-life diversified operating base (Constancia, Snow Lake/Manitoba, Copper Mountain) with reserve lives extended into the 2040s in the company's three-year outlook (cited search result); staged copper growth pipeline in tier-1 jurisdictions (Copper World, Cactus, Mason) targeting ~24% higher consolidated copper production by 2027-28 (company guidance, cited search results).
- AGAINST: pure commodity price taker; reserves deplete and production must be replaced via heavy sustaining/growth capex, permitting and M&A (FY2025 capex $466,700,000 vs OCF $707,300,000); returns below the cost of capital through most of the cycle (roic_proxy above); jurisdiction and execution risk (Peru operations, permitting, Copper World FID and construction ahead); serial share issuance for M&A (see DILUTION).
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): ttm.py cannot resolve it for HBM — it exits non-zero for this IFRS filer (no annual FY fact for ocf/sbc/shares and no capex facts under its candidate tags), so the median-of-3 base is used per the worker spec; its median year is FY2023 and the latest filed year is FY2025, so the base is a through-cycle figure, not a stale single year. Post-FY prints (Q2 2026, quoted under Owner's earnings) show record trailing EBITDA on strong prices with guidance reaffirmed — cycle strength the median deliberately does not capitalize into the base.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 14.8%/yr, already deducted via the -5.00pp growth adjustment): SBC is not the driver — SBC was $7,400,000 in FY2023 and $19,300,000 in FY2024 before jumping to $61,300,000 in FY2025 (financials_history.csv), still only a single-digit share of OCF. The driver is M&A issuance: the diluted count was flat at ~261M through FY2021-22, then stepped to 310,953,110 in FY2023 and 377,291,211 in FY2024 (financials_history.csv) as Copper Mountain (acquired June 2023, per cited search results) entered the share count, reaching 396,648,796 in FY2025. It has continued after the filed period: Hudbay closed the all-share acquisition of Arizona Sonoran on June 24, 2026 at 0.242 of a Hudbay share per Arizona Sonoran share (closing release, cited search result), and market data now shows 444,150,000 shares outstanding (cited search result) vs the filed 395,521,903 at December 31, 2025 — roughly 12% further issuance for a pre-production asset (Cactus PFS expected H2 2027). The engine's per-share base is therefore *flattered* relative to the current count; the true current-count OE/share is lower than $0.61, which only deepens a Tier D verdict that already fails by a wide margin. No shares_override was declared: the bounded authority covers filing-sourced figures for the filed periods, and restating the base on a third-party market-data count would not be filing-sourced — the effect is disclosed here instead.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): real and structural — $0.079 to $0.795 per share across FY2021-25 is the copper/gold cycle acting on a price-taker. Handled by the normalized median base and by widening the bear weight (probabilities 15/30/30/17/8, 45% on bear-or-worse). Confidence is capped at low.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): the gap is the structural mining D&A wedge, not a one-off that touches the OE base. In FY2023 net income was $69,500,000 vs OCF $476,900,000 with D&A of $393,100,000 added back; in FY2024 net income was $67,800,000 vs OCF $666,200,000 with D&A of $428,000,000 (financials_history.csv) — D&A alone explains the bulk of both divergences, with working-capital movement the remainder. Reported NI is simply the wrong series for a capital-intensive miner; the OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is unaffected. The mirror image is FY2025, where the maintenance-OE definition explodes to $1,168,300,000 on a $-604,000,000 working-capital swing — also rejected, for the same reason.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (x1.0): direct-listed common shares — see ADS ratio. No scaling applied or needed.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is how far the price is from any owner's-earnings math: at $23.62 the market is paying for the growth pipeline (Copper World, Cactus, Mason) and for copper/gold prices staying at or above today's levels for a long time. On through-cycle filed OE, even the super-bull case — growth starting at 19.1%, OE scaled x1.25 — values the share at $10.88 at a 15% discount rate and earns only an 8.58% IRR. A buyer here is making a commodity-price and project-execution bet, not buying filed cash flow at a discount.
- Method range: the verdict is unusually robust for this sweep. Every scenario is Tier D; the latest-FY base ($0.45/share) instead of the median would print lower still, and restating per-share figures on the post-Arizona-Sonoran share count (~444M, cited market data) would lower them again. No defensible filing-based method gets HBM near a 15% hurdle at $23.62 — it clears 15% only at or below $5.66.
- What the DCF does not credit: reserve/resource value in the ground, the option value of Copper World/Cactus if copper enters a structural deficit, and the post-FY move to a net cash balance sheet at Q2 2026 (cited search result). Those are real, but they are asset/speculation value, not owner's earnings — pass 2 or a NAV method would be the tool for them, not this engine.
- Balance sheet is not the problem: net debt 1.83x normalized OE on the FY2025 basis and net cash by Q2 2026. The problem is purely price vs through-cycle per-share cash flow, compounded by a share count that keeps growing to buy growth.
