## What it is
Global life insurer and retirement manager, 150 years old, ~$1.64 trillion AUM at Q2 2026. Three segments: PGIM (global asset management, $1.49T AUM), U.S. Businesses (Retirement Strategies incl. annuities and pension risk transfer, Group Insurance, Individual Life), and International Businesses (led by Japan — Life Planner / Gibraltar Life). Management's stated go-forward footprint (Q2 2026 call): Global Retirement, Global Asset Management, and select U.S. insurance (Group, Individual Life), funded by a >$3B capital-rotation program exiting emerging-market businesses, plus a $750M cost plan. Q2 2026: adjusted operating income $4.08/share (+14% YoY, beat $3.47 consensus), revenue $14.15B (+4.8%), retail annuity sales $3.6B (+14%), PGIM adjusted operating income $294M (+28%), adjusted operating ROE 16.4%, $743M returned ($250M buybacks + $493M dividends; quarterly dividend $1.40 = $5.60/yr, ~4.9% yield at $113.39).

## Owner's earnings — the engine's OCF base is rejected for an insurer, and why
- **Engine conservative OE (OCF − capex − SBC):** capex and SBC are not tagged (read as $0), so OE = OCF outright: FY2025 $6.271B = $17.73/share, 3-yr median $17.86/share. For a life insurer OCF is not distributable earnings — it contains premium / policyholder-account flows, derivative collateral and reinsurance timing, and it swings violently: quarterly OCF ran −$2.51B (Q1 2025) to +$4.3B (Q3 2025, per filed 10-Q/10-K pattern), FY2022-25 OCF $5.16B / $6.51B / $8.50B / $6.27B against net income of −$1.65B / $2.49B / $2.73B / $3.58B.
- **Maintenance version (engine):** FY2025 $3.704B — much closer to GAAP net income ($3.576B) than to OCF, which is itself the tell that the OCF definition is the distorted one here.
- **TTM OCF override — computed, then rejected (documented, per the STALE_FY rule):** TTM OCF = FY2025 $6.271B − H1 2025 YTD −$1.344B + H1 2026 YTD $3.354B = **$10.969B ÷ 348.8M diluted shares (H1 2026 YTD, EDGAR) = $31.45/share**. That is a timing artifact, not earning power: H1 2025 was an OCF trough, so the TTM construction mechanically doubles the base. Using it would have manufactured a Tier S/A artifact (the exact failure the v4 audit exists to prevent).
- **Base used — TTM adjusted operating income (Prudential's core-earnings definition: excludes realized investment gains/losses, market-risk-benefit revaluation, divested/run-off), built the same TTM way:** FY2025 adjusted operating EPS $14.43 ($5.161B after tax, company FY2025 release) − H1 2025 $6.87 (Q1 $3.29 + Q2 ~$3.58) + H1 2026 $7.69 (Q1 $3.61 + Q2 $4.08) = **$15.25/share**. Cross-checks: TTM GAAP net income is only $11.23/share (FY $3.576B − H1'25 $1.240B + H1'26 $1.582B = $3.918B ÷ 348.8M) because H1 2026 alone carried −$1.542B pre-tax of reconciling items (realized losses −$1.276B, market-risk benefits −$366M); adjusted BV/share was $100.17 at FY2025 vs GAAP BV $92.05, so price = 1.13× adjusted BV and 7.4× the TTM adjusted base. The adjusted base is the *generous* defensible choice — it is above GAAP TTM — and it is still below the engine's OCF median ($17.86). OE yield on the used base: 13.4%.
- Balance sheet (folded in explicitly): the engine's "net debt" $0.587B is a holding-company sliver (cash $19.71B vs debt-current $1.44B + LT debt $18.86B) and says nothing about an insurer — the real balance sheet is ~$500B+ of invested assets against policyholder liabilities, RBC ~440% at the regulated subs at FY2025 per company materials. Leverage risk here is reserve / asset risk, not corporate net debt; that is handled via the sector confidence cap and bear weights, not a net-debt adjustment.

## Moat score: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 4) = **7.2 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to 2.5% terminal over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Scale and distribution that are hard to replicate quickly — PGIM is a top-10 global manager ($1.49T AUM) with institutional and retail channels; Japan life distribution (Life Planner agency force) built over decades; leading U.S. pension-risk-transfer and annuity shelf space with plan sponsors and advisors. (2) Diversification across fee (PGIM), spread and protection earnings, and geographies dampens any single product cycle — adjusted operating income grew through 2023-26 despite GAAP noise. (3) Brand / trust (the Rock, 150 years) and regulatory capital scale matter in long-duration promises; RBC ~440% is a real barrier for sub-scale rivals.
- **AGAINST:** (1) The core products — annuities, life, group benefits, PRT — are price-competed commodities sold on rate and service against MetLife, Lincoln, MassMutual, Athene/private-credit-backed entrants; Prudential has no structural pricing power, and spread earnings are hostage to rates, credit and longevity, not to its own pricing. (2) PGIM fees face passive / fee compression and episodic outflows (Q2 2026: $1.6B net inflows only because third-party inflows offset affiliated outflows). (3) Legacy drags: closed block and divested/run-off books, variable-annuity market-risk-benefit revaluations that swung pre-tax earnings by −$366M in H1 2026 alone, and a Corporate & Other segment that lost −$609M pre-tax in H1 2026. (4) Returns are ultimately capped by insurance regulation and capital intensity — mid-teens adjusted ROE in a *good* print (16.4% in Q2 2026) is the ceiling story, not the floor. That is a 4, not a 5-6: durable franchise, no durable pricing power.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (controlling flag):** Finance / life insurance — OE/capex DCF fits poorly by construction. Resolved the IBKR way: the model's cash-flow definition was replaced (OCF base discarded, base rebuilt on TTM adjusted operating income), confidence capped at **low**, and the tier is explicitly soft. A BV/ROE or dividend model is the right v5 tool for insurers; under that lens (1.13× adjusted BV for ~14-16% adjusted ROE) the stock also screens as modestly cheap, not dramatically so — consistent with a soft B, not an A/S.
- **STALE_FY (FY ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with TTM work shown in full above — both the OCF TTM ($31.45, computed and rejected as a timing artifact) and the adjusted-operating TTM ($15.25, used). Q2 2026 is the latest quarter: adjusted EPS +14% YoY, AUM $1.64T.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2023, 2024, 2025, gaps >40%):** Structural for an insurer, not a single one-off: OCF exceeds net income by $4.02B / $5.78B / $2.70B in 2023/24/25 because GAAP net income is charged with realized investment losses and market-risk-benefit revaluations (H1 2026 reconciling items −$1.542B pre-tax) and reserve movements that do not flow through OCF the same way, while OCF collects premiums and investment income in cash. D&A ($0.13-0.38B/yr) explains none of it. Consequence: neither raw series is trusted — the adjusted operating definition, which strips exactly those reconciling items, is the base.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 4.2× super-bear):** Real — a levered, market-sensitive insurer's 15-year range genuinely runs from a credit/longevity accident (super-bear IV $57.88, −49%) to sustained mid-teens EPS compounding ($242.93). Consequence per the rules: confidence is low (already capped by the sector flag) and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note even the bear case yields 12.5%, because the entry multiple (7.4× adjusted earnings) cushions the downside.
