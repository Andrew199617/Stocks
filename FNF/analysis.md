v5 run, 2026-10-19. All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed.

## What it is
The largest U.S. title insurer (direct premiums $2,574M and agency premiums $3,250M in FY2025) plus majority-owned F&G Annuities & Life (annuities, IUL, pension risk transfer; F&G contributed 30% of FY2025 adjusted net earnings). On December 31, 2025 FNF distributed ~12% of F&G to shareholders as a special stock dividend (~$500M of value, ~16M FG shares), retaining ~70% and control. FY2025 filer-total revenue $14,445,000,000 (fundamentals.json) — Title $8,490M, F&G $5,731M, Corporate & Other $341M, eliminations -$117M. Price $39.62, market cap $10.777B — in scope under $40B.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow; scale leadership, no structural pricing power
Justification: the largest U.S. title insurer with scale, agency/direct distribution, title-plant data and industry-leading adjusted pre-tax title margins (15.9% FY2025, 17.8% in Q2 2026), plus F&G diversifying earnings away from housing — against a commodity, rate-filed product, earnings hostage to housing volumes and F&G marks, and a return record that does not support a wide moat. Return evidence as required: fundamentals.json carries no roic_proxy and no gross margin for an insurer presentation (both null), so the evidence is ROE — GAAP ROE was 7.5% in FY2023, 16.4% in FY2024 and 8.1% in FY2025 (fundamentals.json), after 21.2% in FY2022 and 29.7% in FY2021 in the title boom: volatile, and mostly below a wide-moat level through the recent cycle.
- FOR:
  - Largest U.S. title insurer — scale in direct and agency distribution, title plants/data and claims infrastructure rivals cannot replicate cheaply
  - Industry-leading adjusted pre-tax title margin: 15.9% for FY2025 and 17.8% in Q2 2026 (15.7% H1 2026), with commercial direct revenue +24% YoY in H1 2026
  - F&G second engine: AUM before flow reinsurance $73.1B at FY2025 (+12% YoY) and $74.7B at Q2 2026; F&G contributed 30% of FY2025 adjusted net earnings, diversifying away from housing volumes
  - Capital return at scale: ~$800M returned in FY2025 ($546M dividends + $251M buybacks); DPS $2.02 in FY2025, 4 consecutive growth years, no cut since 2011 (fundamentals.json)
  - Technology/operating leverage in title: inHere platform engaging ~80% of residential sale transactions (Q2 2026), disciplined expense management through a weak housing cycle
- AGAINST:
  - Commodity, rate-regulated product — title premiums are filed/regulated and re-shopped deal by deal; no structural pricing power, agent commissions were $2,518M in FY2025 against $5,824M of direct+agency premiums
  - Housing/mortgage hostage: title volumes track purchase/refinance transactions, which remain at historically low levels; a prolonged housing freeze compresses the core engine
  - F&G earnings are mark- and assumption-driven: GAAP net earnings swung $2,797M (FY2021) to $517M (FY2023) to $1,270M (FY2024) to $602M (FY2025) on recognized gains/losses, market-risk-benefit and reserve changes; credit impairments in the $73B investment portfolio are the tail
  - Returns do not prove a wide moat: GAAP ROE 7.5% / 16.4% / 8.1% in FY2023/24/25 (fundamentals.json), no roic_proxy filed for an insurer presentation
  - Corporate drag and complexity: Corporate & Other lost $458M attributable in FY2025 including the F&G distribution deferred-tax adjustment ($471M); the Dec 31, 2025 ~12% F&G share distribution cut FNF's stake and its share of F&G earnings
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, and here it is extreme. The same final scenario set on the engine's native OCF bases prints **Tier S**: weighted IRR 58.70% on the TTM OCF base (key_numbers.md measured impact, overrides off) and 68.24% with IV@15% $232.69 on the normalized OCF base in the capex-only run — a fake S, produced entirely by counting annuity deposits and policy-benefit builds as earnings while their investment deployment sits in investing. On the scored GAAP-NI median base the company prints **Tier D, weighted IRR 9.73%, IV@15% $22.26**. On the company's own adjusted-earnings base — FY2025 adjusted net earnings of $1,352M, $4.97/share (FY2025 earnings release; FY2024: $4.63) — the identical scenario set prints **Tier B, weighted IRR 18.04%, IV@15% $49.98** (bear 11.09% → bull 22.44%), measured in a separate engine run of this same configuration. Anyone quoting one number for FNF without its method is hiding the ball; the two defensible earnings bases span D to B, and the scored verdict sits at the conservative end of that range, per the sizing rule.
- Why the scored end is the GAAP end: the adjusted base strips every F&G mark and recognized loss as if they were free — for an annuity writer holding a $73B investment portfolio, marks and credit are a recurring cost of the business, not noise. But the GAAP base is not clean either: its median year carries the $471M one-off distribution tax charge, which is why the base-case growth start (+6%) carries normalization and why the range, not the point, is the finding.
- The cross-checks the model cannot do sit between the ends: at $39.62 the market pays 8.0x FY2025 adjusted EPS of $4.97 and a $2.02 dividend (fundamentals.json DPS) — pricing in roughly the adjusted run-rate with little housing recovery, which is closer to the B-end economics than to D. What the D is really saying is narrower: on *GAAP* earnings as filed, with F&G marks charged in full, $39.62 does not clear a 15% hurdle; it clears 15% only below $22.26 on that base ($49.98 on the adjusted base).
- What would change the verdict: a housing recovery (title is the operating leverage — purchase orders are at historic lows, so volume normalization is the super-bull path), F&G credit losses landing (the bear path), or further F&G distributions/sales shrinking FNF's share of the steadier earnings stream (the Dec 2025 distribution already cut the stake to ~70% and triggered the tax charge).
