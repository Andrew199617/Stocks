## What it is
One of Korea's big-four financial holding companies (with KB, Hana and Woori): Shinhan Bank at the core, plus Shinhan Card, Shinhan Life, Shinhan Securities and other financial subsidiaries, with growing overseas earnings (Japan and Vietnam called out in Q2 2026). Q2 2026 (per the company's earnings presentation): consolidated net income KRW 1,820.1 billion, up 12.2% QoQ; ROE 12.4% and ROTCE 13.9%; CET1 provisionally 13.43%; the board approved a further KRW 700 billion buyback tranche and a KRW 741/share Q2 dividend, taking announced buybacks through October 2026 to KRW 1.4 trillion. Management guides an ROE range of 10–12% with a 10% floor, noted a 42bp credit cost ratio in Q2 2026 (up QoQ on conservative provisioning), and flagged insurance income softness and FX-driven RWA moves.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate, standard big-four Korean franchise
Justification: a regulated big-four oligopoly position with a large sticky deposit franchise and diversified group fee businesses, against a commodity banking product with no structural pricing power, full credit/rate-cycle exposure, and regulatory direction over capital return.
- FOR:
  - Regulated big-four oligopoly in Korean banking: scale deposit franchise at Shinhan Bank, high entry barriers, sticky retail/SME relationships
  - Diversified group earnings: bank plus Shinhan Card, Shinhan Life and Shinhan Securities fee businesses reduce single-line cyclicality
  - Capital strength funding returns: CET1 13.43% at Q2 2026, KRW 1.4 trillion of buybacks announced through October 2026 plus quarterly dividends
  - Overseas growth leg: overseas earnings up ~13% QoQ in Q2 2026, led by Japan and Vietnam
  - Returns discipline: management ROE range 10–12% with a 10% floor; Q2 2026 ROE 12.4% and ROTCE 13.9%
- AGAINST:
  - Commodity product: deposits and loans are fungible; no structural pricing power over NIM through a cycle
  - Credit-cycle exposure: Q2 2026 credit cost ratio 42bp and rising QoQ on conservative provisioning; a Korean downturn hits provisions first
  - Regulatory/political direction: Korean authorities steer bank dividends, buybacks, household-debt growth and windfall measures
  - Mature low-growth home market: Korean nominal growth caps loan and fee growth; overseas is still a minority of earnings
  - Rate and FX sensitivity: NIM depends on market rates management does not control, and KRW moves drive both RWA and USD-translated earnings
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:sbc** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing-sourced override — see OVERRIDE. data_gap_uncovered is empty in the final run.
- **ADS_RATIO_MISSING** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing-sourced ads_ratio of 1.0 — see OVERRIDE; the final run flags ADS_RATIO_APPLIED instead.
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 21.42 months ago): NOT resolvable by the standard route — ttm.py exits 3 for this IFRS filer (its exact message is quoted in the Owner's earnings section), so no TTM base exists and the median FY2022–24 base is used per spec. Two staleness facts are on the record: (1) a FY2025 Form 20-F was filed on 2026-04-22, but the engine's EDGAR companyfacts history still ends at FY2024 — the base is a year staler than the filer's own reporting; (2) the prints since FY2024 run above the base year's economics (Q2 2026 net income KRW 1,820.1 billion, ROE 12.4% vs the 10–12% guide range). The staleness therefore cuts conservative here — it understates current earnings power rather than flattering it — and it is still not corrected by hand, because no code-computed newer base exists. Confidence is low in part for this reason.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): the volatility is in bank operating cash flow, not in earnings — net income over FY2022–24 printed $3,756,856,060 / $3,448,060,000 / $3,099,555,600 while conservative OE printed $5,398,577,970 / $170,759,820 / $2,934,762,360 on OCF swings. The normalized median base is the designed answer, and the bear weight is already 35% combined with 0.75/0.90 OE multipliers; probabilities were not skewed further because the flagged series (OCF) is not the series a bank's earnings power follows. Named, not hidden: if a real credit cycle hits, the bear cases — not the base — are the relevant anchor.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023): net income $3,448,060,000 vs OCF $407,981,420 in FY2023. No one-off gain or loss in net income was found; the divergence is the IFRS bank cash-flow presentation itself — deposit, loan and trading-balance flows pass through operating cash flow, so a year of balance-sheet flow timing collapses OCF while earnings move modestly (net income fell only single digits vs FY2022's $3,756,856,060). This is structural for a bank, recurs across the history (OCF was negative in FY2018 and FY2020 on the same presentation), and is the core reason the sector flag below applies. It does not contaminate the median base directly: the median lands on FY2024, where OCF ($3,145,883,320) and net income ($3,099,555,600) are close.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: reporting currency KRW converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (recorded in dcf_numbers.json: 0.00077 for 2023-12-31, 0.00068 for 2024-12-31); flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average, so the won's 2024 depreciation mechanically depresses USD-translated FY2024 OE/share relative to the underlying KRW economics. The sbc_override was declared in KRW and converted by the same pass.
- **USD_TRANSLATIONS_USED**: the issuer files its own USD convenience translations alongside its KRW reporting currency; where present, the engine used the filed USD figures rather than its own conversion.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: per-share figures scaled x1.0 ordinary shares per ADS per the FY2024 20-F (see OVERRIDE) — a no-op numerically at a 1:1 ratio, recorded so the gate is satisfied from a filing source rather than assumed.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance): accepted in full. A bank's owner's earnings are not its distributable cash — loan growth consumes regulatory capital (CET1 13.43% at Q2 2026 is the constraint that actually sizes the buyback), provisions are a through-cycle cost the OCF line carries inconsistently, and bank value is normally judged on ROE vs cost of equity and price-to-book, none of which this model computes. Confidence is low and the tier is soft; no tier override is taken, because in the base year the OCF-based OE is not systematically flattering (FY2024 OCF and net income are close) — unlike the insurer cases where the base capitalized float.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc**: see OVERRIDE — gap, decision, single-value imperfection, and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.50x): real — a bank's outcome range across a credit cycle IS wide (super-bear IV@15% $24.58 vs super-bull $110.68). Confidence is low (also mandated by the sector flag), and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
