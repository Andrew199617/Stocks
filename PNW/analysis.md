## What it is
Phoenix-based regulated utility holding company whose principal subsidiary is Arizona Public Service (APS), Arizona's largest electric utility: generation (including the APS-operated Palo Verde nuclear station, the largest nuclear plant in the US), transmission and distribution across one of the fastest-growing service territories in the country. Essentially all earnings are regulated by the Arizona Corporation Commission (ACC). The model is rate-base compounding: a $10.35B capital plan for 2025–2028 (generation, transmission, distribution), weather-normalized sales growth guided at 5%–7% through 2030, and long-term EPS growth targeted at 5%–7% (search results). A rate case is pending: APS requests roughly a $611M net revenue increase (a ~14.75% day-one customer bill impact) on a 10.70% requested ROE, plus a proposed formula rate adjustment mechanism to cut regulatory lag (search results).

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Exclusive regulated electric franchise (Arizona Public Service) in a top-growth territory — customer growth 2.1% and weather-normalized sales +5.6% in Q2 2026, 5%–7% sales growth guided through 2030, semiconductor/large-load demand, and Palo Verde baseload — a durable, visible compounding machine. Capped at 6 because the Arizona Corporation Commission sets the allowed return (pending case requests 10.70% ROE; measured roic_proxy is only 4.09%–5.32% over FY2021–FY2025, at/below the cost of capital through the cycle), there is no pricing power beyond rate cases, and the build is funded with debt (net debt $10,556M) plus $1.0B–$1.2B of planned equity issuance on a share count already growing +2.45%/yr.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated electric franchise in one of the fastest-growing US service territories — APS customer growth 2.1% in Q2 2026, weather-normalized sales growth 5.6%, long-term weather-normalized sales growth guided 5%–7% through 2030; Palo Verde nuclear station — APS-operated, the largest US nuclear plant — gives low-marginal-cost baseload, with 20-year license renewals sought through 2065–2067; semiconductor and large-load expansion in the Phoenix metro drives commercial load growth on top of residential growth — total sales +9.6% in Q2 2026; essential, inelastic demand with a 12-year dividend growth record (fundamentals.json) and a $10.35B 2025–2028 capital plan that enters rate base and earns regulated returns once recovered.
- AGAINST: The ACC caps the economics: the pending rate case requests a 10.70% ROE and ~$611M net revenue increase with a ~14.75% day-one bill impact — recovery can be trimmed, lagged or politicized (the ACC's rooftop-solar surcharge was vacated by the Arizona Court of Appeals in June 2026); measured returns are thin: roic_proxy in fundamentals.json runs 4.09%–5.32% over FY2021–FY2025 and ROE 8.28%–10.77% — at or below the cost of capital, as regulated returns are designed to be; growth is debt- plus equity-funded: net debt $10,556M plus $3,737M lease liabilities, and $1.0B–$1.2B of new equity financing planned through 2028; share count already grows +2.45%/yr, diluting rate-base growth per share; extreme-heat / wildfire / water exposure in the Arizona service territory and Palo Verde concentration raise physical and remediation cost tails that sit with equity if recovery is denied.
- Evidence on returns (fundamentals.json, last five fiscal years): roic_proxy FY2021 4.86% / FY2022 4.09% / FY2023 4.73% / FY2024 5.32% / FY2025 5.19%; ROE 10.77% / 8.28% / 8.40% / 9.27% / 8.96% over the same years. Gross margin is not split out for this filer in fundamentals.json (utility cost-of-service presentation), so the ROIC series is the returns evidence. That is exactly the regulated profile: returns pinned near the allowed level, never well above the cost of capital — which is why the moat cannot score 7+ under the sweep's rubric despite the franchise's durability.
