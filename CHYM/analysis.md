## What it is
US neobank for mass-market / underbanked consumers (founded 2012, IPO June 2025 at $27): free checking/savings, early direct deposit, MyPay earned-wage access and Instant Loans, delivered through partner banks (Stride Bank, The Bancorp Bank) — Chime itself held no charter in the filing period. Revenue is primarily debit interchange (payments revenue $430M of $670M in Q2 2026) earned at the Durbin small-issuer exemption rate via those sub-$10B-asset partners, plus platform-related revenue ($240M in Q2 2026, +48% YoY). 10.4M active members at Q2 2026 (+20% YoY), average revenue per active member $260. Price $29.67 (nasdaq_quote_api); engine market-cap estimate $7.01B on the FY2025 weighted diluted count — the TTM diluted count is 396,943,274, so the live market cap is higher (press put it at ~$11.4B in October 2026); in scope under $40B either way.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate
Justification: a real brand and primary-account direct-deposit base in an underserved segment, with an owned processing stack, but the core economics rent a regulatory exemption (Durbin small-issuer interchange) through partner banks Chime did not own in the filing period, consumer switching costs are low, and there is no through-cycle record of returns on capital.
- FOR: 10.4M active members with primary-account direct-deposit relationships — the paycheck anchor is the stickiest position in mass-market consumer finance; highest engagement in the category (members transact far more frequently than Cash App peers, per Q2 reporting); ChimeCore owned processing stack — migration completed Q4 2025, company-estimated ~60% lower transaction processing cost, supporting the gross-margin record below; brand leadership in the underbanked segment.
- AGAINST: ~two-thirds of revenue is interchange priced off the Durbin small-bank exemption — a regulatory loophole, not an owned asset; any Durbin expansion or BaaS tightening hits the core directly; no bank charter of its own in the filing period (deposits and issuance sit with Stride / Bancorp); low consumer switching costs vs Cash App, SoFi, Dave, Current and the incumbent banks; member base (household income roughly $35–75K) is the most employment- and spend-cyclical segment; lending expansion (MyPay, Instant Loans) imports credit risk the model has not carried through a downturn.
- Evidence: gross margin 82.8% (FY2023) → 87.6% (FY2024) → 88.0% (FY2025) → 88.7% TTM — high and rising, consistent with an asset-light cost advantage. The `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json (8.5% FY2023, 2.2% FY2024, −87.8% FY2025) is NOT meaningful evidence either way: invested capital was negative through the pre-IPO years (equity −$1,953,613,000 in FY2023, −$1,930,600,000 in FY2024, with preferred stock outside equity) and the FY2025 figure is the IPO SBC catch-up flowing through operating income. There is no clean year of ROIC above the cost of capital on the record — that absence, not the distorted series, is why the moat cannot score higher.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range is the story: on the 3-yr median base (−$3.06/share) the name is an unscoreable Tier F; on the cleanest current base (TTM, IPO SBC catch-up removed by the TTM subtraction itself) it is a Tier D with a weighted IV@15% of $1.55 — 5% of the price. No defensible OE method gets anywhere near $29.67; the market price is a bet on adjusted-EBITDA-style earnings (FY2026 Adj. EBITDA guided to $465–475M) that treat ~$260M+/yr of ongoing SBC as free. In this system SBC is a real cost, and on that basis Chime currently converts $2.46B of TTM revenue into $30.07M of owner's earnings.
- The operating business is genuinely improving — revenue +27% in Q2 2026, gross margin 88.7% TTM, second consecutive GAAP-profitable quarter, guidance raised, platform revenue +48% — and none of it closes a 95% valuation gap on OE math. Improvement would have to show up as OCF growing much faster than the SBC run rate for several years before a re-run could move this.
- Post-filing event (after the FY2025 10-K, before this run): on September 8, 2026 Chime agreed to acquire its partner Stride Bank for $590M cash, expected to close H1 2027 pending OCC approval — roughly 56% of its cash + marketable securities at FY2025. Owning the charter internalizes partner-bank economics (company-estimated >$100M net synergies) but adds bank capital/liquidity requirements and a hard strategic constraint: preserving the Durbin small-issuer exemption means keeping the combined bank under $10B of assets (combined assets already ~$7B per analyst estimates), which caps the deposit growth the model depends on. Not in any filed number used here; a re-run after close will need the new balance sheet. Also announced after Q2 2026: a ~10% workforce reduction (August 2026) — cost action that supports the SBC/opex normalization story but is not yet in filed figures.
- Insider sales of ~$75M in the days after the Q2 beat (largely under 10b5-1 plans, plus a pre-IPO backer sale) are noted, not weighted — they do not change the OE arithmetic.
