## What it is
Commercial-stage biotech built on one product: ANKTIVA (nogapendekin alfa inbakicept), an IL-15 receptor agonist given with BCG for BCG-unresponsive non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer (NMIBC) with CIS, FDA-approved April 2024. Everything else (NSCLC, BCG-naïve NMIBC, hematologic malignancies, Lynch-syndrome prevention) is pipeline. The commercial ramp is real: revenue was $622,000 in FY2023, $14,745,000 in FY2024, $113,288,000 in FY2025 (financials_history.csv); the company reported Q2 2026 net product revenue of $50.7M (+92% YoY, eighth consecutive sequential-growth quarter) and H1 2026 revenue of $94.8M, with ANKTIVA approved or authorized in five jurisdictions / 34 countries and an FDA sBLA for papillary-only disease (PDUFA Jan 6, 2027; secondary reporting says the expansion was granted early on Oct 14, 2026). None of that has yet produced a dollar of owner's earnings.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: a genuinely first-in-class approved product with strong durability data, but a single-product company with negative equity, no positive OE in a decade of filings, total dependence on one indication's adoption curve and on BCG co-supply, and a financing lifeline (founder-affiliated debt and repeated equity issuance) that is a property of the founder, not of the franchise. Return on capital is not measurable in the usual sense — equity is negative (−$500,469,000 at FY2025) and operating income has been negative every filed year — so there is no ROIC history above the cost of capital to support a higher score; gross margin history is likewise not meaningful at this revenue scale against a full R&D/commercial cost base.
- FOR: only approved IL-15 receptor agonist; ANKTIVA + BCG is an established bladder-preserving standard in BCG-unresponsive NMIBC CIS with long-term responder data (84% three-year bladder preservation among responders, per company reporting); approvals/authorizations in five jurisdictions spanning 34 countries; exclusive Tokyo-172 BCG supply agreement (Japan BCG Laboratory) securing the co-administered input competitors also need.
- AGAINST: one product = 100% of revenue; label expansion and every pipeline indication are still binary regulatory events; competing bladder-preserving therapies with different mechanisms are evolving in the same niche; the regimen requires BCG co-administration, so supply and treatment-center capacity cap growth; persistent going-concern language in the 10-Qs (mitigated only by the founder's stated intent and ability to fund); share count compounding at 32.0%/yr (dcf_numbers.json) with ~167M potential shares from convertibles/warrants/options and an ATM program still available — per-share economics are diluted away as fast as revenue grows.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years — academic here, since there is no positive base OE to project.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a venture-style binary, not a DCF security: the $8.44B market cap prices ANKTIVA becoming a multi-indication platform, while the filing record shows a company that has never generated positive owner's earnings and funds itself with dilutive equity and founder-affiliated debt-like instruments. The revenue ramp (+92% YoY in Q2 2026, eight straight sequential growth quarters) is the strongest bull fact and it is already in the price many times over.
- What would change the verdict: TTM OCF turning positive (the burn narrowed from −$391M in FY2024 OCF to −$281M TTM — the trajectory, if it simply continued, crosses zero within a few years), the papillary expansion and BCG-naïve filings converting to label, and dilution slowing to single digits. A re-run on a future filing with positive TTM OE would be scoreable; today it is not.
- Method range: there is none to report — normalized base, latest-FY base, and TTM base are all negative, so every defensible OE method lands on F. The disagreement a buyer is underwriting is about future approvals, which this model deliberately does not price.

## Final verdict
