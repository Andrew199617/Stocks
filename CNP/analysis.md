## What it is
Houston-based regulated energy-delivery utility: exclusive electric transmission and distribution in the Houston metro area, plus regulated natural-gas distribution (LDC) businesses across several states (Texas, Indiana, Minnesota and others; the Ohio gas business was sold October 1, 2026 — see Thoughts). It earns regulated tariffs and allowed returns on rate base; it does not compete for delivery customers. Growth is a capital plan story: a reported $66.7B 10-year capital investment plan for 2026–2035 (search-cited Q2 2026 release), aimed at Houston large-load demand and grid resilience.

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped returns
Justification: exclusive regulated franchises (Houston electric T&D, multi-state gas LDCs) with essential service and no delivery competition, earning allowed returns on a growing rate base — but prices and ROE are set by state commissions, and storm/regulatory politics, Texas concentration, leverage and perpetual external funding erode the franchise. In line with the scored regulated-utility peers in this sweep (5–6/10).
- FOR: Exclusive regulated electric T&D franchise in the Houston metro — no competing wires, customers cannot switch delivery providers
- FOR: Regulated natural-gas LDC monopolies across multiple states — essential, non-discretionary service
- FOR: Allowed return on rate base: earnings compound with the $66.7B 2026–2035 capital plan as it enters rate base
- FOR: Large-load / population growth in the Houston service territory supports durable volume and rate-base growth
- FOR: Cost recovery riders / trackers shorten the lag between capex and recovery in key jurisdictions
- AGAINST: No true pricing power — prices and ROE are set by state regulators (PUCT etc.), not the company
- AGAINST: Political / regulatory backlash risk is live: Hurricane Beryl (2024) restoration cost recovery and reliability scrutiny in Texas
- AGAINST: Texas / Houston concentration — storm, hurricane and ERCOT-system exposure in one metro
- AGAINST: High leverage (net debt $22,942M per dcf_numbers.json) plus a $66.7B capex plan needs continuous debt and equity funding — dilution / rate pressure
- AGAINST: Gas LDC long-term transition risk and rate-case lag if regulators disallow or defer recovery
- A 6 gives CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 6) = **9.8 years** — moot for the DCF (no positive OE to fade), but it records the fade the engine would apply if OE turned positive.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is the model's known blind spot, flagged as such — the same verdict shape as AEE / ATO / DTE / ED / FE / PCG / PEG / PPL in this sweep: a profitable regulated monopoly whose conservative owner's earnings are negative in every year because capex for a multi-year rate-base build runs ahead of operating cash flow. An OE screen reads the regulated funding model (build now, recover with a return over decades, fund the gap with debt and equity) as negative distributable cash. That reading is honest about today's cash and incomplete about the regulated claim on future tariffs.
- On the business's own terms the trajectory is a guided high-single-digit EPS compounder: reported 2026 non-GAAP EPS guidance $1.89–$1.91 (~8% growth at the midpoint over 2025) and a reported long-term target of 7–9% annual EPS growth through 2035, backed by the $66.7B plan and Houston large-load demand. Even taken at face value, 7–9% EPS growth plus a dividend does not clear a 15% hurdle at $39.68 — the guided path is a bond-like compounder, not a 15% IRR story, unless the multiple expands or the price falls materially. No hurdle-clearing price is computable from this method: with negative OE there is no price at which the OE DCF itself clears 15% (a lower price only helps once a positive OE base exists).
- What would change the tier: the capex pace bending toward maintenance levels (the plan is explicitly a 2026–2035 build, so not soon), or several years of filings in which OCF growth from a larger rate base outruns capex — i.e. the first positive conservative-OE year. Until that exists, CNP stays F in this sweep regardless of rate-case headlines.
- Risks that are real, not model artifacts: storm exposure in its home metro (Hurricane Beryl restoration — the FY2025 10-K describes a settlement under which Houston Electric is entitled to recover $1.1B of distribution-related system-restoration costs, largely via securitization approved in October 2025: the recovery mechanism worked, but it took a political fight), allowed-ROE compression if rates stay high, and execution/funding risk on a plan that needs capital markets to stay open and cheap.
- Method range: unusually, there is none on the conservative definition — latest-FY, 3-yr-median and filing-derived TTM bases are all negative, so every defensible run of this method gives Tier F. The maintenance definition is positive ($113,000,000 in FY2025) but too working-capital-volatile to underwrite a tier, and forcing one from it would be exactly the kind of definition-shopping the v5 gate exists to prevent.
