# PCG — PG&E Corporation
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Utilities / Regulated Electric & Gas (California). Price $13.33 (Nasdaq quote API, final engine run). Market cap ~$29.4B — in scope under $40B.

## What it is
Oakland-based holding company for Pacific Gas and Electric Company, the regulated electric and gas utility serving ~16 million people across Northern and Central California (~5.6M electric, ~4.6M gas accounts), plus the Diablo Canyon nuclear plant (license renewal approved by the NRC in 2026). Almost all earnings are regulated: a multi-year program of wildfire hardening — undergrounding (>1,900 miles planned by end-2027, up to 5,000 more miles post-2028 under the regulatory framework), covered conductor, and monitoring — drives a guided ~9-10% rate-base CAGR. Management reaffirmed FY2026 non-GAAP core EPS guidance of $1.64-$1.66 with Q2 2026 results and targets **at least 9% core EPS growth each year through 2030**, funded with **no new common equity** (the 2024-2028 equity plan is complete). Over 12 GW of data-center projects are in the pipeline, a real load-growth kicker. The equity story is inseparable from its history: wildfire liabilities drove the 2019-2020 Chapter 11, and California's inverse-condemnation doctrine still makes the utility liable for equipment-caused fires without proof of negligence.

## Owner's earnings — conservative OE is structurally negative; the usable base is TTM core earnings
- **Conservative OE (OCF − total capex − SBC; SBC is nil — no SBC tag, utility comp is cash):** FY2025 $8,716M − $11,787M = **−$3,071M = −$1.39/share**. Negative in **0 of the last 5 fiscal years** (FY2021 −$5,427M through FY2025 −$3,071M). This is structural, not a slump: a utility compounding rate base at ~9-10%/yr spends depreciation-plus-growth on plant every year, funded by debt. OCF − total capex treats growth capex that enters rate base and earns an allowed return as if it were maintenance — the wrong yardstick here, exactly why the maintenance definition exists.
- **Maintenance OE (engine formula NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC):** the engine leaves it blank for every year because PG&E carries no usable `DepreciationDepletionAndAmortization` annual tag in EDGAR companyfacts (used_tags da = null). As with ED and PEG, reported core (non-GAAP operating) earnings are the maintenance proxy: they strip the wildfire-fund accrual noise and one-offs that core earnings are designed to exclude, and they track the regulator-allowed return on rate base.
- **TTM conservative OE, filing-derived from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (for the STALE_FY resolution):** OCF TTM = FY25 $8,716M − H1'25 $3,905M + H1'26 $3,336M = **$8,147M**; capex TTM = $11,787M − $5,700M + $6,323M = **$12,410M**; conservative OE TTM = $8,147M − $12,410M = **−$4,263M ≈ −$1.94/share** on 2,202M diluted shares. Still deeply negative — the TTM update confirms the FY picture; it does not rescue the conservative definition for a rate-base grower mid-undergrounding-program.
- **Base used — TTM non-GAAP core EPS through Q2 2026 = $1.69/share:** FY2025 $1.50 − H1 2025 $0.64 + H1 2026 $0.83 = **$1.69**. Cross-check: TTM GAAP EPS = $1.23 (FY25 NI $2,703M / 2,202M shares) − $0.51 + $0.72 = **$1.44**; the $0.25 gap to core is the items management excludes (mainly wildfire-fund and claims charges — the 2025 toll alone was a $352M Wildfire Fund charge plus ~$100M of wildfire claims costs after recoveries — and other non-core items). Price = **7.9× TTM core earnings**; OE yield 12.7% on the final run — a steep wildfire/regulatory discount to utility peers (PEG 16.5×, ED 18.5× on the same proxy).

## Moat score: 5/10 — a real legal monopoly whose prize is capped and whose tail can kill the equity
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3×5) = **8.5 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Legal-monopoly franchise across Northern/Central California — no customer switching, essential inelastic demand, and a service territory now adding data-center load at scale (12+ GW in the project pipeline, 3.6 GW at final engineering as of Q4 2025); (2) unusually visible growth: CPUC general rate cases and clause recovery fund a guided ~9-10% rate-base CAGR, and management converts it to **≥9% guided core EPS growth through 2030 with no new equity** — the funding plan's equity leg is already done; (3) Diablo Canyon is a scarce, carbon-free baseload asset with a renewed license; (4) execution proof: three straight years with no major equipment-caused wildfires, 2-4%/yr non-fuel O&M cost reductions funding bill restraint (bundled residential rates down since 2024), and guidance met or raised repeatedly (FY2025 core $1.50 landed at the top of the $1.48-$1.52 guide).
- **AGAINST:** (1) The CPUC caps the prize — authorized ROE is regulator-set (2026 cost-of-capital proceeding sought 11.3%; allowed returns will land well below what a 9% EPS grower would earn unregulated), and the 2027-2030 GRC is filed into an affordability-first political environment; (2) **inverse condemnation is an equity-killer tail**: no negligence need be proven, and the 2019 bankruptcy is the proof of concept — one catastrophic fire season can wipe the equity regardless of the moat; (3) the backstop is thinner than the headline — the original ~$21B Wildfire Fund is expected to be largely consumed by the (Southern California Edison) Eaton fire, and SB 254's $18B Continuation Account holds no cash yet, with utility contributions not scheduled until 2029 and PG&E's disallowance share at 47.85%; the August 2026 attempt (SB 492) to harden the liability framework died in the Assembly; (4) new restitution law AB 2700 (effective Jan 1, 2027) directs the CPUC to pursue "full restitution" for pre-2019 fire victims — ~$6B of determined Fire Victim Trust losses remain unpaid — with mechanisms that explicitly include **reduced or deferred shareholder dividends and equity contributions**; (5) growth is debt-funded (net debt $57.5B, see HIGH_NET_DEBT), so the equity sits behind a growing debt stack while the dividend is only now rebuilding (payout guided to ~20%, doubled for 2026 but still small).

Full filing arithmetic is in the OE section: TTM OCF $8,147M, TTM capex $12,410M, TTM conservative OE −$4,263M (−$1.94/share), and the base actually used — TTM non-GAAP core EPS $1.69 ($1.50 − $0.64 + $0.83). H1 2026 core EPS grew +29.7% YoY, so the TTM base is current, not stale.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (final run: 15.4× normalized OE on the engine's basis):** Engine net debt at Dec 31, 2025 = debt current $821M + long-term debt $57,387M − cash $713M = **$57,495M**, ~15.4× the TTM base OE total (~$3,721M), and still rising — the Utility issued another $4.4B of bonds in H1 2026. Resolution: for a regulated utility this leverage is structural and regulator-sanctioned — debt is serviced out of allowed rate-base returns, not free cash flow, and the no-new-equity funding plan means the stack grows by design. But PG&E's version is heavier than peers' and it compounds the wildfire tail: in a disallowance, restitution (AB 2700 names equity contributions as a mechanism), or catastrophic-fire case, the equity behind $57.5B+ of debt absorbs it first. Folded into the verdict via the defensive scenario weights (40% combined bear weight) and low confidence.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (final run: 2023, 2024, 2025):** Resolved — the gap is structural depreciation, not a one-off. NI vs OCF: 2023 $2,256M vs $4,747M (52% gap), 2024 $2,512M vs $8,035M (69%), 2025 $2,703M vs $8,716M (69%). Net income is stated after depreciation on a ~$66B+ net plant base (several $B/yr), which OCF adds back; OCF also benefits from deferred taxes and non-cash wildfire-fund accrual timing. The flag's D&A exemption could not clear it mechanically because the engine found **no D&A tag at all** for PCG in EDGAR companyfacts (used_tags da = null) — a tag-coverage gap, not an earnings-quality signal. Direction matters too: OCF runs *above* NI persistently, the opposite of the KO tax-deposit pattern this flag was built for. Base unaffected (the base is core EPS, cross-checked to GAAP EPS $1.44 TTM).
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (agent-identified, in substance — regulated utility, the same class the audit names for banks/insurers/REITs):** Conservative owner's earnings scores a growing regulated utility at ~−$2 to −$3B/yr forever, which would make every rate-base compounder a Tier F. The verdict therefore rests on the maintenance-proxy (TTM core earnings) base, with confidence **low** and the tier treated as soft. Direction check: unlike PEG/ED, PCG's discount is so wide (7.9× core earnings vs 16.5-18.5× for peers) that even the GAAP base ($1.44) at guided growth clears 15% — the B does not depend on choosing core over GAAP; it depends on the wildfire tail not firing.
- **History caveat (no flag fired, stated for the record):** the 2016-2020 rows straddle the wildfire-crisis/bankruptcy era (FY2018 NI −$6,837M, FY2019 −$7,642M, FY2020 OCF −$19,130M on claims payments) — no CAGR or median off those years is meaningful, and none was used; the base and growth inputs are built entirely from FY2024-2026 prints and guidance. Share count has crept up ~1.1%/yr (2,132M → 2,202M diluted over the window, post-bankruptcy issuance and the now-completed equity plan), a small real drag already in the engine's blend.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** mechanical engine run on the conservative base: Tier F — rejected as a definition artifact (it prices a company earning $2.7B GAAP / ~$3.7B core at less than zero). GAAP-EPS base ($1.44) with the same scenarios would land ~15-16% weighted — still B, barely. Corrected core base: **18.2%, Tier B**. The honest range is B-with-a-tail: the cash-flow scenarios all clear or approach the hurdle, and the real risk sits outside the scenario table (a catastrophic fire is a step-function to near-zero, as 2019 proved).
- Quality vs peers cuts both ways: PCG guides faster EPS growth (≥9%) than PEG (6-8%) or ED (~mid-single-digit) and trades at half their multiple. That gap is the market pricing inverse condemnation, AB 2700 restitution, an unfunded Continuation Account, and CPUC politics — not operations, which have delivered three clean fire years and repeated guidance beats.
- What would change the answer: a catastrophic PG&E-caused fire (thesis over, not a downgrade); a funded, durable wildfire-liability framework out of the 2026-27 Sacramento/CPUC process (multiple re-rates toward peers, IV well above the bull case); or an adverse 2027-2030 GRC / cost-of-capital outcome cutting allowed ROE (bear case becomes base).
- **Hurdle math:** the stock already clears 15% at $13.33 — weighted IV@15% is ≈ **$17**; with a 20% margin of safety the buy price is ≈ **$13.60**, i.e. roughly today's price. The margin of safety here is the multiple discount itself, and it is compensation for a binary tail, not a free lunch.
