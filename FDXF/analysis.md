# FDXF — FedEx Freight Holding Company, Inc.
v5.4 run, 2026-10-16 (first pass). All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, ttm.py output, or fundamentals.json — none are recomputed.

## What it is
The largest North American less-than-truckload (LTL) carrier — Priority / Economy / Direct services, ~86,000 average daily shipments, ~365+ locations, more than 26,000 service-center doors, nearly 30,000 vehicles, ~40,000 employees, serving 98% of U.S. ZIP codes plus Canada and Mexico. Spun off from FedEx Corporation on June 1, 2026: 80.1% of shares distributed to FedEx holders (1 FDXF share per 2 FDX shares), FedEx retained a 19.9% stake it expects to monetize within 24 months, and FDXF funded a ~$4.1B pre-separation cash dividend to FedEx with new debt. Fiscal year-end changed from May 31 to December 31 effective June 1, 2026, so the period June–December 2026 is a seven-month transition period. Market cap $16.98B at $113.58 (nasdaq_quote_api) — in scope under $40B.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: the densest LTL network in North America is a real scale asset and terminals are costly and slow to replicate, but LTL service is replicable by well-run rivals, pricing is cyclical, and FedEx Freight is not the service or margin leader — Old Dominion is.
- FOR: Largest NA LTL carrier by revenue ($8,782M in the 2025 industry ranking vs Old Dominion's $5,446M) — network density across 365+ locations / 26,000+ doors gives transit-time and coverage advantages (98% of U.S. ZIP codes); high barriers to entry — service-center terminals are costly and time-consuming to build, and the top five carriers collectively hold the majority of the market; dual Priority/Economy model lets it segment time-definite vs deferred freight off one network; proprietary digital platforms (LTL Select) and route-optimization tooling.
- AGAINST: Not the quality leader — Old Dominion has been ranked the top national carrier in shipper surveys for 16 consecutive years and earns structurally higher margins; FDXF's operating margin (fundamentals.json) fell 18.6% → 15.8% → 6.1% GAAP over FY2024–26 (FY2026 adjusted margin 12.6%, per the company release), all below best-in-class LTL levels; demand is industrial-cycle freight — average daily shipments fell 4% in FY2026 and 5.9% in Q4, with industry excess capacity pressuring yield; labor is the dominant cost and wage rates are rising; switching costs for shippers are low — freight is re-bid, and Estes, XPO, Saia and R+L run comparable networks.
- **ROIC evidence (fundamentals.json, roic_proxy): 62.5% (FY2024), 42.5% (FY2025), 9.9% (FY2026).** The first two prints are carve-out artifacts, not franchise proof: the denominator (equity + debt − cash) was the parent's small net investment in a business whose terminals and fleet were carried inside FedEx, and it bore no standalone debt. FY2026's 9.9% is the first print on a standalone capital base — and equity went negative (−$497M) after the debt-funded dividend, which makes the proxy itself unstable. Gross margin is not filed (no COGS tag in the carve-out statements — fundamentals.json gross_margin is null for all three years), so the margin evidence is the operating-margin series above: mid-to-high teens pre-spin, collapsing in FY2026. A moat above 5 would need demonstrated through-cycle ROIC on the standalone balance sheet, which does not exist yet — one of the reasons confidence is low.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **THIN_HISTORY: only 3 fiscal years of data** — real and structural: the entire filed history is the carve-out prepared for the June 1, 2026 spin-off (10-K filed 2026-08-05). There is no longer standalone history to consult and no quarterly history at all (fundamentals.json assembled 0 quarters). Handled by confidence low, widened bear weight (40% bear-or-worse), and scenario sizing to the bear end.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE: latest FY OE/share −1.50 vs 3-yr median 7.14** — the *latest* year is the distorted one. FY2026 absorbed $492M of separation and other costs (professional fees and an employee incentive plan associated with the spin-off, per the FedEx 10-K segment disclosure; FDXF reported FY2026 adjusted operating income of $1.1B vs $616M segment GAAP, a 25.6% adjusted decline, not a wipeout), the transfer of 1,500+ employees from Federal Express, higher wage rates, a lapped prior-year facility-sale gain, and a $1,002M ΔNWC outflow (financials_history.csv) as carve-out working capital normalized at separation — OCF fell to $167M from $1,531M while revenue fell only 1.1%. The median ($7.14) is corroborated by two independent years (FY2024 $7.14, FY2025 $7.25) at a stable level, so the median is the base; the known forward distortion in the *other* direction (new standalone interest, absent from all three history years) is handled in the OE multipliers, and the working-capital question — how much of the $1,002M swing recurs — is exactly what the first standalone 10-Q will answer.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT: net debt is 4.2x normalized OE** — the balance sheet from the filing: total debt $4,527M (debt_current $43M + debt_lt $4,484M, per financials_history.csv) against essentially no separately-held cash in the carve-out presentation, plus lease liabilities of $1,838M, total assets $6,884M, and **negative equity of −$497M**. The debt is not operating leverage gone wrong — it was issued at separation to fund the ~$4.1B cash dividend to FedEx — but it is real, permanent standalone leverage: guided interest of ~$135M for the seven-month transition period, against a normalized OE base of ~$1,068M/yr. A freight downturn now hits a levered equity. This is priced into the super-bear/bear multipliers (0.65/0.80) and the widened bear probability.
- **TIER_BAND: bear-case tier D (6.4%) vs bull-case tier C (10.1%)** — the verdict spans tiers only at the low end; no scenario reaches B. Sized to the bear end: final tier D.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Method range: on the preliminary default run (defaults for probs/mults/offsets, growth start −3.4%) the weighted IRR was 7.83% with IV@15% $49.63 — Tier D. On this final run (guidance-checked growth, interest-haircut multipliers) it is 8.23% / $51.88 — Tier D. The defensible methods agree: this is a D at $113.58, roughly 2.2x the weighted IV@15%. The argument for the stock is not the DCF at this price; it is that a normalized mid-cycle OE well above the $7.14 median (FY2024-25 operating margins of 15.8–18.6% on a larger revenue base, plus standalone cost-out) eventually re-rates it — the super-bull case here still only reaches a 12.76% IRR.
- What would change the picture: (1) the first standalone 10-Q (expected with the Oct 29, 2026 earnings) — does OCF normalize after the separation working-capital swing, and what is the real standalone cost base? (2) Volume: shipments were −4% for FY2026 and −5.9% in Q4; revenue growth so far is fuel and weight, not freight. (3) The FedEx 19.9% stake overhang — monetization within 24 months is a known supply event. (4) Q4 FY2026 showed the trough shape: revenue $2.4B +4.8%, GAAP operating income $158M vs adjusted $363M — the gap between GAAP and adjusted is the separation-cost story, and it must actually close.
- Balance-sheet risk is the tail that matters: negative equity, $4,527M debt + $1,838M leases, and a cyclical revenue base. None of that is fatal for the largest LTL network in a normal cycle; all of it matters if the freight recession extends into 2027.
- Data notes (non-blocking enrichment): fundamentals.json carries 3 annual years and 0 quarters (no 10-Q yet), gross margin is null (no COGS tag filed), and the FY2024 balance sheet is largely untagged in the carve-out presentation (net debt null that year in fundamentals.json; the engine's history carries the FY2025 $73M → FY2026 $4,527M debt step-up). Enrichment: segments.json, statements.json, profile.json and street.json written; prices.py returned no price rows for FDXF (exit 2 — the price-history source has no series for the newly listed ticker), so reprice.py skipped by design and there is no live.json or prices.json.
