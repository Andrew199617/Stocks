# STLD — Steel Dynamics, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. Engine v4. Price $240.63 (Nasdaq quote API, final run), market cap ~$35.7B (engine estimate; $34.2B in the universe file) — in scope (<$40B).

## What it is
One of the largest US steel producers and metals recyclers (Fort Wayne, Indiana, founded 1993). Electric-arc-furnace (EAF) mini-mills melt scrap into flat-rolled and long-product steel; vertically integrated through OmniSource metals recycling (scrap supply), steel fabrication (joists/deck, order backlog +45–50% YoY in 2026, extending through Q1 2027), and a new aluminum flat-rolled platform (Aluminum Dynamics, Columbus, Mississippi) still in commissioning/ramp. End markets: non-residential construction, automotive, energy, manufacturing — currently boosted by infrastructure funding, reshoring, and data-center construction.

Latest earnings: Q2 2026 (reported 2026-07-20) — net sales $6.1B (+33% YoY), net income $534M / $3.69 diluted EPS (including a $16M non-cash impairment for relocating the planned second aluminum recycled slab center to Columbus), operating income $700M, adjusted EBITDA $921M, OCF $428M in the quarter, record steel shipments of 3.7M tons at 90% mill utilization vs ~81% for the industry. Steel operations operating income was $721M; aluminum operations lost $33M. On 2026-09-17 the company guided Q3 2026 EPS to $5.34–$5.38 (vs $3.69 in Q2 2026 and $2.74 in Q3 2025) on wider metal margins, record shipments, higher realized prices, and lower scrap costs, with metals recycling expected to weaken. Liquidity was $2.0B at 2026-06-30. Leadership succession announced: Theresa Wagler becomes CEO on 2027-01-01, co-founder Mark Millett moves to Executive Chairman.

Red flags: (1) earnings are a steel-spread derivative — net income went $3.86B (2022) → $2.45B (2023) → $1.54B (2024) → $1.19B (2025) with no moat stopping the slide, and the current surge is the same cycle in reverse; (2) the aluminum platform is unproven and loss-making (Q1 2026 operating loss ~$65M including a process/quality issue and inventory write-down, Q2 −$33M), with management targeting ~90% utilization only by end-2026; (3) the investment case at this price leans on US trade protection (Section 232 tariffs and resolved trade cases are explicitly credited by management for current spreads) — a policy input, not a company asset; (4) CEO transition at the cycle upswing.

## Moat score: 5/10 — moderate, a real cost advantage inside a commodity business
FOR — EAF mini-mill economics are a genuine structural cost advantage: variable, scrap-based cost structure with lower capex per ton than blast-furnace rivals, the ability to flex production with demand, and consistently higher through-cycle utilization (90% vs ~81% industry in Q2 2026). Vertical integration into OmniSource recycling secures scrap supply and captures the recycling spread; the geographic footprint (Midwest/South, plus Mexican recycling supporting Columbus and Sinton) sits next to customers and cuts freight. Product diversification into value-added flat-rolled, long products/rail, and fabrication dampens any single spread. Capital allocation has been disciplined and shareholder-heavy: three-year after-tax ROIC of 13% (per the Q2 2026 release) is best-in-class for US steel, and the share count has fallen every year for a decade.
AGAINST — Steel is a commodity: STLD is a price-taker on both the steel price and the scrap spread, and its largest rival, Nucor, runs the same EAF model at larger scale — the cost advantage is shared, not proprietary. There is no switching cost, no network effect, and no pricing power; when spreads compressed, net income fell 69% from 2022 to 2025. Part of the current spread environment is trade policy (tariffs/trade cases), which a government can withdraw. The aluminum expansion — the main growth leg — has no moat yet: it is loss-making, had a quality failure in Q1 2026, and must earn automotive qualifications against entrenched rollers.
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 5 = **8.5 years** (growth holds 2 years, then fades to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-yr projection).

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.4 months ago): resolved with the TTM override above — TTM OE $886.1M = $6.11/share, built line-by-line from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (FY25 − H1'25 + H1'26 for OCF, capex, and SBC) and used as the final base via `--base-oe-ps 6.11`.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (fired on the preliminary run at 1.2% on the $2.91 median base): the TTM base lifts the yield to 2.5%, so the flag no longer fires — but 2.5% is still a thin cash yield for a cyclical at $240.63; the valuation rests on the upcycle continuing, which is precisely what the scenario set refuses to assume.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.3× super-bear IV): real — the spread is the steel cycle itself (OE/share has ranged from −$0.58 to $18.92 in five years). Confidence is capped at medium, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note the spread does not create tier ambiguity here, because even the super-bull IV ($105.04) is 56% below the price.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 8.0× normalized OE on the preliminary run; 3.8× the TTM base on the final run): the multiple is inflated by the capex-depressed OE denominator — against earnings power, STLD is an investment-grade credit with $3.44B net debt, >$2B of liquidity, and a stated <2× through-cycle net-leverage target. Not a solvency concern. It still matters in the bear cases: conservative OE went negative in 2020 and 2024, and a levered equity claim on a flow that can vanish for a year deserves the wider bear weight it got (45% combined).
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count −7.0%/yr, +5pp credited in the growth blend): verified as real and continuing, not stale — diluted shares fell 207M (2021) → 148M (2025) → 145M (H1 2026), with $900M repurchased in 2025, $350M in H1 2026, and a further $261M in Q3 2026 through September 17. The credit is earned; it is also already inside the engine's g_base, so no additional adjustment was layered on.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue move): **price-cycle artifact, not a corporate event.** Revenue jumped +92% in 2021 ($9.59B → $18.41B) on the post-COVID steel-price spike (and fell −15.5% in 2023 as prices normalized) — no divestiture, large M&A, or restatement explains it; the Sinton mill was greenfield and aluminum is a new segment, not a basis change. Consequence: the 5-yr revenue CAGR (13.6%) and the 3-yr OE/share CAGR (−47.9%) are both unusable as growth inputs, which is why scenario starts were set by judgment offsets with the sanity check above rather than taken from the blend.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range >3× over 5 years): structural — spread cyclicality plus the 2023–25 growth-capex surge (conservative OE/share: $18.92 → $10.75 → −$0.58 → $2.91). Handled by the TTM base on normalized capex, wider bear/super-bear weight (45% combined), and a 0.75 super-bear OE multiplier.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** every defensible base lands in Tier D. The engine's median base ($2.91) gave a preliminary weighted IRR of 3.9% and IV@15% of $26.74. The TTM base used here ($6.11) gives 5.3% / $52.57. Because the engine's IV scales linearly with the base, even doubling the TTM base to ~$12.2/share — roughly what a full year at the guided H2 2026 earnings pace with ~$600M capex would imply — lifts weighted IV@15% only to ~$105, still 56% below the price. The honest headline is a robust D, not a borderline one.
- The market is pricing STLD on earnings and EBITDA at a guided record quarter (Q3 EPS $5.34–$5.38), plus an aluminum platform option. On owner's earnings — cash after *all* capex and SBC — the stock offers a 2.5% yield at the base and no scenario in the set, including super bull (15.1% growth start, 1.25× OE), produces an IV@15% above $105. There is no path in this model where buying at $240.63 clears 15%; it does not clear 10% in any single scenario (best IRR 8.4%).
- Quality is not the question: STLD is arguably the best-run US steelmaker — best-in-class ROIC, a real EAF cost edge, disciplined buybacks, investment-grade balance sheet. The question is price: 39× TTM owner's earnings for a commodity spread business whose earnings fell for three straight years before this upcycle, with the upcycle partly underwritten by trade policy.
- **Hurdle math:** the weighted path clears 15% at **~$53** (the weighted IV@15%); with a 20% margin of safety, ~$42. At $240.63 you are paying roughly 4.6× the hurdle-clearing price.
