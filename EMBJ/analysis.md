## What it is
Brazilian aerospace manufacturer (20-F filer, CIK 1355444): Commercial Aviation (E175, E190-E2, E195-E2 regional jets), Executive Aviation (Phenom, Praetor), Defense & Security (C-390 Millennium, A-29 Super Tucano) and Services & Support, plus the Eve eVTOL subsidiary. It deliberately does not compete head-on with Airbus/Boeing in large narrowbodies; it dominates smaller regional, light/midsize executive and tactical airlift niches. Q2 2026 (company earnings release, via web search): revenue $2.24B, +22.9% YoY, 65 deliveries, firm order backlog a record $34.5B, +16% YoY (Commercial $15.1B, Executive $7.8B, Defense $6.1B, +42% YoY, Services $5.5B); management raised 2026 adjusted EBIT margin guidance to 10.0-10.6% and adjusted FCF (ex-Eve) guidance to at least $400M. Q3 2026 deliveries were 66 aircraft.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: Narrow-to-moderate aerospace niche moat: Embraer is the only in-production scope-compliant 76-seat regional jet maker (E175) and holds strong executive-jet (Phenom/Praetor) and growing C-390 defense franchises with a record $34.5B backlog and a large installed base feeding Services & Support, but gross margin has stayed thin at 17.3-18.0% in FY2023-2025 (15.7-20.1% over FY2021-2025, fundamentals.json) and returns have not stayed above the cost of capital through a cycle — roic_proxy was negative in 2017, 2019, 2020 and 2022, near zero in 2018, and only strong in FY2023-2025 (17.6% / 23.9% / 25.8%). Scale disadvantage versus Airbus/Boeing, A220 overlap at the top of the E2 range, and the US scope clause blocking the E175-E2 cap the score at 5. CAP 8.5y.
- FOR: Only in-production scope-compliant 76-seat regional jet (E175) — dominant in US regional fleets with no direct in-production rival in that class; diversified portfolio across Commercial, Executive, Defense & Security and Services & Support smoothing any single cycle, with firm backlog a record $34.5B in Q2 2026 (+16% YoY); Executive Aviation franchise (Phenom 300 family, Praetor) with Q2 2026 revenue +32% YoY and adj. EBIT margin 23.4%; installed base of 9,000+ aircraft feeding recurring Services & Support revenue (Q2 2026 +24% YoY, adj. EBIT margin 18.7%); C-390 Millennium defense momentum (Defense backlog +42% YoY to $6.1B in Q2 2026) and NATO/export adoption.
- AGAINST: Thin, cyclical economics (gross margin 17.5% in FY2025; roic_proxy negative or near-zero in 2017-2022 before recovering in 2023-2025 — no through-cycle pricing power proven); scale disadvantage versus Airbus and Boeing; Airbus A220 overlaps the E195-E2; US scope clauses (76 seats / 86,000 lb MTOW) block the more efficient E175-E2 from the largest regional market, development paused toward 2027-2029; customer/program concentration, supply-chain/GTF engine exposure, Brazilian political/FX overhang and Eve eVTOL cash burn.
- Evidence (fundamentals.json): gross margin FY2021 15.7%, FY2022 20.1%, FY2023 17.3%, FY2024 18.0%, FY2025 17.5%. roic_proxy FY2021 16.6% (on a small positive operating income against a depressed capital base — net income was still negative), FY2022 -8.7%, FY2023 17.6%, FY2024 23.9%, FY2025 25.8%; earlier years include -24.6% in FY2020. A moat of 7+ would require that return profile to have held through a cycle; it has not.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:sbc (preliminary run only):** resolved by the filing-sourced sbc_override above; the final run's data_gap_uncovered is empty and the flag appears as OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc:** see OVERRIDE — gap, filing source, and measured impact ($3.6524 → $3.6230 base, 12.65% → 12.58% weighted IRR) are recorded in judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact and the registry.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED:** ratio 4.0 from the filer's own 20-F Description of Securities exhibit (see OVERRIDE); all per-share DCF figures are per ADS and comparable to the $72.34 ADS price.
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 10 months ago / 9.5 months in diagnostics): ttm.py cannot build a TTM base for this IFRS annual filer (see Owner's earnings) — the median-of-3-FY base is used per spec. Mitigation: the FY2025 base year is the strongest of the three (OE/share $0.9249 per ordinary share, the highest of FY2023-2025), Q2 2026 revenue grew +22.9% YoY with backlog at a record, and 2026 margin/FCF guidance was raised — there is no evidence in the post-FY prints that the FY2025 economics have deteriorated, so a stale-base haircut is not applied; the bear-weighted probabilities carry the staleness risk instead.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): real and structural — this is cyclical aerospace (the last-3-FY per-ordinary OE/share alone runs $0.5076 → $0.9249, and the COVID years were deeply negative). Handled by the normalized median base (not the latest year) and by shifting probabilities bear-ward to 15/30/30/17/8 (45% on bear-or-worse).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income vs OCF differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): OCF exceeds net income in all three years (FY2025: net income $359,000,000 vs OCF $870,000,000; FY2024: $354,600,000 vs $871,200,000; FY2023: $164,300,000 vs $617,000,000, financials_history.csv). The gap is the normal aerospace pattern, not a one-off: depreciation & amortisation of $259,500,000 / $243,600,000 / $241,700,000 in those years is a large non-cash add-back, and the remainder is working-capital / customer-advance timing inherent to aircraft deliveries. Because OCF is consistently *above* net income and the base is built from OCF directly, the divergence does not flatter the OE base — if anything the maintenance definition ($525,800,000 in FY2025) is lower. No tax-deposit or impairment one-off was found in the research pass.
- **DEBT_TAGS_BLANK** (no debt tag reports for the latest fiscal year — net debt UNKNOWN, not zero): the engine's Borrowings tags stop reporting after FY2020 in companyfacts, so dcf_numbers.json net_debt_latest is null and no leverage verdict is taken from the engine. From the company's own Q2 2026 earnings release (web search): Embraer standalone gross debt $2,151.9M and cash $1,937.4M at Q2 2026, a net debt position of $214.5M (net leverage 0.2x as reported in Q2 coverage); including Eve, consolidated net debt was $123.6M. Balance sheet from fundamentals.json FY2025: total assets $12,920,200,000, equity $3,812,000,000, cash and equivalents $1,949,800,000, lease liabilities $118,100,000 (dcf_numbers.json). Verdict in words: leverage is low in absolute terms — roughly one-third of one year's normalized OE total — and is not a fragility here; it is simply not engine-measured.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.4x super-bear IV): real — the outcome range for a cyclical OEM at a growth inflection is wide. Confidence is capped at medium (never high with this flag), and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **TIER_BAND** (bear tier C vs bull tier B): the verdict spans tiers; the final tier is the weighted C and any sizing should be to the bear end (bear IV@15% $42.37, well below the price).

## My thoughts / overlay
- Quality is real but priced: record $34.5B backlog, broad-based Q2 2026 growth, raised guidance, low leverage — and a $72.34 price that already discounts a bull-leaning path. The weighted IV@15% ($56.46) sits 21.9% below the price; even the base case alone ($57.68) does not clear the price at a 15% discount rate. Only the bull and super-bull scenarios (25% combined probability as weighted here) value the ADS above today's price.
- Method range: on the zero-fill SBC baseline the weighted IRR was 12.65% (still Tier C); on the latest-FY base instead of the median the result would be modestly higher but still in the C band — no defensible input change found in this pass moves the name to B, because the binding constraint is the $72.34 price against a ~$3.6/ADS OE base (5.01% OE yield), not the SBC or ADS mechanics.
- Risks: aero cycle and delivery timing (Q4-weighted delivery pattern), GTF engine / supply-chain constraints, E2 campaign pricing against the A220, scope-clause stalemate keeping the E175-E2 shelved, Eve cash burn, and BRL/political overhang. Upside risks: C-390 export acceleration, Executive Aviation mix (23.4% adj. EBIT margin in Q2 2026) and Services growth structurally lifting margins above the thin historical 17-18% gross margin.
