# CBRE — CBRE Group, Inc. (NYSE: CBRE)
Run date: 2026-10-13. Engine v4. Price $131.35 (Nasdaq quote API), market cap ~$39.5B — in scope (<$40B), near the top of it.

## What it is
The world's largest commercial real estate services and investment firm (Dallas HQ, 100+ countries, ~140,000 employees incl. affiliates). Four segments after the 2025 resegmentation: **Advisory** (leasing, property sales, capital markets, mortgage origination, valuation — the cyclical, transactional core), **Building Operations & Experience** (facilities management / enterprise accounts — long-term outsourcing contracts, the largest and most recurring block; includes the fast-growing critical-infrastructure / data-center services business), **Project Management** (Turner & Townsend majority-owned), and **Real Estate Investments** (CBRE Investment Management + Trammell Crow development). The strategic story of the last decade is mix shift: resilient outsourcing/PM/IM revenue now outweighs pure transaction fees, which dampens — but does not remove — cycle exposure. FY2025 gross revenue $39.90B (+13.4% YoY); note gross revenue includes large pass-through costs (especially in facilities management), so it overstates economic scale and growth vs fee revenue.

## MOAT — score 6/10 (moderate-strong, not wide). CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 6 = 9.8 years
**FOR:**
- Clear global #1 at scale: FY2025 revenue ~$39.9B gross, the leading broker share in tracked rankings (e.g. #1 in the $5–25M sales segment every year since 2019 per Green Street, 20.8% share in H1 2025), #1 or #2 in leasing, sales, outsourcing, and investment management simultaneously — no rival (JLL, Cushman & Wakefield, Colliers, Newmark) matches the breadth.
- Recurring-revenue ballast: multi-year facilities-management/outsourcing contracts embedded in client operations (Building Operations & Experience) plus project-management and IM fees — switching a global facilities provider is a genuine operational disruption, not a phone call.
- Data and talent density: the largest managed portfolio and transaction dataset in the industry feeds pricing/valuation credibility with institutional clients; scale funds the tech/AI spend ($1B+ cumulative) smaller rivals cannot match.
- Diversification across property types and geographies demonstrably softened the 2023 trough vs a pure brokerage (OE stayed positive; the resilient segments kept growing).

**AGAINST:**
- It is still a people business at the profit core: producers own the client relationships and can walk to JLL/Newmark/boutiques; there is no owned asset, patent, or network effect locking in the advisory fee pool.
- Cyclicality is un-moated: OE/share fell ~93% peak-to-trough into 2023 when rates froze transactions. A moat that cannot stop that is a scale advantage, not a wide moat.
- Sophisticated institutional clients negotiate fees hard; consolidated margins are thin (pass-through revenue) and pricing power is unproven through a full cycle — returns are good, not elite.
- Growth is increasingly bought (Pearce ~$1.2B, Industrious remainder ~$468M in 2025): M&A adds goodwill/integration risk and flatters reported growth vs organic OE compounding.
- AI/automation threatens standardized valuation, brokerage, and reporting tasks — CBRE invests heavily, but so do JLL and every proptech entrant; no evidence yet of a durable tech edge.

A 6 respects the #1 scale + recurring mix without pretending a brokerage/outsourcing franchise is a wide-moat compounder.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY2025 ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the full TTM build above — TTM OE $2.62/share computed line-by-line from 10-Q YTD OCF/capex/SBC. Not used as the base, reason documented: H1 bonus/WC timing amplified by acquisition-driven growth (H1'26 OCF −$687M vs −$489M while NI and revenue rose double digits and FY26 guidance was *raised*). The current-quarter evidence (Q2 core EPS +30%, guidance up) is reflected in the scenario starts instead.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 4.4× super-bear):** Real, not a modeling defect — it is the honest shape of a cyclical services franchise (2023 OE/share was $0.25; a boom year prints multiples of that). Consequence applied: confidence capped below high, bear weight widened to 40% combined, and the verdict is sized to the weighted/conservative end.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (net debt 3.0× normalized OE):** FY2025 net debt $3,257M (debt $71M current + $5,050M LT − cash $1,864M) against trough-sensitive OE — in a 2023-repeat, OE of ~$79M would not cover interest comfortably and the leverage is real. It has since risen: at Q2 2026 LT debt was $5,731M and cash $1,489M (net debt ≈ $4.3B+ before current maturities), because CBRE borrowed to fund Pearce and $940M of H1'26 buybacks while H1 OCF was seasonally negative. Mitigants: OE is measured after cash interest (no double-count in the DCF), maturities are termed out, and H2 collections seasonally rebuild cash. Folded into the verdict via the 40% combined bear weight and the super-bear OE multiplier — leverage is a reason the bear cases bite, not a separate tier override.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND (share count −2.8%/yr):** Confirmed and already credited in the growth blend (and partly *removed* again in my recalibration, since the blend double-counts it against acquisition-inflated revenue growth). Buybacks are real — $968M in FY2025, $940M in H1 2026 alone, diluted shares 340.5M (2019) → 300.8M (2025) → 293.9M (Q2 2026) — but at $131 they retire stock at 36.8× OE, which destroys rather than creates per-share value at this price; the tailwind is arithmetic, not a bargain signal.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs):** Confirmed — $5.80 (2021) → $0.25 (2023) → $4.07 (2024) → $3.57 (2025). Resolution: normalized median base (not a single year), bear weight widened to 40%, super-bear start set negative. This flag is the single best description of the stock: the franchise is durable, the earnings stream is not smooth.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (>40% gap in 2024):** Found and explained — it is working-capital timing, not an earnings-quality one-off in net income. 2023: NI $986M vs OCF $480M (ΔNWC was a ~$770M *use* — receivables/accrued-comp build into the trough). 2024: NI $968M vs OCF $1,708M (ΔNWC swung to a ~$562M *release* as the 2023 build unwound, on top of $674M D&A — NI + D&A = $1,642M ≈ OCF). No tax deposit, impairment, or gain is driving the gap; the divergence is the mirror image of the OE_VOLATILE flag and is exactly why the base is a 3-yr median of the OCF-based series rather than either single year.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (universe sector = "Finance"):** Classification artifact, addressed head-on: CBRE is filed under Finance/Real Estate in the universe, but it is an operating services company — no loan book, no insurance float, no deposit funding — and the OE/capex DCF fits it materially better than a bank or insurer (asset-light, real OCF, 10 clean EDGAR years). The genuine model-fit caveats are the Real Estate Investments segment's co-invest/development exposure and the pass-through revenue inflation, both handled above. Per the sweep rule the flag still caps confidence at low and the tier is treated as soft — noted here so the low grade is read as rule + spread + cyclicality, not as a claim the model is meaningless for this name.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** every defensible input set lands in Tier D. Preliminary run (median base, default growth/probs, moat 5): weighted IRR 8.0%, IV@15% $52.88. Final run (recalibrated growth, bear-skewed probs, moat 6): 6.4%, $37.94. TTM base ($2.62) would scale IV@15% to ~$28 and IRR to roughly 4.5–5% (deeper D). Even the final run's *super-bull alone* — 18% growth start, OE ×1.25 — yields only a 10.0% IRR at $131.35. There is no honest version of this model where today's price clears 15%; it does not clear 10% on the weighted path.
- Quality is not the debate: #1 global franchise, mix shifting the right way, data-center/infrastructure exposure is a genuine AI-adjacent tailwind (+68% critical-infrastructure growth in Q2), buybacks are large and consistent. The debate is purely price: at 36.8× normalized OE and a 2.7% OE yield, the buyer pre-pays for a decade of clean compounding from an earnings stream that printed $0.25/share three years ago, funded partly with rising debt.
- **Hurdle math:** the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$38** (weighted IV@15%); with a 20% margin of safety the buy price is ≈ **$30**. That is a ~71% fall from $131.35 — i.e. the market price embeds the bull case as the base case.
- Watch items that could change the picture: a rate-driven transaction boom large enough to lift OE/share sustainably above ~$6; capital return discipline if leverage keeps climbing (net debt up ~$1B in two quarters); whether Pearce/infrastructure margins hold once acquisition accounting washes out.
