## Moat: 6/10 — moderate, durable franchise, portable advisors
Justification: a decades-old, through-cycle wealth franchise with recurring fee-based revenue, strong advisor recruiting and ~19% returns on common equity (fiscal Q3 2026 annualized ROE 18.8%, adjusted ROTCE 23.5%, per the earnings release) — against the structural fact that advisors can leave and take clients with them, and heavy exposure to market levels and rates that no moat controls.
- FOR: Recurring fee-based revenue engine — record $1.15 trillion in Private Client Group fee-based assets at June 30, 2026 (release), which converts market appreciation into revenue without new sales; advisor platform + brand trust built since 1962 with consistent net new asset growth (domestic PCG net new assets $21.7B in fiscal Q3 2026 alone, release); diversification across wealth, capital markets, asset management and banking smooths any one line; disciplined capital return — share count CAGR −1.37%/yr (engine) with buybacks continuing ($400M repurchased in fiscal Q3 2026, release); strong regulatory capital (total capital ratio 22.5% at June 30, 2026, per release coverage).
- AGAINST: Advisors are the moat and they can walk — client relationships are portable and rivals (Morgan Stanley, Schwab, Fidelity, large RIAs) recruit aggressively; revenue is geared to equity-market levels via AUM-based fees, so a drawdown cuts the top line mechanically; bank/sweep earnings are rate-sensitive (cash-sorting already showed clients will move balances for yield); capital markets revenue is deal-cycle cyclical; no network effect or switching-cost lock-in comparable to an exchange or payments network.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 12.45 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through June 30, 2026, quoted in full above. Note the TTM base is *lower* than both the median and latest-FY bases because TTM operating cash flow is depressed by the same balance-sheet flows discussed under NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE; that conservatism is accepted and disclosed in the method range, not adjusted away.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over 5 years spans >3x): real and structural for this model — conservative OE was negative in FY2022 and FY2023 while net income grew both years. Handled by (a) widening bear-or-worse probability to 45%, (b) grading confidence low, and (c) reporting the method range rather than a single-point verdict.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023): identified — FY2023 net income was $1,739,000,000 while operating cash flow was −$3,514,000,000; the cash-flow statement's net change in operating assets/liabilities was roughly −$5.7 billion that year (client cash sweep balances were leaving for higher-yield alternatives — clients' domestic cash sweep balances fell ~31% YoY by February 2023 per company operating data — while the bank loan book absorbed cash, including the TriState Capital acquisition). It is a balance-sheet/funding swing, not an earnings one-off: FY2023 earnings, revenue ($12,992,000,000) and the dividend all grew. Consequence: the OCF-based conservative OE series understates this firm's earnings power in outflow years and overstates it in inflow years (FY2021: $6,441,000,000) — which is exactly why the sector flag below caps confidence.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance): accepted in full. Owner's earnings = OCF − capex − SBC is a poor lens for a broker-dealer/bank, whose "working capital" is its product. Confidence is graded **low**, the tier is treated as soft, and the maintenance/earnings-based picture is reported alongside (see Thoughts) instead of forcing the OCF verdict to stand alone.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.81x super-bear IV): real — the outcome range for a market-geared financial IS wide. Confidence is capped at medium by the engine and graded low here on the sector flag; the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
