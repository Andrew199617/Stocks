## What it is
Pure-play government managed care: Medicaid (the large majority of premium), Medicare dual-eligible products, and ACA Marketplace plans. ~4.9 million members at June 30, 2026, down 14% year over year on Medicaid market contraction, contract expirations and deliberate Marketplace shrinkage. SIC 6324 (Hospital & Medical Service Plans). It is exiting its traditional Medicare Advantage Part D product (~$1B premium) for 2027 and shrinking Marketplace by about $1B in 2027, while the new Florida Children's Medical Services Medicaid contract starts Q4 2026 (~$6B annual premium at full scale, with an expected start-up drag). Management calls 2026 the trough year for Medicaid margins and raised full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to at least $5.25 in Q2 2026 (premium revenue guidance ~$42B, down from FY2025).

## Moat: 4/10 — weak to moderate
Justification: pure-play government-programs expertise, incumbency and a low cost base, but no pricing power (states/CMS set rates), rebid risk on every contract, contracted (not owned) providers, and subscale versus the diversified payers.
- FOR: ~8% national Medicaid share and expertise in complex, high-acuity populations; multi-year state contracts with an RFP track record (Illinois retained, Wisconsin renewed, Florida CMS won — ~$6B annual premium at full scale); G&A ratio 6.5% in FY2025, a focused-footprint cost advantage; integrated dual-eligible offering aligned with policy direction.
- AGAINST: no pricing power — 2026 Medicaid MCR guided 92.9% because rates lagged 5% medical trend; contracts are rebid and losable (membership -14% YoY); subscale vs UnitedHealthcare/Elevance/Centene; providers contracted, not owned; effectively all revenue is government premium, exposed to redeterminations, ACA subsidy expiry and state budgets.
- Return evidence (fundamentals.json): gross margin (1 minus MCR) 15.9% FY2023 → 15.3% FY2024 → 13.1% FY2025; operating margin 4.6% → 4.2% → 1.7%. The roic_proxy series is not meaningful for this balance sheet — it divides by equity + debt − cash only, so regulated cash/investments held against claims liabilities drive it to absurd levels (10.94 in FY2020, 0.17 in FY2025) — the margin series is the usable evidence and it does not support a moat above 4.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Thoughts / method range
- This is an insurer-style cash-flow series run through an OCF-minus-capex model: single years are close to meaningless (OE/share has printed both $33.62 and -$12.91 within five years), so the honest headline is the range. Preliminary engine inputs (median base, 15.71% growth): 9.70% weighted IRR. Final recalibrated inputs: 6.10%. A TTM-base variant would be lower still. All variants are Tier D — the tier is robust, the exact IRR is not.
- The investment debate is really about the 2027-29 margin recovery (management's path leans on ~90bp of Medicaid MCR improvement and a $25 EPS target for 2029). Even granting the super-bull version of that recovery as the *expected* case, the IRR at $207.05 is only 9.34% — the price already pays for the recovery.
- Quality watch items: S&P downgraded the senior notes to BB- in April 2026; debt-to-capital 47% at Q2 2026; the Florida CMS start-up drag ($1.50/share guided burden in 2026, including MAPD underperformance) and the Marketplace risk-adjustment drag are guided, not hypothetical.
- Confidence is **low**: OE volatility, settlement-timing noise in every base candidate, and a verdict that depends on a state rate cycle the company does not control.
