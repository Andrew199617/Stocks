## What it is
Dallas-based regulated natural gas utility: the largest pure-play gas distribution company in the US footprint model — local distribution across eight states (Texas-heavy) plus Atmos Pipeline–Texas (APT), an intrastate transmission/storage system riding Permian/Waha flows. Essentially all earnings are regulated. The model is rate-base compounding: a ~$26B capital plan for fiscal 2026–2030 (over 85% safety/reliability), rate base guided from $18.8B in FY2025 to $40–44B by FY2030, and EPS growth guided at 6–8% through fiscal 2030 (search results). Fiscal year ends September 30.

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Exclusive regulated gas-distribution franchises with captive, essential-service customers and rider mechanisms that put new capex into rates quickly, plus the APT intrastate pipeline/storage franchise in Texas — a durable, visible rate-base compounding machine ($26B FY2026–30 plan, rate base guided $18.8B → $40–44B). Capped at 6 because regulators set the allowed return (no pricing power beyond rate cases and riders), Texas political/regulatory scrutiny of riders and customer bills is rising, safety litigation is a live exposure, and the plan is funded with continuous debt plus equity issuance that grows the share count ~5.2%/yr, diluting the rate-base growth per share.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated gas distribution franchises — captive, essential-service customers across eight states, Texas-weighted; rider/rate mechanisms recover safety and reliability capex quickly (annualized operating-income increases of $396M implemented fiscal YTD 2026, $227M of rate increases — Q3 2026 call highlights, search results); $26B FY2026–2030 capital plan with rate base guided from $18.8B to $40–44B by FY2030; APT intrastate pipeline and storage benefits from Permian/Waha through-system demand (APT spreads averaged $4.66 vs $1.77 prior year, fiscal YTD 2026); steady customer growth — nearly 51,000 added in the 12 months to June 30, 2026, ~39,000 in Texas.
- AGAINST: Regulators cap returns — allowed ROE and rider recovery are set politically; Jefferies cut its price target on October 1, 2026 (search results) citing Texas elections, scrutiny of regulatory riders and customer bills as the biggest risk to the shares; safety litigation and reputational exposure (a wrongful-death suit after the Dallas Oak Cliff gas explosion, per search results); growth is debt- plus equity-funded — a $700M 6% senior-note offering in October 2026 (8-K, search results) and share count +5.15%/yr, so per-share compounding lags rate-base growth; long-run gas-distribution transition risk if electrification policy turns against residential gas.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 2025-09-30, 12 months ago): ttm.py was run and exited non-zero with the message: "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for ATO (CIK 731802): sbc: no post-FY YTD fact (no 10-Q since the 2025-09-30 fiscal year end)". Per the sweep rules the engine's median base is therefore used, with the reason stated here. The quarterly evidence that does exist (search results above) shows fiscal 2026 capex running at ~$4.2B for the year — above FY2025's $3,561,399,000 — against guided EPS of $8.40–$8.50, so a TTM base would be negative as well; the verdict is F on every available base. Note also that FY2026 itself ended 2026-09-30 and its 10-K is not yet filed, so the filed history cannot yet include it.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 5.15%/yr): this is equity-issuance dilution, not SBC — SBC is trivial at $12,731,000 in FY2025 (financials_history.csv). The count rose from 145,166,000 (FY2023) to 152,666,000 (FY2024) to 160,573,000 (FY2025) as Atmos funds the capital plan with forward sale agreements / ATM issuance (~$937M of net proceeds available under existing forward sale agreements at June 30, 2026 — Q3 2026 call highlights, search results). The engine already deducted the maximum −5pp via the share-count growth adjustment; the verdict does not double-count it, but a holder should: rate base compounds at double digits while per-share figures compound at the guided 6–8%.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2025): two different mechanics, both visible in financials_history.csv. In FY2023, OCF of $3,459,743,000 ran far above net income of $885,862,000 — the recovery side of the February 2021 Winter Storm Uri gas-cost cycle: FY2021 OCF was −$1,084,251,000 when Atmos paid extraordinary purchased-gas costs, and the subsequent rate recovery / surcharge collections of those deferred gas costs inflated later OCF, which is also why FY2023 is the only positive-OE year in the last five. In FY2025, OCF of $2,049,456,000 vs net income of $1,198,754,000 is mostly the normal utility relationship — D&A of $734,745,000 is a large non-cash charge added back in OCF — plus working-capital timing. Neither divergence distorts the base in Atmos's favor: the conservative OE series is built from OCF directly, and it is capex ($3,561,399,000 in FY2025), not the NI/OCF gap, that drives OE negative.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Utilities): accepted in full — the OE/capex DCF fits regulated utilities poorly, because growth and safety capex is rate-base investment earning a regulated return, not value destruction. Confidence is graded **low** and the tier is read as "not modelable on this screen," not as "bad business."

That is a bond-like high-single-digit total-return profile before any multiple change — nowhere near Andrew's 15% hurdle on any defensible reading, so the F here agrees with the hurdle conclusion even though the model's mechanism (negative OE) is a sector artifact. No method range spanning tiers is claimed: on the v5 method there is one result, F.
- Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (dividend / rate-base), which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the v5 method cannot support.
- Real risks for a holder (outside the model): Texas regulatory/political pushback on riders and customer bills into the election cycle, safety-litigation cost and recovery (Dallas Oak Cliff), refinancing and funding a $26B plan at current rates while issuing equity into a +5.15%/yr share-count trend, and a premium multiple for the sector (P/E ~19–22x in search results) that leaves no room for a regulatory disappointment.
