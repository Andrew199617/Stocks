## What it is
One of the global "ABCD" agricultural merchandisers and the world's largest oilseed processor: grain origination, storage and merchandising, soybean and softseed crushing/refining, specialty oils, and renewable-diesel feedstocks, across food, feed and fuel. The Viterra acquisition closed in July 2025 (via search), roughly doubling the origination footprint and adding North American / Australian grain handling to Bunge's processing base; management completed the related $2 billion share-repurchase program in Q2 2026 (Business Wire Q2 2026 release, via search). A low-margin commodity toll-taker: earnings are crush/merchandising spreads and working-capital timing, not branded pricing power.

Latest earnings: Q2 2026 (Business Wire release, via search) — revenue $24.041B vs $12.769B a year earlier (the jump is Viterra consolidation, not organic growth), GAAP diluted EPS $3.47 vs $2.61, adjusted EPS $2.00 vs $1.31, driven by Soybean and Softseed Processing and Refining; full-year 2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $9.25–$9.75 from $9.00–$9.50. So the *earnings* trajectory is recovering strongly — while the *cash* owner's-earnings trajectory below is negative. That tension is the whole analysis.

Red flags: (1) conservative owner's earnings were negative in 3 of the last 5 fiscal years (engine: OE positive 2/5 years); (2) capex has stepped up hard — $399M (2021) to $1,723M (FY2025) in financials_history.csv — on the enlarged post-Viterra footprint; (3) net debt $12,916,000,000 (dcf_numbers.json); (4) diluted share count jumped with the Viterra issuance — FY2025 weighted diluted shares 166,466,940 vs 142,223,221 in FY2024, and 195,733,665 on the Q1 2026 YTD basis (ttm.py) — so every pre-deal per-share figure flatters today's per-share economics; (5) earnings are hostage to crush margins, trade flows and biofuel policy, all outside management's control.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow; scale without pricing power
Justification: the post-Viterra origination-plus-processing network is genuinely hard to replicate and the integration is delivering (guidance raised, synergy expectations raised in Q2 2026 reporting, via search), but Bunge still prices neither what it buys nor most of what it sells — crush and merchandising margins are market-set, through-cycle margins are ~1%-of-revenue thin, and equally scaled rivals (ADM, Cargill, COFCO) contest every flow.
- FOR: combined Bunge + Viterra origination, storage, port and crush/refining footprint across 50+ countries — the scale and geographic balance to reroute flows when trade patterns shift, which Q2 2026 demonstrated; largest oilseed-processing position globally, with specialty-oils and renewable-feedstock optionality; integration traction — FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance raised and the $2B Viterra-related buyback completed (Business Wire, via search); balance-sheet staying power through cycles as a century-old merchant.
- AGAINST: commodity price-taker on both sides — crush, merchandising and refining margins are set by markets and by biofuel/trade policy, not by Bunge; thin structural margins and weak through-cycle returns on capital, with net income falling every year in the filed history ($2,243,000,000 in 2023 → $1,137,000,000 in 2024 → $816,000,000 in 2025, financials_history.csv) even before the cash-flow problems; working-capital-heavy model absorbs cash exactly when the footprint and commodity prices grow — conservative OE negative in 3 of the last 5 years; Viterra integration and its stepped-up capex ($1,749,000,000 TTM) are unproven through a down-cycle, and the deal was paid for partly in shares (diluted count up to 195,733,665).
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.49 months ago): ttm.py was run and its full inputs are quoted above. The TTM base was *not* adopted — see BASE_DIVERGENCE; the median base is used, with the TTM figure disclosed as the bear-side evidence (a --ttm pass was run during this analysis and returns Tier F on the -$6.32 TTM base — that is the conservative end of the method range in Thoughts). The Q2 2026 earnings recovery is reflected in the bull-case growth starts and in rejecting any claim that the FY2025 cash trough is the earnings run rate.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share -$5.72 vs 3-yr median $3.23): the single-point bases are the distorted ones — both the latest FY and the TTM sit inside a working-capital build (FY2025 ΔNWC +$6,593,000,000 in financials_history.csv; TTM OCF only $588,000,000) layered on a step-change in capex and share count from the Viterra close, while reported earnings rise. The median is the least-distorted base, but it is flattering in one specific way: it was earned on 142–151M shares vs 195,733,665 now. Resolved by using the median, weighting bear-or-worse at 45%, and capping confidence at low.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024: net income $1,137,000,000 vs OCF $1,900,000,000): non-cash D&A of $468,000,000 (financials_history.csv) plus commodity mark-to-market and working-capital timing — the normal merchandiser gap between accrual earnings and cash, in the *favorable* direction this year; the same mechanism ran violently the other way in 2021–2022 (OCF -$2,894,000,000 and -$5,549,000,000 against positive net income). No tax-deposit or impairment one-off is needed to explain it. Consequence: neither NI nor any single year's OCF is a run rate for this company, which is precisely why the base is a 3-yr median and why confidence is low.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt $12,916,000,000 = 24.04x normalized OE): folded into the verdict three ways. Mitigant, stated plainly: much of a merchant's debt funds readily marketable inventories — liquid, commodity-backed assets — so 24x overstates structural leverage relative to a manufacturer, and the company extended its revolving facilities in September–October 2026 with maturities pushed to 2029/2031 (SEC filing reporting, via search). Against that: current debt alone is $5,220,000,000 against cash of $1,135,000,000 (financials_history.csv), and with TTM cash OE negative there is no current cash cushion — the debt is safe only as long as inventory values and bank lines hold. Net effect: bear-or-worse weighted at 45%, confidence low, and no credit in the verdict for the balance sheet.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** on the median base ($3.23) the weighted result is Tier D (5.29% IRR, IV@15% $24.15). On the TTM base (-$6.32) the engine returns Tier F — no positive OE to discount. On the latest-FY base (-$5.72) the same. The honest headline is **D-to-F, sized to the conservative end**: every defensible input set fails the 15% hurdle at $107.01 by a wide margin — even the super-bull scenario alone (10% growth start, 1.25x OE) yields only an 8.03% IRR and an IV@15% of $45.12, less than half the price.
- The market is pricing the earnings recovery (adjusted EPS guided to $9.25–$9.75 for 2026, stock up strongly through 2026 per search reporting) as if it were cash. On the filing-derived cash numbers it is not — yet: TTM OCF of $588,000,000 does not cover TTM capex of $1,749,000,000, before SBC. Bulls need the working-capital build to reverse and Viterra synergies to show up in OCF, not just in adjusted EPS; that is a 2027 proof, not a 2026 fact.
- The engine's revenue series is a tagging artifact and was not used for any growth claim: Q2 2026 reported revenue alone ($24.041B, Business Wire via search) exceeds the engine's FY2025 revenue figure ($16,944,000,000, financials_history.csv). The OE series is built from OCF/capex/SBC tags and shows no such break; net income in the filed history ($816,000,000 FY2025) is consistent with the reported results.
- Quality is not the question — Bunge will be originating and crushing crops in 15 years, SBC is trivial ($73,000,000 FY2025), and the asset base is real. The question is price versus cash: a 3.02% OE yield (diagnostics, on the normalized base; negative on TTM) for a levered, policy-exposed commodity spread business.
- **Hurdle math:** the weighted path clears 15% at **$24.15** (the weighted IV@15%) — roughly a quarter of the current price; with a margin of safety against the bear cases ($12.78–$18.92 IV@15%), nearer the high-teens. Nobody needs to fine-tune this: at $107.01 the gap is not a modeling disagreement, it is a different security being priced.
