## What it is
The Western world's flagship uranium producer: tier-1 Saskatchewan mines (Cigar Lake — stake raised to 57.4% in July 2026 after buying out TEPCO Resources' 5%; McArthur River/Key Lake), the Inkai JV in Kazakhstan, fuel services (conversion, fuel manufacturing), and a 49% equity stake in Westinghouse (AP1000 reactors and services; Westinghouse confidentially filed a draft S-1 in Q2 2026). Utilities buy from Cameco for security of supply as much as for pounds. 2026 uranium production guidance (Cameco's share) is 19.5–21.5M lb, maintained through Q2 despite flooding on the northern Saskatchewan supply route and a ~2-week Cigar Lake suspension after quarter-end from the McClean Lake mill disruption.

## Moat: 6/10 — strong assets, no pricing power
Justification: Scarce tier-1 high-grade, low-cost uranium assets (Cigar Lake, McArthur River/Key Lake) that cannot be replicated on any investable timeline, plus a long-term contract book and an integrated fuel-cycle position (fuel services, 49% of Westinghouse) — but Cameco is ultimately a uranium price-taker, its reserves deplete (Cigar Lake ~2036, McArthur River shortly after 2040), and production is concentrated in northern Saskatchewan with Kazakhstan JV exposure.
- FOR: World's largest high-grade uranium reserves; tier-1 low-cost assets (Cigar Lake, McArthur River/Key Lake) with decade-plus permitting barriers to any replacement supply; long-term contract book — five-year average contracted deliveries exceeding 28M lb/yr with floors in the high $70s/lb and escalated ceilings around $160/lb; integrated nuclear fuel cycle — uranium, conversion/fuel services, and 49% of Westinghouse (AP1000 pipeline of 91 reactor opportunities); Western supply-security premium as utilities bifurcate away from Russian/Kazakh supply; net-cash balance sheet funds opportunistic stake increases (Cigar Lake to 57.4% in July 2026).
- AGAINST: Commodity price-taker — earnings are driven by the uranium price, which Cameco does not control; finite reserves (Cigar Lake ~2036 with extension work ongoing, McArthur River concludes shortly after 2040); geographic/operational concentration (2026 flooding and the McClean Lake mill disruption both hit operations; Inkai JV is in Kazakhstan); Kazatomprom and new supply can undercut on cost, and reactor-policy shifts sit outside Cameco's control; Westinghouse earnings are lumpy and equity-accounted (the ~US$170M Dukovany milestone flattered 2025 and did not recur in 2026).
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.4 months ago): ttm.py was run and exited non-zero — it reads us-gaap facts only, and for this IFRS filer it reported no annual (FY) facts for OCF/SBC/shares — expected per the sweep spec; the engine's median base is used and the reason is stated here. The quarterly evidence was still checked: Q2 2026 (net earnings C$25M, adjusted EBITDA C$391M, production guidance maintained at 19.5–21.5M lb) shows H1 2026 running *below* the H1 2025 comparison on the Westinghouse milestone non-recurrence, so there is no sign a TTM base would be materially higher than the FY2025 run-rate.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real and structural — this is a uranium price cycle trough-to-recovery path (conservative OE totals from financials_history.csv: $116.6M in 2022 → $778.4M in 2025). Handled by the 3-yr median base and by keeping the default 35% combined weight on bear/super-bear rather than skewing bullish.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): persistent and structural, not a one-off tax deposit. From financials_history.csv: net income $272,300,618.88 / $119,431,908.42 / $430,577,895.12 vs OCF $519,322,476.48 / $629,184,212.88 / $1,028,662,395.52 across 2023–25. Net income carries non-cash equity-accounted Westinghouse earnings (lumpy — the ~US$170M Dukovany milestone sat in 2025) and other non-cash items, while OCF captures cash generation and working-capital swings (2023's delta_nwc was −$752,380,220.80, a release). Because the base series is the OCF-based conservative OE, the divergence does not inflate the base; note the NI-based maintenance OE is the *lower* of the two definitions in both 2024 ($315,763,627.02) and 2025 ($392,631,595.99).
- **FX_CONVERTED**: Cameco reports in CAD; the engine converted all flow and balance-sheet fields to USD at period-end ECB reference rates recorded in dcf_numbers.json (FY2025 rate 0.73036; FY2024 0.69501). Flows use the period-end rate, not a period average — a documented simplification; year-to-year OE comparisons carry a small FX component on top of the operating trend.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED (x1.0)**: Cameco is a direct-listed 40-F filer — the 40-F cover registers "Common Shares, no par value" (symbol CCJ) directly on the NYSE, so the traded security is the ordinary share itself, not an ADS. judgment.json declares ads_ratio 1.0 with that source; per-share figures and price are on the same basis and nothing is scaled.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $1.79 vs 3-yr median $1.10): the median is the cyclically dragged one — it still contains the 2023 ramp year before McArthur River/Key Lake hit stride and before repriced contracts flowed through, while FY2025 reflects the fuller production and price deck. The median is nonetheless retained: it is the only base the method allows for an IFRS filer (no TTM pin exists), H1 2026 softened rather than extended the FY2025 run-rate, and the choice is not verdict-relevant — the weighted IV would have to more than quadruple to reach the $81.40 price, a gap far wider than the base divergence.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (1.35%): the central fact of this valuation. At $81.40 the buyer is paying for a decade of contract repricing and uranium-price appreciation, not for cash the business currently returns. The tier is therefore fragile in the *downward* sense only — there is no plausible near-term OE print that makes the current price cheap on this model.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.79x): real — uranium price paths genuinely span that range. Confidence is held at medium (never high under this flag), and the verdict leans on the fact that even the widest end of the spread (super bull, 9.03% IRR) fails the 10% tier boundary, let alone the 15% hurdle.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a quality-vs-price result, not a quality result. Cameco is arguably the best-positioned uranium franchise in the West — tier-1 assets, a contract book with floors in the high $70s and ceilings around $160/lb, a fuel-cycle position, a Westinghouse option the market may soon price separately via the IPO filing, and a net-cash balance sheet. None of that is in dispute. What the DCF says is narrower: at $81.40, with normalized OE/share of $1.0950 (a 1.35% OE yield), even flawless execution of the repricing story compounds at ~6% weighted, and the single most bullish scenario the method allows still returns only 9.03%.
- The market is pricing CCJ as a scarce-asset option on the uranium price (it traded as high as $135.24 in the last year per market data), not as a claim on current owner's earnings. An owner's-earnings screen will keep failing it until either the price falls a long way or realized contract prices converge to spot long enough to lift the normalized base several-fold.
- Method range: the defensible-input spread here is unusually *un*important. Median base vs latest-FY base, moat 5 vs 7 — every combination run or implied by this analysis lands in Tier D, because no scenario in the table clears 10%. The honest headline is the distance: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at or below the weighted IV@15% of **$18.72**, and a buyer wanting a margin of safety against the bear cases ($12.53 IV@15%) would need a price in the low teens. That is not a forecast that CCJ trades there — it is what this screen requires.
- Watch items that could change the base, not the verdict mechanics: realized uranium price progression in H2 2026 prints, the Westinghouse IPO pricing (a market mark on 49% of Westinghouse is not in the OE base at all — it is an asset the DCF only sees through equity earnings), Cigar Lake life-extension results, and any production move above the 19.5–21.5M lb band.
