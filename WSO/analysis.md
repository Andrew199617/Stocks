## What it is
The largest distributor of air-conditioning, heating and refrigeration (HVAC/R) equipment and related parts in North America: 685+ locations serving 125,000+ contractors and dealers, an estimated high-teens share of a fragmented ~$74B market with ~1,400–2,200 independent distributors (company/industry summaries via search). Demand is mostly residential replacement, which is steadier than new construction. Built by buy-and-build — 70+ acquisitions since entering distribution in 1989, still running (three Sunbelt deals in 2025; Jackson Supply closed 2026-06-01, adding ~$230M of annual sales per the Q2 2026 call summary). E-commerce is +13% in H1 2026 at 37% of trailing sales, and OnCallAir generated $1.9B of GMV (Q2 2026 call summary via search).

## Moat: 7/10 — strong, not wide
Justification: largest North American HVAC/R distributor (~685+ locations, 125,000+ contractors, estimated high-teens share of a fragmented $74B market) with scale purchasing power, local availability and a scaling digital ecosystem (e-commerce 37% of sales); ROIC proxy stayed 20–34% over the last five fiscal years and never below 17% in ten, and gross margin expanded from 24.2% (2020) to a record 28.0% (2025) — but it sells other makers' products into a still-fragmented field of ~1,400–2,200 distributors, so the moat is strong, not wide.
- Evidence first (fundamentals.json): **ROIC proxy** (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) — 20.1% (2016), 21.1% (2017), 21.3% (2018), 17.2% (2019), 20.8% (2020), 26.1% (2021), 34.0% (2022), 26.3% (2023), 24.0% (2024), 20.3% (2025). Above any plausible cost of capital in every one of ten years, through COVID, the post-COVID surge and the A2L downturn — that is what a 7 sits on. **Gross margin**: 24.2% (2020) → 26.6% → 27.9% → 27.4% → 26.8% → a record 28.0% (2025), with TTM at 27.5% as the 2025 OEM/A2L pricing tailwind normalizes (Q2 2026: 27.5% vs 29.3%).
- FOR: scale — largest HVAC/R distributor in North America, estimated 18–20% share vs ~$3B-revenue next rivals, 685+ locations and 125,000+ contractors; ROIC proxy 20.3–34.0% over FY2021–25 and 17–21% in FY2016–20 — above cost of capital through the whole cycle; pricing power proven — gross margin 24.2% (2020) to record 28.0% (2025) via pricing-optimization technology, expanded even as 2025 volumes fell; digital lock-in — e-commerce +13% in H1 2026 at 37% of trailing sales, OnCallAir $1.9B GMV, tools small distributors cannot match; replacement-driven demand plus a debt-light balance sheet funding 70+ buy-and-build acquisitions since 1989.
- AGAINST: no proprietary product — distributes OEM equipment (Carrier and others); OEM concentration and direct/OEM-channel shifts are a standing hostage risk; the market remains fragmented (~1,400–2,200 distributors), so local availability moats erode branch by branch and price competition is constant in commodity segments; gross margin is only 27–28% and the 2025 record leaned on OEM/A2L pricing actions that reversed in Q2 2026 (27.5% vs 29.3%); housing and consumer-durables cyclicality — FY2025 revenue fell 5% on A2L-transition volume softness and slower homebuilding; share count creeps up ~1.7%/yr with no buyback offset and the dividend absorbs essentially all earnings, leaving little reinvestment cushion.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *range* across defensible bases, and unusually it does not change the verdict: the same moat, probabilities and scenario starts on the normalized median base ($13.59/share) give a weighted IRR of 6.89% and IV@15% of $104.30 (engine run, this pass) vs 8.13% / $133.56 on the TTM base — Tier D either way. The preliminary default-input run printed 6.70% / $101.06, also D. Anyone quoting one number for WSO without its base is hiding the ball, but every ball lands in D at $295.91.
- Quality is not the debate: a decade of 17–34% ROIC proxy, record gross margin earned in a down-volume year, net debt 0.07x normalized OE, OE positive 5/5 years, and a dividend raised to $13.20. This is one of the cleanest distribution franchises in the sweep.
- The debate is purely price: at $295.91 the market is paying ~17x the (working-capital-flattered) TTM OE and ~22x the median OE for a business whose revenue fell 5% last year and whose earnings fell double-digits in the latest quarter. The weighted IV@15% is 55% below the price; the stock would need to fall to ~$134 to clear Andrew's 15% hurdle on the weighted path.
- Watch items: whether the Q2 margin normalization (27.5% vs 29.3%) stabilizes at the ~27% baseline management cites or keeps sliding; whether July's mid-single-digit organic growth holds through the seasonally big Q3; Jackson Supply integration; and the payout — a $13.20 dividend on $12.25 of FY2025 EPS leaves no slack if OE stays at the median level.
