# KVUE — Kenvue Inc.
Sweep run, 2026-10-13. In scope (market cap ~$34.2B < $40B). Queue position 49.

## What it is
The J&J consumer-health spin-off (IPO May 2023, full separation via the J&J exchange offer in August 2023), headquartered in Skillman, NJ. Three segments: Self Care (Tylenol, Motrin, Zyrtec, Benadryl, Nicorette), Skin Health & Beauty (Neutrogena, Aveeno, OGX, Rogaine), and Essential Health (Listerine, Band-Aid, Johnson's). ~$15.1–15.4B of revenue that has gone nowhere in five years ($15.05B in the FY2021 carve-out year, $15.12B in FY2025) — this is a bond-like brand portfolio, not a growth business. Dividend ≈$0.84/yr (≈4.7% yield at $17.76), which consumes roughly 90% of TTM owner's earnings per share.

**Deal overlay (dominates the price):** Kimberly-Clark is acquiring Kenvue for **$3.50 cash + 0.14625 KMB shares per KVUE share**. At KMB's $98.29 close on 2026-10-13 (Nasdaq quote API) that consideration is worth **≈$17.87** — the $17.76 market price is a merger-arb price sitting ~0.6% under the deal value, not a standalone-fundamentals price. Both shareholder votes have passed, the U.S. antitrust waiting period has expired, and closing is expected in Q4 2026 subject to remaining foreign approvals. Kenvue is giving no standalone guidance while the deal is pending. The DCF below is therefore the *break-value* analysis: what KVUE is worth standalone if the deal fails, and what a fundamental buyer is actually paying for if it closes (a claim on the combined company, whose claimed ~$2.1B synergy target belongs to the combined entity, not to standalone KVUE cash flows).

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong brands, no growth engine
FOR — Category-leading, pharmacist/retailer-entrenched brands (Tylenol in analgesics, Listerine in mouthwash, Band-Aid, Neutrogena, Aveeno, Zyrtec) with habitual repeat purchase; ~60% adjusted gross margin is direct evidence of pricing power (Q2 2026 organic growth was price-led, +0.9% price / +0.7% volume, in a soft category); global shelf and pharmacy distribution scale a challenger cannot replicate quickly; portfolio diversification across pain, allergy, skin, oral and wound care smooths single-category shocks.
AGAINST — Five years of ~0% revenue growth despite those brands: pricing power has defended margin, not produced growth. No patent or regulatory exclusivity — the moat is advertising-and-habit, re-bought every year against private-label/store brands and retailer bargaining power. Skin Health & Beauty lost ground post-spin (only now recovering: H1 2026 organic +4.4%). The flagship brand carries a live litigation/regulatory overhang (below). Input-cost and tariff pressure took 70bp off adjusted gross margin in Q2 2026.
CAP from moat 6 = 2 + 1.3×6 = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-year projection).

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY2025 ended 2025-12-28, ~9.5 months ago) → resolved with the TTM override.** From EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures: OCF TTM = FY2025 $2,197M − H1 2025 $1,049M + H1 2026 $1,177M = **$2,325M**; capex TTM = $475M − $267M + $203M = **$411M**; SBC TTM = $136M − $81M + $61M = **$116M**. TTM conservative OE = 2,325 − 411 − 116 = **$1,798M**, ÷ 1,922M diluted shares (H1 2026 YTD weighted) = **$0.9355/share**, used via `--base-oe-ps`. Q2 2026 detail corroborates: sales $3,955M (+3.0%), adjusted EPS $0.31 (+7%), H1 operating cash flow $1,177M (+12% YoY).
- **DILUTION (share count +3.9%/yr) → artifact, neutralized via the growth recalibration above.** The 1,716M share counts in the FY2021/FY2022 rows are pre-IPO J&J carve-out counts; the count stepped up through the May 2023 IPO and August 2023 exchange offer to ~1,923M and has been flat since (FY2024 1,923M, FY2025 1,924M, H1 2026 1,922M; shares outstanding 1,921M at 2026-07-31). There is no ongoing 3.9%/yr dilution — SBC actually fell to $136M in FY2025 and $116M TTM from $254M in FY2024. Treating the spin step-up as perpetual dilution would double-punish the growth rate.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2023, 2024) → identified one-offs; OE base is trustworthy.** 2024: net income ($1,030M) was crushed by **$578M of asset-impairment charges (including a $479M intangible impairment)** plus $185M of restructuring charges, while OCF held at $1,769M — the gap is non-cash charges in NI, not phantom earnings; Q2 2024 net income of just $58M marks the impairment quarter. 2023: OCF ($3,168M) ran $1,504M ahead of NI ($1,664M) on D&A ($627M), SBC ($188M) and a large working-capital release in the separation year (engine ΔNWC −$846M) — i.e. FY2023 OE/share of $1.36 is flattered and should not anchor the base. Both distortions are why the TTM base supersedes both years.
- **Engine balance-sheet miss (no flag fired — found manually):** the engine reports net debt of $391M because the `LongTermDebt` tag Kenvue used through 2023 is discontinued; total debt is actually $8.48B at 2026-06-28 under the current debt tags, for **net debt ≈ $7.37B ≈ 4.1× TTM OE** (not 0.22× as the engine diagnostics imply). HIGH_NET_DEBT should have fired. The leverage is folded into the verdict explicitly: at 4.1× OE with ~90% of OE paid out as dividends, deleveraging is slow and equity IRR is capped — consistent with the D-tier math rather than a reason to soften it.
- No other flags fired: no BASE_DIVERGENCE override problem (median $0.824 vs TTM $0.9355 — the TTM is higher on H1 2026 cash recovery, and both produce Tier D), no SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (consumer health fits the OE model), TV share low, spread 2.49× is real but not extreme.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is two numbers, not one: standalone IV@15% ≈ **$7.36** vs a deal consideration worth ≈ **$17.87** at KMB $98.29. The $10.50 gap is the market paying for Kimberly-Clark's balance sheet and synergy claims, not for Kenvue's standalone owner's earnings. Anyone buying KVUE at $17.76 is underwriting deal completion (shareholder votes done, U.S. antitrust cleared, Q4 2026 close guided, foreign approvals remaining) and then owning the combined company — a different security from the one this DCF values.
- Method range: the preliminary run (normalized $0.82 base, unadjusted −4.9% growth) gave weighted IRR 6.1% / IV@15% $5.39; the final filing-derived TTM run gives 7.7% / $7.36. Every defensible variant is Tier D, and even the super-bull scenario alone is 10.9% — the verdict is robust, not assumption-dominated. The method spread here changes the size of the shortfall, never its sign.
- Litigation is the live break-risk and downside-risk in one: the Tylenol prenatal-exposure MDL was dismissed in 2024, but the Second Circuit reinstated ~550 suits in July 2026 (restoring 3 of 5 plaintiffs' experts), and Kenvue/retailer defendants petitioned for rehearing in September 2026. Regulators and medical bodies still say a causal link is not established, but a revived MDL plus 2025-era political pressure on acetaminophen is exactly the kind of overhang that can complicate a closing or, standalone, pressure the flagship franchise (Self Care is the largest segment and its H1 2026 organic sales already fell 0.9% on weak illness incidence).
- Quality is not the debate: ~60% gross margins, strong cash conversion, a $250M transformation program targeting ~$200M of annualized savings, and Skin Health inflecting up. The debate is price vs earning power: 19.0× TTM owner's earnings for ~0–2% growth and 4.1× net leverage does not clear a 15% hurdle standalone, full stop.

## Final verdict
