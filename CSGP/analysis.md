# CSGP — CoStar Group, Inc.
Engine 5.4 run, 2026-10-18. In scope (market cap $13.593B at $32.31, nasdaq_quote_api — under the ~$40B cutoff). All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json, fundamentals.json or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed.

## What it is
The dominant commercial real estate information and marketplace company: the CoStar suite (proprietary property database, 327,000 subscribers), LoopNet, Apartments.com, Ten-X, STR, plus a residential push built around Homes.com and a string of acquisitions — Matterport (closed Feb 2025), Domain in Australia (2025), OnTheMarket (UK) and Zonda (~$800M). Subscription model, gross margin 79–82% every year 2016–2025. It also now owns its Arlington HQ tower (Central Place, bought Feb 2024 for a reported $339M) and collects rent from tenants in it — a small lessor side-business inside a data company.

## Moat: 6/10 — strong core franchise, returns currently spent away
Justification: the CoStar suite is the CRE industry data standard (93% renewal rate; 89% overall renewal, 94% for customers of 5+ years — Q2 2026), with a proprietary property database accumulated over decades and gross margin 79–82% held for ten straight years. But the moat score is on the consolidated company, and its ROIC proxy (fundamentals.json) fell from 12.5% (2019) / 12.0% (2022) to 6.7% (2023), 0.1% (2024) and −0.7% (2025): the core's economics are being consumed by the residential portal war and acquisition prices, so the consolidated franchise does not currently earn like a 7+.
- FOR: CoStar suite is the CRE data standard — 93% renewal, 327,000 subscribers +19% YoY (Q2 2026); proprietary database + workflow embedding = real switching costs (94% renewal at 5yr+ customers); gross margin 79–82% sustained 2016–2025 is pricing-power evidence; network effects in LoopNet/Apartments.com; 61 consecutive quarters of double-digit revenue growth through Q2 2026.
- AGAINST: ROIC proxy collapsed to ~0% (2024–25) — capital allocation is destroying the core's returns; in residential, the network effect belongs to Zillow and Homes.com is the challenger spending to buy share (Third Point and D.E. Shaw both campaigned against exactly this in 2025; Third Point exited entirely in Apr 2026 calling it a "reckless drain"); acquisition spree at full prices (Matterport, Domain, Zonda ~$800M, OnTheMarket) lifted goodwill to 47% of assets; SBC $194M in 2025 and owned-real-estate capex are recurring-looking claims on OE; CRE transaction cyclicality (Ten-X revenue down 5% YoY in Q2 2026) and founder-CEO concentration.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, legs quoted above.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024, 2025): net income ($139M in 2024, $7M in 2025) sits far below OCF ($393M, $430M) because the P&L carries large non-cash charges that OCF adds back — SBC of $89M/$194M and D&A of $147M/$263M (the D&A jump is acquired-intangible amortization from Matterport/Domain) — while the Homes.com operating spend depresses both. No tax-deposit or working-capital one-off was found; the divergence is the SBC/D&A wedge, and since the base is OCF-based conservative OE (which deducts both capex and SBC in full), the divergence does not flatter the base — if anything the maintenance definition (−$16M FY2025) tells the same negative story.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH**: a classification artifact — the Nasdaq screener files CSGP under sector "Finance" / industry "Real Estate" (SIC 7389, business services), which trips the finance-misfit flag. CoStar is a subscription information/marketplace business, not a lender or insurer, so the OE/capex model fits its economics; the owned HQ tower's lessor income is immaterial to the model. Per spec the flag still caps confidence at low.
- **IRR_AT_FLOOR** (super bear): the super-bear IRR of 2.95% sits within 0.5pp of the 2.5% terminal growth rate — read it as "at most ~3%", i.e. on bearish paths the price is beyond what this model can express. It changes nothing at the weighted level (weighted IRR 3.85% is not at the floor) and every scenario is Tier D regardless.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (0.67%): real and central — at $32.31 essentially none of the price is supported by current owner's earnings; the entire valuation rests on OE *recovery* (margin normalization + capex/SBC roll-off), which this model deliberately does not capitalize beyond the scenario multipliers (max 1.25x). Stated plainly in the verdict.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.82x): real — super-bull IV@15 $5.83 vs super-bear $1.21. Confidence is low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note even the super-bull end is 82% below the price, so the spread does not straddle the investment question.
- **BIG_NET_CASH** (net cash 7.1x normalized OE): the multiple is an artifact of normalized OE being negative/tiny. In dollars: net cash $640.0M against a $13.593B market cap — a real but small cushion (~5% of the cap), and it is what remains after the 2024 cash pile ($4,681M) was spent on acquisitions. It does not rescue the valuation.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range is unusually honest here: Tier F on the 3-yr median base, Tier D on the TTM base — both say the same thing in different volumes. There is no defensible OE base on current filings that gets CSGP anywhere near clearing 15% at $32.31; even the super-bull scenario (24.5% growth start, 1.25x OE) yields IV@15 $5.83 and a 5.32% IRR.
- What a buyer at $32.31 is actually underwriting is a *normalization* story: that Homes.com spending converts to Apartments.com-style economics (Q2 2026 was the first quarter residential Adjusted EBITDA went positive), that capex stays at the TTM $217M run-rate or lower now the tower is bought, that SBC falls back toward ~3% of revenue, and that the core suite's historical ~$0.70–0.93 OE/share (2021–2023, on far less revenue) is a floor rather than a peak. If OE/share merely returned to the 2022 level and grew from there, this DCF would look completely different — but that is a forecast of management behavior, not a reading of filed OE, and the sweep scores the filings. Pass 2 territory: segment-level OE (commercial vs residential) is exactly the masked-growth question this name raises.
- The stock has already de-rated hard (from ~$66 in Jan 2026 per the Third Point exit reporting, and a ~$33B market cap at the Feb 2024 HQ purchase, to $13.59B now) — the market has taken out a lot of the Homes.com skepticism. It has not taken out enough to make current OE support the price.
- Governance overhang is real but two-sided: the 2025 Third Point / D.E. Shaw settlements added directors, Third Point's Apr 2026 exit removes the loudest seller of the "shut residential" thesis, and management's 2026 expense cuts (~$100M vs plan) plus raised FY2026 Adjusted EBITDA guidance ($780–820M) show the pressure is producing discipline.

## Final verdict
