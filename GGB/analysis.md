# GGB — Gerdau S.A.
Pass-1 run, 2026-10-19 (engine 5.5.0). Brazilian long- and special-steel producer, NYSE ADS (each ADS = one preferred share, ads_ratio 1.0 — see OVERRIDE). Market cap $10.225B at $5.08 (key_numbers.md) — in scope under $40B.

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed.

## What it is
The largest long-steel producer in the Americas: scrap-based mini-mills and integrated plants in Brazil, North America and South America, plus special steels and captive iron ore. 11.6 Mt shipped in 2025 (+5.9%, company 4Q25 release). North America produced 62% of FY2025 consolidated adjusted EBITDA; Brazil is squeezed by record import penetration. A commodity cyclical, priced in BRL at home and as a 1:1 preferred-share ADS in New York.

## OVERRIDES — shares (filing) and ads_ratio
- **The gap:** the preliminary engine run exited 4 with `DATA_GAP:shares`. EDGAR companyfacts carries no diluted share count for FY2023, FY2024 or FY2025; the only filed counts are dei outstanding counts for the COMMON class alone (717,782,619 at December 31, 2025), while the 20-F cover states 717,782,619 common shares **and** 1,283,097,330 preferred shares issued. The zero-fill baseline on that common-only count printed a base OE/share of $1.19 and a weighted IRR of 21.09% (key_numbers.md measured impact) — a fake near-A tier built on a third of the capital.
- **The decision:** judgment.json carries `shares_override = {value: 2012732574, kind: "filing"}` — the FY2025 20-F EPS note's total diluted denominator: common shares outstanding 718,139,434 + preferred diluted total 1,294,593,140 (weighted-average preferred 1,285,879,767 + 8,713,373 potential shares from long-term incentive plans). This is the matching denominator for consolidated owner's earnings because the EPS note allocates earnings identically per share to both classes (diluted EPS R$0.69 / R$2.18 / R$3.56 for common **and** preferred, FY2025/24/23) and the traded ADS is itself a preferred share. It is not a share_class_mismatch: the EPS note presents EPS for the traded class itself, on the same per-share economics as the common.
- **Known approximation, disclosed:** the engine applies the single value to every period with no filed diluted count (key_numbers.md lists periods filled 2016-12-31 through 2025-12-31). FY2025 is exact. FY2024 OE/share is computed on 2,012,732,574 instead of its own filed total of 2,099,379,471 (overstates FY2024 OE/share by ~4.3%) and FY2023 on 2,012,732,574 instead of 2,109,593,750 (~4.8% overstatement) — the direction makes the median base slightly generous, not conservative. The pre-2023 years are outside the median window; their per-share history is approximate on any basis (see SHARES_FALLBACK).
- **Measured impact (key_numbers.md / score.json override_impact):** base OE/share $1.19 (no override) → $0.42 (with override), −64.23%; weighted IRR 21.09% → 10.21% (−10.89pp). Because the tier exists only because of this override, both alert gates are closed (score.json exit_alert_suppressed_reason: "tier exists only because of a judgment override").
- **ads_ratio:** judgment.json declares `{ratio: 1.0}` from the same 20-F: the NYSE Level II ADSs are equivalent to one preferred share (GGBR4). No scaling applies; the declaration exists so the ADS price and the per-share figures are confirmed on the same unit.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: scale without pricing power. Gross margin (fundamentals.json) compressed from 26.6% (FY2021) to 22.8% / 16.4% / 13.7% / 11.4% across FY2022–25, and roic_proxy fell from 41.7% (FY2021) to 29.3% / 18.2% / 12.5% / 4.4% over the same years after printing −6.7% in FY2016 — returns clear the cost of capital only at cycle peaks.
- FOR: largest long-steel producer in the Americas (11.6 Mt shipped 2025, +5.9%) with a scrap-based mini-mill cost position; North America is 62% of FY2025 adjusted EBITDA with ~90 days of backlog and trade-measure shelter; captive iron ore (Miguel Burnier, ~R$1.1B potential annual EBITDA at full ramp per the 4Q25 release) lowers delivered cost; disciplined balance sheet (0.76x net debt/EBITDA vs a ≤1.5x policy) and R$2.4B returned in FY2025 dividends + buybacks, with the preferred count bought down from 1,377,906,016 weighted (FY2023) to 1,285,879,767 (FY2025).
- AGAINST: commodity product with prices set globally and Brazilian prices capped by imports (~22% import share of the domestic market in 2025); ROIC and gross margin both in a four-year slide; scale rivals on both continents (Nucor, Steel Dynamics, ArcelorMittal, CSN) plus low-cost import supply; BRL/Brazil macro exposure and a family control layer (Metalúrgica Gerdau); capex intensity rising into the trough (FY2025 capex $1,251.1M against OE of $179.9M).
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): no TTM base is available — ttm.py exited 3 because it reads US-GAAP facts only and Gerdau is an IFRS 20-F filer; the median base is used per spec. Interim evidence (6-K releases) cuts both ways and is not pinned: Q2 2026 consolidated adjusted EBITDA of R$3.4B was the strongest since Q3 2023 on North America (shipments +7% YoY, EBITDA +15% QoQ), with positive quarterly free cash flow — a recovery from the FY2025 trough is under way, but FY2026 capex is still guided at R$4.7B, so interim OE remains well below the median base; that is priced through the bear-tilted weights, not a TTM pin.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $0.09 vs median $0.42): neither figure is an error — the latest FY is the distorted one as a run-rate (peak Miguel Burnier capex of $1,251.1M plus trough Brazil spreads in a single year), and the median is generous as a run-rate (it embeds FY2023–24 spreads). The base kept is the median, with the distortion handled explicitly: bear/super-bear OE multipliers of 0.85 / 0.60 and 45% weight on bear-or-worse. Anyone quoting the $0.42 base without that adjustment is overstating current earnings power.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:shares**: see OVERRIDES — gap, construction, approximation and measured impact (−64.23% on the base, −10.89pp on weighted IRR, unblocks the tier) are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: ratio 1.0 declared from the filer's own 20-F (each ADS = one preferred share); per-share figures and the $5.08 ADS price are on the same unit, so no scaling distorts the result.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: the >60% revenue jump is FY2021 (R$43,814,661 thousand → R$78,345,081 thousand in fundamentals.json, +78.8%) — the post-COVID global steel price spike, not a divestiture, acquisition or restatement (the small 2025 deals — Gerdau Summit buy-in, Kloeckner Metals Brasil — are immaterial to the revenue base). - **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real cyclicality, not noise — this is a steel spread cycle (OE/share $0.58 → $0.42 → $0.09 across FY2023–25 on the override count, financials_history.csv). ## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the base, not the tier: on the filed common-only share count this name screens as a fake 21.09% weighted IRR; on the correct consolidated diluted count it is a 10.21% weighted IRR whose base ($0.42) is itself a mid-cycle construct sitting 4.7x above the latest FY print ($0.09). Method range across defensible bases runs from clear-D (latest-FY base) to the printed C (median base with bear-tilted scenarios).
- What would have to go right: capex actually falling to the R$4.7B 2026 guide, Miguel Burnier delivering its ~R$1.1B EBITDA potential, North America holding its 62%-of-EBITDA form, and Brazilian trade defense biting on imports. Q2 2026 (adjusted EBITDA R$3.4B, strongest since Q3 2023) is the first print consistent with that path — one quarter, not a trend.
- Quality is not the debate: OE was positive 5 of the last 5 years, SBC is negligible ($27.2M), leverage is 0.76x on the company's measure, and the buyback is shrinking the preferred count. The debate is purely what a normalized steel spread is worth — and at $5.08 the market is already paying for a good part of the recovery (price is 62% above the weighted IV@15% of $3.14 and above even the base-case IV@15% of $3.31).
