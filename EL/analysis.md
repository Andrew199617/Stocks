# EL — The Estée Lauder Companies Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. In scope (market cap ~$36.2B < $40B). Fiscal year ends June 30.

## What it is
The largest US pure-play prestige beauty company: skin care (Estée Lauder, La Mer, Clinique), makeup (MAC, Tom Ford Beauty, Too Faced), fragrance (Jo Malone London, Le Labo, Tom Ford), hair care (Aveda, Bumble and bumble). FY2026 (ended 2026-06-30) net sales $15.05B, +5.0% reported / +3% organic — the first growth year after three straight annual declines (FY2023 $15.91B → FY2024 $15.61B → FY2025 $14.33B) that erased roughly $100B of market value from the early-2022 peak. The February 2025 "Beauty Reimagined" strategy plus the Profit Recovery and Growth Plan (PRGP) is the turnaround vehicle: ~10,000 net job cuts (hit the high end of guidance), cumulative restructuring program cost ~$1.75B pre-tax, program described as completed on 2026-06-30. FY2026 adjusted operating margin 11.2% (+320bp), adjusted diluted EPS $2.51 (+66%). FY2027 guidance: organic sales +3–5%, adjusted operating margin 12.7–13.5%, adjusted EPS $3.10–3.35 — but operating cash flow guided DOWN to $1.3–1.4B on higher restructuring payments and working-capital needs, and management says transformational M&A is off the table while it deleverages.

## Moat score: 5/10 — moderate, real but narrowing
FOR — a prestige brand portfolio built over decades (La Mer, Estée Lauder, MAC, Le Labo, Jo Malone London) with genuine premium pricing power: gross margin 75.5% in FY2026, expanded +150bp in a recovery year. Global prestige distribution plus a record 34% of sales online in FY2026. It gained prestige-beauty share in mainland China in Q4 and the full year FY2026 (China sales +9% for the year), and fragrance (+10% FY2026) is a structurally attractive category where it is strong. Scale in R&D and marketing that sub-scale rivals cannot match.
AGAINST — the moat has visibly eroded: three straight years of revenue decline (FY2023–25) while L'Oréal kept growing; repeated nine-figure impairments on acquired brands are management's own admission that bought brand equity decays (Dr.Jart+, Too Faced, Tom Ford — see flags). Structural channel decay in US department stores and Asia travel retail (once ~15% of sales, still uneven). Rising local Chinese brands and digital-native indies move faster on TikTok-speed cycles than EL's legacy launch process. Lauder-family dual-class control limits outside discipline on capital allocation (the Tom Ford acquisition and its write-downs happened under it). Beauty is discretionary and tariff-exposed.
A 5 gives CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 5 = **8.5 years**: growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection. Long enough to respect the brands, short enough to admit the lead is contestable.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag from the final dcf_numbers.json, resolved
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs): RESOLVED, and it caps confidence.** OE/share swung $4.57 → $1.28 → $3.09 → $1.02 → $2.76 while revenue only ranged $14.3–17.7B — the volatility is a charge/cash-conversion cycle (impairments, restructuring, China/travel-retail collapse), not demand disappearing. Handled three ways: (1) normalized 3-yr-median base instead of any single year; (2) bear weight widened — super bear raised to 15% probability and given a −8% start matching the actual FY2025 print; (3) confidence held at medium despite the tier itself being robust. Note the guided FY2027 OCF decline to $1.3–1.4B means cash OE stays depressed one more year even in the recovery case — the volatility is not finished.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (NI vs OCF differ >40% in 2024, 2025, 2026): RESOLVED year by year — the gap is non-cash impairments and restructuring/litigation charges, and it is why the OCF-based OE base can be trusted while reported EPS cannot.**
  - FY2024: NI $409M vs OCF $2,360M. One-offs: $471M goodwill/other-intangible impairment (Dr.Jart+), $124M restructuring and other charges, $23M DECIEM acquisition-option fair-value expense — plus $825M of ordinary D&A that NI bears and OCF adds back.
  - FY2025: NI −$1,133M vs OCF $1,272M. One-off: $1,286M impairment of goodwill and other intangible assets per the cash-flow reconciliation (including Dr.Jart+ ~$375M and Too Faced ~$50M), $362M restructuring and other charges, and a $159M talcum-litigation settlement. The impairments are non-cash — they destroyed reported earnings without touching operating cash flow.
  - FY2026: NI $182M vs OCF $1,773M. No impairments this year (cash-flow reconciliation shows $0 vs $1,286M in FY2025). The gap is D&A $796M plus restructuring charges ($517M in the first nine months alone, including a securities class-action litigation settlement of $84M in that period) and a $539M build in accounts payable/other liabilities, partly offset by −$164M deferred taxes. Operating income reported $780M vs ~$1,685M adjusted (11.2% margin) tells the same story.
  - Conclusion: the divergence is explained, not sinister — but "restructuring" here is a multi-year, ~$1.75B pre-tax program with real cash costs still flowing through FY2027 OCF. It must NOT be added back to OE, and it has not been: the conservative OE base already bears the cash portion.
- **Balance-sheet data caveat (found in this run, not flagged by the engine):** the engine's `net_debt_latest` of $503M is wrong — EL's long-term-debt and cash tags came back blank in financials_history.csv, so only the current portion was captured. Actual FY2026 balance sheet: total debt ~$7.3B, cash $3.50B, net debt ~$3.8B ≈ **3.8× normalized OE** and ~2.1× OCF, equity only $3.81B after impairments and buybacks. This is material leverage for a company mid-turnaround: it explains the FY2025 dividend cut ($0.66 → $0.35/quarter) and management's explicit deleveraging/no-M&A stance. Folded into the verdict: no tier change (already D on cash flows alone), but there is no "fortress balance sheet" offset to the weak OE, and leverage is a second reason the bear weight was widened.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The turnaround is real but the stock is not cheap on any owner's-earnings math. Sales are growing again, margins are recovering, China stabilized (+9% FY2026), and the restructuring program is declared complete. None of that closes a gap between $23.50 of weighted IV@15% and a $99.13 price.
- **Method range:** this verdict is unusually robust. Even the super-bull scenario (13% growth start, OE ×1.25) yields only $43.58 IV@15% / 8.4% IRR. Even if you aggressively threw out the trough-median base and used FY2022's pre-collapse OE/share of $4.57 with the same scenario set, weighted IV@15% scales to roughly $39 — still ~61% below price, still Tier D. There is no defensible input set on current filings that gets EL to the 15% hurdle at $99.
- What the price actually capitalizes: adjusted-EPS recovery ($2.51 → guided $3.10–3.35 → presumably ~$4+ by FY2028) at a prestige multiple. That is an earnings-multiple story, not an owner's-earnings story — cash conversion lags because restructuring cash, the $508M dividend, and working capital all claim OCF first (FY2027 OCF guided down to $1.3–1.4B proves the point).
- Hurdle math: EL clears a 15% hurdle at the weighted IV@15% of **~$23.50**; with a 20% margin of safety, **~$19**. The stock would need to fall ~76% from $99.13. Alternatively, normalized OE/share would need to roughly quadruple from $2.76 at unchanged growth — i.e., a full return to, and beyond, FY2021 peak earning power ($7.24/share that year, a COVID-recovery print nobody should underwrite as normal).
- Watch items that could change the picture over time: Q1 FY2027 print (2026-11-02) — North America acceleration is the named test; whether FY2027 cash OCF really troughs and rebounds in FY2028 once restructuring payments end; further acquired-brand impairments would confirm the moat is narrower than 5.
