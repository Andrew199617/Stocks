## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-narrow
Justification: a real platform with developer mindshare, one-stack breadth across search/observability/security and genuine migration friction, plus ~75% gross margins — but no cycle of positive ROIC to show for it, hyperscalers forking and bundling against it, and SBC eating most of the cash it generates.
- Evidence first (fundamentals.json): gross margin ran 73.1% / 72.3% / 74.0% / 74.4% / 76.1% across FY22–FY26 and 75.5% TTM — the product carries software economics. The ROIC proxy (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) ran the other way: -1.134 in FY22, -0.537 in FY23, -0.134 in FY24, -0.056 in FY25, -0.025 in FY26 — improving every year but still negative, because GAAP operating income is still negative. A moat of 7+ on that ROIC history would need a reason the history understates the franchise; the honest reason available (SBC-heavy growth spend) is itself the moat problem, since the spend is what the owners pay.
- FOR: Elasticsearch is the default search/data platform for a large developer base, embedded across search, observability and security on one stack; data integration and cluster-tuning switching friction — workloads, indexes and pipelines are costly to migrate once in production; ~75% gross margins; enterprise cohort compounding (Q1 FY27 added the most >$100K ACV customers in company history, cRPO +21%, RPO +27% YoY, AI-feature use in that cohort 37% vs 21% a year ago, per the company release).
- AGAINST: ROIC proxy negative every recent year; hyperscalers and larger rivals on every flank (AWS OpenSearch — a fork of Elastic's own platform — Splunk/Cisco, Datadog, Microsoft, Google, per the 10-K competition section); open-source roots cut both ways, with native vector databases (Pinecone, Qdrant, Weaviate) and MongoDB Atlas attacking the AI-search growth story; no proven pricing power at the GAAP line (operating margin -1.9% in FY26, -5% in Q1 FY27 despite ~75% gross margins); SBC $303,282,000 TTM with the diluted count growing 3.9%/yr.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 3.9%/yr): real and structural — TTM SBC is $303,282,000, and Q1 FY27 SBC of $74,782,000 vs the prior-year quarter's $69,935,000 (ttm.json legs) shows it still rising in dollars. It is already deducted twice in the right places: in full in the OE base, and via the -3.85pp growth adjustment. Buybacks ($380,104,000 TTM per fundamentals.json) roughly offset issuance in count terms but are a cash cost the OCF-based OE does not refund.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real — the series runs from deeply negative through $0.04 (FY25) and $0.22 (FY26) to $0.44 TTM as OCF scaled faster than SBC. Handled by the TTM base plus the bear-shifted probabilities (15/30 on bear-or-worse vs the 10/25 default).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024, 2025): in both flagged years the gap is non-cash add-backs and tax, not operations. FY25: net income -$108,114,000 vs OCF $266,168,000 — a $374,282,000 gap whose largest bridges in statements.json are the SBC add-back of $257,782,000, deferred revenue +$147,112,000 and deferred income taxes +$57,431,000, partly offset by the receivables build (-$48,903,000 — the balance grew, and statements.json carries the line at cf_sign -1) and other working-capital lines; FY25's tax line was a $76,545,000 expense, so no valuation-allowance benefit distorts that year. FY24: net income of $61,720,000 was flattered by a $184,476,000 income-tax benefit with a $186,166,000 valuation-allowance change (and FY26 net income was positive only because of a $370,067,000 benefit dominated by a $390,506,000 valuation-allowance release, FY26 10-K tax note). Net income is unusable as an earnings measure across this window; the OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is unaffected by these non-cash items.
- **SHARES_FALLBACK** (2019): the engine found no count under its diluted-share tags for 2019 and fell back to the period-end outstanding count (73,675,083) recorded in the shares_tag column. statements.json's extraction does carry a filed weighted basic-and-diluted count of 54,893,365 for FY19 (basic and diluted are equal in a loss year), on which FY19 OE/share is -$1.23 rather than the CSV's -$0.91 — the fallback flatters that one history year, and the engine's tag coverage is a code matter. - **IRR_AT_FLOOR** (super bear): at $95.25 the super-bear IRR (2.84%) sits within 0.5pp of the 2.5% terminal growth rate, so read it as "at most ~3.0%", not a precise figure. The weighted IRR (3.30%) is not at the floor, but the message is the same: this price is beyond the model's useful range.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The debate on ESTC is not growth — revenue compounding in the mid-to-high teens with RPO growing faster is fine — it is conversion. Free cash flow looks strong only because it adds SBC back; on the owner's definition, $354,063,000 of TTM operating cash flow becomes $45,755,000 after $303,282,000 of SBC and $5,026,000 of capex.
- What would change the answer is SBC dollars flattening while OCF keeps compounding: OE/share has gone $0.04 → $0.22 → $0.44, so the direction is right, but the base would need to multiply many times over — not grow by tens of percent — before a $95.25 price clears even a 10% hurdle (weighted IV@10% is $9.42).
- Risks to the short side of this verdict: an AI-search re-acceleration (the super-bull tail), a hard SBC reset under new comp discipline, or a takeout — none of which the owner's-earnings method can price, and none of which rescue the tier on filed numbers.
