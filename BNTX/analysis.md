## What it is
German mRNA biotechnology company best known for the COVID-19 vaccine co-developed and commercialised with Pfizer. That franchise is shrinking fast, and the company is spending heavily to pivot to oncology — mRNA cancer immunotherapies, antibody-drug conjugates and pumitamig under a Bristol Myers Squibb collaboration — with no approved oncology product yet. Reported Q2 2026 results (search, company release via StockTitan/Markets Financial Content, 2026-08-04): revenues €105.6 million, down from €260.8 million a year earlier on lower COVID-19 vaccine demand, net loss €820.8 million, H1 2026 revenues €223.7 million with a net loss of €1,352.7 million, FY2026 revenue guidance cut to €1.6–1.9 billion from €2.0–2.3 billion, and a new CEO to take office by February 1, 2027 at the latest. Cash, cash equivalents and security investments were €16.6 billion at June 30, 2026 in that release.

## MOAT: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate
Justification: Proven mRNA platform, manufacturing know-how and the Pfizer COVID-19 partnership, funded by a very large cash and investments balance, but no durable commercial franchise beyond the shrinking COVID vaccine: oncology pipeline (pumitamig/BMS, ADCs, mRNA immunotherapies) is unapproved and binary, the COVID economics are shared with Pfizer and commoditising, and leadership is in transition.
- FOR: Proven mRNA platform and manufacturing that delivered a commercial COVID-19 vaccine at global scale with Pfizer; large cash and security investments balance funding years of oncology R&D without dilution; BMS collaboration on pumitamig and a broad late-stage oncology pipeline (14 pivotal trials in Q2 2026 reporting); deep mRNA / immunotherapy know-how and CureVac acquisition consolidating mRNA IP.
- AGAINST: COVID-19 vaccine demand collapsing (Q2 2026 revenue -59.5% YoY and FY2026 guidance cut to €1.6-1.9B in the cited release); no approved oncology product yet — pipeline value is binary clinical/regulatory risk, not a commercial moat; COVID economics shared with Pfizer, with competition from Moderna and other vaccine/oncology platforms; founder/CEO leadership transition (incoming CEO by Feb 1, 2027) and heavy R&D/SG&A burn widening losses.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years (dcf_numbers.json cap_years): growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest tension: this DCF values current owner's earnings, and on that basis BNTX is a clear D — even the super-bull scenario yields only 4.02% IRR and $10.34 IV@15. What the model does not capitalise is (a) the €16.6 billion cash and securities position reported at June 30, 2026 and (b) pipeline option value. Both are real, neither is owner's earnings, and the sweep's rule is not to invent a tier from them. I state the limitation rather than overriding the tier.
- The $1.0 billion ADS repurchase authorisation reported in 2026 (search: 6-K, May 2026) and the Q2 repurchases are mild support — management buying at around these prices — but buybacks do not create owner's earnings.
- Method range: defensible inputs do not span tiers here. The preliminary default-probability run gave weighted IRR 3.23% and IV@15 $5.54; the final bear-skewed run gives 3.19% and $5.27. Both are D by a wide margin, and no scenario in either run clears 10%.
- Price that clears 15%: the probability-weighted IV@15 itself, $5.27 — i.e. the ADS would need to trade near $5.27 for this OE base to clear Andrew's 15% hurdle with no margin of safety at all. With any margin of safety the required price is lower still. Stated plainly: at $88.77 there is no margin of safety on current earnings; a buyer is paying for pipeline success and the balance sheet.
