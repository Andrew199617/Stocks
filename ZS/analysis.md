## What it is
The pure-play zero-trust / SASE vendor. Its Zero Trust Exchange (150+ edge locations) sits between users, workloads and applications: ZIA (secure internet/SaaS access), ZPA (private-app access replacing VPN), ZDX (experience monitoring), plus fast-growing data-security, Security-for-AI and Agentic SecOps (built on the Red Canary acquisition, closed at the start of FY26, ~$141M of ARR). Sold as per-user subscriptions to large enterprises; fiscal year ends July 31.

Latest earnings — Q4 / FY2026 (ended Jul 31, 2026; reported Sep 3, 2026), read past the headline:
- FY26 revenue $3,353M (+25.4% latest rev YoY per dcf_numbers.json), Q4 revenue $898.2M (+25%). ARR $3.771B (+25%) — but only +20% excluding Red Canary's $141M ARR; organic net new ARR grew +17% in Q4, improving from +7-10% in prior halves.
- Non-GAAP operating income $767.1M for FY26, non-GAAP EPS $4.21. GAAP net loss -$63.2M (financials_history.csv: -$63,179,000). Company-defined FCF $779.1M; the engine's OCF − capex for FY26 is $852,350,000 (financials_history.csv) — the company's figure also deducts capitalized software.
- **FY27 guidance is the story: revenue $3.908-3.938B = +16.6-17.5%** — a guided deceleration of ~8 points, reaffirmed at the October 2026 Investor Day alongside Q1 FY27 revenue guidance of $935-939M. ARR guided +17%, FCF margin guided 23.0-23.5%. Also a ~3% workforce restructuring to fund AI initiatives, and a CRO transition (new CRO took over Oct 1, 2026).
- Cooling leading indicators: RPO growth decelerated three straight quarters, deferred revenue growth below revenue growth. Peer read-across is unflattering: CrowdStrike and Palo Alto platform ARR momentum is running well ahead of ZS's organic net-new ARR pace.
- AI is a genuine offsetting tailwind: Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially in Q4, ~70% of those deals attached data security, metered offerings ~30% of new/upsell ACV with ARR +100% YoY. Investor Day laid out a path to >$8B ARR by FY31.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, platform leader under bundling attack
Justification: Zero Trust Exchange architecture with real enterprise switching costs and telemetry scale, and working land-and-expand — against platformization/bundling by larger rivals, no endpoint/firewall base, unproven pricing power as growth is guided down, and rising data-center capex intensity.
- FOR: Architectural switching costs — once an enterprise routes all user/app traffic through the Zero Trust Exchange, ripping it out means re-architecting access, not swapping a tool; ranked #1 in ZTNA-as-a-service and #2 in SASE (PeerSpot). Scale and telemetry: the exchange sees enormous transaction volume, feeding threat/data models smaller SSE rivals can't match. Installed-base compounding: 785 customers >$1M ARR (+18%), 4,182 >$100K (+20%), >$10M-ARR customers nearly doubled, dollar-based net retention ~115% and stable. Land-and-expand working into new categories from the same footprint — data security attaches to ~70% of AI deals, Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially, Red Canary extends it into SecOps.
- AGAINST: Platformization — Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Fortinet and Microsoft (Entra) can bundle SSE/ZTNA into suites ZS must sell standalone, and peers' current ARR momentum is stronger than ZS's organic pace. No endpoint, no firewall base, no owned user surface — distribution is an expensive enterprise sales motion, and the talent war behind it is exactly what the SBC line is paying for. Pricing power is not showing up: revenue growth has stepped down from the 5-yr CAGR of 37.87% (dcf_numbers.json) toward the guided ~17%, and RPO growth has decelerated three straight quarters — a widening moat usually decelerates more gently. Rising infrastructure intensity (capex $277.3M in FY26, building its own data centers) erodes the pure-software cost story.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag from the final dcf_numbers.json, resolved
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE (latest FY OE/share 0.19 vs 3-yr median 0.72):** the *latest* year is the distorted one — depressed by the pulled-forward capex spike ($277.3M in FY26 vs $164.3M in FY25, incl. the $218.5M Q4 capex+capitalized-software surge to lock in equipment prices) and a step-up in SBC. Median $0.72 kept as base; it sits below the FY25 print ($0.95), so the choice is conservative, not generous.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD (0.31%):** real and structural, not a timing artifact — SBC of $821.9M against OCF of $1,129.7M has not delevered. The valuation rests entirely on (a) decades of growth and (b) SBC eventually collapsing toward software norms. The model grants (a) via a 9.8-yr CAP and grants none of (b). Confidence capped at low.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (5.00x):** real — it is what a $0.72 base against a $228.66 price does to any scenario math. Confidence capped at low accordingly. Note the spread does not rescue the stock: the *super-bull* IV@15% ($24.73) is still a small fraction of the price.
- **DILUTION (share count growing 3.4%/yr):** verified in the filings — diluted shares 144,942,000 (FY23) → 160,219,000 (FY26, financials_history.csv), driven by the SBC above ($444.8M → $821.9M over the same span). The engine already deducts 3.40pp in the growth blend (share_count_adj), and SBC is also charged inside OE — the audit's accepted conservative double-count. ZS runs no offsetting buyback.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs):** investigated — no divestiture, no restatement, no basis break. The trip is FY22 revenue growth off a small base (revenue $673.1M in FY21 → $1,090.9M in FY22, financials_history.csv) in the pandemic-era zero-trust demand surge; growth has decelerated organically since. The one inorganic item is Red Canary (closed start of FY26, ~$141M ARR) plus the small SPLXAI deal — it flatters FY26 ARR growth (+25% headline vs +20% organic) but is immaterial to the CAGRs, and the forward scenario starts are anchored to the FY27 guide, so nothing straddles a break blindly.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range spans >3x over the last 5 yrs):** verified — FY22 -$1.11, FY23 -$0.55, FY24 +$0.72, FY25 +$0.95, FY26 +$0.19 (financials_history.csv totals / the base figures above). This is not cyclicality; it is a young P&L where SBC exceeded OCF − capex until FY24, and where a single capex decision (FY26) can cut OE by three-quarters. Handled via the 3-yr median base and the widened bear weighting (super bear + bear = 45% vs 35% default).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2024, 2025, 2026):** structural, not a one-off — no tax deposit, impairment or WC event explains it. GAAP net losses (-$57.7M / -$41.5M / -$63.2M, financials_history.csv) vs OCF (+$779.8M / +$972.5M / +$1,129.7M) differ almost entirely by non-cash SBC added back ($527.7M / $661.4M / $821.9M), plus deferred-revenue growth from annual upfront billing. Conclusion: OCF systematically flatters this company, and the conservative OE definition (which subtracts SBC) is precisely the correction — the base is trustworthy *because* the divergence is understood.
- Balance-sheet note (no flag fired): the engine's net debt is null — debt tags are empty for FY25-26 and its cash line excludes short-term investments, so no HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag could fire. It doesn't matter to the verdict: the entire FY26 cash-and-equivalents balance is $928,354,000 (financials_history.csv) against a $36.64B market cap.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is not the precise IV — on a 0.31% OE yield any point estimate is fragile — it's the *distance*: the price is ~20x the weighted IV@15%, and the gap survives every defensible sensitivity. Preliminary default run: IV@15% $14.57, weighted IRR 3.50%. Final recalibrated run: $11.14, 3.26%. The method range spans Tier D only — even the super-bull scenario (30% growth start, 1.25x OE) yields an IRR of just 4.3% at this price. There is no input set consistent with the filings and the FY27 guide that gets ZS near a 15% hurdle at $228.66; the market price is paying for SBC to become a rounding error *and* for 20%+ growth to persist well past the guided deceleration.
- Quality is not the debate: 23% non-GAAP operating margin, $779.1M of company-defined FCF, net retention ~115%, category-defining architecture. The debate is purely what you pay for earnings that are currently paid out to employees in stock — SBC of $821.9M is the shadow P&L shareholders are funding.
- What would change the answer: SBC breaking down decisively as a share of OCF while growth holds the high teens (OE/share would inflect — FY25 showed the torque), or the price falling to a level where the *current* OE math matters. Neither is close.
- Margin of safety: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at or below the weighted IV@15% of **$11.14**; with a 25% margin of safety, roughly **$8**. That is ~95% below the current price — this is a valuation verdict, not a "wait for a dip" verdict.
