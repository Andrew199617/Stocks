# ZS — Zscaler, Inc.
Run 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: ~$36.0B at the engine price ($224.50 × 160.2M diluted shares; universe screener $38.1B at $233.73).

## What it is
The pure-play zero-trust / SASE vendor. Its Zero Trust Exchange (150+ edge locations) sits between users, workloads and applications: ZIA (secure internet/SaaS access), ZPA (private-app access replacing VPN), ZDX (experience monitoring), plus fast-growing data-security, Security-for-AI and Agentic SecOps (built on the Red Canary acquisition, closed at the start of FY26 for ~$692M, ~$141M of ARR). Sold as per-user subscriptions to large enterprises; fiscal year ends July 31.

Latest earnings — Q4 / FY2026 (ended Jul 31, 2026; reported Sep 3-4, 2026), read past the headline:
- FY26 revenue $3.353B (+25.4%), Q4 $898.2M (+25%). ARR $3.771B (+25%) — but only **+20% excluding Red Canary's $141M**; organic net new ARR grew +17% in Q4, improving from +7-10% in prior halves.
- Non-GAAP operating income $767.1M (23% margin), non-GAAP EPS $4.21. GAAP net loss -$63.2M. Company-defined FCF $779.1M (23% margin; engine's OCF − capex is $852.4M — the company's figure also deducts capitalized software).
- **FY27 guidance is the story: revenue $3.908-3.938B = +16.6-17.5%** — a guided deceleration of ~8 points. ARR guided +17%, non-GAAP OI $924-932M, FCF margin 23.0-23.5%. Also a ~3% workforce restructuring ($30-33M charges) to fund AI initiatives.
- Cooling leading indicators: RPO growth decelerated three straight quarters (34.5% → 29.8% → 27.6%), deferred revenue +19% (below revenue growth). Peer read-across is unflattering: CrowdStrike net new ARR +51% and Palo Alto's NGS ARR roughly doubling over comparable periods vs ZS organic NNARR +17%.
- AI is a genuine offsetting tailwind: Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially in Q4, ~70% of those deals attached data security, metered offerings ~30% of new/upsell ACV with ARR +100% YoY. Investor Day (Oct 2026) laid out a path to >$8B ARR by FY31.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong, platform leader under bundling attack
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 6 = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection).
- FOR: (1) Architectural switching costs — once an enterprise routes all user/app traffic through the Zero Trust Exchange, ripping it out means re-architecting access, not swapping a tool; ranked #1 in ZTNA-as-a-service and #2 in SASE (PeerSpot). (2) Scale and telemetry: the exchange sees enormous transaction volume, feeding threat/data models smaller SSE rivals can't match. (3) Installed-base compounding: 785 customers >$1M ARR (+18%), 4,182 >$100K (+20%), >$10M-ARR customers nearly doubled, dollar-based net retention ~115% and stable. (4) Land-and-expand is working into new categories from the same footprint — data security attaches to ~70% of AI deals, Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially, Red Canary extends it into SecOps.
- AGAINST: (1) Platformization — Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Fortinet and Microsoft (Entra) can bundle SSE/ZTNA into suites ZS must sell standalone, and peers' current ARR momentum is stronger than ZS's organic pace. (2) No endpoint, no firewall base, no owned user surface — distribution is an expensive enterprise sales motion, and the talent war behind it is exactly what the 25%-of-revenue SBC line is paying for. (3) Pricing power is not showing up: revenue growth has stepped down 48% → 34% → 23% → 25% → guided ~17%, and RPO growth has decelerated three straight quarters — a widening moat usually decelerates more gently. (4) Rising infrastructure intensity (capex 8.3% of revenue, building its own data centers) erodes the pure-software cost story.
A 6 credits a real, sticky architecture position with a decade of enterprise proof; it withholds 7+ because the moat is being actively bundled against by larger platforms and the growth prints no longer behave like a widening moat.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag from the final dcf_numbers.json, resolved
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE (latest $0.19 vs median $0.72):** the *latest* year is the distorted one — depressed by the pulled-forward capex spike ($277.3M, incl. a $218.5M Q4 capex+capitalized-software surge to lock in equipment prices) and a step-up in SBC. At a normalized capex level FY26 OE ≈ $0.90/share, consistent with FY25's $0.95. Median $0.72 kept as base; it sits below both adjusted-latest and FY25, so the choice is conservative, not generous.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD (0.32%):** real and structural, not a timing artifact — SBC is 24.5% of revenue and has not delevered. The valuation rests entirely on (a) decades of growth and (b) SBC/revenue eventually collapsing toward software norms. The model grants (a) via a 9.8-yr CAP and grants none of (b). Confidence capped at low.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (5.0×):** real — it is what a $0.72 base against a $224.50 price does to any scenario math. Confidence capped accordingly. Note the spread does not rescue the stock: the *super-bull* IV ($24.69) is still 89% below price.
- **DILUTION (share count +3.4%/yr):** verified in the filings — diluted shares 144.9M (FY23) → 160.2M (FY26), driven by the SBC above ($444.8M → $821.9M over the same span). The engine already deducts 3.4pp in the growth blend, and SBC is also charged inside OE — the audit's accepted conservative double-count. ZS runs no offsetting buyback.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT (>60% revenue jump in last 5 yrs):** investigated — no divestiture, no restatement, no basis break. The trip is FY22 revenue +62% (pandemic-era zero-trust demand surge off a small base); growth has decelerated organically since. The one inorganic item is Red Canary (closed start of FY26, ~$692M, ~$141M ARR ≈ 4% of ARR) plus the small SPLXAI deal — it flatters FY26 ARR growth (+25% headline vs +20% organic) but is immaterial to the CAGRs, and the forward scenario starts are anchored to the FY27 guide, so nothing straddles a break blindly.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs):** verified — FY22 -$1.11, FY23 -$0.55, FY24 +$0.72, FY25 +$0.95, FY26 +$0.19. This is not cyclicality; it is a young P&L where SBC exceeded OCF − capex until FY24, and where a single capex decision (FY26) can cut OE by three-quarters. Handled via the 3-yr median base and the widened bear weighting (super bear + bear = 45% vs 35% default).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2024, 2025, 2026):** structural, not a one-off — no tax deposit, impairment or WC event explains it. GAAP net losses (-$57.7M / -$41.5M / -$63.2M) vs OCF (+$779.8M / +$972.5M / +$1,129.7M) differ almost entirely by non-cash SBC added back ($527.7M / $661.4M / $821.9M), plus deferred-revenue growth from annual upfront billing. Conclusion: OCF systematically flatters this company, and the conservative OE definition (which subtracts SBC) is precisely the correction — the base is trustworthy *because* the divergence is understood.
- Balance-sheet note (no flag fired): the engine's net debt is null — debt tags are empty for FY25-26 and its cash line excludes short-term investments, so no HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag could fire. It doesn't matter to the verdict: even the entire $928M cash-and-equivalents balance is $5.79/share gross (before any convertible debt) against a $224.50 price.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is not the precise IV — on a 0.32% OE yield any point estimate is fragile — it's the *distance*: price is ~20× the weighted IV@15%, and the gap survives every defensible sensitivity. Preliminary default run: IV $14.57, IRR 3.5%. Final recalibrated run: $11.14, 3.3%. **Aggressive sensitivity: treat SBC as entirely free** (base OE = FY26 OCF − capex = $5.32/share, the street-style cash number the method explicitly rejects) and the same scenarios give **IV@15% $82.38, IRR 7.7% — still Tier D, still ~63% below price.** There is no input set consistent with the filings and the FY27 guide that gets ZS near a 15% hurdle at $224.50; the market price is paying for SBC to become a rounding error *and* for 20%+ growth to persist well past the guided deceleration.
- Quality is not the debate: 77% gross margin, 23% non-GAAP operating margin, $779M of FCF, net retention ~115%, category-defining architecture. The debate is purely what you pay for earnings that are currently paid out to employees in stock. At $5.13/share of SBC, shareholders are funding a second, shadow P&L.
- What would change the answer: SBC/revenue breaking below ~15% while growth holds the high teens (OE/share would inflect violently — FY25 showed the torque), or the price falling to a level where the *current* OE math matters. Neither is close.
- Margin-of-safety math: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at or below the weighted IV@15% of **~$11**; with a 25% margin of safety, **~$8**. That is ~95% below the current price — this is a valuation verdict, not a "wait for a dip" verdict.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (weighted IRR 3.3% on the final v4 inputs; every scenario and every sensitivity tested, including SBC-free, is also D — the method range spans D only)
- Confidence: **Low** — on the point estimates, as the flags require (0.32% OE yield, 5.0× scenario spread, OE history that was negative 2 of the last 5 years). The *tier* is nonetheless unusually robust: it survives the most aggressive base the filings can be stretched to.
