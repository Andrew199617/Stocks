## What it is
A leading global alternative asset manager built around private credit and direct lending, with Real Assets, Private Equity, and Secondaries groups on top. Total AUM $671.3B at Q2 2026 (+17% YoY), fee-paying AUM $409.9B (+17% YoY), available capital $170.0B. Q2 2026: record fundraising of $36.4B in the quarter, management fees $1.02B, fee-related earnings (FRE) $491.1M (+20% YoY), after-tax realized income $467.6M = $1.29/share (+25% YoY), quarterly dividend declared $1.35/share (+20% YoY). The growth levers are structural — bank retreat from lending, institutional / wealth / insurance demand for private credit — and management's Investor Day targets are FRE CAGR 16-20% and realized income CAGR 20-25% through 2028. The earnings question is not growth; it is what the engine's cash-flow definition means for this structure (below), and what multiple the growth deserves.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- FOR: Scale and origination in private credit — one of the largest direct-lending platforms in the world, with deal flow, sponsor relationships and workout capability smaller lenders cannot replicate, inside a $671.3B AUM / $409.9B fee-paying platform
- FOR: Durability of the fee base — long-dated and perpetual capital is a large share of fee-paying AUM, AUM not yet paying fees is a visible future fee pipeline, and FRE (the recurring management-fee core) grew +20% YoY in Q2 2026
- FOR: Diversification and distribution — Credit, Real Assets, Private Equity and Secondaries, sold through institutional, wealth and insurance channels, so no single fund vintage or channel carries the firm
- FOR: Through-cycle fundraising record — a record $36.4B raised in Q2 2026 alone, $170.0B of available capital
- AGAINST: No structural pricing power — LPs allocate each new vintage competitively among Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Brookfield, Blue Owl and Ares, and direct-lending spreads have compressed as that competition scaled; Ares wins share, it does not set price
- AGAINST: Earnings geared to the private-credit cycle — a default / non-accrual wave hits carried interest, marks, fundraising and the insurance book at the same time
- AGAINST: Growth partly bought, not only earned — GCP International was acquired in 2025 (roughly doubling Real Assets AUM) and BlueCove followed in Feb 2026; integration and contingent-consideration risk is real, and acquired AUM does not prove organic moat
- AGAINST: Regulatory and reputational overhang on private credit and affiliated insurance (Aspida), plus partner-economics dilution — share count growing 3.5%/yr (engine share CAGR), already deducted from growth

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (controlling flag):** Finance / alternative asset manager — the OE/capex DCF fits poorly by construction: the base capitalizes consolidated-fund OCF (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE), which is not the manager's distributable earnings. Resolution follows the ACGL/HIG precedent: the standardized engine base is kept for comparability, confidence is capped at **low**, and the final tier is softened one notch by tier_override (see verdict). On the manager's own cash-earnings definition the picture is Tier C (method range, below) — consistent with a softened C, not with the B the raw engine prints.
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.43 months ago): ttm.py was run and its full inputs are quoted in the OE section; its TTM base ($0.4157/share) was NOT used, for the documented reason that its OCF leg is a consolidated-fund timing swing (H1 OCF $2,409,904,000 → $55,070,000) contradicted by every manager-earnings print in the same period (FRE +20% YoY, realized EPS +25% YoY in Q2 2026). The median base is used instead, with that reason stated. Q2 2026 is the latest quarter.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 3.5%/yr): real — equity comp, AOG-unit exchanges, and acquisition stock (GCP). Already deducted inside g_base via the −3.54pp share-count adjustment; SBC is charged in full in the base OE ($740,549,000 in FY2025, $704,007,000 TTM per ttm.py) and is rising, which is one reason scenario starts sit below the revenue blend. Not double-counted in the verdict.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs): identified, two parts. (1) The historical breaks are performance-fee / consolidated-fund revenue volatility in the GAAP revenue line (FY2021 revenue $4,212,091,000 on a strong carried-interest year, FY2022 $3,055,443,000), not a divestiture restatement of the fee business — which is why no GAAP revenue CAGR was trusted for scenario starts. (2) A genuine inorganic step inside the current window: GCP International closed in 2025 and roughly doubled Real Assets AUM, contributing to FY2025 revenue +44.19% YoY. Growth inputs were therefore anchored to FRE / realized-EPS prints and the company's FRE CAGR guide, which are post-GCP run-rate measures, instead of to any CAGR straddling the acquisition.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025, gaps >40%): structural for a consolidating alt manager, not a one-off. Consolidated OCF includes the investment activity of consolidated funds / CLOs (purchases and sales of fund investments, fund borrowings) and swung from −$233,261,000 (2023) to +$2,791,154,000 (2024) to +$3,266,959,000 (2025) while net income attributable moved $474,326,000 → $463,742,000 → $527,362,000 (financials_history.csv). D&A ($31,400,000-$45,200,000/yr in the same file) explains none of it. Consequence: the OCF base is treated as an upper-bound standardized screen, the TTM OCF base is rejected outright, and the verdict is sized to the realized-earnings end of the method range.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:capex / OVERRIDE_APPLIED:shares:** see OVERRIDE — gap, decisions, sources, band checks, and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied / override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.39x super-bear): real — a credit-cycle-levered manager's 15-year range genuinely runs from a credit accident (super-bear IV@15% $51.49) to a decade of guide-beating compounding ($226.30). Consequence per the rules: confidence is low (already capped by the sector flag) and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note even the bull case (25% weight) yields 19.22% on the engine base but the realized-earnings definition puts the weighted result in the low teens (below).
- No BASE_DIVERGENCE flag fired on the final run (latest FY OE/share and the 3-yr median are the same $7.5045 with the overrides applied) and no HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag fired (engine net debt is null for this filer; see the balance-sheet note).

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range (the real headline):** on the engine's standardized consolidated-OCF base, this run prints Tier B (weighted IRR 15.95%, IV@15% $122.19). On the manager's own cash-earnings definition — TTM after-tax realized income of $5.17/share through Q2 2026, built in the 2026-10-13 v4 run from the company's earnings releases — the same scenario structure printed a weighted IRR of 11.9% and IV@15% of $84.17 at a $118.15 price, Tier C. The v4 run's raw-engine variants were spurious in both directions (Tier F on the unoverridden median, high-teens on the latest-FY OCF figure). Every defensible variant on the manager's own definition is C; the standardized B is the generous end of the range, and the final verdict is sized to the conservative end, per the wide-spread rule.
- Quality is not the debate: FRE is recurring, growing at +20% YoY, and covers the business through a fundraising winter in a way carried interest never did; AUM not yet paying fees is a visible future fee pipeline; the dividend was raised +20% to $1.35/quarter. The debate is price and definition: at $111.985 the buyer is paying for something between the base and bull paths on the engine's most generous earnings definition.
- Red flags to monitor: private-credit defaults / non-accruals and spread compression as Blackstone / Apollo / Blue Owl compete for the same loans; fundraising cyclicality if institutional allocators pause; realized-income lumpiness if exit markets close; GCP contingent consideration and integration; regulatory scrutiny of private credit and of affiliated insurance float (Aspida); and continued ~3.5%/yr share-count creep with SBC rising in absolute terms.
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine base the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$122** (weighted IV@15%) — i.e. it already sits ~9% below that figure, with no margin of safety against the bear cases (bear IV@15% $77.18). On the realized-earnings definition it clears 15% only at ≈ **$84** (the v4 run's weighted IV@15%), and with a 20% margin of safety the buy price on that definition is ≈ **$67**.
