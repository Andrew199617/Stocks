# ARES — Ares Management Corporation
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Finance / Investment Managers (alternative asset management). Price $118.15 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap $38.72B in universe.csv — in scope under $40B.

## What it is
A leading global alternative asset manager, built around private credit and direct lending, with Real Assets, Private Equity, and Secondaries groups on top. Total AUM $671.3B at Q2 2026 (+17% YoY), fee-paying AUM $409.9B (+17-19% YoY), available capital ~$156-158B, and AUM not yet paying fees ~$79B. Q2 2026: raised $36.4B in the quarter (a record), management fees $1.02B (+13% YoY on the Zacks summary of the release), fee-related earnings (FRE) $491.1M (+20% YoY), after-tax realized income $467.6M = $1.29/share (+25% YoY vs $1.03). Q1 2026: FRE $464.4M, after-tax realized income $452.4M = $1.24/share, AUM $644.3B. The growth levers are structural — bank retreat from lending, institutional / wealth / insurance (Aspida) demand for private credit — and management's 2024 Investor Day targets are FRE CAGR 16-20% and realized income CAGR 20-25% through 2028 toward >$750B AUM. The earnings question is therefore not growth; it is what multiple that growth deserves, and whether the engine's cash-flow definition means anything for this structure. It does not, unadjusted (below).

## Owner's earnings — the engine's OCF base is rejected for an alternative manager, and why
- **Engine conservative OE (OCF − capex − SBC):** SBC is not tagged (read as $0), capex is ~$0 in most years, so OE = consolidated OCF outright. That series is fund plumbing, not manager earnings: OCF was −$2.60B (FY2021), −$0.73B (FY2022), −$0.23B (FY2023), then +$2.79B (FY2024) and +$3.27B (FY2025), because Ares consolidates certain funds / CLO vehicles whose investment purchases, sales and borrowings run through consolidated cash flow while the manager's own economics are the fees and carried interest on those funds. OE was positive in only 2 of the last 5 fiscal years on this definition.
- **Consequence of the raw definitions:** latest-FY OE/share on the engine basis is $8.91, the 3-yr median is **−$0.76/share**, and the preliminary run therefore printed Tier F with "no positive normalized OE." Both figures are artifacts of the same defect — neither is a verdict on the manager. The maintenance version (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC) collapses to roughly GAAP net income for the same reason (FY2025 $572.6M on the engine sheet) and inherits the GAAP distortions below.
- **GAAP net income is also not the base:** FY2025 GAAP net income attributable to Ares Management Corporation was $527.4M, and net income attributable to Class A and non-voting stockholders after Series B preferred dividends was $426.1M = $1.71/share diluted. GAAP is charged with unrealized carried-interest / investment marks, consolidated-fund income that belongs to fund investors and to non-controlling interests in the Ares Operating Group, and large equity-compensation charges (e.g. $257.9M of equity-based compensation expense in Q1 2025 alone, per the 10-Q note). It understates the cash earnings the dividend is actually paid from — the quarterly dividend was raised to $1.35/share in 2026 (+20% YoY, $5.40 annualized), which GAAP EPS alone would not cover.
- **Base used — TTM after-tax realized income per share through Q2 2026 (Ares's distributable-earnings proxy: FRE plus realized fee-related performance and realized investment income, after tax), built with the standard TTM construction applied to this series instead of OCF, exactly as the IBKR run applied it to attributable income and the PRU run to adjusted operating income:** FY2025 $4.76 − H1 2025 $2.12 (Q1 $1.09 + Q2 $1.03) + H1 2026 $2.53 (Q1 $1.24 + Q2 $1.29) = **$5.17/share**. Cross-checks: FRE alone was $1,775.3M in FY2025 ($527.7M in Q4 alone) and $955.5M in H1 2026 ($464.4M + $491.1M), so realized income is FRE plus a real but lumpier realized-performance layer, not a soft add-back invention; consensus in the Q1 2026 preview coverage put FY2026 after-tax realized EPS at ~$5.99 (+25.8% vs FY2025), i.e. the TTM $5.17 is the trailing, not the forward, figure. Price = **22.9× TTM realized OE**; OE yield on the used base: 4.4%.
- Balance sheet (folded in explicitly): the engine's net debt is null — its cash / debt tags do not capture this balance sheet, and consolidated-fund debt is generally non-recourse to the manager. No HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH adjustment is therefore taken in either direction; corporate leverage and insurance-affiliate (Aspida) risk are handled through the sector confidence cap and the bear weights, not through a fabricated net-debt number.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to 2.5% terminal over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Scale and origination in private credit — one of the largest direct-lending platforms in the world, with the deal flow, sponsor relationships and workout capability that smaller lenders cannot replicate, inside a $671B AUM / $410B fee-paying platform. (2) Durability of the fee base: long-dated and perpetual capital is a large and growing share of fee-paying AUM, $79B of AUM is already raised but not yet fee-paying, and FRE — the recurring, management-fee core — grew +20-26% YoY in H1 2026 with a ~40%+ FRE margin story. (3) Diversification and distribution: Credit, Real Assets (doubled by GCP, below), Private Equity and Secondaries, sold through institutional, wealth and insurance channels, so no single fund vintage or channel carries the firm. (4) A through-cycle fundraising record — $113.2B gross raised in FY2025 and a record $36.4B in Q2 2026 alone.
- **AGAINST:** (1) No structural pricing power: LPs allocate each new vintage competitively among Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Brookfield, Blue Owl and Ares on near-identical pitches, and direct-lending spreads have compressed as that competition scaled — Ares wins share, it does not set price. (2) The earnings are geared to the private-credit cycle: a default / non-accrual wave hits carried interest, marks, fundraising and the insurance book at the same time; nothing in the moat insulates realized income from credit. (3) Growth has been partly bought, not only earned — GCP International was acquired for $3.91B of consideration ($1.79B cash + $1.66B equity + $0.47B contingent, closed 1 Mar 2025), plus Walton Street Mexico, Crescent Point and BlueCove (Feb 2026, +$5.5B AUM) — integration and contingent-consideration risk is real, and acquired AUM does not prove organic moat. (4) Regulatory and reputational overhang on private credit and affiliated insurance (Aspida), plus key-person / partner-economics dilution: the share count has grown ~+2.5%/yr (equity comp, AOG-unit exchanges, acquisition stock), which the engine already deducts from growth. That is a 6: a strong, scaled franchise in a business where the customer can always take the next vintage elsewhere.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (controlling flag):** Finance / investment managers — the OE/capex DCF fits poorly by construction. Resolved the IBKR/PRU way: the model's cash-flow definition was replaced (consolidated-fund OCF discarded, base rebuilt on TTM after-tax realized income), confidence capped at **low**, and the tier is explicitly soft. A fee-earnings-multiple / dividend model is the right v5 tool for alt managers; under that lens (22.9× TTM realized earnings, $5.40 forward dividend = 4.6% yield) the stock also screens as fully priced, not cheap — consistent with a soft C, not a D or a B.
- **STALE_FY (FY ended ~9 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override shown in full above — FY2025 after-tax realized EPS rolled forward with H1 2025 / H1 2026 quarterly realized EPS from the company's earnings releases ($4.76 − $2.12 + $2.53 = $5.17). The standard OCF TTM construction was computed on the engine series only to confirm it is unusable here (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE) and was not used. Q2 2026 is the latest quarter: AUM $671B, FRE +20%, realized EPS +25% YoY.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2023, 2024, 2025, gaps >40%):** Structural for a consolidating alt manager, not a one-off. Consolidated OCF includes the investment activity of consolidated funds / CLOs (purchases and sales of fund investments, fund borrowings) and swung from −$0.23B (2023) to +$2.79B (2024) to +$3.27B (2025) while attributable net income moved $474M → $464M → $527M. D&A ($31-45M/yr) explains none of it. Consequence: OCF was removed from the base rather than "adjusted" — the divergence *is* the reason for the realized-income base.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs):** Identified, two parts. (1) The historical breaks are performance-fee / consolidated-fund revenue volatility in the GAAP revenue line (FY2021 revenue +139% to $4.21B on a strong carried-interest year, FY2022 −27%), not a divestiture restatement of the manager's fee business — which is why no GAAP revenue CAGR was trusted for scenario starts. (2) A genuine inorganic step inside the current window: GCP International closed 1 Mar 2025 for $3.91B consideration and roughly doubled Real Assets AUM to ~$96B, contributing to FY2025 GAAP revenue +44% YoY and to the AUM step-up; BlueCove followed in Feb 2026. Growth inputs were therefore anchored to FRE / realized-EPS prints and the company's FRE CAGR guide, which are post-GCP run-rate measures, instead of to any CAGR straddling the acquisition.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 4.4× super-bear):** Real — a levered-to-the-credit-cycle manager's 15-year range genuinely runs from a credit accident that halves realized earnings power (super-bear IV $35.47, −70%) to a decade of guide-beating compounding ($155.90). Consequence per the rules: confidence is low (already capped by the sector flag) and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note even the bull case (25% weight) yields only 14.3%, below the 15% hurdle.
- No BASE_DIVERGENCE flag fired on the final run because the normalized OCF median is negative (the flag requires a positive median) — the underlying divergence is the largest in the sweep so far (latest engine OE/share $8.91 vs median −$0.76 vs the $5.17 base used) and is documented in full above rather than waved off. No HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag fired (engine net debt is null for this filer; see the balance-sheet note). Share-count growth of +2.5%/yr did not trip the 3% DILUTION flag but is real (equity comp, AOG exchanges, GCP stock consideration) and is already deducted inside g_base.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** on the FY2025 realized base alone ($4.76, no TTM roll) the weighted IV@15% would scale to ~$77.50 and the weighted IRR to roughly 11% — still Tier C. On the engine's raw latest-FY OCF base ($8.91) the model would print a spurious high-teens / Tier B answer, and on its median OCF base it prints F; both are rejected for the reasons above. Every defensible variant on the manager's own cash-earnings definition is C. The final number uses the most current defensible base (TTM realized), so if anything it is the generous end of the defensible range.
- Quality is not the debate: FRE is recurring, growing at +20%+, and covers the business through a fundraising winter in a way carried interest never did; $79B of not-yet-fee-paying AUM is a visible future fee pipeline; the dividend was raised +20% to $1.35/quarter. The debate is price: at 22.9× TTM realized earnings the buyer is pre-paying for the bull path.
- Red flags to monitor: private-credit defaults / non-accruals and spread compression as Blackstone / Apollo / Blue Owl compete for the same loans; fundraising cyclicality if institutional allocators pause; realized-income lumpiness if exit markets close (the European-waterfall catch-up is back-ended by the company's own guidance); GCP contingent consideration and integration; regulatory scrutiny of private credit and of affiliated insurance float (Aspida); and continued ~2.5%/yr share-count creep.
- **Hurdle math:** the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$84** (weighted IV@15%) on the TTM realized base — i.e. it needs to fall ~29% from $118.15 to clear; with a 20% margin of safety the buy price is ≈ **$67**. At today's price it clears 15% only in the 10%-weight super-bull world.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: C** (= tier by numbers, no override — but soft: sector-model mismatch, low confidence)
- Confidence: **Low** (SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH + WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD; the direction — fully priced on its own cash-earnings definition — is more robust than the level)
- One-liner: Scaled private-credit-led alt manager, moat 6/10 — the engine's Tier F is a consolidated-fund OCF artifact (median OE −$0.76/sh rejected); on TTM after-tax realized earnings of $5.17/share the $118.15 price (22.9×) yields an 11.9% weighted IRR with IV@15% of $84.17, and it clears 15% only at ~$84 (~$67 with a margin of safety).
