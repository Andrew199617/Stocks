## What it is
A diversified regulated utility holding company: 10 electric and gas utilities in five Canadian provinces, ten U.S. states and the Caribbean — ITC (U.S. transmission), UNS Energy (Arizona), Central Hudson (New York), FortisBC (gas + electric), FortisAlberta, Newfoundland Power, Maritime Electric, FortisOntario, Caribbean Utilities. Essentially all earnings are regulated. FY2025 revenue $12B CAD and total assets $75B CAD per the FY2025 annual report; the growth engine is a $28.8B 2026–2030 capital plan expected to lift midyear rate base from $42.4B (2025) to $57.9B (2030), a ~7% CAGR, supporting guided 4–6% annual dividend growth through 2030 after 52 consecutive years of dividend increases.

That overstates FY2024, whose disclosed total was C$82M (PSU C$53M + RSU C$29M, same Note 20) — the error runs in the conservative direction (more SBC → lower OE) and is immaterial against a base that is negative by over a billion dollars a year.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow (regulated franchise, capped returns)
Justification: exclusive regulated service territories and a visible rate-base growth runway are durable, but regulators cap the return on that rate base and can reset it downward — there is no pricing power beyond the allowed ROE.
- FOR: regulated franchise monopolies — exclusive electric/gas service territories across five Canadian provinces, ten U.S. states and the Caribbean; visible rate-base growth — $28.8B 2026–2030 capital plan driving ~7% midyear rate-base CAGR ($42.4B 2025 to $57.9B 2030 guided); diversification across 10 utilities and many regulators, so no single rate case decides the company; 52 consecutive years of dividend increases with 4–6% growth guided through 2030; essential-service, low-cyclicality demand — H1 2026 net earnings C$897M, up from H1 2025, on rate-base growth.
- AGAINST: returns capped at regulator-allowed ROEs — FERC's October 2024 MISO base ROE decision cut ITC retroactively (a $20M drag on 2024 results); regulatory lag — costs of rate-base growth not yet in customer rates already pressure UNS Energy earnings; heavy leverage (net debt $24.47B) with rate-sensitive funding costs; perpetual DRIP equity issuance dilutes (~+1.7%/yr share count); multi-jurisdiction political/regulatory exposure plus CAD/USD translation exposure.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection. (Moot here — the scenario machinery never engages, see DCF.)

## My thoughts / overlay
- Fortis is the sweep's cleanest example of a model/company mismatch: a genuinely high-quality, diversified regulated utility whose owner's earnings are negative in every filed year because it is mid-supercycle on rate-base investment ($28.8B five-year plan). On this screen there is no price that clears 15% — with a negative OE base, no entry price produces a positive OE-DCF value, so no hurdle-clearing price exists to quote.
- What the F does NOT say: that the dividend is unsafe or the equity is distressed. Earnings and the dividend are backed by regulated rate base, and H1 2026 printed net earnings growth (C$897M, up $14M vs the first half of 2025, per the Q2 release) with the capital plan on track. An income investor underwriting 4–6% dividend growth plus allowed-ROE earnings is playing a different game than this 15%-hurdle OE screen.
- Real risks to that other game, for the record: allowed-ROE compression (the MISO precedent), regulatory lag at UNS, the funding load of the plan (net debt $24.47B and rising), DRIP dilution, and CAD/USD.
- Method range: none to report — every defensible input set on the conservative definition gives a negative base and Tier F; the only alternative lens (maintenance OE) is positive but is not the screen's base definition, so it informs the narrative, not the tier.
