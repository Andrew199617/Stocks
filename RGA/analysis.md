## Moat: 6/10 — moderate, real but not wide
Justification: a concentrated life/health reinsurance oligopoly where RGA is the leading pure-play, built on decades of biometric data, underwriting expertise, A+ rated balance-sheet capacity and long-duration in-force treaties that switch slowly — against no structural pricing power over mortality/longevity outcomes, capacity competition on large asset-intensive blocks including from PE-backed rivals, structural earnings volatility from biometric shocks and remeasurements, and capital/ratings constraints.
- FOR: concentrated global life/health reinsurance oligopoly — few scaled rivals (Munich Re, Swiss Re, Hannover Re) and high balance-sheet/rating barriers to entry; decades of proprietary biometric data and underwriting expertise — the core input for pricing mortality/longevity risk cannot be bought quickly; long-duration in-force treaties and embedded client relationships — reinsurance programs renew with incumbents and switch slowly; diversified global platform (U.S./LatAm, Canada, EMEA, Asia Pacific plus financial solutions) — regional and product diversification dampens any single mortality pool.
- AGAINST: no pricing power over biometric outcomes — mortality/longevity experience and assumption updates drive earnings, not RGA's price list; large asset-intensive blocks are capacity-competed, including by private-equity-backed reinsurers with cheaper asset origination; earnings volatility is structural — COVID-era mortality, remeasurement gains/losses and investment marks swing GAAP results year to year; capital and ratings hostage — growth is capped by insurance capital rules and the need to defend the A+ financial strength rating.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the headline, as with APP and PFG:** on the median-of-3-FY base the same inputs printed tier S (weighted IRR 30.959579312335983%, IV@15% $663.0112342364839 in the preliminary final-form run) because the median year is FY2025's $60.16467065868263 and FY2024's spike sits in the window; on the TTM base actually used, tier A by numbers (23.916478746749326%); on any earnings-based lens (FY2025 net income $1,182,000,000, maintenance OE $1,225,000,000) the name screens lower still. The defensible range is therefore B-or-lower on earnings lenses to A on the TTM cash-flow base, with the S print resting entirely on the rejected-for-an-insurer median-OCF definition; the verdict is sized to the conservative end, B — the same one-notch insurer-float softening applied to PFG/HIG/ACGL/WRB, here applied to the A.
- **Insurer caveat on this base (the controlling judgment in this file):** for a life reinsurer, OCF runs structurally above net income because premiums are collected in cash while reserve builds for assumed blocks are charged to earnings unpaid, and OCF carries float that belongs to future policyholders. That makes even the TTM OE/share base ($43.86363636363637) an upper bound on distributable earnings, not earning power — GAAP diluted EPS and adjusted operating EPS run at a per-share level far below this base (Q2 2026 adjusted operating income was $8.89 per diluted share for the quarter, per the company's release). The engine's numbers are therefore treated as an upper-bound method, and the final tier is softened one notch rather than the base being re-pinned by hand.
- Quality is not the debate: OE positive in all of the last 5 fiscal years on the conservative definition, a leading pure-play franchise in a concentrated oligopoly, adjusted operating ROE of 17.4% TTM, a dividend raised 5.4% for Q3 2026 and $50,000,000 of Q2 buybacks (company release, search results). The debate is the entry multiple for a biometric-risk business whose cash conversion is dominated by block timing.
- Red flags to monitor: mortality/longevity assumption updates at the annual review, credit losses in a $100B-plus investment portfolio in a downturn, the pace and pricing of asset-intensive block deals (each one mechanically inflates OCF and OE), PE-backed reinsurance capacity competing spreads down, and H1-style negative operating cash flow persisting into full years.
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine's numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at $483.37476737035706 (the weighted IV@15%) and trades 81.24967841702242% below that figure at $266.69 — but that clearing price inherits the float-inflated base, so it overstates the true margin of safety; a margin-of-safety buy price would need to sit materially below the weighted IV@15%, in words, and no hand-computed figure is quoted for it. It clears 20% at $333.64459420690486 and does not clear 25% (IV@25% $252.46029561250825 is below the price). On the conservative (final-tier B) reading, the honest claim is "clears 15% with room on engine numbers, sized like a B", not "81% upside".
