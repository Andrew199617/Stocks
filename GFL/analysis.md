## What it is
The fourth-largest diversified environmental services company in North America: solid-waste collection, transfer, landfilling and recycling across Canada and 19 U.S. states, ~17,000 employees. Built by founder/CEO Patrick Dovigi as an acquisition roll-up. In March 2025 it sold its Environmental Services business (see basis break below) and is now essentially a pure solid-waste company; on September 1, 2026 — after the fiscal year-end in the engine data — it closed the acquisition of SECURE Waste Infrastructure Corp., financed partly with 75,126,306 newly issued subordinate voting shares and a new US$1B senior secured term loan (per the company's 6-K report of the closing). Q2 2026 also brought unsolicited take-private interest and a special committee; that is an overlay, not a DCF input.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: North America's fourth-largest diversified environmental services company: route density in its collection markets, scarce permitted landfill assets, and long-term municipal/commercial contracts give real pricing power (core pricing +6.1% in Q2 2026) and moderate switching friction — but collection itself has low entry barriers, Waste Management / Republic Services / Waste Connections run larger and more profitable networks, growth has been bought via a debt-funded roll-up rather than earned organically (organic growth was 6.4% of Q2 2026's 16.3%), and the fleet/landfill base demands heavy, perpetual capex that consumes nearly all operating cash flow.
- FOR: Route density in collection markets — dense routes lower cost per stop and are hard for entrants to replicate profitably; scarce permitted landfill and transfer-station assets (new landfill permits are extremely difficult to obtain); long-term municipal and commercial contracts with demonstrated pricing power (core pricing +6.1% in Q2 2026); scale as the fourth-largest diversified environmental services company in North America.
- AGAINST: Larger, more profitable rivals (Waste Management, Republic Services, Waste Connections) with stronger margins and balance sheets; growth is acquisition-led (9.9pp of Q2 2026's 16.3% revenue growth came from M&A) with integration/overpayment risk on every deal (SECURE included); capex intensity ($833.6M of capex against $961.2M of FY2025 operating cash flow); leverage and dual-class founder control (net debt $5.36B, engine figure, pre-SECURE; founder/CEO holds outsized voting power via multiple voting shares); recycled-commodity and diesel exposure, with volumes a slight drag (−0.6pp) in Q2 2026.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py was run and cannot compute a TTM for this IFRS filer (message quoted verbatim under Owner's earnings) — the expected us-gaap-only limitation, so the engine's median-of-3-FY base is used, per spec. Post-FY-end prints (Q2 2026, quoted above) were checked and do not change the OE structure.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real — conservative OE swung from −$150.6M (2023) to +$168.5M (2024) to +$16.9M (2025) as capex and SBC moved against a ~$1B OCF base. The median base already damps this, and the verdict is insensitive to weighting: even a 100% super-bull run yields a 0.10% IRR, far below the 10% D/C line.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): the gaps are net-income-side, not OCF-side — OCF was comparatively stable ($739,888,272 / $1,070,454,402 / $961,153,760) while NI swung ($24,300,696 / −$512,708,877 / $2,785,154,824). The dominant 2025 item is the Environmental Services divestiture: closed March 1, 2025 at an $8.0B enterprise value to Apollo and BC Partners funds, with GFL retaining a ~44% equity stake initially recognized at $1.7B and a pre-tax gain on divestiture of ~$4.5B recorded in discontinued operations — a non-operating gain that inflates NI but never touches operating cash flow. The 2023–24 gaps are the mirror image (NI depressed by non-cash charges and discontinued-operations results while OCF stayed positive). The OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is therefore the cleaner series, and the base is not contaminated by the gain.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: reporting currency CAD converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (frankfurter.app); flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average — a documented simplification. The rates used are recorded in dcf_numbers.json (FY2025: 0.73036).
- **ADS_RATIO_MISSING**: resolved in judgment.json with ads_ratio 1.0 — GFL is a direct-listed foreign private issuer: the NYSE security (symbol GFL) is the subordinate voting share itself, the same class that trades on the TSX; there is no depositary/ADS program. Source and reason are recorded in the judgment.json ads_ratio field.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Utilities): the universe classifies GFL as Utilities / Environmental Services. Waste collection/landfilling is an industrial route-and-asset business, not a regulated utility, but it shares the utility problem for this model — perpetual heavy capex against regulated-like pricing — so confidence is graded low per the flag, without forcing the tier.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (base OE yield 0.10%): accepted as the finding, not a modeling inconvenience — at $0.0446 of normalized OE per share against a $43.55 price, essentially the entire market value rests on growth and on adjustments (e.g. treating much capex as "growth") that this standardized model deliberately does not make.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.48x): real, and confidence is low — though here the spread runs from $0.21 to $0.94 of IV@15%, i.e. every scenario is ~99% below the price, so the spread does not threaten the tier.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 317.6x normalized OE): net debt is $5,358,651,320 (dcf_numbers.json) — the multiple is extreme because normalized OE is near zero, not because the absolute debt is unusual for the sector; still, the balance sheet is levered, and it got more so after FY-end: the SECURE closing (Sep 1, 2026) added a US$1B term loan alongside 75,126,306 new shares, neither of which is in the engine's FY2025 base or share count. The ES-sale proceeds (up to $3.75B earmarked for debt repayment per the January 2025 announcement) explain the deleveraging into FY2025; SECURE re-levers it.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the gap between the two languages this company is priced in. On Adjusted EBITDA (Q2 2026 margin 30.4%) and "Adjusted Free Cash Flow" ($236.7M in Q2 2026), GFL looks like a compounding waste franchise. On standardized owner's earnings — OCF minus *all* capex minus SBC — it earned $16.9M in FY2025 on a $16.49B market cap. A bespoke model that splits maintenance from growth capex would land somewhere between those poles, but the maintenance share would have to be implausibly small to close a ~99% gap to the weighted IV@15%.
- Basis break (not engine-flagged, identified in research): Environmental Services was reclassified to discontinued operations around its March 2025 sale, so FY2024–25 revenue ($4,266,527,388 / $4,831,988,724) is continuing-operations while earlier years are total-company — that is why revenue "fell" in 2024 and why the 3-yr revenue CAGR (−1.09%) is not a demand signal. Growth inputs above were set on the continuing-operations prints, not across the break.
- Post-FY-end events cut both ways and are not in the base: SECURE adds scale (and 75.1M shares of dilution plus US$1B of debt); reported take-private interest at a premium (special committee formed in Q2 2026, no assurance of a transaction) could deliver value the DCF cannot see. Neither rescues an owner's-earnings case at $43.55; both argue for low confidence rather than a tier change.
- Margin of safety: none on this model. The price that clears the 15% hurdle is the weighted IV@15% itself, ~$0.49 — roughly 99% below the current price.
