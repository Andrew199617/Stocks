## What it is
The world's largest pest-control company (Rentokil brand globally, Terminix in North America since the December 2022 acquisition) plus a Hygiene & Wellbeing division, operating in 80+ countries on a route-based, recurring-contract service model. Pest Control revenue was $5,703M of FY2025's $6,908M total (company preliminary results). New CEO Mike Duffy succeeded Andy Ransom in 2026; activist Trian is on the board. Portfolio is being simplified: France Workwear was sold in 2025 and SOLitude Lake Management was agreed for sale in September 2026 for $230M.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
- FOR: Global #1 pest-control scale — route density and national-account coverage in 80+ countries; recurring contracted service revenue (route-based, repeat-visit model) with pricing power through the cycle; the Rentokil + Terminix brand pair in North America, the world's largest pest-control market; H1 2026 proof the franchise still grows (organic revenue +3.6%, adjusted operating profit +6.6%, FCF conversion 96%).
- AGAINST: ROIC collapsed post-Terminix and is still falling (below); Rollins out-executes in North America (~22.4% margins vs Rentokil's ~15.5% group adjusted margin, per Morgan Stanley's September 2026 downgrade note) while local/regional rivals and PE-backed consolidators take share; low switching costs in residential pest control and structural technician churn; the Terminix integration ran years late and the North America >20% margin target for 2027 was withdrawn; deal leverage (net debt $3,575M at H1 2026, 2.4x EBITDA) is only just back inside the 2.0–2.5x target range.
- Evidence (step 5): fundamentals.json `roic_proxy` — 21.0% (FY2020), 12.2% (FY2021), 3.2% (FY2022, the Terminix deal year), 6.5% (FY2023), 5.5% (FY2024), 5.0% (FY2025). A moat of 7+ cannot sit on ROIC that has fallen for four straight years to ~5%; 5/10 prices the real route-density/recurring-revenue franchise against returns that have not yet covered the cost of the capital deployed. Gross margin is not presented in the IFRS filings (fundamentals.json gross_margin is null for every year), so the ROIC series is the margin evidence; the adjusted operating margin trend points the same way (H1 2026 15.5%).
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 9.6 months ago): TTM was attempted — ttm.py exits 3 for this IFRS filer (US-GAAP facts only), so no TTM base exists and the FY2023–25 median base is used, per spec. The H1 2026 interim cross-check above (organic +3.6%, adjusted OP +6.6%, FCF +12.8%, FY2026 guidance in line) shows the median is not stale in a way that changes the tier: even the latest-FY base ($1.33/ADS vs the $1.23 median) leaves every scenario far below the price.
- **DILUTION** (share count +8.0%/yr): an artefact of the Terminix stock consideration, not ongoing dilution. financials_history.csv: diluted shares 1,866M (FY2021) → 2,014M (FY2022) → 2,527M (FY2023) on the deal's ~643M new shares, then essentially flat — 2,527M / 2,528M / 2,535M across FY2023–25. SBC is small and stable ($34.0M / $25.0M / $28.0M, FY2023–25, ~3% of OCF). The engine's −5pp growth adjustment penalizes revenue components for a one-off deal issuance; scenario starts were set from revenue prints instead (above), so the artefact does not drive the verdict.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income vs OCF differ >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): from the CSV — FY2023 NI $474M vs OCF $916M; FY2024 NI $392M vs OCF $868M; FY2025 NI $470M vs OCF $972M. The gap is non-cash charges sitting between the two lines, principally depreciation and amortisation (including Terminix intangible amortisation): the engine's `da` tag is null for this IFRS filer so it cannot net the gap itself, but the H1 2026 interim report shows the same structure (EBITDA $745M vs statutory operating profit $349M / adjusted $556M). There is no single one-off year — the divergence is structural for this filer, it is stable in size, and the OE base is built from OCF directly, so it does not distort the base beyond the acquired-intangibles caveat noted under Owner's earnings.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: reporting currency is GBP; dcf_numbers.json records conversion at period-end ECB reference rates for the GBP-filed years. Flow facts at period-end rather than period-average rates is a small distortion next to the ~48% gap between price and IV@15%.
- **USD_TRANSLATIONS_USED**: for FY2023–25 the engine used the issuer's own filed USD convenience translations (financials_history.csv figures are the USD figures from the 20-F filed 2026-03-25) — the most recent three years, which are the base years, carry no worker-side FX math at all.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: resolved by the judgment.json `ads_ratio` declaration (ratio 5.0, kind filing-equivalent per step 8(d)): the 20-F for FY2025 (filed 2026-03-25) states each ADS represents five ordinary shares under the 2018 deposit agreement. Effect: base OE/share $0.25 per ordinary share → $1.23 per ADS, market cap $53.742B → $10.748B, weighted IRR 4.06% → 9.15%. The tier is D both ways; the preliminary run's 4.06% was an ADS-mismatch artefact and is superseded.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 5.22x normalized OE): balance sheet from the filing (CSV latest row): debt current $1,411M + debt LT $4,156M − cash $2,319M = net debt $3,248M, plus lease liabilities $563M recorded separately. On the company's own metric, leverage was 2.4x EBITDA at H1 2026 (net debt $3,575M), back inside the 2.0–2.5x target range for the first time since Terminix, helped by the France Workwear proceeds; the September 2026 SOLitude sale ($230M) deleverages further. The bear cases are already stressed (OE mults 0.75/0.90, super-bear growth −2.0%) and still clear 6–7.6% IRR — leverage makes the equity fragile, it does not rescue the valuation.
- **TIER_BAND** (bear tier D at 7.57% vs bull tier C at 10.50%): the verdict is sized to the bear end — Tier D. Even the bull case (10.50%) and the super bull (13.16%) sit below the 15% hurdle at this price, so the band does not straddle any buy decision.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the distance, not the precision: at $21.20 the ADS prices in a North America recovery that even the bull case (7% growth, 1.10x OE) does not deliver at a 15% return — bull IV@15% is $13.32. The stock has already fallen hard in London (GBp 299.20 on 2026-10-05 vs a 52-week high of GBp 506.80, per press reports), and it still does not clear.
- Method range: latest-FY base instead of the median, or the engine's unadjusted 5.86% blend instead of the recalibrated starts, both move the weighted IRR up by roughly a point at most — Tier D is robust to every defensible input choice tried; the ADS correction was the only input that moved the answer materially (4.06% → 9.15%), and it moved it toward the price, not past it.
- What would change the answer: two or three halves of North America organic growth at Rollins-like rates with margins rebuilding, bought back stock at these levels shrinking the count (the count is flat, not shrinking — no buyback tailwind is credited), or a price near $11.
- Confidence is low: IFRS/TTM unavailable, the base is a 3-year median across a portfolio that is still being reshaped (France Workwear out, SOLitude going), and the acquired-intangibles caveat on OE is unquantified. Low confidence does not soften the verdict — the gap to the hurdle is ~48% of price.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (weighted IRR 9.15%; bear 7.57% / bull 10.50%; sized to the bear end per TIER_BAND)
