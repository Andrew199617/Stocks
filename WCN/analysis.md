# WCN — Waste Connections, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. In scope: market cap ~$39.0B (universe) / ~$39.7B at the engine's $153.71 price — just under the $40B cutoff.

## What it is
North America's third-largest solid waste company (after Waste Management and Republic Services): non-hazardous waste collection, transfer and disposal (including by rail), recycling/resource recovery and renewable fuels (landfill-gas RNG), serving ~9 million residential/commercial/industrial customers across 46 U.S. states and 6 Canadian provinces, mostly in exclusive and secondary markets. Plus E&P (oilfield) waste treatment/disposal and Pacific Northwest intermodal. Q2 2026 revenue mix: collection ~70%, disposal/transfer ~18%, E&P waste ~8%, recycling ~2.4%, intermodal/other ~2%. Asset-heavy and acquisition-driven: growth = core pricing (~+5.5-6.5%/yr) + tuck-in M&A, with volumes currently slightly negative.

## Moat score: 7/10 — strong
**FOR:**
- Permitted landfill airspace is a scarce, effectively irreplaceable asset — new landfill permits are nearly impossible to obtain, and WCN keeps internalizing disposal (internalization 59% in Q2 2026, targeting low-mid-60s%), capturing the disposal margin itself instead of paying WM/Republic.
- Route density + exclusive/secondary-market positioning: dense collection routes feeding owned transfer/disposal is a local cost advantage that compounds with every tuck-in.
- Proven pricing power through cycles: core price +6.5% in FY2025, +5.8% H1 2026, held above cost inflation — industry-leading adjusted EBITDA margin (33% FY2025, 32.8% Q2 2026) is the receipt.
- Long-duration municipal/franchise contracts (incl. maximum allowed zones in NYC's new commercial franchise system, converting by 2028) give revenue visibility rivals can't bid away overnight.
- Disciplined M&A machine in a fragmented industry: decades of accretive tuck-ins, and the balance sheet to keep doing them.
**AGAINST:**
- No network effects and no lock-in beyond contract terms — contracts rebid, and WM and Republic are larger, equally disciplined bidders for the same assets and routes.
- The moat asset is also the liability: landfills carry closure/post-closure and environmental obligations (Chiquita Canyon, below) that can convert "scarce asset" into open-ended cost.
- Volumes are not growing (unit volume −1.7% H1 2026); growth is price + M&A, both of which have limits — price pushes churn eventually, M&A multiples have risen.
- Recycled commodity prices and E&P waste volumes are cyclical drags management cannot control (both were headwinds in 2025-26).
- Capital intensity is real and rising (RNG build-out, land purchases): OE conversion of EBITDA is only ~35%.
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 7 = **11.1 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to 2.5% terminal over the CAP, 15-yr projection).

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override above — TTM OE $4.53/share built from Q2 2026 10-Q YTD OCF/capex/SBC, replacing the $4.35 median. TTM is essentially flat vs FY2025, so staleness was not hiding growth; it was hiding a capex surge.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 5.2× super-bear):** Real but mechanical at a 2.9% OE yield: when the base cash flow is this small vs price, plausible growth paths fan out enormously in IV terms. It caps confidence at medium. Critically, the spread does not straddle the hurdle — even the super-bull IV ($105.57) is 31% below the price, so the spread creates no tier ambiguity.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (NI vs OCF >40% apart in 2023, 2024, 2025):** Resolved — structural, not a one-off distorting the OE base. (1) D&A is $1.00B / $1.16B / $1.23B in 2023/24/25 against NI of $0.76B / $0.62B / $1.08B: a landfill/truck fleet this size mechanically makes OCF ~2× NI. The audit refinement (ignore D&A-explained gaps) did not suppress the flag here because the gap exceeds even D&A in 2024. (2) The 2024 excess is identified: Chiquita Canyon closure — a $480.8M increase to the closure/post-closure liability plus a $116.1M landfill impairment (10-K), non-cash charges that crushed 2024 NI ($617.6M) while OCF held at $2,228.9M. 2025 impairments were smaller ($109.7M net: $67.4M environmental liability at an operating facility, $30.0M contract/permit write-offs, $12.7M disposal losses) and 2026 H1 added another $138.0M of closure-cost adjustments. Because the conservative OE base is built from OCF − capex − SBC, these non-cash charges do not inflate it; if anything the recurring cadence of "one-off" closure charges argues the base is not understated.
- **Balance-sheet data gap (no flag fired — engine issue, recorded here):** the engine returned net_debt = null (debt tags missing for 2023-25 in its series). From the Q2 2026 balance sheet: debt $8.09M current + $9,283.8M long-term = $9,291.9M, cash $98.2M → **net debt ~$9,193.7M ≈ 8.0× TTM OE, but only ~2.8× guided EBITDA** — standard investment-grade waste leverage (debt/book cap 54%). The OE stream here is an equity cash flow (OCF is after interest), so debt is not subtracted again; the leverage is a risk overlay, not a valuation deduction. It does mean equity OE is the residual after a large, permanent capex + interest bill — another reason the 34× multiple is unforgiving.

## Thoughts / overlay
- This is a genuinely good business at a price that pays for perfection: pricing power, scarce landfills, disciplined M&A, industry-leading margins — and a 2.9% owner's-earnings yield. At $153.71 the market is capitalizing WCN like a compounder that will grow OE at double digits for a decade; its own TTM OE is flat and its 3-yr OE/share CAGR is 1.5%.
- **Method range:** preliminary run (median base $4.35, moat 5) → weighted IV@15% $47.58, IRR 6.6%. Final (TTM base, moat 7) → $52.05, 7.1%. Aggressive variant (guided FY26 OE ~$5.20/share as base) → ~$60, still Tier D. Every defensible input set lands in D; there is no tier ambiguity to size around.
- Red flags, honestly sized: Chiquita Canyon is the big one — closed 12/31/2024, an ongoing elevated-temperature event, ~9,200 civil lawsuits plus an LA County suit per reporting on the Q1 2025 filing, $100-150M of closure outlays guided inside 2026 OCF, and $138M more closure adjustments in H1 2026. It has not broken the cash machine (OCF grew through it), but the tail is unquantifiable and it is the template for landfill-liability risk across the asset base. Lesser flags: unit volumes negative, recycled commodity values at multiyear lows, fuel-cost spikes (Q2 2026 margin pressure), acquisition-price discipline as the obvious growth lever gets more competitive.
- SBC/dilution: clean. SBC $79M TTM, share count now shrinking (record H1 2026 buybacks, $614.5M) — buybacks at 34× OE are, however, a questionable use of that cash.
- **Hurdle math:** the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is **$52.05**; with a 20% margin of safety, **~$42**. Even the super-bull case only justifies $105.57. The gap is not a close call.
