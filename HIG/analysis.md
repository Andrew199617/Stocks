# HIG — The Hartford Insurance Group, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Finance / Property & Casualty Insurance. Price $131.14 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap $34.97B in universe.csv (engine intraday estimate $37.57B on 286.5M shares — in scope either way, under $40B).

## What it is
Hartford, Connecticut P&C and employee-benefits insurer founded in 1810. Three engines: Business Insurance (the franchise piece — a top small-commercial and workers' compensation writer, plus middle/large and Global Specialty), Personal Insurance (anchored by the exclusive AARP auto/home program), and Employee Benefits (group life/disability/supplemental; a top-tier carrier, acquiring Equitable's Employee Benefits business — ~$500M of premium, ~300 employees and its tech platform — expected to close Q4 2026). Hartford Funds (mutual funds) was sold to Wellington Management in 2026 and sits in discontinued operations — a non-core exit. FY2025 total revenue was $28.37B (+6.9%), net income $3.84B. Q2 2026: revenue $7.26B (+8.1% YoY), core earnings $945M / $3.42 per diluted share (+1% / +6% YoY), trailing core ROE 18.7%, book value per share ex-AOCI $78.91 (+7% from year-end, +15% YoY). Q3 2026 reports Oct 26, 2026.

## Owner's earnings — the engine's OCF base is rejected for an insurer, and why
- **Engine conservative OE (OCF − capex − SBC):** FY2025 $5.753B = $20.08/share; 3-yr median base $19.30/share. Two mechanical problems on top of the sector problem: SBC reads $0 because Hartford tags it as AllocatedShareBasedCompensationExpense ($141M in FY2025 — real, ~2.4% of OCF, a small overstatement), and the revenue tag captures only the $1.51B fee/other-income slice (RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax) of a $28.37B revenue company, so the engine's ~2% revenue CAGRs are artifacts — actual revenue compounded +6.1%/yr FY2021-25 and +8.2%/yr FY2022-25.
- **Why OCF is not distributable earnings here:** for a growing P&C insurer, OCF runs structurally ~$1.7-2.8B/yr *above* net income (2023: $4.220B vs $2.504B; 2024: $5.909B vs $3.111B; 2025: $5.922B vs $3.836B) because premiums are collected in cash while loss reserves and unearned premiums — money owed to future claimants — accrue unpaid. That float growth is a liability build, not owner cash. A DCF capitalizing it manufactures value (see method range below).
- **TTM OCF override — computed, then rejected:** TTM OCF = FY2025 $5,922M − H1 2025 YTD $2,276M + H1 2026 YTD $2,234M = **$5,880M**; TTM capex = FY $169M − H1'25 $78M + H1'26 −$84M (the tagged line went negative in H1 2026 — proceeds exceeded purchases; capex is immaterial for an insurer either way) = $7M; less ~$141M/yr SBC ≈ **$5,732M ÷ 278.1M diluted shares (H1 2026 YTD, EDGAR) ≈ $20.61/share**. Rejected: same float/reserve artifact as the median base, ~42% above core earnings power.
- **Base used — TTM core earnings per share (Hartford's own adjusted definition: excludes net realized gains/losses and other non-recurring items):** FY2025 $13.42 − H1 2025 $5.44 (Q1 $2.20 + Q2 $3.24) + H1 2026 $6.51 (Q1 $3.09 + Q2 $3.42) = **$14.49/share**. Cross-checks: the same TTM build in dollars — FY2025 core $3.8B − H1'25 $1,571M ($639M + $932M) + H1'26 $1,811M ($866M + $945M) ≈ $4.04B ≈ $14.5/share on 278.1M shares. TTM GAAP EPS is higher (~$15.7) only because Q2 2026 net income carries a $318M discontinued-operations item (the Hartford Funds disposal) that core correctly strips. Price = 9.0× the TTM core base and 1.66× BV/share ex-AOCI ($78.91). OE yield on the used base: 11.0%. One forward caveat: the divested Hartford Funds segment contributed ~$51M of core earnings in Q1 2026 (~$0.70/share annualized); an ex-Funds forward base is ~$13.8 — that variant still lands in Tier B (see overlay).
- Balance sheet (folded in explicitly): the engine's "net debt" $4.371B is just gross corporate debt (its cash tag came back null) against $85.997B of total assets / $18.979B of equity at FY2025 — for an insurer the real balance sheet is the investment portfolio against loss reserves, and the risk is reserve adequacy and asset marks, not the holding-company debt load (net debt / normalized OE 0.79× on the engine base, 1.05× on the core base — modest either way).

## Moat score: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 5) = **8.5 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Business Insurance specialization that is genuinely hard to replicate — leading small-commercial position (Q2 2026 written premiums +5%, small business +7% at an 86.5 underlying combined ratio) and workers' comp scale/expertise built over decades of agency relationships. (2) The exclusive, decades-long AARP personal-lines program — a distribution asset no rival can bid away quickly — now producing an 86.3 underlying combined ratio after the personal-lines turnaround. (3) Employee Benefits scale (top-tier group benefits; the Equitable deal adds ~$500M premium and a modern platform). (4) Demonstrated underwriting discipline through the cycle: underlying combined ratios in the high-80s across segments and core ROE of 18.7-20.3% TTM — returns most P&C peers don't sustain.
- **AGAINST:** (1) The products themselves are price-competed commodities — commercial and personal P&C is shopped on price against Travelers, Chubb, Progressive and regionals; Hartford has cycle-following pricing, not pricing power. (2) Catastrophe and loss-cost inflation exposure is structural and rising (Q1 2026 CAT losses $230M even in a *light* quarter vs $467M a year earlier). (3) Legacy long-tail liabilities keep biting: an A&E (asbestos & environmental) reserve charge in FY2025 and a $70M general-liability reserve increase in Q1 2026 for 1970s-80s sexual-abuse exposures — social inflation is a permanent tax on this book. (4) Earnings are partly hostage to investment markets: Q2 2026 net investment income rose 22% YoY largely on limited-partnership marks, which reverse. That is a 5: a real, durable franchise in chosen niches, capped by commodity products and tail risk.

## Thoughts / overlay

That A is an artifact of capitalizing float growth and is rejected. - Quality is not the debate: high-80s underlying combined ratios, ~19-20% core ROE, BV/share ex-AOCI compounding +13-15%/yr, disciplined capital return. The debate is the entry multiple for a commodity-product insurer mid-hard-market, with CAT and legacy-reserve tails.
