## What it is
Hartford, Connecticut P&C and employee-benefits insurer founded in 1810. Three engines: Business Insurance (the franchise piece — a leading small-commercial and workers' compensation writer, plus middle/large and Global Specialty), Personal Insurance (anchored by the exclusive AARP auto/home program), and Employee Benefits (group life/disability/supplemental at top-tier scale). Hartford Funds (mutual funds) was sold to Wellington Management in 2026 as a non-core exit. Latest quarter Q2 2026 (reported Jul 23, 2026): revenue $7.26B (+8.1% YoY), core earnings $945M / $3.42 per diluted share (+5.6% YoY per share), trailing core ROE 18.7%, book value per share ex-AOCI $78.91. Q3 2026 reports Oct 26, 2026. Sell-side consensus expects FY2026 EPS to decline ~4.6% to ~$12.80 (Hartford Funds earnings leaving, CAT/prior-year-development normalization).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: a real, durable franchise in chosen niches — leading small-commercial/workers' comp specialization, the exclusive decades-long AARP personal-lines distribution, top-tier Employee Benefits scale, high-80s underlying combined ratios and 18.7% trailing core ROE — against price-competed commodity P&C products with no structural pricing power, rising catastrophe exposure, recurring legacy long-tail reserve charges, and earnings partly hostage to investment/limited-partnership marks.
- FOR: Business Insurance specialization that is hard to replicate — Q2 2026 written premiums +5%, Small Business +7% at an 86.5 underlying combined ratio, Global Specialty underlying combined ratio 85.8; decades of agency relationships in small commercial and workers' comp.
- FOR: The exclusive, decades-long AARP personal-lines program — a distribution asset no rival can bid away quickly — now producing an 86.3 underlying combined ratio after the personal-lines turnaround.
- FOR: Employee Benefits scale (top-tier group life/disability/supplemental carrier).
- FOR: Demonstrated underwriting discipline through the cycle — underlying combined ratios in the high-80s across segments and trailing core ROE of 18.7% (Q2 2026), returns most P&C peers do not sustain.
- AGAINST: The products are price-competed commodities — commercial and personal P&C is shopped on price against Travelers, Chubb, Progressive and regionals; Hartford has cycle-following pricing, not pricing power.
- AGAINST: Catastrophe and loss-cost inflation exposure is structural and rising — CAT losses were $748M in 2025 and $768M in 2024, and $222M in Q2 2026 alone.
- AGAINST: Legacy long-tail liabilities keep biting — Q2 2026 saw general-liability and commercial-auto reserve increases, and the NICO asbestos & environmental adverse-development-cover arbitration (NICO has suspended payments, with a reported $814M of recognized deferred gains at risk) is a live balance-sheet uncertainty.
- AGAINST: Earnings are partly hostage to investment markets — Q2 2026 net investment income rose 22% YoY to $800M largely on limited-partnership marks, which reverse.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the headline, as with APP:** the engine's raw OCF-median base produces Tier A (weighted IRR 20.31593138371507%). Every earnings-based way of looking at this insurer — core earnings per share, book value against core ROE — has consistently landed a notch lower, in B territory, because the OCF base capitalizes float/reserve growth that belongs to future claimants. The prior v4 pass, on an earnings-based base, also landed at Tier B. The defensible method range here is therefore B–A, with the A end resting entirely on the rejected-for-an-insurer cash-flow definition; the verdict is sized to the conservative end, B.
- Quality is not the debate: high-80s underlying combined ratios, 18.7% trailing core ROE, BV/share ex-AOCI compounding, disciplined capital return (~$450–475M/quarter of buybacks plus dividends). The debate is the entry multiple for a commodity-product insurer mid-hard-market, with CAT and legacy-reserve tails.
- Red flags to monitor: the NICO A&E arbitration (payments suspended; a reported $814M of deferred gains at risk), reserve development turning adverse (general-liability and commercial-auto increases already appeared in Q2 2026), CAT normalization after light years, commercial pricing softening as the hard market fades, LP/investment-income reversal, and the Hartford Funds exit removing a small earnings stream (consensus FY2026 EPS −4.6%).
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine's numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at $201.3929917752237 (the weighted IV@15%) and trades 46.370546790218925% below that figure at $137.5912 — but that clearing price inherits the float-inflated base, so it overstates the true margin of safety; a margin-of-safety buy price would need to sit materially below the weighted IV@15%, in words, and no hand-computed figure is quoted for it. On the conservative (final-tier B) reading, the honest claim is "clears 15% with room, sized like a B", not "46% upside".
