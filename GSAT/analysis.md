## What it is
Mobile satellite services operator: a low-earth-orbit constellation providing wholesale capacity to Apple (Emergency SOS, messaging, location on iPhone/Apple Watch — 85% of network capacity allocated to Apple, ~63–64% of revenue), plus Commercial IoT, SPOT consumer devices, duplex voice/data, government services, and the XCOM RAN / Band n53 terrestrial private-network effort. On 14 April 2026 Amazon agreed to acquire Globalstar for $90/share in cash or 0.3210 Amazon shares (election capped at $90), ~$11.57B, expected to close in 2027 subject to FCC and other approvals and satellite-deployment milestones; the HSR waiting period expired in July 2026 and management has suspended guidance and earnings calls (Q2 2026 release, cited in research). At $82.38 the stock is priced primarily as merger arbitrage against that $90 consideration, not as a standalone DCF.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak; an asset moat, not a pricing-power moat
Justification: Licensed MSS/Band n53 spectrum and an operating LEO constellation are scarce assets, but Globalstar has never converted them into returns on capital: ROIC proxy was -0.02% (FY2023), -0.16% (FY2024) and +1.50% (FY2025) in fundamentals.json, operating income was ~zero in FY2023-24 and $7.4M on $273.0M revenue in FY2025, and a single anchor customer (Apple, ~63-64% of revenue, 85% of network capacity) sets the economics, funds the capex and holds a 20% SPE stake. That is an asset moat, not a pricing-power moat. Gross margin cannot be quoted for recent years: fundamentals.json files no gross-profit figure for FY2018 onward (no COGS caption in the filed form), so the ROIC and operating-margin evidence above carries the moat call.
- FOR: Licensed L-band MSS spectrum plus Band n53 terrestrial rights — scarce, regulator-granted, hard to replicate; operating LEO constellation plus global ground-station network already in service for Apple Emergency SOS / messaging; long-dated wholesale capacity contract with Apple (85% of network capacity allocated) and record Commercial IoT activations (+20% TTM gross activations in Q2 2026).
- AGAINST: Single-customer concentration: Apple ~63-64% of revenue; the anchor funds the build, takes 20% of the SPE and dictates terms — no demonstrated pricing power; ROIC proxy ~0% through the cycle (FY2023 -0.02%, FY2024 -0.16%, FY2025 +1.50%, fundamentals.json) and persistent net losses (FY2025 -$8.7M, TTM to Q2 2026 -$54.5M); direct-to-cell competition from Starlink, AST SpaceMobile and Iridium with far deeper capital; standalone future is ending: Amazon acquisition agreed April 2026 caps the equity at the $90/share deal consideration if it closes in 2027.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): TTM was run (ttm.py through Q2 2026, legs quoted above) and deliberately NOT used as the base: TTM OE/share $3.65 is the same Apple-prepayment cash stream as FY2025, while H1 2026 printed a $41.4M net loss on falling revenue. The median base is retained and the rejection reason is written here.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real, and explained — it is not cyclicality, it is the prepayment cycle: conservative OE/share went −$0.87 (FY2023, capex year) → $2.37 (FY2024) → $3.95 (FY2025) as Apple advances arrived. Handled with 45% weight on bear-or-worse and a 0.60 super-bear OE multiplier.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): the one-off is identified, from statements.json cashflow rows — the change in deferred revenue was +$301,773,000 in FY2024 and +$481,542,000 in FY2025 (Apple's infrastructure prepayment, up to $1.1B, recorded as deferred revenue / contract liability for the Extended MSS Network; ~$813M advanced per research citing the filings). Cash arrived before the service obligation; net income stayed negative because the revenue is recognized later while D&A and network costs run now. Long-term deferred revenue on the balance sheet rose from $3,213,000 (FY2023) to $288,171,000 (FY2024) to $806,930,000 (FY2025) (statements.json). Consequence: the OCF-based conservative OE counts tomorrow's revenue as today's earnings — the base is flattered in both the median and TTM candidates, which is why confidence is low and the verdict is sized to the conservative end. A second, offsetting distortion runs the other way: FY2025 investing cash flow was −$550,383,000 (statements.json) against the $97,061,000 capex tag the engine deducts, and PP&E net rose $673,632,000 → $1,305,458,000 — satellite construction is being funded (largely by the prepayment itself, through the SPE) well beyond the tagged capex line, so true capital intensity is also understated in the OE leg.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $3.95 vs 3-yr median $2.37): both are distorted by the same prepayment, the latest year more so (it holds the largest deferred-revenue build, +$481,542,000). The median is used because it dilutes the spike across FY2023-25; neither figure is a clean recurring base, and the maintenance-definition totals ($4.9M-$62.5M/yr, financials_history.csv) mark how far a working-capital-cleaned base would fall.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the range, and unusually both ends are below the price: on the engine's reported-cash bases the weighted IV@15% is $21.29 (this run, median base, recalibrated growth); the preliminary default run (median base, engine growth, default weights) printed IV@15% $33.98 and weighted IRR 8.11%; a deferred-adjusted or maintenance-based OE would print lower still, toward Tier F territory. No defensible method gets this business, standalone, anywhere near $82.38 — because $82.38 is not a standalone price.
- What the price actually is: merger arbitrage. $90/share Amazon consideration vs $82.38 is ~9% gross spread to a close expected in 2027 (FCC approval and satellite milestones remain) — a mid-single-digit annualized return if it closes on schedule, with a hard fall if it breaks (the standalone IVs above are the downside reference). That is a deal-risk decision, not a DCF decision, and on DCF terms the tier is D either way.
- Quality is not the debate: the spectrum is genuinely scarce and the Apple/Amazon validation is real. The debate is who captures the spectrum's value — so far the anchor customers have (capacity, SPE equity, a $90 capped exit), while common holders got a decade of negative net income and a share count that only looks stable after the February 2025 reverse split restated the base (statements.json flags the split tagging; diluted counts run 122-127M across FY2023-25).
