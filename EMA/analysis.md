## What it is
A regulated utility holding company headquartered in Halifax: Tampa Electric and Peoples Gas in Florida, Nova Scotia Power, New Mexico Gas, Barbados Light & Power, Grand Bahama Power, and Emera Energy/non-regulated holdings — roughly 2.6 million electric and gas customers. Essentially all earnings are regulated and Florida-weighted (Florida operations contribute roughly 70% of adjusted net income). The growth engine is a $20B 2026–2030 capital plan (record $4.0B planned for 2026, ~80% directed to Florida) supporting guided 7–8% rate-base growth and 5–7% adjusted EPS growth through 2030. Post-period event: on October 6, 2026 Emera and Canadian Utilities announced a predominantly all-share merger (~$72B combined enterprise value, ~$45B combined rate base, Emera shareholders ~60%, close targeted Q3–Q4 2027, subject to shareholder and regulatory approvals) — the standalone plan below is what was scored; the merger is a risk/overlay item, not an input.

## OVERRIDE — SBC (filing-sourced, bounded authority)
- **The gap:** the preliminary engine run exited 4 with `DATA_GAP:sbc` — SBC missing for fiscal years 2023, 2024, 2025 of the last 3. Emera files no SBC expense figure under any candidate tag in EDGAR companyfacts (sbc_tag blank for every year 2016–2025); the expense is disclosed only by plan in the financial-statement notes.
- **The decision (worker, under the 2026-10-14 bounded override authority, kind "filing"):** judgment.json carries `sbc_override = {value: 89000000, kind: "filing", ack_reason: <below>}`. C$89M is Emera's own disclosed FY2025 stock-based compensation cost from Note 32 of its FY2025 consolidated financial statements (filed as Exhibit 99.3 to the Form 40-F for the year ended Dec 31, 2025, filed 2026-02-23, accession 0001193125-26-062876): compensation cost recognized — ECSPP C$3M + stock options C$3M + employee/director DSUs C$29M + PSU plan C$31M + RSU plan C$23M. The DSU/PSU/RSU plans are largely cash-settled liability plans; their recognized cost is nonetheless a real compensation cost of the period, and treating the full disclosed total as SBC errs conservative. The engine FX-converts the CAD override at each period's end rate (fx_conversion.overrides_converted includes sbc; FY2025 filled value $65,002,040).
- **ack_reason (required because the override is mechanically out of band):** the sanity band anchors on the last FILED value and Emera has never filed an SBC value companyfacts carries (last_filed_value: null), so no in-band value exists. The C$89M is not an estimate — it is the sum of the five compensation-cost figures the filer itself disclosed.
- **Single-value caveat, stated plainly:** one override value fills every untagged year (2016–2025), so earlier years carry the FY2025 figure. That overstates FY2024, whose disclosed total was C$52M (ECSPP C$4M + options C$2M + DSU C$13M + PSU C$18M + RSU C$15M, same Note 32) — the error runs in the conservative direction (more SBC → lower OE) and is immaterial against a base that is negative by over a billion dollars a year.
- **Measured impact (dcf_numbers.json override_impact):** normalized base −$1.9184/share zero-fill → **−$2.1637/share with the override**. The override's real effect is binary, same as APP's and Fortis's: without it there is no tier at all (exit 4); with it the run completes and the tier is F on the numbers either way.
- **ADS ratio:** judgment.json also carries `ads_ratio {ratio: 1.0}` — Emera common shares are listed directly on the TSX and NYSE (TSX/NYSE: EMA); there is no depositary/ADS program, so filed per-share figures and the NYSE price are on the same basis. Cross-check: the FY2025 statements' EPS note denominator (weighted average diluted shares 299.7M) matches the engine's shares_diluted_latest exactly (engine flag ADS_RATIO_APPLIED ×1.0).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow (regulated franchise, capped returns)
Justification: exclusive regulated service territories and a visible, Florida-weighted rate-base growth runway are durable, but regulators cap the return on that rate base — and the measured franchise earns little above its cost of capital: the roic_proxy series in fundamentals.json (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash, CAD as filed) runs 2.77% (FY2021), 4.52% (FY2022), 5.01% (FY2023), 2.58% (FY2024), 5.28% (FY2025) — low single digits through the cycle, exactly what allowed-ROE economics predicts. Gross margin is not a usable signal here: fundamentals.json carries no cost-of-revenue split for this filer (gross_margin null in every year).
- FOR: regulated franchise monopolies — exclusive electric/gas territories in Florida, Nova Scotia, New Mexico, Barbados and Grand Bahama; visible rate-base growth — $20B 2026–2030 capital plan driving guided 7–8% rate-base CAGR and 5–7% adjusted EPS growth through 2030, ~80% of the plan directed to Florida; Florida weight — roughly 70% of adjusted earnings from a fast-growing service territory; essential-service, low-cyclicality demand — H1 2026 operating cash flow up 8% year over year; regulatory clarity into 2028 at Peoples Gas and a consensus settlement path at Nova Scotia Power filed September 2025.
- AGAINST: returns capped at regulator-allowed ROEs — no pricing power, and measured ROIC (above) sits at or below a plausible cost of capital; Nova Scotia political/regulatory risk — provincial rate-cap politics and fuel-cost recovery fights have repeatedly overridden normal rate-making at Nova Scotia Power; heavy leverage (net debt $15.67B) plus perpetual DRIP/ATM equity issuance diluting ~4.1%/yr; storm/climate exposure concentrated in Florida and the Caribbean; the pending Canadian Utilities merger (announced October 6, 2026) adds approval, integration and combined-leverage risk, plus CAD/USD translation exposure on U.S.-dollar earnings (a stronger CAD knocked a reported $43M off U.S.-dollar earnings translation in H1 2026 coverage).
