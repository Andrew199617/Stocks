## What it is
One of the largest landowners in Texas: ~882,000 surface acres, principally in the Permian Basin, plus a 1/16th nonparticipating perpetual royalty interest (NPRI) under ~371,000 acres, a 1/128th NPRI under ~85,000 acres, and ~33,000 additional net royalty acres — ~224,000 net royalty acres in total (FY2025 10-K). TPL is not a producer: it collects oil and gas royalties, sourced-water sales and produced-water royalties (Water Services and Operations segment), and easements/leases/material sales (Land and Resource Management segment) from operators working its land. Q2 2026 was a record quarter (BusinessWire release, 2026-08-05): consolidated revenues $246.1M (+31.2% YoY per the earnings summary), net income $153.9M ($2.23 diluted EPS), royalty production 39.7 thousand Boe/d, produced-water royalty volumes 4.9 million bbl/d; H1 2026 revenues were $482.88M with net income $296.83M.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong asset moat, no pricing power
Justification: irreplaceable Permian land and perpetual royalty acreage with a zero-cost royalty structure and a debt-free balance sheet — a strong asset moat — but the company is a price-taker on oil and gas, volumes depend on third-party operators and Permian depletion, and essentially all value is concentrated in one basin facing long-run hydrocarbon decline, so it is strong (7), not wide (9-10).
- FOR: Irreplaceable Permian acreage: ~882,000 surface acres plus perpetual nonparticipating royalty interests (~224,000 net royalty acres) that cannot be replicated; royalties bear no well capex or operating-expense burden — a structural cost advantage no producer can match; same acreage monetized repeatedly across the well lifecycle: easements, leases, caliche/sand, sourced water and produced-water royalties; scale water network in the Permian (produced-water royalty volumes of 4.9 million bbl/d in Q2 2026); debt-free balance sheet with cash on hand — survives commodity troughs without dilution or distress.
- AGAINST: No pricing power: royalties are a price-taker claim on oil and gas prices; volumes depend on third-party operators' drilling pace and on Permian depletion; single-basin concentration: essentially all value sits in West Texas; long-run hydrocarbon demand decline caps the terminal story; water segment is capital-intensive and competitive, unlike the royalty segment, and growth acreage is now bought at market prices ($454.244M of royalty-interest acquisitions in FY2025).
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:capex** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing-sourced capex override — see OVERRIDE. data_gap_uncovered is empty in the final run.
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.46 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, with all inputs quoted above.
- **DILUTION** (share count "growing 43.9%/yr"): a split-restatement artefact, not dilution. The filed share history mixes three scales — FY2021 diluted shares of 7,752,054 on the pre-split scale, FY2022's 23,180,427 after the first three-for-one split (distributed March 26, 2024), and FY2023–FY2025 at ~69M after the second three-for-one split (December 2025). On a consistent basis the count is flat to shrinking: 69,179,535 (FY2023) → 69,059,252 (FY2024) → 69,027,492 (FY2025), and 69,019,380 for the current TTM YTD period. SBC is rising in dollars (FY2023 $10,343,000 → FY2024 $12,498,000 → FY2025 $15,131,000 → TTM $16,700,000, all from the filed history / ttm.json) but remains small against TTM OCF of $603,151,000, and it is fully deducted in the conservative OE definition. The engine's −5pp share-count growth adjustment rests on the artefact, so the scenario offsets were shifted +5pp to neutralize it (see the DCF section) instead of letting it double-count.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): partly the same split-scale artefact in the per-share series, and partly real commodity cyclicality — filed revenue fell to $302,564,000 in FY2020 (oil crash) before recovering to $798,190,000 in FY2025. Handled with the normalized/TTM base, a 15% super-bear weight and 40% on bear-or-worse outcomes.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:capex**: see OVERRIDE — gap, decision, and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact), not just here.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (5.20x): real — the outcome range for a commodity-linked royalty stream IS wide. Confidence is held at medium, and the verdict below is sized to the conservative end. Note the spread does not rescue the price: even the super-bull scenario alone (IV@15% $247.09, IRR 11.93%) sits well below the $354.50 price.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range here is unusually honest: every defensible base lands in the same place. The TTM base ($7.6344/share) gives weighted IRR 7.24% (Tier D); the normalized median base ($6.0621/share) is lower than the TTM base, so a median-based run would not improve the verdict; and no single scenario — not even super bull at a 26.75% growth start and a 1.25 OE multiplier — reaches a 15% IRR (11.93%). There is no C-to-A range to hide behind: on owner's earnings, this price is not close.
- What the buyer at $354.50 is actually paying for is the scarcity value of the acreage itself — a perpetual, zero-cost claim on Permian development, plus optionality the DCF cannot see (data-center/power land deals such as the Chevron Project Kilby agreement to provide land and brackish water, announced with Q2 2026 results). That may prove worth a great deal, but it is a land-bank bet, not an owner's-earnings return: the filed OE yield at this price is 2.15%.
- Quality is not the issue and neither is the balance sheet: OE positive 5 of 5 years, conservative and maintenance OE definitions essentially identical in FY2025 (both quoted above), no funded debt, and a royalty stream that bears none of the producers' capex. The single problem is price.
- Hurdle math: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle at the weighted IV@15% of **$116.50** — roughly a third of the current price. Even at the IV@12% of $161.01 it would only clear a 12% hurdle. A margin-of-safety buyer anchoring on the bear case ($79.42) would need a far lower price still; no such price is computed beyond the engine's own scenario IVs, which are quoted above.
