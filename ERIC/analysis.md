## What it is
Swedish telecom equipment maker: radio access networks (RAN) and core networks sold to carriers, cloud software & services, and an enterprise segment built around Vonage (CPaaS, acquired 2022) and private networks. One of only two Western RAN suppliers (with Nokia). Revenue is carrier-capex driven and currently flat: Q2 2026 sales SEK 52.7B, −6% reported / −1% organic (lower IPR licensing after a one-off settlement in Q2 2025), adjusted gross margin 48.4%, adjusted EBITA SEK 6.9B (13.1% margin). Q3 2026 reports tomorrow (Oct 15, 2026); consensus expects a hard YoY comparison because Q3 2025 contained a large IPR settlement.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: one of only two Western RAN vendors with a top-tier cellular patent portfolio, deep carrier installed base and scale R&D — but RAN is a re-bid, capex-cycle product business with no end-customer lock-in, Huawei leads globally on price, the market is flat between 5G/6G cycles, and the Vonage excursion showed capital allocation can destroy the moat's earnings.
- FOR: one of two Western RAN suppliers (with Nokia); EU/US high-risk-vendor phase-outs structurally exclude Huawei/ZTE from much of its market; deep installed base with the world's largest carriers plus multi-year managed-services/software contracts — real switching friction in live networks; top-tier cellular SEP portfolio generating a high-margin IPR licensing annuity; scale R&D and standards leadership in 5G/early 6G that smaller vendors cannot match.
- AGAINST: RAN is a re-bid, price-competitive product business — carriers squeeze vendors and no end user is locked in; global RAN market flat between cycles, growth hostage to carrier capex timing (India rollouts, North America digestion); Huawei remains the global share leader and price aggressor outside restricted markets; IPR licensing income is lumpy and settlement-driven, and margins are exposed to component (memory) cost inflation not immediately passable to customers; Enterprise/Vonage capital allocation destroyed value — SEK 32B (2023) and SEK 15.1B (2024) impairments.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range is narrow for once: zero-fill SBC (indefensible, but the bounding case) gives weighted IRR 13.27%; the filed-SBC run gives 12.64%. Both are Tier C. There is no defensible input set found in this run that clears 15% at $9.58 — even the bull case only reaches 16.07% IRR.
- What the price is paying for: at $9.58 the market is capitalizing the margin-recovery story (adjusted gross margin 48.4% and rising) plus buyback accretion, in a market Dell'Oro-style trackers expect to stay flat in 2026, with management itself guiding H2 margins down on memory costs. The Q3 print lands tomorrow (Oct 15) against a settlement-inflated comparison — a weak print is the priced risk, not a surprise.
- Quality is genuinely decent: net cash, OE yield 9.68% on the normalized base, capex only ~8% of OCF, a raised dividend plus a first-ever buyback. This is a cash-return story at the wrong price for a 15% hurdle, not a broken business. Leadership is also in transition (Per Narvinger succeeded Börje Ekholm as President and CEO, Ekholm's retirement effective September 30, 2026) — execution continuity is a watch item, not yet a flag.
- Hurdle math: the stock clears 15% (weighted) at the weighted IV@15% of **$7.75**; with a margin of safety against the bear case (IV@15% $5.41), a buyer wanting insurance needs the low-$6s. At $9.58 it clears ~12% at the 12% discount column (IV@12% $10.23) — a C-tier return profile.
