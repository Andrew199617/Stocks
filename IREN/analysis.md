## What it is
Australian-founded, Nasdaq-listed data-center operator mid-pivot from Bitcoin mining to AI cloud (neocloud). It owns and operates renewable-powered data centers in North America (Childress, Texas is the flagship) and sells GPU capacity to hyperscalers and AI labs. FY2026 revenue was still mostly the old business: Bitcoin mining $578.2M of the $707,007,000 total (~81.8%), AI Cloud Services $128.8M — up from $16.4M in FY2025, and in Q4 FY2026 AI cloud ($70.5M) overtook mining ($66.7M) for the first time (FY2026 results reporting). The forward book is real: a five-year $9.7B Microsoft contract (200 MW of GB300 capacity at Childress, first 50 MW Horizon 1 deployment delivered August 2026), a five-year ~$3.4B NVIDIA AI cloud contract, and a multi-year Anthropic contract announced 2026-10-12, plus Cohere, Perplexity and Figure AI. Management cited $1B of operating ARR against $4B of contracted ARR for 2026 capacity as of 2026-08-26, targeted to be operational by 2026-12-31 — an operating measure, not GAAP revenue, and recognized revenue may be materially lower until capacity is built, energized, tested and accepted.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, a scarce power head start without proven returns on capital
Justification: the secured power portfolio and vertical integration are a genuine, hard-to-replicate head start, and hyperscaler-grade contracts validate it — but the measured economics show no moat yet. The roic_proxy series in fundamentals.json (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) is: FY2022 +0.06%, FY2023 −52.19%, FY2024 −3.11%, FY2025 +0.73%, FY2026 −14.06% — never sustainably above any plausible cost of capital in the last five years. Gross margin where filed rose from 47.8% (FY2023) to 53.5% (FY2024), 68.3% (FY2025) and 68.9% (FY2026), but that margin is before the capital charges that define this business — D&A of $417,729,000 and a $638.8M miner impairment in FY2026 alone took operating income to −$1,046,714,000. A high headline gross margin on a GPU-rental asset base is not pricing power until it survives depreciation and GPU obsolescence through a cycle.
- FOR: Secured power at scale — 810 MW operational, 2,100 MW under construction, 1,600 MW in development, more than 4.5 GW secured (company IR figures in search reporting) — grid interconnection and power procurement are the binding constraint in AI infrastructure and take years to replicate; vertically integrated model (owns land, data centers and operations, no data centers pledged as collateral); blue-chip contracted demand — Microsoft $9.7B / 5 years, NVIDIA ~$3.4B / 5 years, Anthropic multi-year — with customer prepayments covering a large share of GPU capex; renewable, low-cost power heritage from the mining era.
- AGAINST: GPU cloud capacity is close to a commodity — the customers (Microsoft, NVIDIA) are also the best-capitalized potential competitors and set the price at renewal; contracts run ~3–5 years while GPUs are depreciated over five, so years 4–5 economics and obsolescence sit with IREN; customer concentration — Microsoft alone is ~$1.94B/yr of the $4B contracted 2026 run-rate, most of the rest is non-investment-grade AI developers; returns on capital are negative (ROIC above) and the model consumes external capital continuously — FY2027 capex guided at $25–30B vs ~$14B committed financing/prepayments; Bitcoin still supplied ~81.8% of FY2026 revenue, so the cash engine being dismantled is crypto-price exposed until the pivot completes.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years — moot here, since there is no positive base OE to project.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 79.3%/yr — already deducted via the growth adjustment): real and structural, and worse than the headline CAGR. Diluted shares per the filed history: 54,775,771 (FY2023) → 99,640,920 (FY2024) → 223,245,651 (FY2025) → 316,123,145 (FY2026); shares outstanding (DEI) were already 357,378,674 as of 2026-04-30. SBC is compounding with it: $14,356,000 (FY2023) → $23,636,000 (FY2024) → $42,642,000 (FY2025) → $205,023,000 (FY2026), a ~4.8x jump in one year. On top of the filed count sit the convert overhang — $6,745.7M principal of convertible notes at FY2026 end — and, per the August 2026 prospectus reporting, up to 165,640,942 shares issuable on conversion, 38,645,505 RSUs and a 30,000,000-share NVIDIA purchase right at $70. Any future positive OE must be divided by a much larger share count than today's.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (a >60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs): not a divestiture, restatement or filing-basis break — it is a scale-up plus a business-mix break. Revenue went $7,898,000 (FY2021) → $59,037,000 (FY2022) → $75,509,000 (FY2023) → $187,192,000 (FY2024) → $501,023,000 (FY2025) → $707,007,000 (FY2026) as mining scaled from a near-zero base, and the mix is now breaking deliberately: mining hardware is being decommissioned to convert sites to AI (the $638.8M FY2026 impairment), and AI cloud went from $16.4M (FY2025) to $128.8M (FY2026). All revenue CAGRs in dcf_numbers.json straddle that mix change and were not used for any growth input.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2024, 2026): explained, and it flatters OCF rather than earnings. FY2024: net income −$28,920,000 vs OCF $52,219,000 — the gap is the non-cash addbacks, D&A $50,470,000 plus SBC $23,636,000. FY2026: net income −$702,621,000 vs OCF $2,100,418,000 — a ~$2.8B gap built from the $638.8M non-cash impairment on retired miners, D&A $417,729,000, SBC $205,023,000, a $111.8M debt-conversion inducement expense (10-K reporting), and customer prepayments received ahead of delivery (Microsoft's contract carries a 20% prepayment; committed GPU financing and prepayments were reported at $14B, with prepayments covering 45–55% of GPU capex). Prepayment cash is a liability to deliver capacity, not earned owner's earnings — and even with it, conservative OE is −$1,102,611,000 after capex. The divergence therefore does not rescue the base; it makes the negative base more damning, not less.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance sector — OE/capex DCF fits finance names poorly; grade confidence low): the screener's "Finance" label is a classification misfit — IREN is a capex-heavy data-center / neocloud operator, not a financial. The model warning still bites in substance: an OE/capex DCF deducts all capex, including growth capex building contracted future capacity, so it is harshest exactly in a buildout phase. That caveat is noted and confidence is graded **low** as the flag instructs — but the verdict does not depend on it, because the maintenance definition (capex capped at D&A) is also negative in FY2026 (−$544,806,000) and conservative OE has been negative in all five of the last five years, including pre-pivot mining years. The ranking lists this name under sector misfits.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a power-and-execution story trading at $8.532B, not a cash-flow story. The OE-DCF has nothing to grip: five straight years of negative owner's earnings on the conservative definition, a normalized base of −$1.66/share, and an FY2026 in which $2,100,418,000 of (prepayment-flattered) OCF still could not cover $2,998,006,000 of capex plus $205,023,000 of SBC.
- The bull case is not a DCF case: if the $4B contracted ARR is delivered and accepted on schedule, FY2026 revenue is irrelevant to the forward business, and the secured-power portfolio is genuinely scarce. That outcome is binary on delivery (Horizon 2–4 due later in 2026), on GPU economics in contract years 4–5, and on raising the FY2027 capex gap ($25–30B guided vs ~$14B committed) without crushing per-share value — the share count has already grown 79.3%/yr and the convert/RSU/NVIDIA overhang sits above the filed count.
- There is no hurdle-clearing price to quote: with negative owner's earnings at every base, no positive price produces a 15% OE-implied return. The 15% question only becomes askable after the company prints its first positive-OE year on delivered AI capacity; until then any entry price is paying for contracted-ARR optionality, not cash flows.
- Sizing: the F is a statement about the screen, not a short thesis — buildout-stage infrastructure fails this screen by construction, and the sector-misfit flag is on the record. But on Andrew's hurdles there is nothing here to underwrite today.
