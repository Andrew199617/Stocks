## Moat: 4/10 — weak-moderate
Justification: scale, national footprint, bonding capacity, utility/telecom incumbency and a record backlog give real bidding advantages on large programs, but the work is competitively bid, labor- and fleet-based, with thin margins and no durable pricing power. Return evidence (fundamentals.json): roic_proxy and gross margin are null — the filer does not tag the lines those series need — so the computed return series available is ROE: 12.9% (2021), 1.2% (2022), −1.8% (2023), 5.6% (2024), 12.2% (2025), at or below the cost of capital through most of the cycle; FY2025 net margin was 2.79%. Management itself noted on the Q4 2025 call (search result) that ROIC met its WACC hurdle in 2025 for the first time since 2021.
- FOR: scale and national footprint — one of the few contractors that can execute large multi-region power, pipeline, clean-energy and communications programs; record 18-month backlog $21.4B at Q2 2026 (+30% YoY, ~1.2x book-to-bill) gives unusual revenue visibility for a contractor; incumbency and long relationships with utilities, telecom carriers and energy customers, with bonding and safety record gating large-project bidding; diversified end markets plus data-center exposure via Superior.
- AGAINST: competitively bid, project-based work — no recurring revenue or durable pricing power; returns at/below the cost of capital through most of the cycle with a 2.79% FY2025 net margin; labor- and fleet-intensive cost structure — wage pressure and fixed-price execution flow straight to OE; growth partly bought (serial acquisitions; Superior $1.65B debt-financed) with integration and leverage risk; segment cyclicality (Communications softness from RDOF roll-off and wireline deferrals; pipeline project timing).
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The market is paying for the backlog and the guidance — $21.4B backlog, +27% guided revenue, +42% guided adjusted EPS — at $190.70. The owner's-earnings record says the cash conversion of that growth is the weak link: TTM OE/share *fell* to $2.63 on TTM revenue of $16,109,103,000 (fundamentals.json) because growth consumes working capital and fleet capex before it pays. Adjusted EPS and OE are telling opposite stories, and the sweep prices OE.
- Method range: the base choice moves the IV a lot (median $5.95 vs TTM $2.63 is a >2x base gap) but not the tier — on the most generous base, even the super-bull case (25.8% start, 1.25x OE) reaches only $144.66 at a 15% discount, 12.34% IRR. There is no defensible pass-1 method that gets MTZ to Tier C at this price.
- What would change the answer: two or three years of OE conversion at the guided EBITDA margins — i.e., the 2024-style combination of growth *without* a WC build — or a price near $74. Watch H2 2026 OCF (Q4 is seasonally dominant), Superior integration, and whether pro-forma leverage comes back under the company's stated policy by year-end as management guided.
