## What it is

Self-managed industrial REIT (SIC 6798, Maryland, NYSE: EGP) owning shallow-bay, multi-tenant distribution buildings in supply-constrained Sun Belt markets (Florida, Texas, Arizona, California and other high-growth states) — last-mile / infill warehouses leased to regional and national distribution tenants. Portfolio 65.8 million sq ft at June 30, 2026 (Q2 2026 release, search results). Q2 2026: FFO $2.36/share, +6.8% YoY, $0.02 above the guidance midpoint; record leasing of 3.9 million sq ft in the quarter; quarter-end occupancy 95.6%, same-store occupancy 96.9%, portfolio 96.8% leased; cash same-store NOI +8.3% in Q2 (+8.8% YTD); leasing spreads +34% GAAP / +19% cash. Full-year 2026 FFO guidance midpoint raised to $9.59/share (+6.8% over 2025), same-property NOI guidance midpoint 6.8%, development starts guidance raised to $325 million and acquisitions guidance to $215 million; development pipeline 17 projects / 3.2 million sq ft / $487 million projected cost. Dividend $1.55/share quarterly (DPS TTM $6.20, fundamentals.json), 186 consecutive quarterly dividends, payout ~66% of FFO. Top 10 tenants are only 6.6% of rents.

## OVERRIDE — capex (filing, bounded authority)

- **The gap:** the preliminary engine run exited 4 with `DATA_GAP:capex` — capex missing for FY2025 of the last 3, because in the FY2025 10-K EastGroup stopped filing `PaymentsForConstructionInProcess`, the minor tag the engine read for FY2022–24. That tag's FY2024 fact (-$17,170,000) is a signed construction-payables change, not capex at all (statements.json flags the FY2024 fact as a sign slip). The filer's recurring real-estate capex is the cash-flow line "Capital improvements" (us-gaap `PaymentsForCapitalImprovements`), which statements.py reads as its capex series but which is not in the engine's candidate tag list — the same dead end documented for MAA, AMH, ELS and others in this sweep.
- **The decision (worker, bounded authority (a) — kind "filing"):** judgment.json carries `capex_override = {value: 59288000, kind: "filing", source: "Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2025 (filed 2026-02-11, accession 0000049600-26-000010), Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows, investing activities line 'Capital improvements' (us-gaap PaymentsForCapitalImprovements): $(51,116) thousand for FY2023, $(59,288) thousand for FY2024 and $(75,830) thousand for FY2025; the override value is the median of the three filed years (the FY2024 figure). Cross-checked against statements.json, whose capex series is exactly this line."}`. The band check anchors on the -$17,170,000 payables fact; on absolute value the override is 3.45x that anchor, inside the 0.1x–10x band, and the engine marks it in-band (override_checks: out_of_band false, ack_reason_present true, last_filed_value -$17,170,000); the judgment carries an ack_reason anyway because the anchor is not a capex figure.
- **How it was applied:** the engine filled every period with no filed capex (periods_filled: 2016, 2017, 2018, 2019, 2020, 2021, 2025 — capex_tag "OVERRIDE" in financials_history.csv); filed values are never replaced. ttm.py filled all three capex legs on the OCF anchor timeline (FY leg $59,288,000 over 365 days; both YTD legs $29,400,350.68 over 181 days; they cancel in the TTM math, leaving TTM capex $59,288,000).
- **Measured impact:** without the override there is no tier at all (the gap blocks scoring). On the TTM base actually used (key_numbers.md, same final configuration with overrides toggled off): base OE/share $9.19 (no override) → $8.09 (with override), −12.02%; weighted IRR 8.59% → 7.63% (−0.96pp); unblocks_tier: YES. On the engine-native normalized base (override_impact in dcf_numbers.json): base OE/share $8.65 (zero-fill) → $7.76 (with override). The single-value fill understates FY2025 capex by $16,542,000 against the filed $75,830,000 (flatters the latest year — stated in the override's reason, not hidden).
- **Excluded, deliberately:** "Development of real estate" ($321,934,000 in FY2025, us-gaap PaymentsToDevelopRealEstateAssets) and "Acquisition of real estate" ($143,099,000 in FY2025, PaymentsToAcquireCommercialRealEstate) are growth investment presented separately by the filer; charging them as capex would double-count growth the portfolio expansion already paid for (same treatment as AMH/AHR in this sweep). "Lease commissions" ($34,776,000 in FY2025) are likewise a leasing cost of new/renewed tenancy presented separately, not in the filer's capital-improvements line.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow

Justification: infill shallow-bay scarcity in Sun Belt markets, demonstrated rent pricing power (leasing spreads +34% GAAP / +19% cash, cash same-store NOI +8–9%) and a development channel that creates assets at a ~9.4% stabilized yield give EastGroup more than a generic landlord's moat, but the assets remain replicable with capital, there is no lock-in beyond leases, growth is bought with continuous equity issuance, and filed returns are moderate — not the sustained well-above-cost-of-capital record a 7+ requires.

- **Evidence (fundamentals.json):** roic_proxy is null for every year and gross margin is null — the filed REIT presentation does not split out the operating-income / gross-profit lines the tool reads, so neither series exists to quote (stated plainly, per the spec: if a figure is not in the computed outputs, say so in words). What the filings do show: ROE 9.54% (FY2022) → 7.69% (FY2023) → 6.92% (FY2024) → 7.36% (FY2025), ROA ~4.4–4.7%, net margin ~35–36% and OCF margin 66.6% in FY2025. A 5 does not need the written exception a 7+ would: the measured record does not show returns durably well above the cost of capital, and the score does not claim it does.
- FOR: infill shallow-bay locations in supply-constrained Sun Belt markets — zoning/permit friction and scarce infill land make the 65.8M sq ft portfolio hard to replicate near book; quarter-end occupancy 95.6%, same-store 96.9% (Q2 2026); demonstrated pricing power — Q2 2026 leasing spreads +34% GAAP / +19% cash on a record 3.9M sq ft signed, cash same-store NOI +8.3% in Q2 and +8.8% YTD; development alpha — self-developed projects transfer at a ~9.4% stabilized yield (management, Q2 2026 coverage), well above acquisition cap rates, a structural cost advantage over REITs that must buy growth; balance sheet as a competitive weapon — debt/total market cap 12.9%, debt/EBITDA 3.0x, coverage 15.1x, no revolver drawn ($675M available) at Q2 2026; diversified rent roll (top 10 tenants 6.6% of rents), 186 consecutive quarterly dividends, FFO/share compounded ~10.7%/yr over five years (peer coverage).
- AGAINST: real estate is replicable with capital — no network effects; tenant lock-in is a multi-year lease, and shallow-bay space competes with new industrial supply; filed returns are moderate (ROE above); growth is equity-funded — share count CAGR 7.3%/yr, issuance proceeds $692,312,000 (FY2023), $717,659,000 (FY2024), $264,071,000 (FY2025), so per-share economics lag portfolio growth; Sun Belt concentration and a post-COVID industrial supply wave cap rent growth as the 34% spreads normalize — same-property NOI guidance (+6.8% for 2026) is already below the +8.8% YTD print; development is cyclical — the $487M pipeline must lease up through whatever demand does next, and starts guidance was cut one quarter and raised the next in 2026.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved

- **DATA_GAP:capex** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing override — see OVERRIDE. data_gap_uncovered is empty in the final run.
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, legs quoted above.
- **DILUTION** (share count CAGR 7.33%/yr): it is equity-raise dilution, not SBC — issuance proceeds were $692,312,000 in FY2023, $717,659,000 in FY2024 and $264,071,000 in FY2025 (fundamentals.json), funding the development/acquisition program; shares outstanding reached 53,773,317 at 2026-07-21 (dei, dcf_numbers.json) vs the TTM denominator of 53,665,000. SBC itself is small and only slowly rising ($8,292,000 FY2022 → $11,793,000 FY2025 → TTM $12,210,000; ~2.5% of TTM OCF) — a real cost already deducted, not the dilution driver. The −5pp growth adjustment is the engine's cap; the scenario starts carry the rest of the drag (see the sanity check).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and OCF differ by >40% in 2025): non-cash real-estate depreciation, reconciled in the 10-K cash-flow statement. FY2025: net income $257,458,000 vs OCF $480,734,000 — the bridge is depreciation & amortization of $216,732,000 (statements.json / financials_history.csv), plus SBC $11,793,000 and other non-cash items, partly offset by working-capital movements. No tax-deposit, impairment or one-off working-capital swing is doing the work; the OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is unaffected by the divergence — it is the normal REIT NI/OCF gap.
- **DEBT_TAGS_BLANK** (no debt tag for the latest FY — net debt UNKNOWN, not zero): balance sheet from the FY2025 10-K (EDGAR companyfacts) — unsecured debt $1,611,026,000 (us-gaap UnsecuredDebt; DebtInstrumentCarryingAmount $1,615,000,000 at a 4.9% weighted-average rate), total liabilities $1,935,219,000 against total assets $5,431,807,000 (real estate investments at cost $6,699,988,000, net $5,116,456,000) and total equity $3,496,201,000; cash $1,007,000 (combined tag — see the next flag), so net debt is ~$1.61B (the $1.6B figure peer coverage also cites). ## My thoughts / overlay

- The method range does not save the price. On the normalized median base ($7.76/share) with identical scenarios the weighted IRR is 7.43% and IV@15% is $75.32 — Tier D; on the TTM base ($8.09/share) it is 7.63% / $78.55 — Tier D. The two defensible bases span 7.4–7.6%, and even the super-bull scenario (IRR 11.01%, IV@15% $129.64) does not reach the 15% hurdle at $197.44. The verdict is not base-sensitive, not scenario-sensitive within the computed set, and not capex-sensitive at any defensible recurring-capex figure: charging FY2025's full filed $75,830,000 instead of the $59,288,000 median would lower the base further.
- What the screen cannot see, honestly stated: REIT value here is an asset/NAV and FFO-multiple question. EastGroup is arguably the best-run name in its niche — five-year FFO/share CAGR ~10.7% vs ~3.6% for the industrial REIT industry (peer coverage), developments stabilizing at ~9.4% yields, 3.0x leverage, and a portfolio the market prices at a 15–42% EV/EBITDA premium to STAG, Rexford and First Industrial. None of that is in dispute. The DCF's narrow point is that after ~$59–76M/yr of capital improvements and a share count compounding at 7.3%/yr, the owner's-earnings stream per share ($8.09 TTM on a 4.10% OE yield) supports a value near $79, not $197 — the price is paying for the guided FFO story ($9.59/share for 2026, +6.8%), not for owner's earnings after real capex.
- Risks to the *short* side of this verdict (what would make the D too harsh): development capex excluded here is genuinely value-creating at a ~9.4% stabilized yield — if that spread over cap rates persists for a decade, per-share OE compounds faster than the bull path; equity issued at premium prices to fund that development can be NAV-accretive per share even while OE/share lags; and shallow-bay supply scarcity could keep spreads and same-store NOI in the high single digits longer than the fade assumes. All three are bull-case arguments, and the bull case still prints 8.87%.
- Hurdle-clearing prices (dcf_numbers.json hurdle_prices): the shares clear 15% at ~$78.55, 20% at ~$55.21, 25% at ~$42.38 — i.e., the price would have to fall ~60% to clear Andrew's 15% hurdle on these inputs.
