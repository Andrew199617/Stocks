## What it is
Core banking technology for US community and regional banks and credit unions — the smallest of the Big Three core providers (Jack Henry vs Fiserv and FIS). FY26 revenue $2,544,339,000 (+7.12% YoY) by segment (segments.json): Payments $936,006,000 (36.8% of revenue, +7.2% YoY), Core $768,452,000 (30.2%, +4.9%), Complementary $752,214,000 (29.6%, +8.3%), Corporate Services $87,667,000 (3.4%, +18.3%). Products include the Jack Henry Core and Symitar core platforms, Banno digital banking and Payrailz payments. FY26 was a record sales year: 58 competitive core wins, 14 at institutions with more than $1B in assets. FY27 guidance (earnings release): revenue $2,684–2,709M (+5.5–6.5% GAAP), operating margin 24.5–24.7%, EPS $7.33–7.38. Headcount 7,300 (profile.json).

### Method limit — capitalized software development is NOT in the scored capex (read this before quoting the tier)
The engine's capex leg is the PP&E tag only. Jack Henry separately capitalizes software development, an investing cash outflow under the tag PaymentsToDevelopSoftware of $167,175,000 in FY24, $172,445,000 in FY25 and $184,243,000 in FY26 (statements.json line "Purchases of intangibles / software"; fundamentals.json capex_intangibles — verified against EDGAR companyfacts as entirely PaymentsToDevelopSoftware for these years). That is roughly three times the PP&E capex deducted in each base year, it is recurring, and it is rising — it is the cash cost of building the product the moat rests on. The scored OE, base and IRR therefore exclude a real maintenance cost on the order of a quarter of the printed OE base, and the printed weighted IRR of 10.07% is an **upper bound**: a software-inclusive owner's-earnings base would be materially lower and the IRR with it. No override was used to patch this: the engine's capex definition is a system-wide method choice applied identically to every software name in the sweep (the same treatment was noted, not overridden, at ZS), overrides only fill missing periods and cannot replace a filed figure, and redefining the capex leg is a main-agent decision. Confidence is graded **low** for this reason as well as the scenario spread, and the caveat is carried in the one-liner so it travels with the ranking.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong
Justification: Mission-critical core processing with very high switching costs and a decade of ROIC proxy at 19–25% (23.5% in FY26), but the smallest of the Big Three core providers, a structurally shrinking US bank/credit-union client base, and moat maintenance that costs $167–184M/yr of capitalized software development.
- Evidence (fundamentals.json): ROIC proxy (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) printed 19.2–25.2% in every year FY17–FY26 — 19.8% in FY23 and 23.5% in FY26 — never approaching the cost of capital, through COVID and the 2023 regional-bank stress. Management reported ROIC of 23.2% for FY26 in the earnings release. Gross margin held 41–44% for a decade (43.7% in FY26, the best of the last five years) and operating margin recovered to 25.0% in FY26 from 22.1% in FY24 — pricing power with no margin erosion. A 7 sits on that ROIC record; it is not an 8 because the franchise's served market shrinks structurally and its two rivals out-scale it.
- FOR: Mission-critical core platforms (Jack Henry Core for banks, Symitar for credit unions) — a core conversion is the highest-risk project a bank runs; multi-year contracts, very low churn. Integrated stack raises switching costs with each product (Core, Payments, Complementary, Banno). Record 58 competitive core wins in FY26, 14 over $1B in assets — taking share from Fiserv/FIS. ROIC and margin record above.
- AGAINST: Smallest of the Big Three — Fiserv and FIS have far greater scale and R&D budgets. US bank/credit-union consolidation deletes a customer at every merger; deconversion fees are one-time, the recurring revenue is gone. Moat maintenance is expensive and rising (capitalized software, above). Cloud-native and modular challengers (nCino, Mambu, Temenos, fintech point solutions) unbundle pieces of the stack. Served market grows slowly — revenue CAGR ~7% over 5 years, FY27 guided +5.5–6.5% — and the book is concentrated in US community/regional institutions.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

- Street context (street.json, cache, not scored): consensus Buy, n=15, target $190.56. The gap to this model is the usual one for JKHY — the Street underwrites the franchise premium and mid-single-digit growth at a lower discount rate; at a 10% discount rate the weighted IV is $154.82, essentially the current price, which is another way of saying the price already assumes roughly a 10% required return.
- Watch items: FY27 delivery against the +5.5–6.5% revenue guide (Q4 FY26 GAAP revenue grew only 4.7%, with deconversion revenue inside it); whether capitalized software keeps outgrowing revenue; bank M&A pace (each merger deletes a client and books a one-time deconversion fee); the Jack Henry Platform cloud migration's effect on the Core segment's +4.9% growth, the slowest of the three main segments.
