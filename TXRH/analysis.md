## What it is
US casual-dining steakhouse operator: the Texas Roadhouse concept plus Bubba's 33 (sports bar) and Jaggers (fast-casual). 832 locations at Q2 2026 quarter-end per the Q2 2026 reporting, most company-operated. The model is everyday value — hand-cut steaks at prices deliberately held below peers — high throughput, and restaurant-level managing partners. Louisville, Kentucky; founded 1993.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate, execution-led, not structural
Justification: best-in-class casual-dining execution with ROIC proxy sustained well above the cost of capital, but no switching costs, no network effects, deliberately limited pricing power and an unhedged beef input.
- **ROIC / margin evidence (fundamentals.json, required by the spec):** roic_proxy (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash) was 31.25% in 2021, 31.13% in 2022, 29.87% in 2023, 39.31% in 2024 and 30.85% in 2025 — well above any plausible cost of capital in every post-COVID year. Restaurants do not file a clean gross profit line in this feed (gross_margin is null in fundamentals.json); the filed operating margin was 7.64% in 2023, 9.61% in 2024 and 8.08% in 2025, and the reported restaurant-level margin in Q2 2026 was 16.4%, down from 17.1% a year earlier (Q2 2026 reporting). High ROIC on thin margins is a throughput story: record average weekly sales over $175,000 in Q2 2026, a $9.6M annualised AUV pace.
- FOR: ROIC proxy 29.9–39.3% every year 2021–2025 (fundamentals.json) — returns well above cost of capital through a cycle; traffic-led comps (+6.2% in Q2 2026 on +3.0% traffic and +3.2% check) while many peers grow on price alone; everyday-value brand with record average weekly sales; managing-partner operating model and disciplined development (~35 company openings planned for 2026, core-brand pipeline through 2028); net-cash funded balance sheet (see DEBT_TAGS_BLANK) funding growth, dividends and buybacks internally.
- AGAINST: no switching costs, network effects or proprietary product — a steak dinner is replicable; beef concentration (roughly half of food costs; 7.0% commodity inflation in Q2 2026 compressed restaurant margin to 16.4% from 17.1%); deliberately limited pricing power (menu hikes of about 1.9% at the start of Q2 2026 and ~1% planned for Q4 run below cost inflation by design); wage inflation 3.9% in Q2 2026 in a labour-intensive model; single dominant concept, US casual-dining cyclicality, and peak-hour waits capping same-store throughput.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## DCF — final run (2026-10-19)
**Growth recalibration (written reason):** the engine's blended growth is 17.26% and g_base is 17.26% (key_numbers.md), but its components overstate what this company is now printing: rev CAGR 5y 19.63% spans the COVID rebound, OE/share CAGR 2y 26.99% is measured off the depressed 2023 OE/share of $2.74, while the current prints are latest rev YoY 9.39% (FY2025) and +11.1% revenue in Q2 2026 on +6.2% comps, with 2026 guidance for positive comps and 5–6% store-week growth. A 17.3% base-case start sits above every recent revenue print and ignores the margin side (Q2 2026 EPS fell year on year on 7.0% commodity inflation). Scenario starts were therefore set via offsets (−0.15 / −0.11 / −0.06 / −0.02 / +0.03 vs g_base) to 2.3% / 6.3% / **11.3%** / 15.3% / 20.3% — the base start matches the current revenue run-rate, the bull start assumes beef relief (2026 commodity guidance was cut to ~5%, with 2–3% expected by Q3 per Q2 reporting) restoring OE leverage, and even the super-bull start (20.3%) stays below the 26.99% OE/share CAGR artifact rather than extrapolating it. Probabilities 10/25/30/25/10 (defaults); OE multipliers 0.75/0.90/1.00/1.10/1.25 (defaults). The probabilities were deliberately left at the defaults: the company-specific evidence — the committed ~35 openings for 2026 with a core-brand pipeline through 2028, and the 7.0% commodity inflation that is the known swing factor — was loaded into the growth starts via the offsets above, which is where it changes the outcome; nothing in the filings supports skewing the odds themselves toward either tail, and the scenario span already runs from a 2.3% stall to a 20.3% full-recovery start. ## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026 (YTD end 2026-06-30), legs quoted above.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): not a one-off. It is the normal restaurant cash-flow shape — depreciation and SBC are large non-cash charges added back to net income to reach OCF. From financials_history.csv, FY2025 net income is $414,335,000 against OCF of $730,067,000, with D&A of $206,640,000 and SBC of $47,765,000 bridging most of the gap; the same pattern holds in 2023 (NI $313,675,000, OCF $564,984,000, D&A $153,202,000) and 2024 (NI $444,345,000, OCF $753,629,000, D&A $178,157,000). No tax deposit, impairment or working-capital one-off is needed to explain it, and the OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is unaffected — if anything the divergence confirms OCF, not NI, is the right anchor for a capex-heavy restaurant operator.
- **DEBT_TAGS_BLANK** (no debt tag reports for the latest fiscal year — net debt unknown, not zero): resolved from the filing balance sheet. At December 30, 2025 (FY2025 10-K consolidated balance sheet, as reproduced in the filing) Texas Roadhouse had cash and cash equivalents of $134,709,000 and **no funded debt line at all** — total liabilities of $2,067,770,000 (financials_history.csv) consist mainly of operating lease liabilities of $974,023,000 ($30,953,000 current + $943,070,000 non-current in the filing), deferred revenue (gift cards) of $448,744,000, accounts payable and accruals. No debt was outstanding at the fiscal year-end — which is exactly why the debt tags are blank. The engine's net debt is therefore null in key_numbers.md and no leverage verdict is taken from it; the correct statement for FY2025 is: debt-free on a funded basis at year-end, with lease liabilities of $974,023,000 as the real fixed obligation, and equity of $1,460,820,000 (financials_history.csv; $1,481,702,000 including noncontrolling interests in the filing). One update past the fiscal year-end (dated correction, 2026-10-19): statements.json shows $50,000,000 of long-term debt outstanding at both 2026-03-31 and 2026-06-30 against cash of $202,427,000 at 2026-06-30 — a revolver draw taken in 2026 — so at the TTM date the funded position is net cash of $152,427,000 rather than literally zero debt. The amounts do not change the verdict (the draw is ~0.4% of market cap and no leverage verdict is taken from the engine), but the present-tense wording in this analysis has been corrected to match.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** fired on the preliminary run (4.40x) and no longer fires on the final run (spread 3.70x in key_numbers.md) after the growth recalibration above. Confidence is still capped at **medium**, not high: a 3.70x spread is still a wide outcome range for a commodity-exposed restaurant chain.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Quality is not the debate: ~30%+ ROIC for five straight years, traffic-led comps when peers are losing traffic, a net-cash funded balance sheet, and both a dividend and buybacks. At an OE yield of 3.19% (key_numbers.md) the price already capitalises a long, clean compounding run.
- The binding constraint is arithmetic, not narrative: even the super-bull scenario (20.3% growth start, 1.25x OE) returns only 11.96% at $168.40. There is no defensible scenario set built from current prints that clears 15% here — the stock would need to fall to about $68.01 (the weighted IV@15%) for the weighted case to clear the hurdle.
- The one genuine method caveat cuts in the company's favour and is worth stating plainly: conservative OE deducts all $396,929,000 of TTM capex, including the growth capex for ~35 new restaurants a year. On the maintenance definition FY2025 OE is $449,618,000 vs $294,306,000 conservative (financials_history.csv). That gap is real future capacity, not waste — but the sweep's rule is to charge it, and even a generous reading does not close a −59.6% gap to IV@15%.
- Risks to the base case: the cattle cycle (beef roughly half of food costs, tight supply, guidance already relying on relief to 2–3%); wage inflation 3.9%; consumer discretionary softness hitting traffic, which is the entire comp story; and eventual saturation of the core concept, partly offset by Bubba's 33 and Jaggers optionality that is too small to pay for today.
