H1 2026 company reporting (operating result EUR 804M, up 9%; net result EUR 608M; operating capital generation EUR 416M, up 27%; free cash flow EUR 392M) shows operating improvement but no OE-yield inflection that changes the verdict at $8.32.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $0.2527 vs median $0.4362): the latest year is the depressed one — FY2025 operating cash flow fell to $509,950,000 even as net income rose, because the IFRS net result carried non-operating/investment effects (below). - **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2025): FY2023 net income −$219,895,000 vs OCF +$954,720,000; FY2025 net income +$1,151,500,000 vs OCF +$509,950,000 (financials_history.csv). The OCF-based conservative OE is the series used throughout, so the divergence does not contaminate the base; it is why the base is OCF-based at all.

## My thoughts / method range
- The honest headline is a range with a robust bottom line. On the engine's OCF-based owner's earnings this is a D by a wide margin: a 5.24% OE yield at $8.32 against a 15% hurdle, a super-bull case that still only returns 10.59%, and a hurdle price of $2.71.
- The known model blind spot cuts the other way and must be stated: insurers' distributable cash generation is better captured by operating capital generation (EUR 1.3 billion for FY2025 in company reporting) than by IFRS operating cash flow ($509,950,000), because insurance investment and contract flows route through the cash-flow statement differently. On an insurer-native capital-generation definition the shares would screen materially cheaper than $2.71 of IV. That is a reason to distrust the precise IV, not a reason to upgrade the tier inside this sweep's stated method — every insurer in the sweep is graded on the same OCF basis, and even a generous growth recalibration (bull/super-bull starts above) does not lift any scenario to 15%.
- What the market is actually pricing at $8.32 is the simplification story: US redomiciliation, Transamerica focus, UK exit, holding-cash release funding buybacks (H2 2026 buyback raised to EUR 350M in company reporting, funded partly from holding cash above target — a one-time resource, not a recurring OE stream), and a ~5%-plus dividend yield on the EUR 0.40 FY2025 dividend. None of that is owner's-earnings growth the DCF can underwrite at this price.
