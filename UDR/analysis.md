# UDR — UDR, Inc.

Price $34.26 · Tier **D** · weighted IRR 7.85% · weighted IV@15% $14.67 · moat 4/10 · confidence **low** (SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH)

## What it is

A large multifamily apartment REIT: 60,259 homes in 186 communities at Q2 2026, of which the same-store pool is 52,426 homes in 156 communities (90.5% of NOI), concentrated in high-barrier coastal markets — roughly 75% of NOI is coastal (West, Northeast, Mid-Atlantic: San Francisco, Orange County, New York, Washington D.C.), the rest Sun Belt (Q2 2026 supplement and earnings-call coverage, search results). NYSE-listed, FY ends Dec 31. Market cap (est) $11.342B — in scope under $40B. Q2 2026 (reported Jul 27, 2026): same-store revenue +1.8%, same-store NOI +1.4%, physical occupancy 96.6%, resident retention a record 60%, blended lease-rate growth 2.1%, FFO as Adjusted $0.64/share; FY2026 guidance raised — FFOA midpoint $2.53/share, same-store revenue growth guided 0.75% to 2.00% and same-store NOI 0% to 1.25% (Q2 2026 supplement, search results). Dividend $1.74/share for 2026 (15th consecutive annual increase), switched to monthly payments in 2026. Capital allocation is active: ~$650M of 2026 dispositions expected at a mid-5% cap rate, ~11.5M shares repurchased for ~$420M since Sep 2025, and the debt & preferred equity (DPE) book is being let to run off (search results).

## Owner's earnings

- Conservative definition (OCF − capex − SBC), capex = the filing override below: FY2023 $546,911,000 = $1.66/share; FY2024 $591,366,000 = $1.79/share; FY2025 $623,546,000 = $1.88/share (financials_history.csv). OE positive 5 of the last 5 fiscal years.
- Maintenance definition (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): FY2025 $804,882,000 and FY2023 $883,334,000 (financials_history.csv) — materially above the conservative figures because net income in those years contains large gains on sales of investment real estate ($242,913,000 in FY2025, $351,193,000 in FY2023, EDGAR companyfacts). The maintenance leg is flattered by property-sale gains; the OCF-based conservative definition is the honest one and is what the base uses.
- **Base used = TTM OE/share $1.80** (base_source: ttm_json). ttm.json legs: OCF FY $902,887,000 − prior YTD $156,216,000 + current YTD $128,743,000 = **TTM $875,414,000**; capex **TTM $252,857,000** (override, prorated evenly — the FY and both YTD legs are the same override value, so the TTM capex leg is a full year at the filed run rate); SBC FY $26,484,000 − prior YTD $7,498,000 + current YTD $9,568,000 = **TTM $28,554,000**; shares (current-YTD weighted diluted) **330,294,000**; **TTM OE total $594,003,000 → $1.7984/share** (ttm.json).
- Why TTM over the normalized median ($1.79/share): the FY ended 9.6 months ago and TTM OCF ($875,414,000) runs below FY2025 ($902,887,000) — the TTM base is the current run rate. In this case the choice barely matters: the two bases sit 0.4% apart, and the median variant computed with identical scenarios also lands in Tier D (method range below).

## OVERRIDE — capex (filing)

- **The gap:** the preliminary engine run exited 4 with `DATA_GAP:capex` — capex missing for FY2023, FY2024 and FY2025 of the last 3 under every candidate tag the engine reads, because UDR reports its recurring real-estate capex on the cash-flow line "Payments for capital improvements", tagged us-gaap `PaymentsForCapitalImprovements`, which is not in the engine's candidate tag list (the engine-read `PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets` series ends at FY2018). The same REIT dead end as MAA and AMH in this sweep.
- **The decision (bounded authority, kind "filing"):** judgment.json carries `capex_override = {value: 252857000, kind: "filing"}` — the median of the three filed years ($295,440,000 FY2023 / $249,886,000 FY2024 / $252,857,000 FY2025), from the FY2025 10-K Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows (filed 2026-02-17, accession 0000074208-26-000013). The engine applies the single value to every missing period (2019–2025 in the history; only 2023–2025 are in the base window). Against the true per-year figures it understates FY2023 capex by $42,583,000 (flatters that year's OE) and overstates FY2024 capex by $2,971,000 (conservative direction); FY2025 is exact.
- **Out-of-band, acknowledged:** the engine's band anchors on the last filed value under a tag it reads ($4,850,000 — the FY2018 PaymentsToAcquireProductiveAssets fact, a minor line that ends in FY2018), putting the override at ~52x the anchor; judgment.json carries the ack_reason — there is no filed value for the real capex line under any engine-read tag, and the override value is the filer's own filed figure, not an estimate.
- **Excluded, deliberately:** development spend (PaymentsToDevelopRealEstateAssets: $155,875,000 / $67,532,000 / $43,695,000 FY2023/24/25) and payments to acquire buildings / real estate — growth investment presented separately by the filer; charging it as capex would double-count growth the unit count already paid for.
- **Measured impact** (key_numbers.md, on the TTM base actually used): base OE/share $2.54 without the override → $1.80 with it (-29.21%); weighted IRR 10.06% → 7.85% (-2.21pp). Unblocks tier: YES — without it the filing gap blocks scoring entirely.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak

Evidence first: the `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json runs 2.93% (FY2020), 3.00% (FY2021), 2.61% (FY2022), 6.46% (FY2023), 3.04% (FY2024), 6.07% (FY2025), and filed ROE runs 1.99%–11.49% over the same years — and even those higher years are flattered, because operating income contains the gains on property sales ($351,193,000 in FY2023, $242,913,000 in FY2025 vs $16,867,000 in FY2024; EDGAR companyfacts). Gross margin is null in every year of fundamentals.json (the REIT filed presentation carries no cost-of-revenue split the tool reads). There is no evidence of sustained returns well above the cost of capital, so a 7+ moat is not available; what exists is location, scale and execution. Moat 4/10, in line with the apartment-REIT peers scored in this sweep (MAA 4, AMH 4).

FOR:
- High-barrier coastal footprint: ~75% of NOI from coastal markets (West, Northeast, Mid-Atlantic — San Francisco, Orange County, NYC, D.C.) where entitlement and land scarcity structurally limit new supply (Q2 2026 supplement / call coverage).
- Scale and operating platform: 60,259 homes in 186 communities at Q2 2026, in-house development/redevelopment, and the Orion data-analytics platform — Q2 2026 resident retention hit a record 60% and blended lease-rate growth was 2.1%.
- Occupancy durability through a record supply wave: physical occupancy held at 96.6% in Q2 2026 with same-store revenue +1.8% and NOI +1.4%.
- Balance-sheet access as a competitive tool: investment-grade, ~$1B liquidity, 5.2x interest coverage (Q2 2026 supplement) — cheaper, longer capital than private landlords, funding buybacks (~11.5M shares, ~$420M since Sep 2025) below NAV.

AGAINST:
- Apartments are a commodity product — 12-month leases, low tenant switching costs, and rent is a market price set against new supply (Sun Belt same-store NOI fell ~2% in Q2 2026 under the supply wave).
- Measured returns are low: roic_proxy 2.6%-6.5% over FY2020-25 and ROE 2.0%-11.5% (fundamentals.json) — no record of returns well above the cost of capital, and the higher years are flattered by gains on property sales inside operating income.
- Returns are capped by replacement cost / cap-rate arbitrage — new supply is the permanent competitor, and dispositions in 2026 are clearing at only a mid-5% cap rate.
- Leverage is real: consolidated net debt / EBITDAre 5.6x at Q2 2026 (supplement) on $5,820,147,000 of net debt (dcf_numbers.json) — the equity is a levered claim on slow-growing rent.
- Mid-Atlantic / Washington D.C. exposure (~19% of same-store revenue per sector coverage) carries federal-workforce demand risk; Southeast/Southwest NOI was negative in Q2 2026.

CAP = 7.2 years (moat 4): growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved

- **DATA_GAP:capex** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing override in the OVERRIDE section above — gap, source, per-year accuracy and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied / override_impact, key_numbers.md). data_gap_uncovered is empty in the final run.
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago; 9.6 months at scoring): ttm.py was run and its legs are quoted in the Owner's earnings section — TTM OE $594,003,000 → $1.7984/share — and the TTM figure **is** the base (the --ttm pin was used), because TTM OCF runs below FY2025. Note the TTM stops at 2026-03-31 in EDGAR companyfacts: fundamentals.json warns the latest 10-Q (period 2026-06-30, filed 2026-07-28) is not yet in companyfacts, so the TTM legs are built from the Q1 2026 10-Q — the Q2 2026 figures cited elsewhere here come from the company's Q2 2026 supplement and earnings-call coverage (search results), not from the computed outputs.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2024): structural REIT arithmetic, not a one-off in the OE base. FY2024 net income $89,585,000 plus D&A $695,473,000 = $785,058,000, against OCF of $876,848,000 (financials_history.csv) — depreciation explains the bulk of the gap, and the residual is the SBC addback ($32,625,000) and other non-cash / working-capital items in the cash-flow statement. FY2024 net income also looks depressed next to its neighbors for an innocent reason: it contained only $16,867,000 of gains on sales of investment real estate, vs $351,193,000 in FY2023 and $242,913,000 in FY2025 (EDGAR companyfacts). Owner's earnings are built from OCF, so none of this distorts the base; no adjustment.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Real Estate): accepted in full — the OE/capex DCF fits REITs poorly in both directions: it charges all capital-improvements capex against OE, and it gives no credit for the underlying property asset value or for FFO/AFFO, the industry's own metrics (FY2026 FFOA guided at $2.53/share; DPS $1.74, a ~5.1% yield at $34.26, that this model does not price). Confidence is graded **low** and the tier is read as "fails the standardized conservative OE screen," not as "the rental business loses cash." The mismatch does not rescue the hurdle math: both computed bases and even the super-bull scenario stay far below 15% at $34.26.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:capex**: see OVERRIDE — filing gap filled for 2019–2025, never replacing a filed value (there were none under engine-read tags); measured impact recorded in key_numbers.md (base $2.54 → $1.80 on the TTM base; weighted IRR 10.06% → 7.85%).
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt is 9.8x normalized OE): the ratio's denominator is owner's earnings after interest and after ~$253M of recurring capex, which inflates it for any REIT. Stated from the filing-side sources: net debt $5,820,147,000 (dcf_numbers.json, TOTAL:LongTermDebt basis) against total assets of $10,605,674,000 (financials_history.csv); on the industry's own measures the Q2 2026 supplement reports consolidated net debt / EBITDAre of 5.6x, interest coverage 5.2x and debt at 32.7% of total assets (search results). That is a moderately levered, investment-grade, well-laddered balance sheet — leverage is real and caps confidence, and the bear cases already stress it (OE multipliers 0.75 / 0.90 on negative growth starts), but it is not the story; price vs OE is.
- **Revenue-tag artifact (diagnostics, not a flag):** the engine's growth components are computed from `RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax` ($11,361,000 FY2025), a component line, while the filer's total revenue is `Revenues` ($1,712,317,000 FY2025) — fundamentals.json carries the same warning in its own words. The scenario starts were recalibrated off the true revenue prints with the reasons written in the DCF section; no growth figure from the engine blend is quoted anywhere in this analysis as a real print.

## My thoughts / overlay

- The honest headline is the same two-lens gap as every apartment REIT in this sweep. On the REIT lens this is a durable operator: 96.6% occupancy and record 60% retention through a record supply wave, guidance raised in Q2 2026, a 15-year dividend-growth streak, dispositions clearing at mid-5% cap rates, and buybacks retiring ~3.5% of the share count below NAV — the per-share compounding machine is intact, just slow. On the owner's-earnings lens the same company converts $875,414,000 of TTM OCF into $1.80/share of OE because keeping ~60,000 aging apartments leasable costs ~$253M a year of capital improvements, forever — that spend is the rent roll's maintenance bill, and the sweep is right to charge it.
- What the price is actually underwriting: at $34.26 the buyer pays ~13.5x guided FY2026 FFOA ($2.53) and gets a ~5.1% dividend yield plus whatever rent growth the post-supply-cliff recovery delivers, levered 5.6x. That can be a reasonable bond-proxy-plus return; it is not a 15% owner's-earnings return on any scenario this run computed, including the super bull (10.60%).
- What would change the tier: the price, mostly — the 15% hurdle clears below ~$15 on the TTM base and the 12% discount-rate value is $19.30. On fundamentals, same-store NOI would have to inflect to sustained mid-single-digit growth with capital improvements flat; the supply data (completions down sharply, coastal deliveries scarce) says the setup for that exists in 2027–28, but it has not filed yet. The DPE runoff and continued buybacks below NAV are the credible per-share accelerants to watch. Next checkpoint: Q3 2026 results on November 2, 2026 (search results).
- Confidence: **Low** (SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH for a REIT and a capex series the engine cannot read natively — though the two bases agree to 0.4% and every computed variant lands in D, so the tier itself is robust to the data problems).

## Final verdict

**Tier D.** Quality coastal apartment REIT, moat 4/10 — a high-barrier coastal footprint (~75% of NOI) and a record-retention operating platform are real, but apartments are a commodity and filed ROIC ran only 2.6%-6.5% over FY2020-25; on a TTM owner's-earnings base of $1.80/share after charging filed capital-improvements capex, weighted IRR is 7.9% (IV@15% $14.67) at $34.26, so it clears the 15% hurdle only below ~$15 — the REIT lens (FY2026 FFOA guided at $2.53/share, DPS $1.74) is kinder, but neither supports 15% at this price.
