## What it is
One of the largest diversified global asset managers, operating as Franklin Templeton: traditional active equity/fixed income (including Western Asset), plus alternatives, ETFs, retail SMAs and the Canvas custom-indexing platform, sold through institutional and wealth channels worldwide. AUM was a record $1,791.6B at June 30, 2026 (Q3 FY26 release), with long-term net inflows of $18.4B in the quarter and $63.3B fiscal year-to-date — a genuine flow recovery everywhere except Western Asset, which still lost $1.1B in the quarter after losing over $140B in FY2025 following the SEC investigation into former co-CIO Ken Leech (a $100M SEC settlement charge, plus $77M of special termination benefits and $33M of intangible impairments, hit Q3 FY26 GAAP results; adjusted EPS was $0.72 in the quarter vs $0.49 a year earlier). The company also announced a corporate name change to Franklin Templeton, Inc., effective August 17, 2026 (ticker unchanged).

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 13 months ago / 12.52 months in diagnostics): ttm.py was run and its full legs are quoted in the OE section; its TTM base ($0.0692/share) was NOT used, for the documented reason that its OCF leg is a consolidated-investment-product timing swing ($1,086,800,000 → $298,500,000 on the nine-month leg) contradicted by every manager-earnings print in the same window (nine-month net income $407,300,000 → $695,200,000; TTM revenue +7.94% YoY; Q3 FY26 adjusted EPS $0.72 vs $0.49). The median-of-3-FY base is used instead.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): real — the filed series runs $1.0741 to $3.3889 over FY2021–25 (and −$0.1521 in FY2019), driven by market-geared fees plus CIP OCF timing. Handled by the normalized median base and by widening the bear weight to 30% (probs 10/30/30/20/10).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and OCF differ by >40% in 2024, 2025): structural, not a tax-deposit one-off. FY2024 net income $464,800,000 vs OCF $971,300,000; FY2025 net income $524,900,000 vs OCF $1,066,100,000 (financials_history.csv). The gap is the non-cash load that depresses GAAP net income without touching cash — SBC ($246,100,000 in FY2024, $214,800,000 in FY2025, added back in OCF), D&A ($116,500,000 / $125,300,000), acquisition-intangible amortization and impairments, deferred taxes, and consolidated-investment-product gains/losses and purchases. Consequence: neither series is clean for this filer — GAAP net income understates cash earnings, consolidated OCF overstates them in FY2024–25 and collapses on CIP timing in the TTM window — which is why the verdict uses the standardized median OE base at low confidence and reports the method range below.
- **CASH_INCLUDES_RESTRICTED** (only cash tag for the latest FY is the combined cash + restricted-cash total): confirmed in financials_history.csv — FY2025 cash is filed as CashCashEquivalentsRestrictedCashAndRestrictedCashEquivalents, $3,573,900,000, with no restricted-cash tag separating the restricted part, so the engine's net debt (−$1,211,900,000 vs total debt $2,362,000,000) *understates* net debt / overstates own net cash by the restricted (CIP/escrow) amount. Resolution: the balance sheet is still net cash on the combined basis by a wide margin and the restricted balances belong to consolidated products, not to a solvency claim on the manager; no leverage adjustment is taken in either direction and no HIGH_NET_DEBT conclusion is drawn from the engine figure.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance): controlling flag for confidence. BEN is an asset manager, not a lender — no loan book and no underwriting float — so capex and SBC are real costs and the OE frame fits better than for a bank/insurer; but consolidated OCF contains CIP/CLO investment plumbing that is not the manager's distributable earnings (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE and the rejected TTM base). Confidence is **low** per the rule, the standardized base is kept for comparability, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end. No tier_override is taken: tier-by-numbers is already D, the conservative end of every defensible variant.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.02x super-bear IV): real — a market-geared manager's 15-year range genuinely runs from a drawdown case ($5.08) to a sustained-print case ($20.42). Confidence is low (already capped by the sector flag) and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; note even the super-bull case yields only 10.98% at $30.96.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range (the real headline):** on the standardized consolidated-OCF median base this run prints Tier D (weighted IRR 6.83%, IV@15% $10.72). On the rejected TTM OCF base ($0.0692/share) the same structure would print a spurious Tier F — rejected above as fund plumbing. On the manager's adjusted-earnings lens (Q3 FY26 adjusted EPS $0.72 in the quarter, nine-month GAAP net income up to $695,200,000) the earnings power is plainly above the $1.3467 standardized base, and a run on that definition would land higher — but no defensible variant *clears 15% at $30.96*: even this run's super-bull case (12.83% OE growth start, 1.25x multiplier) yields only 10.98%. The disagreement is between D and low-C economics, not between cheap and expensive.
- Quality is mixed, not broken: the flow engine has genuinely turned ($63.3B of long-term inflows fiscal YTD, record AUM, alternatives/ETF/Canvas growth, adjusted operating margin 28.0% in Q3 FY26 on the company's adjusted basis), and the capital return is real (DPS TTM $1.31). What the price already pays for is the recovery: at $30.96 the stock is ~2.9x the weighted IV@15% on the standardized base, after a ~39% one-year run in the price.
- Red flags to monitor: Western Asset flow relapse or further Leech-related liabilities; fee-rate compression as mix shifts to ETFs/alternatives; a market drawdown hitting AUM, fees and seed/CIP marks at once; GAAP margin repair stalling (Q3 FY26 GAAP operating margin was 9.2% vs 28.0% adjusted, on the settlement/termination/impairment charges); share-count creep (share CAGR +1.88%/yr in dcf_numbers.json, already deducted from the growth blend).
- **Hurdle math:** the weighted path clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$10.72** (weighted IV@15%); at 20% at ≈ $7.65 and at 25% at ≈ $5.95 (hurdle_prices in dcf_numbers.json). At $30.96 there is no margin of safety against any scenario — the bear IV@15% is $7.67.
