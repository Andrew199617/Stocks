## What it is
The world's largest hardwood (eucalyptus) pulp producer, vertically integrated from its own plantations through mills, ports and logistics into paper. FY2025 revenue mix (segments.json, BRL): market pulp R$37,816,141,000 (75.5% of revenue), printing & writing paper R$8,260,804,000 (16.5%), the rest paperboard/tissue/other. The Cerrado mill at Ribas do Rio Pardo — the world's largest single pulp line, 2.55Mt/yr, started July 2024 — lifted total market-pulp capacity ~20% to ~13.4Mt and is expected to run above nominal capacity (2.57Mt guided for 2026, ~2.7Mt from 2027, per company investor materials). FY2025 prints: sales volume a record 14.2Mt (+15%), revenue R$50.12B (fundamentals.json), pulp cash cost R$817/t (lowest since 2021, company FY2025 release), net income R$13.44B (fundamentals.json).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate (structural cost advantage, zero pricing power)
Justification: World's largest hardwood pulp producer with a structural cost advantage — own fast-growing eucalyptus plantations, integrated logistics and the Cerrado mill put it at the low end of the global cost curve (pulp cash cost R$817/t in FY2025, lowest since 2021) — but it is a pure price-taker in a commodity: gross margin swung 50.2% (FY2022) to 32.4% (FY2025) with the pulp cycle, and roic_proxy fell from 17.9% (FY2022) to 8.5% / 9.9% / 6.8% (FY2023/24/25), i.e. recent returns sit around, not well above, the cost of capital through this part of the cycle. ROIC and margin history are from fundamentals.json (roic_proxy: FY2021 17.7%, FY2022 17.9%, FY2023 8.5%, FY2024 9.9%, FY2025 6.8%; gross margin FY2021 49.7%, FY2022 50.2%, FY2023 36.9%, FY2024 42.2%, FY2025 32.4%). A moat above 6 would need returns that stay well above the cost of capital through the cycle; Suzano's do not — its edge shows up as surviving troughs at lower cost than peers, not as pricing power.
- FOR: structural cost advantage — own eucalyptus plantations with short harvest cycles close to the mills, integrated logistics and ports (pulp cash cost R$817/t FY2025, lowest since 2021); scale — world's largest hardwood pulp producer, Cerrado added 2.55Mt/yr (~20%, total ~13.4Mt) and cut consolidated pulp cash cost 6% in 2024 to R$828/t; vertical integration into paper/paperboard/tissue plus the 2024 US paperboard acquisition diversifies the pulp-only exposure (market pulp still ~75% of FY2025 revenue); through-cycle cash generation — OE positive 5/5 last fiscal years, including the 2023 pulp-price trough.
- AGAINST: commodity price-taker — pulp price is set by the market (China demand, industry capacity), gross margin ranged 32–50% in five years on price alone; returns are cycle-determined (roic_proxy 17.9% peak → 6.8% FY2025); high leverage amplifies the cycle (net debt $14.536B, 8.1x normalized OE); BRL/USD exposure both ways — USD-linked revenues vs BRL costs, and USD debt/hedge marks drove the FY2024 net loss (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE); new industry capacity (including Cerrado's own tonnes) pressures the price it takes, and eucalyptus monoculture draws standing environmental scrutiny.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## DCF — engine 5.4.0 final run (2026-10-18)
Growth recalibration (written reason): the engine's blended growth is 16.66% and is not usable as a scenario anchor for this company. Its components (key_numbers.md) are rev CAGR 5y 9.30%, rev CAGR 3y −0.99%, latest rev YoY 19.37%, OE/share CAGR 2y 50.10%. The latest YoY is an FX artifact — USD-converted revenue rose 19.37% while BRL revenue rose only ~5.7% (R$47.40B → R$50.12B, fundamentals.json), because the period-end BRL rate moved 0.16169 → 0.18256. The OE/share CAGR is a capex-cliff artifact — it runs from the FY2023 trough ($0.90) through the end of Cerrado construction capex to FY2025 ($1.98); that step-up cannot repeat. And the Cerrado volume step (+20% capacity) is a one-off level shift, largely already in the FY2025 base (first full year), with only the above-nominal creep (2.57Mt in 2026, ~2.7Mt from 2027) still ahead. Honest nominal growth for a mature commodity producer with modest volume creep is mid-single digits. Scenario starts were therefore set via offsets (−0.2466 / −0.1666 / −0.1066 / −0.0566 / −0.0066 vs the blend) to −8 / 0 / **6** / 11 / 16%. ## My thoughts / overlay
- Method range: the two defensible bases bracket the answer. On the normalized median ($1.45) the weighted IRR is 21.24% (Tier A). On a trough definition (FY2023's $0.90/share persisting) the name would not clear 15% at all; on the latest-FY base ($1.98) it would print well into A/S territory. The median is the honest middle, and the 45% combined super-bear+bear probability is the price of the cyclicality.
- The capex definition is the biggest modeling caveat after pulp price itself: the engine's OE charges only the PPE-purchases tag (FY2025 R$4.58B). Suzano's total investment including forestry/biological assets runs materially higher (it reported R$17.1B invested in 2024 including the Cerrado build, vs R$9.19B on the PPE tag that year). A full-capex OE definition would lower every year's OE and compress the IRR — pass 2 forensic territory, flagged here, not silently fixed in pass 1.
- FX cuts both ways and is not in the scenarios except through the growth recalibration: BRL appreciation squeezes BRL margins on USD-linked pulp (2Q26 EBITDA/t fell 12% YoY partly on FX), while BRL depreciation inflates the USD-debt marks that produced FY2024's paper loss.
- Margin of safety on the final numbers: weighted IV@15% sits 52.5% above the $8.50 price, and even the bear case ($9.41) sits above it — but the super-bear IV ($5.35) sits 37% below it, and with 8.1x leverage the super-bear is not a tail curiosity for a pulp producer; 2023 already printed an OE/share 0.62x the base used here.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: A** (by numbers; weighted IRR 21.24% on the normalized median base — sized like a B per TIER_BAND: bear-case tier B, super-bear IV below the price, confidence low)

- One-liner: World's largest, lowest-cost hardwood pulp producer, moat 5/10 — Tier A by numbers on the normalized median base ($1.45/ADS): weighted IRR 21.2%, IV@15% $12.96 vs $8.50 price — but a levered commodity price-taker (net debt 8.1x normalized OE; bear case is Tier B at 16.4% IRR and the super-bear IV of $5.35 sits below the price), so size it like a B.
