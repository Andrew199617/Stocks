## What it is
A self-administered, self-managed equity REIT (SIC 6798, Florida corporation, S&P 500 member) that owns, operates and develops grocery-anchored neighborhood and community shopping centers in affluent suburban trade areas. As of December 31, 2025 it owned 391 properties and held partial interests in an additional 90 properties through unconsolidated real estate partnerships (FY2025 10-K, Note 1). More than 85% of centers are grocery-anchored (search results); average household income within 3 miles of its centers is ~$150,000 (search results). The portfolio absorbed Urstadt Biddle Properties in an all-stock asset acquisition that closed August 18, 2023 (+79 grocery-anchored properties in the NY/NJ area; UBP holders received 0.347 REG shares per share — search results and 10-K Note 1).

Current trading (Q2 2026 release, via search results): Nareit FFO $1.21 per diluted share in the quarter; same-property NOI +3.8% YoY; same-property leased 96.9% (anchors 98.4%), commenced 94.5%, with ~$41.2M of signed-not-occupied annual base rent in the pipeline; blended cash rent spreads +10.4% in the quarter (+11.8% trailing twelve months). Management raised FY2026 guidance: Nareit FFO $4.84–$4.88/share, Core Operating Earnings $4.62–$4.66/share (midpoint = >5% YoY growth), same-property NOI growth +3.7–4.1%. In-process development and redevelopment: ~$680M of estimated net project costs at a ~9% blended estimated yield. Pro-rata net debt and preferred stock to TTM operating EBITDAre: 5.0x. Quarterly dividend $0.755/share (paid October 2, 2026, per search results).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: grocery-anchored necessity centers in affluent, supply-constrained suburbs with demonstrated pricing power (Q2 2026 blended cash rent spreads +10.4%, same-property NOI guided +3.7–4.1% for 2026 after +5.3% in 2025) and a development platform building at ~9% yields — but no network effects or lock-in (leases roll, grocer anchors are powerful counterparties), each center's local position is contestable by rival REITs and developers, returns are rate/cap-rate dominated, and the measured returns corroborate only a narrow moat: roic_proxy 7.3% / 8.5% / 9.3% / 7.7% across 2022–2025 (fundamentals.json) — above a REIT cost of capital, not wide-moat territory, and flattered by a depreciated-cost asset denominator. Operating margin runs ~72% (fundamentals.json); the filer reports no gross-margin split.
- FOR: Grocery-anchored necessity tenancy: >85% of centers grocery-anchored (search results), anchors 98.4% leased — weekly-trip tenancy that is e-commerce-resistant and feeds the shop tenants; location scarcity in affluent suburbs: ~$150k average household income within 3 miles (search results), premier suburban trade areas where zoning constrains new competing supply; portfolio 96.9% leased / 94.5% commenced with a $41.2M signed-not-occupied rent pipeline (Q2 2026 release); demonstrated pricing power: blended cash rent spreads +10.4% in Q2 2026 (+11.8% trailing twelve months), straight-line +19.5%, same-property NOI +5.3% in 2025 and guided +3.7–4.1% in 2026, annual escalators in most new leases (search results); development platform: $680M in-process at a ~9% blended estimated yield — building at yields well above acquisition cap rates, funded cheaply by an A-rated balance sheet (pro-rata net debt + preferred / EBITDAre 5.0x; 87.3% of consolidated real estate assets unencumbered, FY2025 10-K); returns corroborate a narrow moat: roic_proxy 7.3% / 8.5% / 9.3% / 7.7% in 2022–2025 (fundamentals.json), operating margin ~72%.
- AGAINST: No network effects or lock-in: leases roll (short shop leases), grocer anchors are powerful counterparties who can relocate or extract concessions at renewal, and anchor consolidation/bankruptcy is a recurring sector hit; local scarcity is contestable: rival REITs (Kimco, Brixmor, Federal Realty) and private developers can build or redevelop competing centers — the moat is a portfolio of local positions, not a franchise; rate and cap-rate exposure dominates: bond-like earnings, a guided 100–150bp refinancing drag as maturing debt reprices (search results), and a valuation multiple that compresses when rates rise regardless of operations; growth is increasingly bought with capital: revenue CAGR is flattered by the all-stock Urstadt Biddle acquisition (closed Aug 18, 2023; +79 properties) and a development ramp from $232.9M (2023) to $435.1M (2025) of annual spend that depresses current owner's earnings; guided per-share growth is ~5% — utility-like; discretionary tenant tail (restaurants, fitness, services) and geographic concentration — California and Florida together are roughly half of annual base rent (search results).
- CAP at moat 5 = **8.5 years** (dcf_numbers.json): growth holds 2 years, then fades to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a genuinely good REIT priced like one. The operating story is the strongest in the open-air retail sector: necessity tenancy, double-digit cash rent spreads, a signed-not-occupied pipeline, and a development machine that builds at ~9% into a market that buys stabilized centers at much lower yields. None of that is in dispute — it is also all in the price, several times over on an owner's-earnings basis.
- The honest tension is the capex line. Charging all $435.1M of development and capital improvements against OE treats a growth program as upkeep; the maintenance definition (OE = net income, $527,460,000 in FY2025) treats none of it as upkeep. The truth for a shopping-center owner sits between — tenant improvements, leasing commissions and building capex recur even in a no-growth world (the 10-K's own capital-expenditures table breaks the $435.1M into land, building and tenant improvements, redevelopment and development) — but no split in the computed outputs separates them, so no middle base was invented. Method range, all on filed figures: TTM at true filed spend ~$1.98/share → IRR ~6%; TTM pin $2.0002 → 6.25%; true per-year median $2.31/share → still Tier D (the zero-fill companyfacts read, the only flattering one, printed 9.81% and is Tier D itself). **Every defensible base gives Tier D.** No tier override.
- What would change the tier: the development ramp actually ending — filings in which development and capital-improvement spend falls back toward the FY2023 level (~$233M) while OCF holds, which would roughly double OE/share from the TTM base — or the price falling toward the low $20s. At $73.24 the buyer is underwriting the FFO multiple and the ~5% guided Core growth, and needs cap rates to cooperate; the OE screen has nothing to add beyond "not at this price."
- Data note (non-blocking): companyfacts had not yet absorbed the Q2 2026 10-Q at run time, so fundamentals/ttm stop at Q1 2026 (see the flags above); the enrichment files carry the same warning.
