## What it is
The world's No.2 agricultural-equipment maker (Case IH and New Holland Agriculture), plus CASE/New Holland construction equipment and a large captive Financial Services arm ($23,105M of financing receivables at FY2025-end, per the FY2025 10-K). Deeply cyclical: management described Q2 2026 as "the trough of the agriculture cycle" (Q2 2026 release, 2026-08-03). Consolidated revenues TTM $18,185M (fundamentals.json, +0.6% YoY); Q2 2026 consolidated revenues $4.8B, +2% YoY, net income $141M vs $217M in Q2 2025, adjusted diluted EPS guidance for FY2026 narrowed to $0.41–0.46 (higher end of prior ranges).

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-narrow
Justification: No.2 global ag-equipment franchise (Case IH + New Holland, ~12–14% global share vs Deere's ~18–20%) with a real dealer network, captive finance arm and a parts/service installed base — but returns prove it is narrow, not wide: ROE fell from 28.1% (FY2023) to 16.2% (FY2024) to 6.6% (FY2025), i.e. at/below cost of capital at the trough, and gross margin (31.8% / 32.7% / 31.5% for FY2023/24/25 on total revenues incl. Financial Services, fundamentals.json) is defended by mix, not pricing power — Agriculture net sales fell 11.5% in FY2025 with North America down 26.4% (FY2025 10-K). The roic_proxy series in fundamentals.json is unavailable for the recent years (debt tags blank — see DEBT_TAGS_BLANK), so the moat rests on the ROE path and margins quoted above; on that evidence a moat above 4 would need returns through the cycle that CNH has not shown. Deere's integrated precision ecosystem is stronger; Raven gives CNH a credible but not leading tech stack.
- FOR: #2 global ag equipment share (~12–14%) via Case IH and New Holland dual brands; global dealer network plus captive Financial Services ($23.1B financing receivables) that finances a large share of its own sales; installed-base parts and service annuity across a long-lived fleet; Raven precision-ag technology stack and full-line breadth (ag + construction).
- AGAINST: Deere's stronger precision ecosystem, brand and pricing power, with AGCO/Kubota pressure from both ends; pure cyclicality — farm-income-driven volumes, FY2025 Agriculture sales −11.5% (North America −26.4%), ROE 28.1% → 6.6% in two years; tariff exposure and South America weakness compressed Agriculture adjusted EBIT margin to 5.2% in Q2 2026 (Q2 2026 release); thin equity under a large captive-finance balance sheet — consolidated net debt $23,427M at FY2025-end (mostly Financial Services, but the risk is CNH's).
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $1.04 vs 3-yr median $0.56): both ends are working-capital artifacts of the cycle — FY2023 OE was crushed by a $2,268M build in trade/financing receivables (OCF $907M despite net income $2,275M), FY2025 OE was flattered by the reverse (inventory liquidation +$749M and receivables +$708M into OCF despite net income of only $510M). - **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2025): 2023 — net income $2,275M vs OCF $907M, driven by the $2,268M receivables build and a $535M deferred-tax drag, partly offset by $911M of provisions (FY2025 10-K cash-flow statement). 2025 — net income $510M vs OCF $2,538M: net income absorbed non-cash charges detailed in the 10-K ($313M writedowns of financial assets, a $172M non-cash IPR&D impairment from the Raven/Bennamann acquisitions, a $62M impairment of investments in unconsolidated affiliates — together in "Other non-cash items" of $647M), while OCF was lifted by working-capital release (inventories +$749M, receivables +$708M). - **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years; positive only 3 of 5 years with true capex): real cyclicality, handled by the TTM base, bear-weighted probabilities (15/30 on super bear/bear), and sizing the verdict to the conservative end.

The consolidated OE base therefore carries a finance company's leverage against an industrial's cash flows; the engine can give no leverage verdict (net_debt_to_normalized_oe: null in diagnostics), and this is one reason confidence is low rather than medium.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The investable question on CNH is cycle timing, not this DCF: at trough OE the stock screens expensive on owner's earnings ($11.60 vs weighted IV@15% $2.76), but trough OE is exactly what a buyer of a cyclical is supposed to look through. The honest statement of what the model can and cannot say: even the super-bull scenario (1.25× trough base, +7% start) yields only 8.47% IRR at $11.60, and the latest-FY OE/share of $1.04 — a WC-release year — capitalized at the base path would still not clear 15%. A bull case here needs mid-cycle OE/share well above $1.04 sustained, i.e. a full ag upcycle with margin recovery, which is a forecast about farm income and Deere-era pricing, not something in the filings yet. Q2 2026 showed stabilization (+2% revenue) but Agriculture adjusted EBIT margin fell to 5.2% — the recovery is not in the numbers.
- The preliminary zero-capex run (weighted IRR 14.26%, near Tier B) is a clean example of why the data gate exists: ~$1.19B/yr of real capex was invisible to the tag set, and it moves the answer by ~9pp of IRR.
- Balance-sheet risk is mostly Financial Services: $21.4B of FS net debt against receivables is normal captive-finance structure, but credit losses already produced a $313M writedown of financial assets in FY2025 — in a farm downturn, FS is where the tail risk sits.
