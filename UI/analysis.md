# UI — Ubiquiti Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Technology / communications equipment. Price $619.56 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap ~$37.5B (engine, 60.57M diluted shares) — inside the <$40B scope. Fiscal year ends June 30; FY2026 ended 2026-06-30, only ~3.4 months ago, so the latest FY is current, not stale.

## What it is
Designer of networking hardware and the software that runs it: UniFi access points, switches, gateways, Protect cameras, Access door controls and NAS, plus the declining UISP/Service Provider line for broadband ISPs. Enterprise Technology is ~93% of revenue (Q4 FY2026: $868.3M of $937.3M). The model is deliberately lean: sold through distributors/resellers and a community of installers, no large direct sales force, no per-device licensing fees — enterprise-grade kit at a fraction of Cisco/Fortinet total cost of ownership. Founder/CEO Robert Pera owns ~93% of the shares (per press coverage of the 10-K ownership), there are no earnings calls, and the float is tiny — the stock's 52-week range is roughly $500–$1,100 and it fell 42% in May 2026 alone.

Latest results (Q4 / FY2026, reported 2026-08-21, via search of the earnings release coverage): Q4 revenue a record $937.3M, +23.5% YoY and +18.9% QoQ; FY2026 revenue $3,274M, +27.2% YoY; GAAP EPS $15.85, non-GAAP $15.95; gross margin 46.2% for FY2026, up from 43.4% in FY2025 (Q4 45.8%, down 120bp QoQ on higher component and shipping costs, which management warned may keep rising with constrained availability). Four straight earnings beats. A $1.00/quarter dividend was declared with an intention to pay at least $1.00/quarter through FY2027, and the $500M buyback authorization was extended to September 2027. No post-FY quarter has filed yet (Q1 FY2027 ends 2026-09-30, earnings expected early November), so TTM = FY2026.

Gross-margin expansion (43.4% → 46.2%) plus operating leverage (operating income $1,185M, a 36% margin, on R&D of only ~7% of sales) drove the jump; it is operating performance, not an accounting artifact.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
**FOR —** (1) UniFi ecosystem switching costs: once an installer/SMB standardizes on UniFi OS, networking, Protect, Access and NAS are managed in one pane of glass, and ripping it out means replacing the whole stack; ~85M devices shipped across 200+ countries is a real installed base. (2) Structural cost advantage: no direct sales force, no licensing-fee infrastructure, R&D ~7% of sales and near-zero SBC support a 46% gross / 36% operating margin while undercutting Cisco/Fortinet TCO by a reported 50–70% — rivals cannot match the price without breaking their own models. (3) Proven pricing power: gross margin *expanded* 280bp in FY2026 even as component costs rose in Q4. (4) Capital discipline: debt eliminated, dividend initiated and raised, buybacks executed when the stock was cheap.
**AGAINST —** (1) No recurring revenue: this is one-off hardware, re-won every refresh cycle; there is no subscription/licensing annuity like the incumbents have. (2) No white-glove enterprise support — a hard ceiling on Fortune 500 penetration, the richest segment. (3) The model is copyable at the low end: TP-Link Omada and others are explicitly mimicking the license-free playbook. (4) Key-man and governance concentration: Pera owns ~93%, is the product visionary, and offers no calls or guidance — a single point of failure, and a tiny float that manufactures volatility rather than pricing information. (5) East-Asia contract manufacturing and component sourcing: tariff, supply and counterfeit/IP exposure, with management itself flagging rising component costs and constrained availability in Q4 FY2026. (6) Service Provider Technology is in decline, so growth is single-engine.
A 6 gives CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** — long enough to credit the ecosystem and cost structure, short enough to admit a hardware franchise with no recurring revenue can be caught.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a high-quality business at a price that has already collected the next decade of growth. Even granting the most current base ($14.88), the best growth the company has *actually printed* (35%), *and* a 25% OE uplift on top, the IRR is 15.2% — the super-bull case barely clears Andrew's base hurdle. Every other scenario is 5–12%.
- The method range is unusually honest here: $208 (median base) to $326 (TTM base) for IV@15%, Tier D under both. There is no defensible trailing input set that gets UI near 15% at $620.
- **Hurdle-clearing price: ~$326** (the weighted IV@15%) clears 15% on the final inputs; **~$228–245 with a 25–30% margin of safety** — i.e. roughly the level the stock traded at before the FY2025–26 re-rating, and well inside its own 52-week range, so a re-entry at those levels is not a fantasy scenario for a stock with this float.
