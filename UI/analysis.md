## What it is
Designer of networking hardware and the software that runs it: UniFi access points, switches and gateways, Protect cameras, Access door controls and NAS, plus the declining UISP/Service Provider line for broadband ISPs. Enterprise Technology is the bulk of revenue (Q4 FY2026: $868.3M of the $937.3M quarterly total, per the earnings release coverage). The model is deliberately lean: sold through distributors/resellers and a community of installers, no large direct sales force, no per-device licensing fees — enterprise-grade kit at a fraction of Cisco/Fortinet total cost of ownership. Founder/CEO Robert Pera owns ~93% of the shares (per the prior pass's review of 10-K ownership coverage), there are no earnings calls, and the float is tiny — the 52-week range is $500.23–$1,099.99 (Finnhub), so the price signal is noisy by construction.

Latest results (Q4 / FY2026, reported 2026-08-21): Q4 revenue a record $937.3M, +23.5% YoY (earnings release); FY2026 revenue $3,274,162,000, +27.2% YoY (engine rev_yoy_latest 27.22%); GAAP diluted EPS $15.85 for FY2026, $4.70 in Q4 (non-GAAP $4.73, a beat vs $4.03 consensus per MarketBeat). A $1.00/quarter dividend was declared, with an intention to pay at least $1.00/quarter through FY2027, and the $500M buyback authorization was extended to September 2027. No post-FY quarter has reported yet: ttm.py exits non-zero for UI — "no post-FY YTD fact (no 10-Q since the 2026-06-30 fiscal year end)" for OCF, capex, SBC and shares — so TTM = FY2026. Q1 FY2027 earnings are expected around November 6, 2026 (MarketBeat estimate). Analyst FY2027 EPS estimates (~$16.94, Zacks via MarketBeat) sit barely above FY2026's $15.85 GAAP EPS — the Street expects a sharp growth deceleration, which matters for the scenario sanity check below.

## MOAT: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
Justification: UniFi ecosystem switching costs plus a structural lean cost advantage and proven pricing power, against one-off hardware revenue with no recurring annuity, no white-glove enterprise support, copycat license-free rivals, and extreme founder/key-man concentration.
- FOR: UniFi ecosystem switching costs — once an installer/SMB standardizes on UniFi OS, networking, Protect, Access and NAS are managed in one pane of glass, and ripping it out means replacing the whole stack; structural cost advantage — no direct sales force, no licensing-fee infrastructure, SBC of only $7,444,000 against OCF of $928,682,000 supports high margins while undercutting Cisco/Fortinet total cost of ownership; proven pricing power — operating income rose from $836,280,000 (FY2025) to $1,185,442,000 (FY2026) on the revenue growth; capital discipline — long-term debt eliminated by FY2025, dividend initiated at $1.00/quarter, buybacks executed when the stock was cheap.
- AGAINST: No recurring revenue — one-off hardware, re-won every refresh cycle, no subscription/licensing annuity like the incumbents have; no white-glove enterprise support — a hard ceiling on Fortune 500 penetration, the richest segment; the model is copyable at the low end — TP-Link Omada and others explicitly mimic the license-free playbook; key-man and governance concentration — Pera owns ~93%, is the product visionary, and offers no calls or guidance, and the tiny float manufactures volatility rather than pricing information; East-Asia contract manufacturing and component sourcing — tariff, supply and counterfeit/IP exposure, with Q4 coverage flagging higher component and shipping costs; Service Provider Technology is in decline, so growth is single-engine.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years (dcf_numbers.json cap_years): growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.
