What it is

Oakland-based holding company for Pacific Gas and Electric Company, the regulated electric and gas utility serving Northern and Central California (~16 million people), plus the Diablo Canyon nuclear plant. Earnings are almost entirely regulator-set: a multi-year wildfire-hardening program (undergrounding, covered conductor, monitoring) drives the rate base. Current prints (company Q2 2026 release, via search results): Q2 2026 GAAP EPS $0.33 and non-GAAP core EPS $0.40; H1 2026 core EPS $0.83 vs $0.64 in H1 2025; full-year 2026 core EPS guidance reaffirmed at $1.64–$1.66; a $73B capital investment plan through 2030 structured to require no additional equity financing; at least 9% annual core EPS growth targeted for 2027 through 2030; a 12+ GW data-center project pipeline; over 1,900 miles of undergrounding planned complete by end-2027. The equity story remains inseparable from wildfire liability: California's inverse-condemnation doctrine drove the 2019–2020 Chapter 11, and SB 254 (signed September 19, 2025) is the legislature's latest attempt to stabilize the framework.

My thoughts / overlay

  • Method range, honestly stated: the v4 run (2026-10-13) put PCG at Tier B / 18.2% weighted IRR on a hand-built TTM core-EPS base; the v5 run is Tier F on the conservative OE base. The spread is entirely a base-definition artifact for a rate-base grower — neither number should be quoted without its method. What survives both methods: guided core EPS growth of ~9–10% is itself below Andrew's 15% hurdle rate, so even the friendlier earnings definition does not clear 15% on growth alone — it needed a multiple-re-rating argument, which is a regulatory-outcome bet (SB 254 implementation, the 2027–2030 GRC), not an owner's-earnings DCF result.
  • The balance sheet belongs in the verdict: net debt $57,495M behind a $29.417B market cap, growing by design under the no-new-equity plan ($4.4B issued in H1 2026 alone). For a regulated utility that leverage is serviced from allowed returns — but it is also why the equity, not the debt, is the shock absorber for any disallowance, restitution, or catastrophic-fire outcome.
  • The wildfire backstop improved on paper in 2025–26 (SB 254's $18B Continuation Account; ~$5.1B 2026 disallowance cap; PG&E's contribution share cut to 47.85%) but it is not cash in hand: IOU contributions run 2029–2045, and S&P analysts (via search results) estimate the January 2025 Eaton Fire could draw ~$15B against the original fund for Edison's liabilities alone. One catastrophic PG&E-caused fire remains a solvency event, not a scenario in a table.
  • Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (earnings-multiple or rate-base), which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the v5 method cannot support.
