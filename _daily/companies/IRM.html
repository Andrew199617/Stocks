What it is

A specialized REIT built on physical records storage, pivoting hard to digital infrastructure. Core Global RIM (records information management): storage rental and services (retrieval, shredding, scanning) for 240,000+ customers, retention ~93–94%. Growth businesses — data centers, Asset Lifecycle Management (IT asset decommissioning/remarketing, much of it hyperscaler kit), and Digital Solutions — grew more than 50% in Q2 2026 and represented 35% of revenue, up from 27.5% a year earlier (search results). Q2 2026: revenue $2.03B (+18.5% YoY, +16.8% organic), adjusted EBITDA $727M, AFFO $433M / $1.44 per share (+17%); records management retention 93.4% in the quarter (search results). Management raised FY2026 guidance: revenue $7.94–$8.01B (~16% growth at the midpoint), adjusted EBITDA $2.945–$2.975B, AFFO $1.76–$1.78B = $5.87–$5.93/share (search results). Data centers are the swing factor: revenue +39% in Q2, 110 MW leased year-to-date through July, ~325 MW scheduled to energize over the next 24 months (search results).

Flags — every fragility flag resolved

  • STALE_FY (latest fiscal year ended 2025-12-31, 9 months ago): resolved by construction — ttm.py was run, its full output is saved to ttm.json, and the final engine pass used the --ttm pin. The TTM base (−$2.10/share, inputs quoted above) is less negative than the latest FY (−$3.60) because TTM OCF rose and TTM capex eased vs FY2025 — but it is still negative, so the verdict is unchanged.
  • NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): structural for a REIT, not a one-off. In FY2025 net income was $152,254,000 vs OCF of $1,339,999,000 (financials_history.csv); the bridge is principally non-cash D&A of $1,024,435,000 (real-estate and data-center assets) plus the $140,280,000 SBC add-back, partly offset by working-capital absorption (ΔNWC +$209,686,000 in FY2025, financials_history.csv). No tax-deposit-style distortion was found. The direction matters: GAAP net income *understates* cash generation here — the opposite of the usual warning — which is why the OCF-based conservative OE, not net income, is the right series for this screen, and why the divergence does not contaminate the base.
  • SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (Real Estate): accepted in full — the OE/capex DCF fits REITs poorly, because most of IRM's capex is data-center *growth* capex (capacity largely pre-leased before it energizes) rather than maintenance, and the sweep's conservative definition charges all of it. Confidence is graded low and the tier is read as "fails the standardized conservative OE screen," not as "the rental business loses cash" — on the REIT lens the company guides FY2026 AFFO to $5.87–$5.93/share (search results), a positive and growing figure. The mismatch caveat does not rescue the hurdle math, though: at $115.00 against guided AFFO/share of $5.87–$5.93, a buyer needs AFFO growth plus the dividend to do all the work with no help from current owner's earnings, and nothing in the filed record supports a 15% owner's-earnings return at this price.

My thoughts / overlay

  • This is the most "explainable" F in the sweep, and the explanation still does not produce a pass. Owners are not being diluted away — share count CAGR is only +0.61%/yr and SBC is $138,876,000 TTM (key_numbers.md / ttm.py) — they are being *built* away: every dollar of operating cash flow, plus more, is poured into data-center capacity whose revenue is largely pre-leased but whose owner's-earnings payoff sits after the build wave. That can be good capital allocation and still be a bad price.
  • Leverage is the amplifier: net debt $16.273B (key_numbers.md) against a negative OE base. Search results put the company's own net lease-adjusted leverage at 4.8x at Q2 2026, the lowest since before the 2014 REIT conversion — but a November 2025 short report (Gotham City Research / General Industrial Partners) argued leverage on its methodology is roughly double the company's figure and that AFFO/dividend coverage look far weaker on a free-cash-flow basis. Those are allegations, not findings — no regulator finding is in the record reviewed — but the unresolved leverage-definition dispute is a reason in itself to size any IRM thesis to the conservative end.
  • Method range: on the v5 method there is one result, F, under every conservative base (latest FY −$3.60, median −$2.41, TTM −$2.10). The prior v4 run's recurring-capex sensitivity — a hand-built base that charged only recurring capex — reached only a below-hurdle tier and is superseded under v5, not averaged in: v5 requires an engine/ttm.py-derived base or a declared override, and neither produces a positive base here. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the sector mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the v5 method cannot support.
  • Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (AFFO/rate-of-return on the data-center pipeline), which is out of scope for this sweep.
  • What would change the tier: the build wave actually ending — consecutive filings in which total capex falls back toward recurring levels while OCF holds, making TTM conservative OE positive. The ~325 MW energization schedule over the next 24 months (search results) makes 2027–28 the first window where that could file.

  • Confidence: Low (sector-model mismatch: the F is partly a model artifact for a REIT in a build wave, though no alternative reading of the filed numbers clears a 15% hurdle either)
