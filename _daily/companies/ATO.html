What it is

Dallas-based regulated natural gas utility: the largest pure-play gas distribution company in the US footprint model — local distribution across eight states (Texas-heavy) plus Atmos Pipeline–Texas (APT), an intrastate transmission/storage system riding Permian/Waha flows. Essentially all earnings are regulated. The model is rate-base compounding: a ~$26B capital plan for fiscal 2026–2030 (over 85% safety/reliability), rate base guided from $18.8B in FY2025 to $40–44B by FY2030, and EPS growth guided at 6–8% through fiscal 2030 (search results). Fiscal year ends September 30.

Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics

Justification: Exclusive regulated gas-distribution franchises with captive, essential-service customers and rider mechanisms that put new capex into rates quickly, plus the APT intrastate pipeline/storage franchise in Texas — a durable, visible rate-base compounding machine ($26B FY2026–30 plan, rate base guided $18.8B → $40–44B). Capped at 6 because regulators set the allowed return (no pricing power beyond rate cases and riders), Texas political/regulatory scrutiny of riders and customer bills is rising, safety litigation is a live exposure, and the plan is funded with continuous debt plus equity issuance that grows the share count ~5.2%/yr, diluting the rate-base growth per share.

  • FOR: Exclusive regulated gas distribution franchises — captive, essential-service customers across eight states, Texas-weighted; rider/rate mechanisms recover safety and reliability capex quickly (annualized operating-income increases of $396M implemented fiscal YTD 2026, $227M of rate increases — Q3 2026 call highlights, search results); $26B FY2026–2030 capital plan with rate base guided from $18.8B to $40–44B by FY2030; APT intrastate pipeline and storage benefits from Permian/Waha through-system demand (APT spreads averaged $4.66 vs $1.77 prior year, fiscal YTD 2026); steady customer growth — nearly 51,000 added in the 12 months to June 30, 2026, ~39,000 in Texas.
  • AGAINST: Regulators cap returns — allowed ROE and rider recovery are set politically; Jefferies cut its price target on October 1, 2026 (search results) citing Texas elections, scrutiny of regulatory riders and customer bills as the biggest risk to the shares; safety litigation and reputational exposure (a wrongful-death suit after the Dallas Oak Cliff gas explosion, per search results); growth is debt- plus equity-funded — a $700M 6% senior-note offering in October 2026 (8-K, search results) and share count +5.15%/yr, so per-share compounding lags rate-base growth; long-run gas-distribution transition risk if electrification policy turns against residential gas.

Neither divergence distorts the base in Atmos's favor: the conservative OE series is built from OCF directly, and it is capex ($3,561,399,000 in FY2025), not the NI/OCF gap, that drives OE negative.

My thoughts / overlay

  • This is the model's known blind spot, flagged as such: Atmos is a profitable, dividend-paying regulated monopoly (dividend $1.00/quarter, $4.00 annualized, paid September 8, 2026 — search results) whose conservative owner's earnings are negative in 4 of the last 5 years because utility capex runs above operating cash flow every year of a $26B rate-base build. The dividend and the build are funded the utility way — rate recovery plus continuous debt and equity issuance — which an OE screen reads as negative distributable cash.
  • On numbers a utility investor actually uses (from search results, not the engine): fiscal 2026 EPS guided $8.40–$8.50 at a $163.94 price, long-term EPS and dividend growth guided at 6–8% through fiscal 2030 on rate base roughly doubling to $40–44B. That is a bond-like high-single-digit total-return profile before any multiple change — nowhere near Andrew's 15% hurdle on any defensible reading, so the F here agrees with the hurdle conclusion even though the model's mechanism (negative OE) is a sector artifact. No method range spanning tiers is claimed: on the v5 method there is one result, F.
