FIG — Figma, Inc.

Run date: 2026-10-14 (engine v5). In scope: market cap < $40B under either share count (see share-count note below). Price: $21.76 (nasdaq_quote_api, key_numbers.md).

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, or ttm.py output — none are recomputed.

What it is

The dominant browser-based collaborative design platform for digital products: Figma Design (the core canvas), FigJam, Dev Mode, Slides, Sites, and the AI line (Figma Make, the Figma agent, monetized via AI credits since March 2026). Subscription revenue per seat plus usage-based AI credits. IPO'd on the NYSE on July 31, 2025 at $33/share; the stock trades at $21.76 — below the IPO price despite revenue growth accelerating for three straight quarters. FY2025 revenue $1,055,788,000 (+40.96% latest rev YoY, key_numbers.md); Q2 2026 revenue $370.1M (+48% YoY, company earnings release via search); FY2026 guidance raised to $1.463–1.467B with net dollar retention of 136% and 15,964 paid customers over $10K ARR (Q2 2026 release via search).

Owner's earnings (SEC EDGAR, filing-direct)

The whole story is stock-based compensation. Figma's pre-IPO RSUs were dual-trigger: almost no SBC was recognized before a liquidity event (FY2023 SBC: $2,703,000), then the bill arrived in lumps — FY2024 SBC was $947,553,000, and the IPO put a $975.7M one-time catch-up into Q3 2025 (FY2025 total $1,364,133,000; the $975.7M figure is from Q3 FY25 earnings coverage via search and was recorded in this folder's prior run). Post-IPO, SBC is recognized normally — and the run-rate is still enormous: $316,552,000 in H1 2026 alone (ttm.py current-YTD input).

  • FY2025: OCF $250,681,000; capex $4,444,000; SBC $1,364,133,000. Conservative OE: −$1,117,896,000 = −$3.32/share (latest FY, key_numbers.md). Maintenance version: −$1,360,326,000 (financials_history.csv) — also deeply negative.
  • Normalized base (median of last 3 FY OE/share): −$3.32/share (key_numbers.md). OE-positive years: 1 of the last 3 — and the one "positive" year (2023, OE $1,040,894,000 on OCF $1,047,334,000) is an artifact: FY2023 OCF includes the $1B termination fee Adobe paid when the merger was abandoned in December 2023 (noted in earnings coverage via search). OE has never been positive on a clean basis.
  • STALE_FY resolution — TTM base through Q2 2026, computed by ttm.py from EDGAR 10-Q YTD facts (inputs quoted verbatim from ttm.py output):
  • OCF: FY2025 $250,681,000 − H1 2025 YTD $159,632,000 + H1 2026 YTD $158,201,000 = TTM $249,250,000

Thoughts / overlay

  • This is the screen working as intended, on a genuinely good business. Figma is not a broken company — it is a category-defining platform growing 41-48% with 136% net dollar retention and ~$1.7B of cash and marketable securities, trading below its IPO price. It is also, today, a business whose recurring equity pay consumes roughly 2.8× its entire operating cash flow. At $21.76 a buyer is not buying an earnings stream; they are buying the forecast that SBC/revenue collapses from its current level toward the teens as revenue scales, while growth holds near 40%. That forecast may well be right — SBC was always going to look grotesque in the first post-IPO year — but no filing yet shows the downtrend (H1 2026 SBC was $316,552,000, ttm.py), and the method grades filed numbers, not forecasts.
  • Method range: unusually narrow for a recent IPO — latest-FY (−$3.32), 3-yr-median (−$3.32), raw-TTM (−$2.74), and ex-catch-up-TTM (−$0.89) bases all give Tier F. The spread in this stock's value lives entirely in one assumption (the future SBC ratio), which is itself the finding. There is no price at which the OE DCF clears a 15% hurdle today, and no margin-of-safety price to quote, because the OE stream being discounted is negative; even at half the current price the buyer would still be underwriting the SBC forecast, not a filed cash stream.
  • What would change the tier: two to three quarters of SBC falling in dollars while OCF grows — i.e., TTM OE ex-catch-up crossing zero — then a positive base exists and the DCF can run. Watch the SBC line in the Q3 2026 print, not the revenue line; revenue is already proven. Governance note: dual-class stock leaves CEO Dylan Field with outsized voting control (noted in this folder's prior run) — minority holders cannot force the SBC discipline this thesis waits on.

Final verdict

  • Final tier: F (= tier by numbers; no override — there is no positive conservative owner's earnings to underwrite any hurdle, under any base)
  • Confidence: Low — high confidence in the arithmetic (OE is negative under every base and both definitions, with the one-time items identified), low confidence that a single tier captures a business mid-regime-change on SBC; if SBC normalizes, this name re-rates through the tiers faster than almost any other F in the sweep.
  • One-liner: Category-defining design platform, moat 7/10, growing 41-48% with 136% NDR and ~$1.7B cash and marketable securities — but owner's earnings are negative under every filed base (TTM −$2.74/share as reported; −$0.89/share even excluding the $975.7M one-time IPO RSU catch-up) because recurring SBC runs ~$700M/yr against ~$249M of TTM operating cash flow; it cannot clear the 15% hurdle at any price on an OE DCF until SBC falls below ~$232M/yr or cash generation roughly triples.
