What it is

The world's largest restaurant franchisor: KFC, Taco Bell, Pizza Hut, and the small Habit Burger Grill, overwhelmingly franchisee-owned and operated (search results put the system at 63,778 restaurants at Q2 2026, up 5% year over year). YUM mostly collects royalties, franchise fees, and rent on franchisee sales — an asset-light royalty toll on other people's capex, plus a minority of company-owned stores. Q2 2026 prints (search results): revenue $2.17B, up 12.2% year over year; worldwide same-store sales +3% (Taco Bell +7%, KFC +2%); worldwide system sales +5% excluding foreign currency; excluding Pizza Hut, system sales +7% ex-FX, unit count +6%, core operating profit +8%, and digital was more than 60% of system sales; adjusted EPS $1.62, up 12.5%.

Moat: 7/10 — strong franchisor moat

Justification: Two global category leaders (KFC, Taco Bell) run as a ~97%-franchised royalty toll with proven pricing power and returns on capital held through cycles — against zero consumer switching costs, demonstrated brand decay inside this same portfolio (Pizza Hut), franchisee-health dependence, and high leverage that limits downturn flexibility.

  • FOR: KFC and Taco Bell are the default global brands in chicken and Mexican-inspired QSR — brand mindshare built over decades is what franchisees pay royalties for; the franchise toll model itself (growth funded by franchisee capex while YUM clips high-margin royalties, OE positive 5/5 years including through COVID and the 2022 margin squeeze); scale in purchasing, advertising funds, and development across ~64k units no regional rival matches; the Byte by Yum! tech platform and >60% digital mix ex-Pizza Hut, raising franchisee switching friction; proven pricing power — repeated menu price takes with system sales still compounding ~5% ex-FX.
  • AGAINST: End consumers have zero switching costs — the moat runs through franchisees and habit, not lock-in, and QSR is in an open value war; brand decay is demonstrably possible inside this portfolio — Pizza Hut shrank to negative comps and is being sold (below); franchisee health is the real balance sheet (a single master-franchisee termination can close hundreds of units at once); secular/regulatory pressure — GLP-1 appetite suppression, labor and commodity inflation, food-safety headline risk; high leverage (see FLAGS) limits buyback support in a downturn.
  • A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.
