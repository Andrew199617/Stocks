What it is

EchoStar is the combined DISH / EchoStar company: DISH TV and Sling TV pay-TV, Boost Mobile retail wireless (moving to a hybrid MVNO model on AT&T's network), and Hughes broadband and satellite services. Every operating segment is shrinking: in Q2 2026 (company release, via search results) revenue was $3.58 billion vs $3.72 billion a year earlier, pay-TV subscribers fell by approximately 241,000 in the quarter to 6.39 million (4.68 million DISH TV, 1.71 million Sling TV), retail wireless fell by approximately 118,000 to 7.38 million, and broadband subscribers fell by approximately 59,000. The equity story is no longer the operating businesses — it is the spectrum sales: agreements to sell spectrum to AT&T (approximately $23 billion, 3.45 GHz and 600 MHz licenses) and to SpaceX (AWS-4, H-block and AWS-3 licenses), which the FCC approved in May 2026 subject to conditions including a $2.4 billion escrow (search results). DISH DBS Corporation filed prepackaged Chapter 11 cases on June 30, 2026 and was deconsolidated, producing a non-cash deconsolidation gain of approximately $9.73 billion in Q2 2026 (company release, via search results).

Moat: 3/10 — weak

Justification: the scarce licensed spectrum was the one genuinely defensible asset, and it is being sold; what remains is a melting pay-TV base, a subscale wireless brand that no longer owns its network path, and a satellite-broadband business losing subscribers to Starlink. There is subscriber inertia and a spectrum-sale payday, but no durable pricing power or switching-cost moat in the operating businesses.

  • FOR: Remaining licensed spectrum holdings are a scarce, regulator-created asset that strategics have paid tens of billions to acquire; installed pay-TV and Hughes subscriber bases with real inertia (losses are large but the base is still 6.39 million pay-TV and 7.38 million wireless subscribers, Q2 2026, via search results); Boost Mobile survives as a hybrid MVNO on AT&T's network with a long-term Starlink Direct-to-Cell commercial agreement attached to the SpaceX deal (search results), so the brands and distribution do not vanish with the network.
  • AGAINST: Pay-TV is in secular decline — subscribers fell every period reported, with no pricing power against streaming; retail wireless is subscale against the three national carriers and now depends on a wholesale partner's network after the 5G build was abandoned and decommissioned; Hughes broadband is losing subscribers against Starlink's scale; the balance sheet and capital structure are distressed (going-concern warning, DISH DBS Chapter 11 — see Flags), which hands bargaining power to creditors and counterparties, not to EchoStar.
  • A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth would hold 2 years, then converge linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.
