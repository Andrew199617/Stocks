What it is

U.S. LNG exporter built on modular, factory-fabricated liquefaction trains ("design one, build many," with Baker Hughes): Calcasieu Pass (commercial operations since 2025), Plaquemines (commissioning/ramping through 2026, 90 of the 127 cargoes exported in Q2 2026, commercial operations targeted Q4 2026), and CP2 under construction (first LNG expected H2 2027, per search results). Q2 2026: revenue $4.58B (+47.6% YoY), EPS $0.51, consolidated adjusted EBITDA $2.49B, net income attributable to common stockholders $1.35B, LNG volumes sold 466.4 TBtu (+41.7% YoY). Management raised 2026 adjusted EBITDA guidance to $8.70–$9.10B and the export outlook to 500–518 cargoes; 91% of 2026 cargoes were contracted at a weighted-average liquefaction fee of $5.05/MMBtu (search results). The earnings story is real and growing fast — the owner's-earnings story is not, yet, because the company is still in heavy build-out.

Owner's earnings

FY2025 (10-K via EDGAR, from financials_history.csv): revenue $13,769M; net income $2,733M; operating cash flow $6,566M; capex $13,365M (tag: PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment); SBC $46M (tag: ShareBasedCompensation — trivially small, a real cost but not the issue here).

  • Conservative owner's earnings FY2025: −$6,845M = −$2.60/share (OCF − capex − SBC). Capex runs at roughly 2x operating cash flow — that is the structural fact of this business today, not a one-off year: conservative OE was −$3,569M in FY2023 and −$11,590M in FY2024, 0 of the last 3 fiscal years positive (only 3 filed years exist — see THIN_HISTORY).
  • Maintenance version FY2025 (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): +$2,179M — positive because it caps capex at D&A ($941M). The gap between the two definitions IS the story: nearly all capex here is construction of new liquefaction capacity (Plaquemines, CP2), not maintenance of existing plant. The sweep's base case is the conservative definition, and on it there is no owner's earnings to discount; the maintenance figure is the standing evidence that the operating plants themselves do earn money.
  • Base used = TTM owner's earnings through Q2 2026, from ttm.py (filing-direct, no hand math; saved to ttm.json):
  • OCF: FY $6,566,000,000 − prior YTD $2,572,000,000 + current YTD $2,835,000,000 = TTM $6,829,000,000

Moat: 5/10 — moderate; a real cost/speed edge, no pricing power over the spread

Justification: Venture Global's modular construction model is a genuine structural advantage — it built and commissioned plants faster than traditional stick-built rivals and reached its 1,000th cargo about four years after its first export — and most near-term volumes are contracted (91% of 2026 cargoes; new 20-year offtake still being signed, e.g. ConocoPhillips 1 MTPA from 2030, per search results). But the product is a commodity spread (U.S. feedgas vs global LNG prices), the company owns no upstream gas, its returns swing with unsold-cargo prices, customer trust has been damaged by the Calcasieu Pass commissioning-cargo arbitrations, and the balance sheet is heavily levered. Durable asset position, cyclical economics — 5/10.

  • FOR: modular "design one, build many" liquefaction trains — faster, cheaper construction than stick-built rivals, with redundancy that let Calcasieu Pass beat its SPA obligations in Q2 2026 despite gas-turbine maintenance; 91% of 2026 cargoes contracted at a $5.05/MMBtu weighted-average liquefaction fee, plus a growing book of long-term SPAs (ConocoPhillips 20-year, 1 MTPA from 2030; expanded Atlantic-SEE, EnBW, TotalEnergies and Vitol agreements, per search results); scale and speed — 1,000th cargo exported ~4 years after the first, with Calcasieu Pass and Plaquemines operating and CP2 in construction; Gulf Coast location with access to U.S. feedgas and Atlantic-basin shipping.
  • AGAINST: no owned feedgas or pricing power — earnings are the Henry Hub vs global LNG spread, and unsold-cargo fee expectations swung from $9.50–$10.50 to $12.50–$13.50/MMBtu within 2026 alone; arbitration overhang from the Calcasieu Pass commissioning period — BP and Galp have won tribunals (Galp's damages capped at $170M, hearing expected 2027 or 2028; BP damages still pending), Orlen is the one case outstanding, and Shell/Repsol were won and Edison settled (search results) — the trust cost with the world's largest LNG buyers is real even where the legal cost is capped; heavy leverage — net debt $31,850M, interest expense +58% YoY in Q2 2026; concentration — two operating sites, both Louisiana Gulf Coast (hurricane, maintenance, single-region risk), with CP2 execution still ahead; governance — dual-class structure, VG Partners held ~97.6% of combined voting power as of February 13, 2026 (10-K, via search results), so Class A holders have essentially no say; Cheniere is larger, investment-grade, and the counterparty buyers trust by default.
  • A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.

No tax deposit or impairment one-off is needed to explain it, and the conservative OE series is OCF-based throughout, so the divergence does not contaminate the base; if anything it confirms the earnings are cash-backed at the operating level — the cash is simply consumed by construction capex below the OCF line.

  • SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (engine classifies VG as Utilities; the business is LNG export infrastructure): accepted in substance — the OE/capex DCF fits a company in mid-build-out poorly, because the capex line is overwhelmingly growth/project construction (Plaquemines completion, CP2) that will either become cash-generating plant or not, not maintenance of a steady-state asset. My thoughts / overlay

  • This is the model's known blind spot, flagged as such — with a twist versus a regulated utility: VG's negative OE is not permanent-by-design, it is a phase. Capex should fall hard once Plaquemines is complete and CP2 (first LNG H2 2027, per search results) is built — but "should" carries execution, spread, and refinancing risk, and the sweep prices the filed present, not the promised future. On the filed present: 0 of 3 years positive, TTM still −$2.67/share, net debt $31,850M.
  • On numbers an LNG investor actually uses (search results, not the engine): 2026 adjusted EBITDA guided to $8.70–$9.10B against a $36,758M market cap, 91% of 2026 cargoes contracted, volumes compounding at +40%+. That is a leveraged bet on LNG spreads and on construction finishing — it may well pay off, but the return driver is EBITDA growth and deleveraging, neither of which is an owner's-earnings yield today, and neither clears a 15% OE hurdle on any figure the filings currently support.
  • Method range: there is no defensible OE method range to report — every base the engine or ttm.py produces (3-yr median −$2.60/share, latest FY −$2.60/share, TTM −$2.67/share) is negative, so all methods land on F. The spread in this name is between the OE screen (F) and the EBITDA narrative (growth at a mid-single-digit EBITDA multiple to market cap) — that disagreement is a model-choice disagreement, disclosed here, not a range within the method.
  • Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (EBITDA/EV or a post-build-out steady-state OE), which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the build-out phase documented, rather than inventing a tier the method cannot support.
  • Real risks for a holder (outside the model): LNG spot spreads compressing as global supply (including VG's own CP2) comes online; arbitration damages (BP quantum pending; Galp capped at $170M with a 2027/2028 hearing; Orlen outstanding); refinancing a $31,850M net-debt stack at current rates while FCF is negative; and a dual-class structure (~97.6% insider voting power) that leaves Class A holders with no recourse if capital allocation turns.

Final verdict

  • Final tier: F (tier by numbers F — no positive conservative owner's earnings in any filed year or on TTM; OE DCF not supportable)
  • Confidence: Low (sector/build-out model mismatch plus only 3 years of history — though no alternative OE reading clears a 15% hurdle either; what would change the tier is capex actually rolling off, which is a 2027+ filing event, not an assumption to make today)
  • One-liner: Fast-growing LNG exporter with a real modular-construction edge, moat 5/10, but conservative owner's earnings are negative in every filed year and on TTM (−$2.67/share) because ~$13.8B/yr of build-out capex swallows operating cash flow — Tier F on this screen; no price clears 15% on owner's earnings until construction capex rolls off.
