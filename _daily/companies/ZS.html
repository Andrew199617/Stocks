What it is

The pure-play zero-trust / SASE vendor. Its Zero Trust Exchange (150+ edge locations) sits between users, workloads and applications: ZIA (secure internet/SaaS access), ZPA (private-app access replacing VPN), ZDX (experience monitoring), plus fast-growing data-security, Security-for-AI and Agentic SecOps (built on the Red Canary acquisition, closed at the start of FY26, ~$141M of ARR). Sold as per-user subscriptions to large enterprises; fiscal year ends July 31.

Latest earnings — Q4 / FY2026 (ended Jul 31, 2026; reported Sep 3, 2026), read past the headline:

  • FY26 revenue $3,353M (+25.4% latest rev YoY per dcf_numbers.json), Q4 revenue $898.2M (+25%). ARR $3.771B (+25%) — but only +20% excluding Red Canary's $141M ARR; organic net new ARR grew +17% in Q4, improving from +7-10% in prior halves.
  • Non-GAAP operating income $767.1M for FY26, non-GAAP EPS $4.21. GAAP net loss -$63.2M (financials_history.csv: -$63,179,000). Company-defined FCF $779.1M; the engine's OCF − capex for FY26 is $852,350,000 (financials_history.csv) — the company's figure also deducts capitalized software.
  • FY27 guidance is the story: revenue $3.908-3.938B = +16.6-17.5% — a guided deceleration of ~8 points, reaffirmed at the October 2026 Investor Day alongside Q1 FY27 revenue guidance of $935-939M. ARR guided +17%, FCF margin guided 23.0-23.5%. Also a ~3% workforce restructuring to fund AI initiatives, and a CRO transition (new CRO took over Oct 1, 2026).
  • Cooling leading indicators: RPO growth decelerated three straight quarters, deferred revenue growth below revenue growth. Peer read-across is unflattering: CrowdStrike and Palo Alto platform ARR momentum is running well ahead of ZS's organic net-new ARR pace.
  • AI is a genuine offsetting tailwind: Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially in Q4, ~70% of those deals attached data security, metered offerings ~30% of new/upsell ACV with ARR +100% YoY. Investor Day laid out a path to >$8B ARR by FY31.

Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, platform leader under bundling attack

Justification: Zero Trust Exchange architecture with real enterprise switching costs and telemetry scale, and working land-and-expand — against platformization/bundling by larger rivals, no endpoint/firewall base, unproven pricing power as growth is guided down, and rising data-center capex intensity.

  • FOR: Architectural switching costs — once an enterprise routes all user/app traffic through the Zero Trust Exchange, ripping it out means re-architecting access, not swapping a tool; ranked #1 in ZTNA-as-a-service and #2 in SASE (PeerSpot). Scale and telemetry: the exchange sees enormous transaction volume, feeding threat/data models smaller SSE rivals can't match. Installed-base compounding: 785 customers >$1M ARR (+18%), 4,182 >$100K (+20%), >$10M-ARR customers nearly doubled, dollar-based net retention ~115% and stable. Land-and-expand working into new categories from the same footprint — data security attaches to ~70% of AI deals, Security-for-AI bookings +50% sequentially, Red Canary extends it into SecOps.
  • AGAINST: Platformization — Palo Alto, CrowdStrike, Fortinet and Microsoft (Entra) can bundle SSE/ZTNA into suites ZS must sell standalone, and peers' current ARR momentum is stronger than ZS's organic pace. No endpoint, no firewall base, no owned user surface — distribution is an expensive enterprise sales motion, and the talent war behind it is exactly what the SBC line is paying for. Pricing power is not showing up: revenue growth has stepped down from the 5-yr CAGR of 37.87% (dcf_numbers.json) toward the guided ~17%, and RPO growth has decelerated three straight quarters — a widening moat usually decelerates more gently. Rising infrastructure intensity (capex $277.3M in FY26, building its own data centers) erodes the pure-software cost story.
  • A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.
