v5 run, 2026-10-14 (supersedes the v4 analysis of 2026-10-13 — see Thoughts for the method change and range). Price $106.08 (nasdaq_quote_api), market cap $34.35B, diluted shares 323,800,000 — in scope under $40B. Sector: Utilities / regulated electric & gas.

What it is

A regulated utility holding company serving electric and natural gas customers in Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, and Minnesota (We Energies / Wisconsin Public Service, Peoples Gas / North Shore Gas, other-states gas distribution, plus a stake in American Transmission Company). Earnings are what state commissions allow on a growing rate base. The growth story in current coverage (search results): a $37.5B all-regulated capital plan for 2026–2030 driven partly by very-large-customer / data-center load in Wisconsin (Microsoft, Vantage/Oracle), a long-term EPS growth target of 7–8% through 2030, FY2026 EPS guidance reaffirmed at $5.51–$5.61 after Q2 2026, and a quarterly dividend of $0.9525/share ($3.81 annualized) — the 23rd consecutive annual increase (all per search results).

Owner's earnings

FY2025 (10-K via EDGAR, from financials_history.csv): revenue $9,800,100,000; net income $1,555,500,000; operating cash flow $3,379,400,000; capex $4,398,100,000 (tag PaymentsToAcquireOtherPropertyPlantAndEquipment in the CSV for 2023–2025); D&A $1,478,500,000; SBC $48,200,000 (tag AllocatedShareBasedCompensationExpense — small, always treated as a real cost).

  • Conservative owner's earnings FY2025: −$1,066,900,000 = −$3.29/share (key_numbers.md / dcf_numbers.json: base_oe_latest_per_share −3.2949). FY2023 was +$515,800,000 and FY2024 +$391,400,000 on the same definition; OE was positive in only 2 of the last 5 fiscal years.
  • Maintenance version FY2025 (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC, from financials_history.csv): $2,161,400,000 — far above the conservative figure. The entire gap between the two definitions is the capex treatment: the conservative definition charges all $4,398,100,000 of capex, most of which is rate-base growth capex for a utility, while the maintenance version caps the charge at D&A. Neither extreme is clean earning power for this company; see BASE_DIVERGENCE and SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH below.

  • Balance sheet: net debt $19,989,900,000 (dcf_numbers.json); PPE net $38,278,100,000 (financials_history.csv, FY2025) — the debt is backed by regulated plant, but on this screen it is 49.9x normalized OE (see HIGH_NET_DEBT).

Earnings are growing — the low OE base is a capex-definition artifact, not an earnings collapse.

Moat: 6/10 — durable legal monopoly, capped economics

Justification: exclusive regulated franchises and a constructive home regulator give WEC durable, visible rate-base compounding, but the prize is a regulator-set return — there is no pricing power beyond filed rate cases, Illinois has already imposed disallowance/settlement costs, and the capital plan is financed with debt plus dilutive equity issuance.

  • FOR: Exclusive regulated electric/gas service territories in WI/IL/MI/MN — captive customers for an essential service, no competing wires or pipes.
  • FOR: Constructive Wisconsin regulation — search coverage puts ~75% of rate base under the PSCW with historically high allowed returns on equity.
  • FOR: $37.5B all-regulated 2026–2030 capital plan (search) converts spending into rate base at allowed returns; the Wisconsin Very Large Customer tariff framework addresses data-center credit risk (search).
  • FOR: Execution record — long streak of meeting guidance and 23 consecutive annual dividend increases (search).
  • AGAINST: Returns are capped by regulators — allowed ROE is the ceiling, and rate-case outcomes, lag, and disallowances are the main earnings risk (Illinois dockets are the proven example, per search coverage of the Rider QIP / bad-debt settlements).
  • AGAINST: The plan must be financed — heavy debt (net debt $19,989,900,000) plus planned equity issuance; share count already dilutes at +0.81%/yr (share_cagr in dcf_numbers.json).
  • AGAINST: Long-run electrification/decarbonization pressure on the gas distribution network, and affordability politics as rates rise to recover the capital plan.
  • A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.
