What it is

Global agricultural merchandiser and processor: Ag Services & Oilseeds (origination, storage/transport, crushing, refining), Carbohydrate Solutions (starches & sweeteners, ethanol via Vantage Corn Processors), and Nutrition (human and animal). FY2025 reported totals (earnings release, via search): revenue $80,269M, net earnings attributable to ADM $1,078M, GAAP EPS $2.23, adjusted EPS $3.43. A low-margin commodity toll-taker: FY2025 gross profit was $5,033M on that $80,269M of revenue.

Latest earnings: Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-04) — revenue $22,681M (+7.2% YoY), GAAP EPS $1.87, adjusted EPS $1.84 (+98% YoY), total segment operating profit up 75% YoY; FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $5.15–5.60 from $4.15–4.70 (search results). The driver is explicit in ADM's own release: the EPA finalized 2026/2027 renewable volume obligations in March 2026, lifting crush and ethanol margins, helped by elevated energy prices. That is a policy-and-cycle rebound off a trough — real, but not a durable growth rate. Adjusted ROIC on the trailing four quarters was only 7.8% per ADM's Q2 release (via search).

Red flags: (1) the 2024 Nutrition intersegment-accounting scandal — sales between Nutrition and the other segments were not recorded at market rates, inflating Nutrition's segment profit; ADM restated segment reporting in November 2024 and settled the SEC investigation in January 2026 for $40M (prior v4 analysis of this folder; segment-only, no impact on consolidated earnings or cash flows, so the consolidated owner's-earnings history used here stands). (2) Earnings are hostage to US biofuel policy, crush margins, crop prices and trade flows — all outside management's control. (3) Owner's earnings are extremely lumpy from working-capital / readily-marketable-inventory swings (see flags).

Owner's earnings

FY2025 (10-K via EDGAR, from financials_history.csv): operating cash flow $5,452,000,000; capex $1,248,000,000; SBC $83,000,000 (always a real cost, and small here).

  • Conservative owner's earnings FY2025: $4,121,000,000 = $8.51/share (latest FY OE/share, dcf_numbers.json)
  • Maintenance version (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): $1,799,000,000 — far below the conservative figure. The gap is the story: FY2025 OCF was flattered by a working-capital release as revenues fell, while net income was depressed by non-cash charges (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE below). Neither definition supports treating $8.51/share as run-rate.
  • OE positive 5 of the last 5 fiscal years (engine), but the conservative OE totals swing hard year to year (financials_history.csv): $5,265,000,000 (2021), $2,012,000,000 (2022), $2,854,000,000 (2023), $1,153,000,000 (2024), $4,121,000,000 (2025). This is a cyclical, working-capital-dominated cash stream, not a compounder.
  • Net debt $6,597,000,000 (dcf_numbers.json), 2.59x normalized OE (diagnostics) — manageable for a commodity merchant with liquid inventories, but it is not a net-cash cushion and it does not rescue the valuation.

Base used = normalized 3-yr median OE/share $5.27 (base_source: median_of_last_3_fy_oe_per_share). Reason: both single-point alternatives are working-capital artifacts in opposite directions — the latest FY ($8.51) is inflated by a WC release in a down-revenue year, and the TTM ($3.15) is depressed by an H1 2026 WC build (H1 OCF $1,299,000,000 vs $3,956,000,000 in H1 2025) that coincided with *rising* earnings (Q2 adjusted EPS +98% YoY; guidance raised). For a commodity merchandiser whose inventories swing by billions seasonally, the median is the only one of the three bases that is not a WC-timing snapshot. The spread between the three defensible bases ($3.15 / $5.27 / $8.51) is itself the headline uncertainty — see Thoughts.

Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate; scale without pricing power

Justification: ADM's origination, storage, transportation and processing network is genuinely hard to replicate and has survived a century of cycles, but it earns commodity returns: crush and ethanol margins are set by markets and by US biofuel policy, not by ADM, and through-cycle returns on capital are high-single-digit at best (7.8% adjusted ROIC on the trailing four quarters, at a cyclical *upswing* print). That is scale, not a wide moat.

  • FOR: global origination / grain-elevator / rail-barge / export-terminal network at a scale rivals cannot quickly replicate, with real logistics optionality when trade flows shift; diversified processing footprint across oilseeds, corn and Nutrition, so no single spread carries the company; equity earnings from unconsolidated affiliates ($648M in FY2025, earnings release) including the Wilmar relationship; balance-sheet staying power through cycles (378 consecutive quarters of dividends, per the Q2 2026 call via search).
  • AGAINST: commodity price-taker on both sides — ADM prices neither what it buys (corn, soy) nor most of what it sells; crush and ethanol margins are market-set, and the entire 2026 earnings recovery is explicitly policy-driven (EPA renewable volume obligations), i.e. the tailwind is a regulation, not an advantage; equally scaled, undifferentiated rivals (Bunge, Cargill, COFCO); Nutrition — the one segment that could have earned a moat premium — is where the intersegment-accounting fraud lived, and it is too small to move consolidated returns; working-capital-heavy model absorbs cash exactly when commodity prices rise; thin structural margins (FY2025 gross profit $5,033M on $80,269M revenue).
