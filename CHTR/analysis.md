## What it is
US cable operator (Spectrum brand): broadband, video, voice and an MVNO mobile product. FY2025 revenue $54.774B (−0.56% YoY). In scope under $40B — market cap $15.167B at the $110.11 price (nasdaq_quote_api). The defining fact of this run: **on August 19–20, 2026 — after every filing the engine read (latest 10-Q is Q2 2026, filed 2026-07-24) — Charter closed both the $34.5B Cox Communications acquisition and the Liberty Broadband merger** (search-cited). Cox Enterprises received the equivalent of just over 46 million Charter shares and now owns ~26% of the combined company's fully diluted shares; ~$12B of Cox debt and finance leases remains outstanding at Charter subsidiaries; the Liberty leg net-retired ~4.7 million shares. Combined footprint ~70M homes passed across 45 states. None of that is in the financial history below — every per-share figure is pre-merger standalone Charter, and the score must be read that way.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak, eroding
Justification: local cable footprint scale and converged broadband+mobile bundling still give pricing power and low churn, but fiber overbuilds and fixed wireless are taking subscribers outright, video is in secular decline, mobile rides on a rival's network (MVNO), and ROIC has only run ~9% — barely above the cost of capital, not wide-moat evidence.
- FOR: scaled local footprint — largest US cable operator after the Aug 2026 Cox close, ~70M homes passed (search-cited deal coverage); converged bundle — Spectrum Mobile grew to 12.5M lines (+406k lines in Q2 2026, search-cited), raising switching friction; operating margin expanded from 17.5% (2020) to 23.6% (2025) on cost discipline despite flat revenue (fundamentals.json); DOCSIS 4.0 network evolution to multi-gig symmetrical speeds defends the core product.
- AGAINST: fiber overbuild + fixed wireless — internet customers fell 172,000 in Q2 2026, −515,000 YoY, and revenue fell −1.7% YoY, the fourth straight shrinking quarter (search-cited); ROIC proxy only 8.91% / 9.38% / 9.04% in 2023 / 2024 / 2025 (fundamentals.json `roic_proxy`) — a thin spread over the cost of capital, which is why this is not a 7+; video in secular decline and broadband ARPU falling under retention promotions (search-cited); mobile is an MVNO on T-Mobile's network — the growth leg rides on a competitor's asset; extreme leverage (net debt $94.279B pre-Cox) leaves no room to answer a price war.
- Evidence note: Charter's filings do not present a gross-profit line (fundamentals.json gross margin is null for every year), so the margin evidence is the operating-margin trend above. ROIC history does not support a high moat: 6.80% (2020) rising to the ~9% plateau of 2022–25 — improvement, but never wide-moat territory through the cycle.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the **method range**: on the normalized median base this is a Tier B (weighted IRR 17.32%, IV@15% $130.33); on the TTM base of $30.03/share from ttm.json the same scenario structure prints Tier S. The entire verdict is the base choice, and the base choice turns on two facts outside the filings: the TTM share denominator was reversed by the Cox issuance in August 2026, and standalone capex stays at the ~$11.4B plateau through 2026 before the guided step-down. Anyone quoting one tier for CHTR without naming its base is hiding the ball.
- A second, larger caveat: **this score is for pre-merger standalone Charter.** The combined company carries ~26% more diluted share equivalents, ~$12B more debt, Cox's own OE stream, and at least $800M of claimed run-rate synergies — none of which is in any filing the engine has read. The first post-merger 10-Q/10-K will move this score materially in one direction or the other; treat this as the standalone anchor, not the combined-company verdict.
- Quality is not the debate: OE positive 5/5 years, operating margin expanding, mobile genuinely growing. The debate is whether a ~9%-ROIC, 39.61x-levered, subscriber-shrinking cableco's OE/share can compound fast enough — via the capex step-down, synergies and eventual buybacks — before fiber and fixed wireless take more of the base. The market price of $110.11 (down from a 52-week range cited in search coverage as $124.05–$422.29 as of June 2026) already prices a lot of the bear case; the bear case here (IV@15 $107.14) agrees with the market, the weighted case sits 18.4% above it.
- Margin of safety: thin. The weighted IV@15% is 18.4% above the price, but the bear IV@15% is below the price and the super-bear IV@15% is $76.34. A buyer here is paid for stabilization plus the capex step-down, and is not insured against the leverage if stabilization fails.
