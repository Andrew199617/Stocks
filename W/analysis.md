## What it is
The largest pure-play online retailer for the home: furniture, decor, housewares and home improvement sold through Wayfair.com and the specialty / luxury banners (Perigold, Birch Lane, Joss & Main, AllModern), mostly drop-shipped from third-party suppliers and delivered through its CastleGate network for bulky items. Asset model is light on inventory but heavy on logistics, marketing and SBC. Q2 2026 (company release, via search): net revenue $3.5B, +7.5% YoY (U.S. +8.7%, International -1.3%), orders delivered 10.6M (+6.0%), active customers 21.7M (+3.3%), average order value $332, repeat customers 80.2% of orders, adjusted EBITDA $242M, operating cash flow $360M in the quarter — described by the company as its strongest free cash flow since 2020.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, scale without pricing power
Justification: Largest pure-play online home-goods retailer with real scale assets — CastleGate bulky-parcel logistics, a deep third-party supplier/selection network, 21.7M active customers with 80.2% of Q2 2026 orders from repeat customers, and specialty/luxury brands (Perigold +35% in Q2 2026) — but no demonstrated return on capital and no pricing power in the numbers: gross margin has been flat at ~30% for four years (28.0% FY2022, 30.6% FY2023, 30.2% FY2024, 30.2% FY2025, 30.1% TTM in fundamentals.json), book equity is negative (-$2,782M at FY2025) so the roic_proxy series is denominator noise (-49.0% FY2020, +236.1% FY2022, -1.4% FY2025), and conservative owner's earnings were positive in only 1 of the last 5 fiscal years. Consumers face near-zero switching costs against Amazon, Walmart, IKEA and the big-box chains. A 4 gives CAP 7.2 years.
- FOR: Scale pure-play in online home goods ($12,457M FY2025 revenue, 21.7M active customers, repeat customers 80.2% of Q2 2026 orders); CastleGate specialized logistics for bulky / large-parcel delivery — the category generalists handle worst; supplier and selection network plus house / specialty brands (Perigold grew >35% in Q2 2026, specialty brands ~20%) with higher-margin mix; share capture in a fragmented market (Q2 2026 revenue +7.5% YoY, U.S. +8.7%, while the home category was roughly flat-to-down).
- AGAINST: No pricing power — gross margin flat at ~30% FY2023–2025 despite scale gains; promotion- and paid-search-driven demand. No demonstrated returns on capital — negative book equity, OE positive 1/5 years. Near-zero consumer switching costs vs Amazon, Walmart, IKEA, Home Depot / Lowe's. SBC-driven dilution — diluted shares 106M (FY2022) → 128M (FY2025) → 131M (current YTD), +6.5%/yr CAGR, SBC still $307M TTM. Levered and cyclical — net debt $1,796M plus $1,025M leases against a just-positive OE base; demand tracks housing turnover, and imported assortment carries tariff exposure.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 10 months ago in the preliminary wording; 9.5 months in the final diagnostics): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, quoted leg-by-leg above.
- **DILUTION** (share count +6.5%/yr): partly mechanical, partly real. Diluted shares rose 106,000,000 (FY2022) → 114,000,000 (FY2023) → 123,000,000 (FY2024) → 128,000,000 (FY2025) → 131,000,000 current YTD, driven by SBC issuance in loss years when buybacks were impossible. The SBC leg behind it is improving in dollars — $605,000,000 (FY2023) → $395,000,000 (FY2024) → $335,000,000 (FY2025) → $307,000,000 TTM — and 2026 convertible repurchases cut potential future dilution, but the count is still rising and the -5.00pp growth adjustment in the blend is earned, not a data error. It is one reason the recalibrated base start (6.7%) sits at, not above, the revenue print.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): resolved — the bridge is non-cash SBC and depreciation, not a tax deposit or a hidden cash one-off. From financials_history.csv: FY2023 net income -$738,000,000 vs OCF +$349,000,000 with SBC $605,000,000 and D&A $417,000,000; FY2024 net income -$492,000,000 vs OCF +$317,000,000 with SBC $395,000,000 and D&A $387,000,000; FY2025 net income -$313,000,000 vs OCF +$534,000,000 with SBC $335,000,000 and D&A $305,000,000. Restructuring charges from the 2024–25 layoff rounds and the Germany exit sit inside the net losses but do not change the bridge's character. OCF adds SBC back as non-cash; the conservative OE deducts it in full, so the divergence makes the base more conservative, not less — and the base is still only $1.95/share.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (base OE yield 1.8%): real and decisive — at $107.28 against $1.95/share of TTM OE, the price is almost entirely a growth-and-margin-expansion claim. Handled by recalibrating growth upward to and beyond the current prints (bull +13.7%, super-bull +21.6%); the tier still lands D by a wide margin, so the flag strengthens the verdict's direction rather than changing it.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 5.6x super-bear IV): real — a business at a profitability inflection on a levered balance sheet has a genuinely wide range. Confidence is capped (set to low, also for the 1/5 positive-OE history), and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; notably the whole spread, $7.34 to $40.82 of IV@15%, sits far below the $107.28 price.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 7.2x normalized OE): stated from the filing — net debt $1,796,000,000 (total debt $3,272,000,000 less cash $1,476,000,000) plus $1,025,000,000 of lease liabilities, against book equity of -$2,782,000,000. The 7.2x multiple uses the engine's normalized-OE denominator and is best read as "leverage is large relative to a just-inflected OE stream": in the bear cases (OE mult 0.75–0.90) there is little residual after logistics capex, which is why bear IVs sit at $7–13. Liability management in 2026 (convertible repurchases, new secured notes due 2030/2034 per company releases) extends maturities but does not delever the OE claim today.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range: F to D, all far below the hurdle.** On the normalized 3-yr median base (-$1.23/share) the preliminary run was Tier F (sub-type sbc_exceeds_cash: on the median window, OCF − capex is positive and SBC consumes it). On the latest-FY base ($1.01/share) the same scenario set would print roughly half the TTM IVs. On the TTM base ($1.95/share) with growth recalibrated to current prints it is Tier D at 5.01% weighted. No defensible base or growth set gets this price near 15%.
- The turnaround itself is not in dispute: revenue has re-accelerated from -1.3% (FY2024) to +5.1% (FY2025) to +7.5% (Q2 2026, U.S. +8.7%), SBC is falling in dollars, OCF is at a post-2020 high, and the company is taking share in a flat category. What is in dispute is the price of that story: $107.28 is ~55x the TTM OE/share and ~1.06x TTM sales for a business whose gross margin has not expanded past ~30% in four years and whose OE was negative in 4 of the last 5 years.
- What would change the tier: OE/share, not revenue. If SBC flattened and OCF margin expanded so TTM OE/share reached roughly $5–6 at the current share count, the same scenario machinery would start printing double-digit IRRs. That is a plausible 2–3 year path — it is just already paid for at this price, with leverage and a housing cycle still to survive.
- Quality note for the file: founder-led, genuinely improving operator, honest capital-return language (management itself calls SBC "a real economic expense" on the Q2 2026 call, per search reporting) — but on this screen quality without OE conversion at the price is a D.
