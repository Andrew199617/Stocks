# FWONA — Liberty Media Corporation, Formula One Group
v5 run, 2026-10-15. In scope (market cap $21.497B at $83.32, nasdaq_quote_api). FWONA is the Series A Liberty Formula One common stock; the Series C (FWONK) tracks the same group economics — the per-share owner's earnings below are per Formula One Group share regardless of series.

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, ttm.py output (ttm.json), or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Since the Liberty Live split-off on December 15, 2025, Liberty Media is a pure-play on the Formula One Group: Formula 1 (exclusive commercial rights to the FIA World Championship) plus MotoGP (Dorna acquired July 3, 2025), with minority investments. The FY2025 10-K presents both Liberty Live and Liberty SiriusXM as discontinued operations, so the consolidated continuing operations the engine reads ARE the Formula One Group — that is what makes the group share count in the OVERRIDE section the matching denominator. Contracted, asset-light rights income: race promotion fees, media rights, sponsorship; promoters fund the venues and teams fund the cars.

## OVERRIDE — shares (filing-sourced, declared under bounded authority)
- **The gap:** the preliminary engine run exited 4 with `DATA_GAP:shares` — EDGAR companyfacts carries no undimensioned diluted share series for CIK 1560385 after Q1 2016, because the filer tags share counts with class/group dimensions that the aggregated facts drop (the same failure mode as STZ). No tier was emitted on the gapped data.
- **The decision:** judgment.json carries `shares_override = {value: 258000000, kind: "filing", source: "Form 10-K for fiscal year ended December 31, 2025 (filed 2026-02-26), EPS note, 'Series A, Series B and Series C Liberty Formula One Common Stock' WASO table: Diluted WASO 258M for 2025 (basic 250M + 8M potentially dilutive shares); same table shows 243M for 2024 and 240M for 2023…"}`. The Formula One Group diluted WASO is the denominator that matches the continuing-operations OE numerator and the FWONA price.
- **Measured impact (key_numbers.md / score.json override_impact):** unblocks_tier = YES — without the override there is no tier at all (a shares denominator has no zero-fill counterfactual, so the without-override base and IRR are recorded as null, per the STZ lesson in the README). With it: base OE/share $2.98, weighted IRR 8.07%. The engine applies the single value to every missing period; because the 3-yr median OE total is the FY2025 figure itself, the base is FY2025 OE divided by the FY2025 diluted count exactly, and using each year's own filed WASO (240M/243M/258M) instead would change only the non-median years — the base is identical either way. The override is recorded in score.json and the central registry, and the ranking badges it.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong, contract-backed, but the teams keep most of the money
Justification: exclusive FIA championship rights with all 11 teams locked into the ninth Concorde Agreement through 2030, a scarce global calendar, long-dated contracted revenue and demonstrated pricing power, now with MotoGP as a second championship run on the same playbook — capped at 7 because prize payments hand the majority of the economics to the teams (thin OE margin), the split resets at each Concorde/media cycle, and leverage is real.
- FOR: Exclusive commercial rights to the FIA Formula 1 World Championship; all 11 teams signed the ninth Concorde Agreement (commercial + governance) through 2030; scarce, global calendar with long-dated contracted revenue — race promotion fees, media rights and sponsorship (Las Vegas GP extended through 2037; Pirelli through 2028); proven pricing power across successive media/hosting/sponsorship renewals; second championship under the same model — MotoGP (closed July 3, 2025), all manufacturers/teams re-signed through 2031; asset-light rights model (promoters and teams fund venues and cars).
- AGAINST: Teams take the majority of the prize fund — the rights holder's OE margin is structurally thin (base OE $2.98/share on $4,482M FY2025 revenue); economics reset at each Concorde / media-rights cycle and teams/manufacturers hold real bargaining power (next reset into 2031); leverage — net debt $4,045M is 5.3x normalized OE including MotoGP acquisition debt; calendar/geopolitical exposure (the 2026 Bahrain GP was rescheduled to Malaysia in October; H1 2026 F1 revenue fell 15% on race timing); diluted WASO rising (240M in 2023 to 258M in 2025 per the FY2025 10-K) so per-share growth trails group growth; single-franchise concentration after the Liberty Live split-off.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:shares / OVERRIDE_APPLIED:shares**: see OVERRIDE — gap, filing source, and measured impact (unblocks the tier; base $2.98, weighted IRR 8.07%) are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **STALE_FY** (FY2025 ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py was run and saved ttm.json. Its cash-flow legs are clean continuing-operations figures — OCF: FY $908M − prior YTD (H1 2025) $629M + current YTD (H1 2026) $673M = TTM $952M; capex: FY $119M − $55M + $65M = TTM $129M; SBC: FY $21M − $8M + $11M = TTM $24M — giving a TTM OE total of $799M. But its share leg matched a stale undimensioned total-company series (FY leg 340M for 2015, current-YTD leg 337M for Q1 2016 — the dead pre-split Liberty Media) and did not apply the shares override, so its printed TTM OE/share of $2.37 divides by a share count that has not existed for a decade. The --ttm pin was therefore NOT used; the engine's normalized base ($2.98) is used instead, corroborated by the TTM total ($799M vs FY2025 $768.0M). H1 2026's race-count timing (above) is a further reason a TTM pin through Q2 would understate the calendar-year run-rate.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: identified, three times over — the Braves split-off (July 18, 2023), the Liberty SiriusXM split-off (September 9, 2024), and the Liberty Live split-off (December 15, 2025), plus MotoGP consolidated from July 3, 2025. Revenue history straddles all of them ($11,400M in 2021 → $3,161M in 2022 on the restated continuing basis). Growth inputs were chosen only from post-break signals (3-yr CAGR, latest YoY) with the recalibration written above; the 5-yr CAGR and the OE/share CAGR were explicitly discarded.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): mostly an artifact of the breaks above — the 2023 OE total ($1,976M) rests on the total-company OCF tag and the 2024 total ($462M) is the first continuing-OCF year, not evidence the F1 franchise's demand swings 4x. Handled as instructed anyway: probabilities put 45% on bear-or-worse, and confidence is held at medium.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): 2024 total net income is −$2,063M (financials_history.csv) against continuing OCF of $567M because total net income carries the discontinued groups — the FY2025 10-K's EPS note shows a $(2,095)M discontinued-operations loss attributable to Liberty SiriusXM stockholders for 2024, the year the SiriusXM split-off closed. 2023's gap (net income $761M vs OCF $2,464M) is the same story from the other side: the 2023 OCF leg carries the total-company tag (NetCashProvidedByUsedInOperatingActivities, per the ocf_tag column) rather than the continuing-operations tag used for 2024/2025. The conservative OE series used for the base is OCF-based and, for the median year (2025), continuing-operations — the divergence does not contaminate the base; it contaminates the 2023 growth leg, which was discarded under BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt $4,045M, 5.27x normalized OE): folded into the verdict explicitly — it is why the bear cases carry 0.75/0.90 OE multipliers and 45% combined weight, and why there is no credit for refinancing or deleveraging in any scenario. The debt is serviced by contracted rights income, so this is a valuation-cushion problem, not a solvency call: at $83.32 there is no cushion at all.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (5.19x): real — outcomes hinge on the 2030 Concorde/media resets and on whether MotoGP replicates F1's trajectory. Confidence is capped at medium and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The franchise quality is not the debate — Concorde through 2030, pricing power, a second championship in MotoGP. The debate is purely the entry price against thin rights-holder economics: a 3.57% OE yield on a levered (5.27x) equity does not leave room for the model to be merely right; it has to be right and then some. Even granting the super-bull its 22% start and 1.25x OE level, the IRR is 14.23% — below the hurdle.
- Method range, honestly stated: this run's own preliminary pass on the engine's poisoned −0.68% blend printed a weighted IRR of 5.93% (IV@15% $23.03); the recalibrated final prints 8.07%. Both are Tier D, and the gap between them is the basis-break judgment, documented above. No defensible input set found in this run gets the weighted IRR near 15% at $83.32 — the verdict is robust to the method choices in a way APP's was not.
- What would change the answer: a price near the weighted IV@15% ($34.23) clears 15% on these inputs; between here and there, evidence that MotoGP's OE conversion (not just revenue) scales the way F1's did, or a Concorde/media reset that shifts the prize split toward the rights holder, would raise the base OE itself rather than just the growth rate.
- Dilution watch: diluted WASO rose 240M → 243M → 258M (FY2023–25, FY2025 10-K). Per-share compounding will keep trailing group compounding while that lasts.
