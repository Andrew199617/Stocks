# FTAI — FTAI Aviation Ltd.

Pass-1 run, 2026-10-16, engine release 5.4.0. Price $169.29 (nasdaq_quote_api), market cap $17,580,244,071 — in scope under $40B. All figures below are copied from dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json, financials_history.csv, or fundamentals.json — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Aviation aftermarket and leasing platform built on the CFM56 and V2500 engine families (the engines on 737NG / A320ceo narrowbodies). Two segments: **Aerospace Products** — the proprietary Maintenance, Repair and Exchange (MRE) model: selling/exchanging refurbished engines and modules ("green time" optimization) instead of full shop visits; and **Aviation Leasing** — historically on-balance-sheet aircraft/engine leasing, now pivoting to the asset-light Strategic Capital Initiative (SCI): closed-end third-party vehicles (2025 SPV raised $2.0B equity, ~$6B deployed; 2026 SPV launched) that FTAI manages and co-invests in, with the SPV fleet captive to FTAI's MRE shops. New third leg: **FTAI Power** — run-out CFM56 cores converted to aeroderivative gas turbines for data-center power, anchored by a $1.465B customer contract for 2027 deliveries (Q2 2026 8-K). Q2 2026: revenue $953.1M (+40.9% YoY), net income $117.6M, Adjusted EBITDA $291.4M; dividend raised to $0.50/quarter.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, real franchise, unproven cash conversion
- FOR: leading independent position in the CFM56/V2500 aftermarket — the two largest narrowbody engine fleets, with a long run-out tail as fleets age; the MRE module-exchange model is a genuine cost/time advantage over full shop visits (airlines buy green time, not overhauls); proprietary PMA parts capability; SCI creates captive MRE demand — SPV-owned engines are serviced exclusively by FTAI's shops; scale in used-serviceable-material sourcing (e.g., the 27-aircraft WestJet teardown/lease deal feeding CFM56-7B modules).
- AGAINST: gross margin has compressed every year as product sales mix grows — 95.7% (2021) → 64.9% (2022) → 57.1% (2023) → 52.4% (2024) → 46.2% (2025) (fundamentals.json) — the opposite of demonstrated pricing power at the consolidated level; concentration in two aging engine platforms whose fleets shrink from here; OEMs (CFM/GE Aerospace/Safran) and Willis Lease compete for the same aftermarket; roic_proxy is not computable from the filed tags (operating income untagged in EDGAR for FTAI — fundamentals.json returns null), so there is no filed ROIC history showing returns above cost of capital through a cycle — a 7+ moat is not supportable without it; ~10x debt/equity leverage means the equity is a stub claim on the asset pool.

## Red flags / research notes
- **Short-seller accounting controversy (Jan 2025):** Muddy Waters alleged whole-engine sales were booked as recurring MRE revenue in Aerospace Products, inflating the growth narrative; Snowcap amplified. FTAI's Audit Committee review (independent legal/forensic advisors) concluded the claims were unsupported; a securities class action followed. Unresolved in the market's mind and directly on-point for this sweep: if a large share of segment profit is asset-sale gains rather than recurring service margin, the "recurring EBITDA" the price capitalizes is lower quality than presented. The filed cash flows are consistent with the skeptics' narrower point — profits are not converting to operating cash.
- **Leverage:** net debt $3,148,415,000 vs book equity $334,174,000 (FY2025). The equity story depends on asset values (used engines/aircraft) holding.
- **Platform concentration:** CFM56/V2500 fleets are in long decline; FTAI Power (data-center turbines) is the hedge but has no deliveries until later 2026 and execution risk is real despite the $1.465B anchor contract.
- SBC is modest ($21,733,000 FY2025; $25,008,000 TTM) and share count is nearly flat (share_cagr +1.46%/yr, a small dilution headwind already in the blend) — dilution is not the issue here.
