## What it is
Latin America's leading flat-steel producer: Mexico and Argentina are its core markets, Brazil comes through Usiminas (consolidated since July 2023), plus Colombia and the US. Integrated iron-ore mining, steelmaking, and a service-center / distribution network selling value-added products (galvanized, painted, automotive and industrial) to manufacturing and construction customers. Demand and pricing are set by the regional steel cycle, import competition and global overcapacity — a high-fixed-cost cyclical, not a compounder. The defining current project is Pesquería, Mexico: new downstream facilities (completed) plus a new steelmaking plant expected to complete in Q4 2026 — the largest expansion in the company's history (FY2025 20-F).

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, regional scale without pricing power
Justification: Leading flat-steel producer in Mexico and Argentina (plus Brazil via Usiminas, consolidated since July 2023) with integrated iron-ore mining, a dense service-center/distribution network and a value-added mix — real regional scale, logistics and customer-embedding advantages. But the product is commodity flat steel with no through-cycle pricing power, and the return evidence says so: the `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json fell every year from 32.1% in FY2021 to 16.1% (FY2022), 9.6% (FY2023), 6.0% (FY2024) and 3.3% (FY2025) — a peak-cycle spike decaying to below any plausible cost of capital, not returns sustained above it through a cycle. Gross margin (fundamentals.json) compressed the same way: 38.5% in FY2021 to 23.9%, 20.2%, 16.4% and 15.1% in FY2025. A moat of 7+ on that record would be indefensible.
- FOR: Regional leadership — leading flat-steel producer in Mexico and Argentina and, via Usiminas (consolidated July 2023), Brazil: scale, integrated iron-ore mining and plants close to manufacturing customers give logistics and availability advantages importers cannot match at distance; Value-added differentiation — galvanized, painted and automotive/industrial products plus a service-center network and the ProPymes SME program embed Ternium in customer supply chains beyond plain hot-rolled coil; Balance-sheet capacity to invest through the cycle — engine net debt $887,783,000 at FY2025 on its cash-only definition (the company reported a $712M net cash position including financial securities at Dec 31, 2025) while funding the Pesquería expansion, the largest in its history.
- AGAINST: Commodity product, no through-cycle pricing power — operating income fell from $5,271,136,000 in FY2021 to $705,422,000 in FY2025 (financials_history.csv) as steel prices normalized; the cycle, not Ternium, sets earnings; Global steel overcapacity and Chinese export pressure drive import competition in Mexico and Brazil, and US trade measures cut both ways for a Mexican producer; Heavy recurring capital intensity — capex rose from $1,201,639,000 (FY2023) to $2,659,101,000 (FY2025) against D&A of $788,308,000, and conservative owner's earnings were negative in FY2025; Usiminas overhang — large noncontrolling interests, a $405M deferred-tax-asset writedown and a recurring litigation provision over the Usiminas participation acquisition in FY2025 (company 4Q25 release); FX and country exposure — MXN/BRL/ARS earnings, Argentine macro and controls, and Brazilian tax/litigation risk sit outside management's control.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *method range*, and it is unusually wide. On the sweep's conservative definition (all capex charged, including the largest growth project in the company's history at its peak spend), the base is $0.4332/ADS and the stock is a Tier D at any plausible growth rate. On the maintenance definition printed in the same filed history, owner's earnings were $1,280,423,000 (FY2024) and $1,585,480,000 (FY2025) — a different universe. The truth depends on how much of Pesquería's capex is really growth: the downstream facilities are complete and the steel shop finishes in Q4 2026, after which the capex leg should fall hard (Q2 2026 capex was already down to $431M in the quarter, 6-K release) — but a steelmaker's maintenance capex is never as low as D&A forever, and slab/ore integration capex recurs.
- Current prints support the recovery leg of that range: H1 2026 earnings per ADS $2.84 (vs $1.44 in H1 2025), Q2 adjusted EBITDA margin 17%, and the FY2025 dividend was $2.70 per ADS (the board later revised the FY2025 proposal to $2.20 per ADS in April 2026, per the company's release). None of that is in the scored base — by design, for an IFRS filer with a trough median — and it is why this Tier D should be read as "the conservative model cannot pay for this price", not as "the business is worth $3".
- What would change the verdict: a filed FY2026 showing capex falling toward D&A while H1's prices hold — that would lift the median base mechanically on the next re-run. What would confirm it: steel prices relapsing before the capex leg falls, or another Usiminas deferred-tax/litigation charge of the FY2025 size.
- Margin of safety: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at or below the weighted IV@15% of $3.03 per ADS on the scored base. At $57.10 there is no margin of safety on this method — the weighted IV@15% sits 94.7% below the price (the engine's upside_at_15 of −94.7%).
