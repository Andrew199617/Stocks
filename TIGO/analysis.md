# TIGO — Millicom International Cellular S.A.
Engine 5.4.0 run, 2026-10-17. All figures below are copied from dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, fundamentals.json, or the cited company releases — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Luxembourg-headquartered Latin American telecom operating as Tigo: mobile and fixed (fiber/cable) connectivity, pay TV, Tigo Money mobile financial services, and Tigo Business (cloud, security, data centers) across Bolivia, Colombia, Costa Rica, El Salvador, Guatemala, Honduras (joint venture), Nicaragua, Panama and Paraguay. In a transformative perimeter expansion it bought Telefónica's operations in Uruguay and Ecuador (closed during 2025), then in early 2026 closed the remaining EPM stake in UNE/Tigo Colombia (January 29, 2026, ~$571M) and Telefónica's 67.5% of Coltel in Colombia (February 6, 2026, ~$214M), with a Chile transaction also completed in 2026. Market cap $13.92B at $82.87 (nasdaq_quote_api) — in scope under $40B.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: Leading local scale, spectrum and fiber assets give a real but narrow moat: gross margins are high and rising and ROIC has inflected to 12–15% in 2024–25, but ROIC ran at 4.5–7.7% through 2020–2023, the customer base is low-ARPU prepaid, and Claro contests nearly every market — a 5, not a 7.
- FOR: Leading (#1/#2) mobile and fixed positions across nine Latin American markets, with spectrum licenses and a fiber footprint of about 14 million homes passed; gross margin high and rising — 71.9% in FY2021 to 77.5% in FY2025 (fundamentals.json); ROIC proxy inflected to 12.2% in FY2024 and 15.0% in FY2025 (fundamentals.json roic_proxy series: 14.2% / 7.7% / 7.4% / 6.1% / 4.5% / 5.1% / 7.7% / 7.0% / 12.2% / 15.0% for FY2016–FY2025); scale in each local market plus Tigo Money and Tigo Business attach on the same customer base.
- AGAINST: ROIC proxy was only 4.5–7.7% in 2020–2023 — at or below the cost of capital through most of the cycle, so the ≥7 rubric test (ROIC well above the cost of capital through a cycle) is not met; América Móvil (Claro) competes in nearly every market with deeper pockets; low-income prepaid base — mobile ARPU $6.2/month in FY2025 (Q4 2025 release) — limited pricing power, high churn; FX and political exposure (Bolivia dollar shortage, Colombian regulation) plus high leverage limits reinvestment flexibility; capex intensity rising on the acquired perimeter (H1 2026 capex +48.8% YoY as reported).
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *method range*: on the pre-expansion FY2025 base the stock needs the super-bull case (15.37% IRR, IV@15% $85.55) merely to clear a 15% hurdle at $82.87. A buyer at this price is paying today for Colombia/Chile integration that has not yet printed a full year — the Q2 2026 evidence (Ecuador and Uruguay already at Millicom-average margins per management, record quarterly equity FCF) says the playbook can work, but the DCF base cannot see it and this pass does not hand-build a pro-forma base.
- Quality is mid-tier telecom: genuinely strong and rising gross margins and a real ROIC inflection in 2024–25, against a decade of mid-single-digit ROIC, $6.2 monthly mobile ARPU, and a levered balance sheet that just absorbed four acquisitions.
- The dividend ($3.00/share declared in May 2026 plus an additional $1.50 interim declared August 5, 2026, per the Q2 2026 release) is a real cash return the OE model only captures indirectly; it does not close a 43.6% gap to the weighted IV@15%.
- Hurdle-clearing price: the shares clear 15% at or below the weighted IV@15% of $46.70 — roughly 44% below the current price. They cleared that level as recently as the past year's trading range (the stock has run from the high-$30s/low-$40s area to $82.87).
