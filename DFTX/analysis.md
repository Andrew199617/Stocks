# DFTX — Definium Therapeutics, Inc. (formerly Mind Medicine / MindMed)
Run date: 2026-10-12 (engine v4). In scope: market cap ~$4.73B < $40B (share-count note below).

## What it is
Pre-revenue, clinical-stage psychedelic psychiatry company (New York HQ, Nasdaq), renamed from Mind Medicine (MindMed) Inc. in January 2026. The entire value story is one asset: **DT120 ODT**, a pharmaceutical-grade orally disintegrating tablet formulation of lysergide (LSD) tartrate (Zydis fast-dissolve), dosed once under supervision (~6 hours to discharge readiness) as a serotonin-2A agonist for brain-health disorders. By this run date it has produced **three positive Phase 3 readouts**: Emerge in major depressive disorder (MDD) in June 2026 (8.1-point placebo-adjusted MADRS improvement at Week 6, 7.3 points at Week 12, no serious adverse events or suicidality signal), Voyage in generalized anxiety disorder (GAD) in August 2026, and Panorama in GAD in September 2026 (HAM-A −9.8 on 100 µg vs −4.7 on placebo at Week 12). DT120 holds FDA Breakthrough Therapy designation in both GAD and MDD. Next steps per the company: a pre-NDA meeting with FDA in late 2026 and an NDA filing targeted for H1 2027; a second MDD trial (Ascend) is enrolling with topline expected 2027, a PTSD trial (Haven) is planned for 2027, and DT402 (an MDMA R-enantiomer) is only in Phase 2a for autism spectrum disorder. There has never been any product revenue: revenue is $0 / not reported in every fiscal year in the EDGAR history. ~106 employees. The April 2026 White House executive order directing agencies to accelerate psychedelic treatments is a genuine policy tailwind, and big-pharma M&A in the space (Lilly/AtaiBeckley, AbbVie/Gilgamesh, Otsuka/Transcend) validates the category — and arms the competition.

## Moat score: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, and entirely contingent on approval
FOR — (1) Execution/data lead in psychedelic psychiatry: three positive Phase 3 trials across two large indications (GAD and MDD), Breakthrough Therapy designation in both, and a pre-NDA meeting already scheduled — the most advanced LSD program in existence, ahead of every rival psychedelic program on breadth of late-stage evidence. (2) Formulation and dosing IP around the ODT lysergide product (Zydis fast-dissolve, dose/regimen patents) plus regulatory exclusivity if approved — a copycat cannot sell the identical product on day one. (3) Delivery know-how: a supervised single-dose model with ~6-hour discharge, faster and simpler than COMPASS's ~6–8-hour psilocybin session, aimed at the existing interventional-psychiatry site base. (4) Balance-sheet staying power (below) means the lead does not die for lack of cash before the FDA decides.
AGAINST — (1) The active molecule is lysergide — a long-known, off-patent Schedule I compound. The moat covers a formulation and a protocol, not the molecule; determined rivals (COMPASS's COMP360 psilocybin, Lilly's AtaiBeckley BPL-003, AbbVie's Gilgamesh program) route around it with differentiated compounds, and big-pharma balance sheets now back them. (2) Zero pricing power demonstrated: no approved product, no payer contracts, and reimbursement for a supervised multi-hour psychedelic session is unproven. (3) Functional unblinding is a live scientific criticism of every psychedelic trial — patients can tell they were dosed — which payers and FDA reviewers can press on effect size. (4) Regulatory moat cuts both ways: DEA and state rescheduling of a Schedule I substance sit between FDA approval and first revenue. (5) Single-asset concentration: DT402 is Phase 2a; if DT120 stumbles at FDA, there is no second leg.
CAP from moat 4: clamp(2 + 1.3×4) = **7.2 years** — moot for the DCF (no positive OE to fade), but it records the protected window even in an approval world.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is the screen working as intended: DFTX is not an owner's-earnings investment at any price — with negative OE in every period, there is **no price at which the OE DCF clears a 15% hurdle**, and no margin-of-safety price to quote. Anyone buying at $35.20 is buying the probability-weighted pipeline: FDA acceptance/approval of an H1-2027 NDA, DEA rescheduling, payer coverage for supervised dosing, and uptake against cheap generic SSRIs/SNRIs, Spravato's installed base, and incoming Lilly/AbbVie-backed rivals.
- Credit where due vs the closest comp in this sweep (CMPS, also Tier F): Definium has the broader late-stage evidence (three Phase 3 wins across two indications vs COMPASS's TRD-only program), a shorter supervised session, and a much larger cash pile (~$1.05B net vs ~$0.38B). None of that changes the OE arithmetic today — it changes the quality of the option, not the tier.
- Momentum cuts both ways: the stock is up >200% in 2026 (52-week range $10.47–$49.70, beta ~2.2), so the approval option is far from unpriced; the June raise at $34.00 is roughly where the stock still trades.
- What would change the tier: approval plus 2–3 years of post-launch filings showing revenue covering the ~$200M+/yr operating burn. Until the first positive-OE year exists, DFTX stays F in this sweep regardless of trial headlines.
- Method range: unusually, there is none — latest-FY, SBC-corrected FY, 3-yr-median, and filing-derived TTM bases all give Tier F. The spread in this stock's value lives entirely outside the OE method (approval probability × peak sales), which is the finding.
