# JD — JD.com, Inc. (Nasdaq ADS; HKEX 9618)
Run date 2026-10-14, engine v5 (v5 re-run of the 2026-10-13 v4 pass). Price: **$26.60 per ADS** (Nasdaq quote API, per dcf_numbers.json).

**ADS basis (read first).** Each JD ADS represents **2 Class A ordinary shares** — stated in the company's own Form 20-F for FY2024 (filed 2025-04-17), Item 9.A, and on the 20-F cover for the registered ADS class. EDGAR's diluted share count (2,978,037,127 latest) is in *ordinary* shares, so judgment.json carries a filing-sourced `ads_ratio` of 2.0 and the engine scaled every per-share figure ×2: all OE/share and IV figures below are **per ADS**, directly comparable to the $26.60 price. One artifact to know: the engine's `market_cap_est` ($79,215,787,578.20) multiplies the per-ADS price by the ordinary-share count and so double-counts the ADS ratio; the universe build that admitted JD to this sweep carried a market cap of $36.409B, under the $40B cutoff. `in_scope_under_40b` is set to false in judgment.json only because make_score.py computes scope from the engine's overstated figure — economically JD entered the sweep in scope.

## What it is
China's leading supply-chain-based retailer: JD Retail (1P direct sales — electronics & home appliances is the stronghold — plus a growing 3P marketplace and marketing/ads), JD Logistics (owned warehouse/delivery network, increasingly sold to third parties; on-demand delivery was reclassified into this segment in January 2026), and New Businesses (JD Food Delivery, Jingxi, JD Property, Joybuy overseas). FY2025 revenue was $187,197,000,000 (financials_history.csv, USD as filed/translated), +17.9% YoY — flattered by the government trade-in subsidy wave in electronics/appliances. The recovery since is bottom-line-led: Q2 2026 revenue fell 2.9% YoY (electronics & home appliances −11.8% on the subsidy high base; general merchandise +5.6%), while non-GAAP net income rose 20.8% YoY as JD Retail printed a record 4.6% operating margin for a promotional quarter and JD Food Delivery's losses narrowed by more than 50% YoY (Q2 2026 results, reported 2026-08-13).

## Owner's earnings
Conservative definition (OCF − total capex − SBC, USD totals from financials_history.csv): FY2023 $7,139,000,000; FY2024 $6,815,000,000; **FY2025 $922,000,000**. The FY2025 collapse is identified, not mysterious: FY2025 operating income fell to $397,000,000 (from $5,307,000,000 in FY2024) as the JD Food Delivery subsidy war against Meituan and Alibaba — launched February 2025 — burned through the group P&L, and FY2025 OCF fell to $2,716,000,000 (from $7,959,000,000) on the same losses plus trade-in-program receivables timing. The maintenance definition (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC) prints $10,618,363,520 for FY2025 — distorted *up* by a working-capital inflow (ΔNWC −$7,033,363,520 in the CSV), i.e. supplier float, not earnings power. The OCF-based conservative definition is the anchor throughout.

**Base used: the engine's normalized base — median of the last 3 FY OE/share = $4.4310/ADS** (base_oe_per_share in dcf_numbers.json; latest FY alone is $0.6192/ADS). Why the median and not a TTM pin: STALE_FY directed a TTM check, and `ttm.py JD` exited 3 with: "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for JD (CIK 1549802): ocf: no annual (FY) fact; sbc: no annual (FY) fact; shares_diluted: no annual (FY) fact; capex: no capex facts in companyfacts under any candidate tag and no capex_override in judgment.json — refusing to zero-fill". ## Moat score: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
**FOR:**
- Owned logistics network (warehouses, employed couriers, full-time delivery rider fleet) sets the same/next-day standard in Chinese e-commerce and is increasingly monetized to third parties.
- 1P supply-chain scale in electronics/appliances: procurement leverage and authenticity trust; JD Retail gross margin expanded 17 straight quarters to a record operating margin in H1 2026 — the core got *more* profitable through the war.
- Mix shift to asset-light services (marketplace, marketing/ads, logistics services) raises the group's structural margin ceiling.
- Fortress liquidity (cash, restricted cash and short-term investments of RMB 235.1B at June 2026, per the Q2 2026 release) plus buybacks fund the investment phase without balance-sheet stress.

**AGAINST:**
- Almost no consumer lock-in: shoppers multi-home across Alibaba, Pinduoduo and Douyin with near-zero switching costs outside the delivery-speed niche.
- Food delivery is a bought, anti-moat position: #3 against Meituan's structural density lead, and it consumed the group's FY2025 economics — proof management will spend core profit on share-buying when it decides to.
- Structurally thin group economics: net margin 2–3% in good years, modest returns on a huge logistics asset base.
- Recurring regulatory/political overhang: a SAMR fine hit H1 2026, regulators have repeatedly intervened in the subsidy war, and the ADS structure carries the standing China/VIE tail risk.
- Category concentration: electronics/appliances demand is now partly a function of fading government trade-in subsidy policy (Q2 2026 category revenue −11.8% YoY).

CAP at moat 5 = 2 + 1.3 × 5 = **8.5 years** (cap_years in dcf_numbers.json): growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag from the final dcf_numbers.json, resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 9 months ago): ttm.py was run and exited 3 — it cannot compute a TTM for JD (no annual FY facts under its us-gaap YTD method for this 20-F filer; full message quoted in the Owner's earnings section). Per spec, the engine's median base is used instead, with the Q2 2026 interim evidence (revenue −2.9% YoY, non-GAAP net income +20.8%, delivery losses narrowing >50%) folded into the scenario design rather than into a hand-built base.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $0.6192/ADS vs 3-yr median $4.4310/ADS): *both* are distorted, in opposite directions. The latest year is the trough of a management-declared peak-investment year — the food-delivery launch took FY2025 operating income to $397,000,000 from $5,307,000,000 — plus receivables timing in OCF. The median is FY2024 economics, which assumed New Businesses ran at near-breakeven; H1 2026 does not yet support a full return to that level. Resolution: the median is the v5-standard normalized base and the only code-produced base available for this filer (ttm.py cannot run), so it is used — and its flattery is charged against the result through 55% bear/super-bear probability weight, a 0.90 bear OE multiplier, downward-recalibrated offsets, and low confidence. The method range this creates is stated in the overlay.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.50× super-bear IV): real — the outcome hinges on whether a segment that consumed FY2025's group profit goes to ~zero losses or stays a structural burn. Confidence is **low** because of it.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range >3× over the last 5 years): structural to current strategy, not noise — group OE is now partly a policy variable set by how much management chooses to invest in New Businesses. Handled by the normalized (not trough, not peak) base, the bear-skewed probabilities, and the 0.75 super-bear multiplier.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023: net income $3,275,000,000 vs OCF $8,383,000,000, financials_history.csv): JD's negative-working-capital float model — customers pay immediately, suppliers are paid on extended terms — makes OCF structurally larger than net income; 2023 net income was additionally weighed by non-cash investment fair-value/impairment-type items that do not touch operating cash flow. This is the float model working as designed, not an earnings-quality red flag, and it is why the OCF-based conservative OE (not the NI-based maintenance version) is the right anchor for JD.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 2.17%/yr, share_cagr −2.17%): real and already credited via the engine's +2.17pp growth adjustment. Caveat from the Q2 2026 reporting: the buyback program that funded the H1 2026 repurchases was nearly exhausted, so the tailwind slows unless the program is renewed.
- **FX_CONVERTED** (reporting currency CNY converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates — flows use period-end rates, not period averages): noted. The rates used are recorded in dcf_numbers.json (e.g. 0.137 at 2024-12-31, 0.14284 at 2025-12-31) and only the da, sbc, debt_current and debt_lt fields required conversion in this run.
- **USD_TRANSLATIONS_USED**: noted — where the issuer files its own USD convenience translations alongside CNY, the engine used the filed USD figures; the base rests partly on those issuer translations.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (per-share figures scaled ×2.0 ordinary shares per ADS): resolved by the filing-sourced ads_ratio in judgment.json (Form 20-F for FY2024, filed 2025-04-17, Item 9.A and cover). Without it the preliminary run compared per-ordinary-share OE against the per-ADS price and understated every IV by half.
- Net-cash note (the preliminary run flagged BIG_NET_CASH; the final run's diagnostics carry the same substance): net_debt_latest is −$13,250,483,520 and net debt / normalized OE is −1.00×, i.e. the engine reads JD as modestly net cash on cash-and-equivalents vs debt lines alone. The company's reported liquidity (RMB 235.1B of cash, restricted cash and short-term investments at June 2026) is larger than the engine's cash line, so leverage is not a reason to discount the OE stream — but the debt funding the logistics/property asset base is real, so no "cash pile" upside is claimed either.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the honest headline.** The tier is base-choice-dominated: the FY2025 trough base ($0.6192/ADS) would not clear the hurdle at all, this run's normalized median base ($4.4310/ADS) produces Tier A, and a recovered-TTM base would land between them. The v5 mechanics prescribe the median here (ttm.py cannot produce a pin for a 20-F filer), and the conservatism has been loaded into probabilities, offsets and confidence instead — but a reader should treat this as a B-quality situation wearing an A tier, and size it accordingly.
- The core is quietly excellent: JD Retail operating profit grew through FY2025 and printed record margins in H1 2026 while absorbing the war's group overhead. If New Businesses merely stopped getting worse, group OE would re-rate mechanically toward the base this run already assumes — which is exactly why the base case needs only +5.99% OE growth, and why the bear case (losses narrow slowly, OE stuck below FY2024) still clears the current price.
- Red flags to keep watching: (1) management's demonstrated willingness to burn a year of group profit on a new front — food delivery today, Joybuy Europe (revenue doubling off a small base, losses rising with scale) tomorrow; (2) the electronics subsidy cliff — Q2 2026 printed the first YoY revenue decline since listing; (3) recurring regulatory friction (SAMR fine in H1 2026, food-delivery oversight) as a standing cost of the strategy; (4) SBC is a real, material cost already deducted in the conservative OE, and the buyback that offsets dilution is nearly exhausted.
- Hurdle math: the weighted IV@15% is **$38.45**, so $26.60 already clears the 15% hurdle on the weighted case, and the bear case ($30.70) sits above the price too — only the super-bear case ($19.08) does not. The price that clears 15% on the weighted case is the IV@15% itself, $38.45; demanding a margin of safety against the *bear* case rather than the weighted case means paying no more than roughly the current price, which is where the market already is.
