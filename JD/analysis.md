## What it is
China's leading supply-chain-based retailer: JD Retail (1P direct sales — electronics & home appliances is the stronghold, ~52% of H1 2026 product revenue — plus a growing 3P marketplace, marketing/ads, JD Health and JD Industrials), JD Logistics (owned warehouse/delivery network, increasingly sold to third parties; H1 2026 revenue +26.5% YoY after absorbing the on-demand delivery business), and New Businesses (JD Food Delivery, Jingxi, JD Property, Joybuy overseas). Three reported segments since the Jan 2026 reclassification that moved on-demand delivery from New Businesses into JD Logistics — segment history before 2026 is not directly comparable, group totals are. Revenue: FY2025 RMB 1,309.1B (+17.9%, flattered by the government trade-in subsidy wave in electronics/appliances), H1 2026 RMB 662.1B (+0.7%), Q2 2026 RMB 346.4B (−2.9% YoY on the subsidy high base; general merchandise +5.6%, services +12.9% in H1).

## Owner's earnings
EDGAR history (conservative = OCF − PPE capex − SBC, USD): FY2023 $7.14B, FY2024 $6.82B, **FY2025 $0.92B**. The FY2025 collapse is identified, not mysterious: New Businesses' operating loss exploded to **RMB 46.6B in 2025 from RMB 2.9B in 2024** — the JD Food Delivery subsidy war against Meituan and Alibaba's Ele.me, launched Feb 2025 — while JD Retail's operating profit *rose* 25% to RMB 51.4B (margin 4.0% → 4.6%). FY2025 OCF was further depressed by trade-in program receivables timing (company FCF fell to RMB 6.5B from RMB 43.7B). The maintenance definition (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC) prints $11.05B for FY2025 — distorted *up* by a $7.5B working-capital inflow (supplier float), so it is not an earnings-power figure for JD; the OCF-based conservative definition is the anchor throughout.

**Base used: TTM owner's earnings through Q2 2026 = US$4.26B = $3.00/ADS** ($1.498/ordinary share), built from the Q2 2026 interim release (GlobeNewswire, 2026-08-13) — JD is a 20-F/6-K filer, so there are no EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures; the release's own TTM table is the filing-grade source:
- TTM OCF: FY2025 RMB 18,991M − H1 2025 RMB 6,147M + H1 2026 RMB 38,273M = **RMB 51,117M** (matches the release's reported TTM OCF of RMB 51.1B / US$7,534M exactly)
- Less TTM capital expenditures, company definition, net of sales proceeds: **RMB 17,963M** (US$2,647M — development properties RMB 8,122M + other RMB 9,841M). This is deliberately *more* conservative than the engine's PPE-only capex tag ($1,118M in FY2025 vs the company's RMB 12,735M ≈ $1,821M): JD's logistics/property build is real capex, and in FY2025 the understatement roughly offset the SBC deduction by coincidence — not a reason to keep it.
- Less TTM SBC: FY2025 RMB 4,727M (EDGAR $676M) − H1 2025 RMB 2,424M + H1 2026 RMB 1,979M = **RMB 4,282M** (~US$631M)
- **TTM OE = 51,117 − 17,963 − 4,282 = RMB 28,872M ≈ US$4,255M**, on H1 2026 diluted 2,841M ordinary shares = RMB 10.16/ordinary share.
- Cross-check: company-reported TTM free cash flow is RMB 31,415M (US$4,630M); OE = FCF + consumer-financing adjustment (RMB 1,739M) − SBC (RMB 4,282M) = RMB 28,872M ✓. TTM FCF has already recovered to ~5× the FY2025 print as delivery losses narrowed.
- Rejected bases: FY2025 alone ($0.62/ADS) is the trough of a management-declared peak-investment year — H2 2025 is the worst of the war and is rolling out of the TTM; the 3-yr median ($4.43/ADS, i.e. 2023–24 economics) assumes New Businesses returns to near-zero losses, which H1 2026 does not support (New Businesses still lost RMB 20.2B in H1 2026, though Q2's loss narrowed >50% YoY and was the segment's best sequential improvement). The TTM base sits between them and is the current, filing-derived figure.

## Moat score: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
**FOR:** (1) The owned logistics network is the hardest asset in Chinese e-commerce to replicate — JD Logistics' warehouses, employed couriers and (since 2025) a full-time food-delivery rider fleet set the same/next-day standard and are now monetized externally (external logistics revenue compounding at +24-27%). (2) 1P supply-chain scale in electronics/appliances: procurement leverage and authenticity trust that show up as *17 consecutive quarters* of JD Retail gross-margin expansion and a record 4.6-5.0% Retail operating margin in H1 2026 — the core is getting more profitable, not less, through the war. (3) Mix shift toward asset-light services (marketplace + marketing +12.9% H1 2026) raises the group's structural margin ceiling. (4) Fortress liquidity (RMB 235.1B cash, restricted cash and short-term investments at Jun 2026) plus a $5B buyback (2.5% of shares retired in H1 2026 alone) funds the war without balance-sheet stress.
**AGAINST:** (1) Almost no consumer lock-in: Chinese shoppers multi-home across Taobao/Tmall, Pinduoduo and Douyin, switching costs are ~zero outside the delivery-speed niche, and Alibaba matches JD's scale while PDD undercuts on price. (2) Food delivery is an anti-moat JD chose to buy: Meituan's density lead is structural, JD is #3, and the segment burned RMB 46.6B of operating profit in one year — proof that management will spend the core's economics on share-buying when it decides to. (3) Structurally thin economics: group net margin is 2-3% in good years, returns on the enormous logistics asset base are modest. (4) Regulatory/political overhang is recurring, not theoretical: a RMB 635M SAMR fine hit H1 2026, regulators have repeatedly intervened in the subsidy war and in food-delivery vendor oversight, and the ADS structure carries the standing China/VIE tail risk. (5) Category concentration: electronics/appliances demand is now partly a function of government trade-in subsidy policy, which is already fading as a tailwind (Q2 2026 category revenue −11.8%).
CAP at moat 5 = 2 + 1.3×5 = **8.5 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-yr projection).

## Flags — every fragility flag from the final dcf_numbers.json, resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY2025 ended 9.4 months ago): resolved with the TTM override above — OCF/capex/SBC rolled forward through Q2 2026 from the interim release, arithmetic shown and tied to the release's own reported TTM OCF (RMB 51,117M) and TTM FCF (RMB 31,415M).
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $0.31/ordinary vs 3-yr median $2.22/ordinary): *both* are distorted, in opposite directions. The latest year is depressed by the peak food-delivery subsidy year (New Businesses loss RMB 2.9B → 46.6B) plus trade-in receivables timing; the median is flattered by pre-war economics that assumed New Businesses ≈ breakeven. The TTM base ($1.498/ordinary) is the resolution — it keeps two quarters of heavy (H2 2025) burn and two quarters of demonstrated narrowing (H1 2026).
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.6× super-bear): real — the outcome genuinely hinges on whether a RMB 46.6B/yr loss segment goes to ~zero or stays ~RMB 40B. Confidence is capped at **low** because of it (and the method range below).
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (engine: net debt 5.7× normalized OE): substantially an artifact of the engine's narrow balance-sheet read — it nets only cash & equivalents ($19.7B) against $44.9B of debt lines and ignores short-term investments. On the company's reported liquidity — RMB 235.1B (~$34.6B) of cash, restricted cash and short-term investments at Jun 30, 2026 (RMB 225.4B at Dec 31, 2025) against those same borrowings — JD is roughly net-debt-neutral to modestly levered, funds buybacks and the war internally, and there is no distress channel. Not treated as net cash either: the debt is real, much of it funding the logistics/property asset base. Verdict impact: leverage is not a reason to discount the OE stream, but it removes any "cash pile" upside argument.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count −2.2%/yr): real and already credited via the growth adjustment. H1 2026: 69.9M ordinary shares repurchased for $1.0B (2.5% of shares out). Caveat: only $1.0B remained under the $5B program at Jun 30, 2026 (expires Aug 2027) — the tailwind slows unless the program is renewed.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs — actually >7× trough-to-peak: $2.25 → $0.31 per ordinary share): structural to the current strategy, not noise — group OE is now a *policy variable* set by how much management chooses to invest in New Businesses. Handled by the TTM (not peak, not trough) base, 45% combined bear/super-bear weight, and a 0.75 super-bear OE multiplier.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023: NI $3.28B vs OCF $8.38B): JD's negative-working-capital model — customers pay immediately, suppliers are paid on ~59-day terms — makes OCF structurally larger than net income; 2023 NI was additionally weighed by non-cash investment fair-value/impairment items and share-of-investee results that do not touch operating cash flow. This is the float model working as designed, not an earnings-quality red flag; it is also why the OCF-based conservative OE (not the NI-based maintenance version) is the right anchor for JD.
- Not flagged but noted: the Jan 2026 segment reclassification (on-demand delivery: New Businesses → JD Logistics) breaks segment-level comparability, and FY2025's +17.9% revenue YoY (a blend component) is subsidy-inflated — the cleaner revenue signals are the 3-yr CAGR (7.3%) and H1 2026 (+0.7%).

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the honest headline, again:** FY2025-trough base → ~Tier D; TTM base (used) → **B, 18.0%**; 3-yr-median base with identical scenarios → IVs scale ~1.48×, weighted IRR ~22-23% → Tier A. The spread is entirely "how fast does the delivery war end," and Q2 2026 evidence (losses halved YoY, group operating income swinging to +RMB 4.5B from −RMB 0.9B, non-GAAP net income +20.8%) supports the TTM base over the trough, but not yet the median.
- The core is quietly excellent right now: JD Retail operating profit +25% in FY2025 and +6% in H1 2026 at record margins, while absorbing the group overhead of the war. If New Businesses merely stopped getting worse, group OE would re-rate mechanically.
- Red flags to keep watching: (1) management's demonstrated willingness to burn a year of group profit on a new front — food delivery today, Joybuy Europe (revenues doubling off a small base, still loss-making) tomorrow; (2) the electronics subsidy cliff — Q2 2026 already printed the first YoY revenue decline since listing; (3) recurring regulatory friction (SAMR fine RMB 635M in Q1 2026, food-delivery oversight actions) as a standing cost of the strategy; (4) SBC is material (TTM RMB 4.28B ≈ 15% of OE) though falling, and the buyback that offsets it is nearly exhausted.
- Hurdle math: the weighted IV@15% is **$33.83**, so $26.76 already clears 15% on the weighted case — but the bear case ($23.52) sits 12% *below* price. A 25% margin of safety against the weighted IV requires **≤ ~$25.40**; at that level the purchase is paid for by the base case and insured against most of the bear case.
