## What it is
Diversified miner focused on copper and zinc after exiting steelmaking coal: Quebrada Blanca and Highland Valley (copper), Red Dog (zinc), a stake in Antamina, and the Trail smelter, across Canada, Chile, Peru and the US. On September 9, 2025 it agreed a merger of equals with Anglo American (1.3301 Anglo shares per Teck share; both shareholder groups approved on December 9, 2025, per the 2025 Annual Information Form in the 40-F) — so the traded price currently also carries merger-consideration value, not just standalone cash flows. Q2 2026 interim results (company release) showed materially higher copper production and prices driving much stronger earnings and operating cash flow than a year earlier, with China the remaining merger approval process per company reporting.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate (resource scarcity, no pricing power)
Justification: Scarce, long-life tier-1 copper and zinc assets in mining-friendly jurisdictions (Quebrada Blanca, Highland Valley, Red Dog, Antamina stake, Trail) that cannot be replicated quickly and were hard to permit — but Teck is a pure commodity price-taker with no pricing power, structurally heavy capex and stripping, depleting reserves, and owner's earnings that were negative in the latest fiscal year after capex; returns are set by the copper/zinc cycle, not by the franchise.
- FOR: Tier-1, long-life copper/zinc reserves (Quebrada Blanca, Highland Valley, Red Dog, Antamina stake) that are scarce and hard to permit or replicate; diversified copper + zinc + Trail smelting footprint across Canada, Chile, Peru and the US; net-cash balance sheet after the steelmaking-coal sale (engine: net debt -9.6x normalized OE) funds the capex cycle without forced dilution; Highland Valley life extension sanctioned to 2046 and the Quebrada Blanca ramp give visible volume growth.
- AGAINST: Pure commodity price-taker — earnings are set by copper/zinc prices it does not control; structurally capex-heavy (latest-FY OE negative after capex despite positive OCF); cyclical, volatile OE; Quebrada Blanca ramp/tailings execution risk and a 2024 asset impairment at the flagship growth complex; pending Anglo American merger (China approval and integration risk; consideration is Anglo shares, not cash); reserves deplete, decommissioning/restoration liabilities grow, and the dual-class structure concentrates voting control.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 9.4 months ago): ttm.py was run and cannot produce a TTM base for this filer — its exact output was: "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for TECK (CIK 886986): ocf: no annual (FY) fact; sbc: no annual (FY) fact; shares_diluted: no annual (FY) fact; capex: no capex facts in companyfacts under any candidate tag and no capex_override in judgment.json". That is the expected IFRS limitation (ttm.py reads us-gaap facts only; Teck files ifrs-full), so the engine's median-of-3-FY base is used, as the sweep spec directs. Direction of the staleness: interim 2026 prints (Q2 2026 company release) were stronger than FY2025 on higher copper prices/production — but even crediting that qualitatively, a stronger interim half does not bridge a base-to-price gap of this size, and no TTM figure was hand-computed.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share -$0.63 vs 3-yr median $0.17): both are "real"; neither is a data error. The latest year is depressed by peak growth capex (capex $1,342,401,680 vs OCF $1,080,202,440), and the median is flattered by including a year whose OE was only barely positive. The median is used as the normalized base per the v5 method; the divergence is itself the finding — this business currently converts almost none of its operating cash flow into owner's earnings after capex on either basis.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: identified — the sale of the steelmaking coal business to Glencore/Nippon Steel, closed July 2024. The filed history is restated to continuing operations, which is why revenue in financials_history.csv falls from $12,790,290,240 (FY2022, total-company basis as filed) to $4,887,307,680 (FY2023, continuing basis) and why the 3-yr revenue CAGR (-14.99%) is meaningless. Growth inputs were therefore anchored on the blend's 5-yr revenue CAGR (2.26%) and the latest continuing-ops YoY (+24.69%), not on any CAGR that straddles the break. The pending Anglo American merger is a second, forward basis break: if it closes, TECK as a standalone security ceases to exist.
- **OE_VOLATILE**: real cyclicality, not an inflection — OE/share spans from negative to strongly positive across the cycle. Handled by the normalized median base, the full default bear weight (35% on bear-or-worse), and low confidence; it does not change the tier because even the super-bull case reaches only a 3.16% IRR.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): explained by non-cash and discontinued-operations items — 2024 net income of $196,687,830 against OCF of $1,939,077,900 reflects the large non-cash asset impairment recorded that year (C$1,053M per the company's interim reporting) plus D&A of $1,199,587,260 (financials_history.csv) and discontinued coal profits in net income that are not in continuing OCF; 2023 shows the same pattern in the other direction (net income $1,741,801,440 vs OCF $3,082,113,120). The OCF-based conservative OE series used throughout is not contaminated by the impairment, which is non-cash.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 2.6%/yr; share_cagr -0.025851700998943516): already credited via the +2.59pp growth adjustment in g_base; not double-counted in the verdict.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: Teck reports in CAD; the engine converted every field at period-end ECB reference rates (e.g. 0.73036 for 2025-12-31; the full rate table is in dcf_numbers.json fx_conversion). Flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average — a documented simplification that adds noise to any single year but does not drive a gap of this size.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (x1.0): resolved by declaration in judgment.json — the 40-F cover registers "Class B subordinate voting shares", trading symbol TECK, directly on the NYSE; the traded security is the share itself, not an ADS, so per-share figures and the $64.00 price are on the same basis and no scaling applies.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (0.3%): the core of the verdict — at $64.00 the valuation rests almost entirely on asset/reserve value, commodity prices, and the merger terms, not on owner's earnings. Stated plainly rather than modeled away.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.17x): real for a cyclical miner; confidence is set to low (below even the medium cap this flag imposes).
- **BIG_NET_CASH** (net cash 9.6x normalized OE; net debt -$809,238,880): noted and folded into the verdict — the cash pile is real and funds the capex cycle, but it is a consequence of the coal sale proceeds, and measured against normalized OE it looks large only because normalized OE is tiny; it does not bridge the price-to-IV gap on this method.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Method honesty: an owner's-earnings DCF is a poor primary tool for a miner in a heavy growth-capex phase — it charges all capex (including Quebrada Blanca growth capex) against current earnings, and miners are conventionally valued on reserve NAV and mid-cycle commodity decks. That caveat is real, but it is not a reason to override the tier: the gap here is not borderline. Even the super-bull scenario (16.85% growth start, 1.25x OE multiplier) produces IV@15% of $2.98 and a 3.16% IRR against a $64.00 price. No defensible OE input set gets this name near the 15% hurdle; the method range is D under every scenario run, not a C-to-A spread.
- What the price actually rests on: reserve/NAV value at current copper prices, a strong 2026 interim run-rate at record copper prices, and the Anglo American exchange ratio (1.3301 Anglo shares per Teck share). A buyer at $64.00 is underwriting commodity prices and Anglo's share price, not Teck's current owner's earnings.
- Balance sheet is not the risk: net cash (net debt -$809,238,880), OE positive 4 of the last 5 years, and buybacks shrinking the count 2.6%/yr. The risks are the copper/zinc price cycle, QB execution, merger completion (China approval outstanding per company reporting), and the simple fact that the current capital cycle has so far produced negative owner's earnings.
- Price that clears 15% on this method: the weighted IV@15% itself, $1.61 — i.e. there is no realistic margin-of-safety price near current levels on an OE basis; the honest statement is that this screen cannot validate the price at any plausible entry, and any case for owning it must be made on NAV/merger grounds outside this method.
