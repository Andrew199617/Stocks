## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: A gold miner is a price-taker on a depleting asset: Kinross has no pricing power, its reserves deplete and must be rebought with heavy capex, and cost inflation (+22% AISC YoY in Q2 2026) claws back price windfalls. What it does have is scale (~2.0M oz/yr across five countries), a net-cash balance sheet, and a real development pipeline (Great Bear, Lobo-Marte) — enough for a weak 3/10, not a durable moat.
- FOR: Scale and diversification — ~2.0M Au eq. oz/yr guided production across mines in the US, Canada, Brazil, Mauritania and Chile, no single mine is the whole company; net-cash balance sheet (engine: net debt −$1,004.1M at FY2025; $1.9B net cash at Q2 2026) with no debt maturities until 2033 per the Q2 2026 release — survives a gold downturn without distress; development pipeline that can replace reserves (Great Bear in Canada; Lobo-Marte NPV $4.3B at a $4,100 gold price, per the Q2 2026 release); capital discipline at current prices — buybacks plus dividends returned ~40% of free cash flow year-to-date 2026, share count shrinking ~1.8%/yr (engine share CAGR −1.79%).
- AGAINST: Pure gold price-taker — no pricing power, and the 2025-26 earnings surge is the gold price (Q2 2026 realized $4,483/oz, +37% YoY), not volume (production fell 4% YoY); finite, depleting reserves — ounces must be continually replaced with capex (2026 attributable capex guided ~$1,500M) just to hold production flat at ~2.0M oz/yr; cost inflation erodes the windfall — Q2 2026 AISC +22% YoY to $1,821/oz, production cost of sales +25% YoY (fuel, royalties, labor, per the Q2 coverage); jurisdictional and single-asset risk across Mauritania (Tasiast), Brazil, Chile and the US, with permitting and tax/royalty regimes outside management's control.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.43 months ago): ttm.py was run and cannot compute a TTM for this IFRS filer (us-gaap only; its exit-3 message is quoted in the Owner's earnings section). The engine's median base is used instead, per spec. The staleness cuts conservative here, not aggressive: the 2026 interim prints (Q2 revenue $2,238.1M, OCF $1,145.9M in the quarter alone) are running *above* the FY2025 pace on the gold price — the verdict does not need them, because the question is what gold does next, not what it just did.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range >3x over 5 years): real and structural — conservative OE went $229.0M (2022) → $2,553.2M (2025) on the gold price. Handled by the normalized median base (not the peak latest year), the bear-skewed probabilities (45% bear-or-worse), and the growth recalibration.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): explained by the miners' non-cash cost structure, not a one-off tax deposit or impairment: 2023 net income $415,400,000 vs OCF $1,605,300,000 with D&A of $986,800,000 filed for the year; 2024 net income $994,000,000 vs OCF $2,446,400,000 with D&A of $1,147,500,000. Depletion/depreciation is the dominant bridge in both years, with working-capital movement (ΔNWC $45,400,000 in 2023, −$109,400,000 in 2024, per financials_history.csv) secondary. The OCF-based conservative OE is the series used throughout, so the divergence does not contaminate the base.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (was ADS_RATIO_MISSING on the preliminary run): resolved with a declared ads_ratio of 1.0 in judgment.json under bounded authority — Kinross is direct-listed, not an ADS program: the Form 40-F filed 2026-03-26 states its common shares are listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange and the New York Stock Exchange, and the sweep README's v5.3 note lists KGC among the direct-listed ordinary-share filers at ratio 1.0. Scaling is ×1.0, so no per-share figure changes; the declaration exists so the basis is documented, not assumed.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $2.08 vs 3-yr median $1.10): the **latest year is the distorted one** — FY2025 OE/share of $2.0849 was earned at the top of a historic gold run (realized prices still rising into Q2 2026 at $4,483/oz). For a price-taking cyclical, normalizing on the peak year capitalizes the peak. The median ($1.1032, which is FY2024's level) is used as the base; the peak year is treated as evidence for the bull cases, not the base. This is the single most consequential choice in the run and it is deliberately conservative.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 5.54x super-bear IV): real — the outcome range for a gold miner at peak prices IS this wide, because it is a leveraged bet on one commodity price. Confidence is set to **low**, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the honest headline.** The preliminary run (default probabilities 10/25/30/25/10, default offsets, i.e. scenario starts up to the engine's gold-price-driven g_base of 33.68%) produced weighted IRR 19.75%, weighted IV@15% $33.84, Tier B. This final run — same median base, same filings, growth recalibrated to volume reality and probabilities skewed to the cycle — produces Tier D at 8.76%. The entire B-to-D span is the growth assumption: whether you believe a gold-price spike is a growth rate. I don't, for the reasons documented above, but anyone quoting KGC's tier without that caveat is hiding the ball.
- Quality is not the debate: net cash, no maturities until 2033 (Q2 2026 release), ~40% of FCF returned year-to-date 2026, share count shrinking. If gold holds anywhere near current levels, Kinross will keep printing cash — the base case already assumes roughly that, and it still only earns 8.8% at $22.24 because the normalized OE yield on the price is 4.96%.
- What would change the verdict: a gold price that keeps rising (super bull only reaches a 15.86% IRR — even it barely clears 15%), or a materially lower entry price. At the weighted IV@15% of $10.85 the 15% hurdle clears by construction; a buyer wanting 15% *with* a margin of safety against the bear cases (bear IV@15% $7.46) needs a price in the single digits — i.e. roughly half the current quote or less.
- Sector note: this is not a SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH case — owner's-earnings logic fits a miner (real capex, real depletion), but for miners the "growth" input is a commodity forecast in disguise, which is why confidence is low rather than medium.
