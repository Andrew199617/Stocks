## What it is
China's leading online music and audio platform: QQ Music, Kugou Music and Kuwo Music (streaming/subscriptions), WeSing (karaoke/social entertainment), and — since the acquisition closed on May 18, 2026 — Ximalaya in long-form audio. Tencent Holdings is the controlling shareholder. Q2 2026: total revenue RMB8.93B (+5.8% YoY), of which music-related services RMB7.61B (+11.0%), membership services RMB4.79B (+8.1%), social entertainment RMB1.33B (−16.4%); gross margin 44.2%; IFRS net profit attributable RMB2.47B. Price $8.63/ADS (nasdaq_quote_api). The engine's market_cap_est ($27.02B) multiplies the ADS price by the ordinary-share count and so double-counts the ADS ratio; on the diluted count in ADS terms (3,130,861,720 ordinary shares = ~1.565B ADS) the cap is roughly half the engine figure — under the $40B scope limit either way.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
Justification: China's dominant online music platform with Tencent as controlling shareholder and ecosystem distribution, major-label content relationships, a fast-growing SVIP subscription tier and, since May 2026, Ximalaya in long-form audio. Gross margin expanded from 30.1% (2021) to 42.3% (2024) and 44.2% in Q2 2026 as the mix shifted to subscriptions; roic_proxy (fundamentals.json, CNY as filed) rose from 6.8% (2021) to 8.6% (2022), 10.6% (2023) and 12.6% (2024) — above the cost of capital and rising, but not the sustained mid-teens-plus of a wide moat, and the proxy denominator excludes most of the cash/term-deposit/short-term-investment pile, so measured ROIC understates the franchise somewhat.
- FOR: dominant share of China online music (QQ Music, Kugou, Kuwo) plus WeSing; Tencent controlling shareholder — WeChat/QQ ecosystem distribution and promotion; major-label and proprietary content relationships, SVIP tier raising per-user monetization; Ximalaya acquisition (closed May 18, 2026) extends the moat into long-form audio; fortress balance sheet (RMB44.22B cash, term deposits and short-term investments at Jun 30, 2026; negligible borrowings).
- AGAINST: no exclusive catalog lock-in since the 2021 SAMR ruling — rivals license the same music; ByteDance (Soda Music) and NetEase Cloud Music competition for listening time; social entertainment services in structural decline (−16.4% YoY in Q2 2026) under regulatory pressure; measured ROIC only ~9–13% in recent years — solid, not wide-moat; China regulatory and geopolitical (ADR/VIE) overhang.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 21.5 months ago): real and unresolved by construction — the FY2025 Form 20-F was filed 2026-04-17 but its facts are not yet in EDGAR companyfacts (fundamentals.py flags the same gap), and ttm.py reads us-gaap only, so no filing-direct TTM base exists for this IFRS filer. Handled per spec: the normalized median base is used, quarterly 6-K prints (Q1/Q2 2026, cited above) were used for the growth sanity check and scenario calibration, and confidence is set to low.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $0.82/ADS vs 3-yr median $0.60/ADS): the median is the conservative one, not the distorted one. FY2022–23 OE was depressed by the social-entertainment revenue collapse and content-cost restructuring; FY2024 reflects the cleaned-up subscription mix (gross margin 42.3% vs 31.0% in FY2022) plus buybacks. The median base is retained as the headline (it is the v5 normalized base), and the latest-FY base is carried as the top of the method range below. Note the FY2025 20-F (not in companyfacts) shows net profit attributable of RMB11,056M vs RMB6,644M in FY2024 — but H1 2026 disclosures show the FY2025 figure included large non-recurring other gains (H1 net profit fell from RMB6,700M in H1 2025 to RMB4,562M in H1 2026 on lower other gains), so it is not a clean run-rate either.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2022): benign. FY2022 net income was $556,463,050 vs operating cash flow of $1,084,370,950 (financials_history.csv, converted). The gap is non-cash addbacks (D&A $168,142,000 + SBC $96,391,750) plus a large working-capital release (ΔNWC −$768,494,350 in the maintenance-OE legs) as social-entertainment content prepayments and payables unwound while that segment shrank. The conservative OE used throughout is OCF-based, so the divergence does not contaminate the base.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count −2.36%/yr): already credited via the +2.36pp share-count adjustment in the growth blend; not double-counted. Note the actual pace has accelerated (~US$400M of ADS repurchases in Q2 2026 alone) — that upside is carried in the bull/super-bull starts, not the base.

## My thoughts / method range
- The honest headline is the range across defensible bases: on the normalized median base the weighted IRR is 11.65% (IV@15% $6.19); on the latest-FY base ($0.82/ADS, run with the same scenarios via --base-oe-ps) the weighted IRR is 14.69% and IV@15% is $8.40, essentially the current price. Both are Tier C — even the generous base does not clear the 15% hurdle at $8.63. A future FY2025-facts run could shift this again: FY2025 reported profit jumped, but a material part was non-recurring other gains, and Ximalaya consolidation adds amortization and opex (Q2 2026 opex +12% YoY).
- Quality is not the debate: OE positive 5/5 years, SBC is a modest $81.65M against $1.41B of FY2024 OCF, capex is ~3% of OCF, the balance sheet holds ~$6.5B of cash-like assets, and capital return (dividend + buybacks) is substantial. The debate is purely price: at $8.63 the market already pays for the recovery the median base hasn't caught up with.
- Risks: ByteDance's Soda Music competing for listening time; music-catalog cost inflation at renewal; social entertainment's decline not yet finished; Ximalaya integration cost and amortization; China ADR/VIE and regulatory overhang that no DCF input captures.

## Final verdict
