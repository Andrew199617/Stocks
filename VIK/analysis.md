## What it is
Bermuda-incorporated, NYSE-listed premium cruise operator: river, ocean and expedition voyages for affluent, English-speaking 55+ travelers ("For The Thinking Person"), direct-sold, destination-focused, no casinos / no kids positioning. Fleet of standardized small ships (96 vessels per Q2 2026 reporting; 90 operated at Q2 2025). Q2 2026: revenue $2.19B (+16.5% YoY), adjusted EBITDA $748.4M, adjusted EPS $1.31, net yield $645 (+6.2% YoY); 96% of 2026 core capacity booked, 2027 advance bookings pacing ahead of the prior season — offset in Q3 2026 by historically low European river water levels disrupting over 50% of river capacity PCDs (ship swaps, no cancellations, future-cruise vouchers issued).

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong premium brand, not wide
Justification: strong premium brand in destination-focused cruising for affluent 55+ travelers — #1-rated river and ocean product, direct distribution, identical-ship fleet design, advance-booking visibility and proven pricing power (Q2 2026 net yield +6.2% YoY; ROIC 45.8% for FY2025 on the company's own 20-F non-IFRS calculation) — capped because cruising has no network effects or switching costs, the fleet advantage is bought with replicable shipbuilding capital, demand is discretionary and cyclical, and growth requires continuous heavy vessel capex.
- FOR: premium brand and product ratings — #1 for Rivers and Oceans (Condé Nast Readers' Choice, five consecutive years per company reporting), "Thinking Person" positioning for affluent 55+ travelers; proven pricing power — Q2 2026 net yield +6.2% YoY on top of capacity growth, FY2025 revenue +21.9%; booking visibility — 96% of 2026 core capacity booked at Q2 2026, 2027 advance bookings pacing ahead of the prior season; identical-ship fleet design lowers crew training, maintenance and inventory costs, young fleet keeps maintenance capex low (company-defined Ongoing Capex $135.1M in FY2025); high returns on capital — ROIC 45.8% FY2025 (company non-IFRS calc); direct sales channel and loyal repeat-guest base in a growing-wealth demographic.
- AGAINST: no network effects or switching costs — guests re-shop each voyage against Royal Caribbean, Norwegian, Oceania/Regent and river rivals; fleet advantage is replicable capital — any well-funded rival can order similar ships, and growth requires ~$1B/yr of vessel capex; discretionary, cyclical demand — travel spending falls in recessions and stops in crises (COVID-era losses); environmental/operational exposure — historically low European river water levels disrupted over 50% of river capacity PCDs in Q3 2026, fuel and emissions-regulation costs are outside management control; leverage and sponsor history — net debt $1.698B after a leveraged pre-IPO structure, and the share count carries a conversion basis break (below).
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, and it is unusually wide for a capex reason: the standardized sweep deducts *total* capex (~$1.03B/yr, mostly new ships), which makes Viking's OE/share $2.84 on the median base and the stock a Tier D at $82.60. The company's own framing deducts only "Ongoing Capex" ($135,075 thousand in FY2025) and reports 45.8% ROIC and adjusted FCF conversion above 100% — on that framing the same company looks like a compounding machine. The truth for an owner sits between: ships are long-lived and the fleet is young, but the growth the market is paying for is bought ship by ship, and a no-growth Viking would still eventually have to replace its fleet. This sweep's standard is total capex, applied to every name alike, so the D stands on the standard — but no one should quote the D without the capex sentence attached.
- Even granting the standardized base, the price does the damage: at $82.60 the super-bull case (26.5% growth start, 1.25x OE) yields only a 15.15% IRR. There is no scenario in the table where the current price clears 15% with room to spare.
- Quality is real: pricing power printed (+6.2% net yield in Q2 2026 on top of capacity growth), 96% of the 2026 core season booked, 2027 advance bookings ahead, net debt 1.34x normalized OE and falling cash balances building ($3,803,944,000 cash at FY2025 per the 20-F cash-flow statement). The risks are the cycle, river-water/climate disruption (already visible in Q3 2026), fuel/emissions costs, and a valuation that has capitalized the recovery twice over.
- Hurdle math on the final numbers: the stock clears 15% on the weighted case only below the weighted IV@15% of $43.23; sized to the conservative end (bear IV@15% $28.95), a buyer demanding a margin of safety against the bear case needs a price in the high-$20s to low-$40s — roughly half the current quote or less.
