## What it is
Premium athletic apparel ("athleisure") sold direct: 825 company-operated stores at Q2 FY2026 plus digital (~39% of Q2 revenue). Proprietary fabrics (Nulu, Everlux), premium prices, no dividend. Americas is the dominant region (~71% of FY2025 revenue per external analysis); China/international is the growth leg. The business is in a demand reset: Q2 FY2026 revenue fell 4% YoY, comparable sales fell 9%, and management cut full-year guidance hard (below).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, and eroding
Evidence first (fundamentals.json): ROIC proxy over the last six fiscal years ran 41.9% / 65.9% / 42.7% / 76.4% / 75.4% / 49.4% — far above any plausible cost of capital through a cycle that included COVID and the FY2023 inventory glut. Gross margin: 56.0% / 57.7% / 55.4% / 58.3% / 59.2% / 56.6% over the same years; TTM gross margin 56.1%. Those are wide-moat economics historically. The score is 5, not 7–8, because the pillars producing those returns are visibly weakening now (AGAINST, below) and a moat score must price the franchise the buyer gets today.
- FOR: proprietary technical fabrics (Nulu, Everlux) and a fit reputation that still supports premium pricing — gross margin held at 56.6% in the latest FY even as demand fell; returns on capital far above cost of capital through a full cycle (ROIC series above); direct-to-consumer model — stores plus digital, no wholesale channel to discount against it; international revenue still growing (+4% in Q2 FY2026, constant-dollar +2%) while Americas fell; net-cash balance sheet funds a turnaround internally.
- AGAINST: the core market is shrinking under it — Americas comparable sales −12% and Americas revenue −8% in Q2 FY2026, with core leggings sales down ~20% as silhouettes shifted to looser fits the franchise was late to; Alo Yoga and Vuori are running lululemon's own community playbook at the same premium price band — reported US athleisure share down ~10pp to 43.9%; product execution failures (the pulled "Get Low" leggings) and a China brand-sentiment backlash (Great Wall campaign) damage exactly the fit/quality reputation the premium rests on; leadership is in transition — CEO departed, interim co-CEOs through 2026, a founder (Chip Wilson) proxy fight, and incoming CEO Heidi O'Neill (Nike veteran) arriving with an unproven plan; the product is premium discretionary — the first category consumers defer — with no cost advantage underneath the brand if the brand fades.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range is the honest headline. On the engine's stale default blend (base growth +10.31%, TTM base, default probabilities) the same filing data printed Tier A at 20.46% weighted IRR with IV@15% of $146.01. On prints-and-guidance scenarios it prints Tier C at 11.72% with IV@15% of $72.45. The entire difference is whether the model is allowed to assume growth the company has explicitly guided against — it is not a data difference.
- What the price still assumes: at $97.76 the market is paying roughly the bull case (bull IV@15% $100.67) as its central expectation — i.e., that a CEO who has not yet presented a plan restores mid-single-digit growth on a refund-stripped OE base. That may happen; it is not the conservative end, and the sweep sizes to the conservative end.
- Quality is not the debate: 5/5 OE-positive years, SBC is small ($91,231,000 TTM against $1,982,033,000 TTM OCF), capex is a single clean filed line every year, no funded debt, and historical ROIC above 40% in every one of the last six years. The debate is purely whether the demand break is a product-cycle miss (fixable) or franchise erosion (not fixable on this timetable). Q2's breadth — leggings −20%, Americas comps −12%, China comps negative after a brand-sentiment incident — argues it is not one bad SKU.
- The balance sheet buys time, not growth: ~$1.8B cash against ~$1.8B of lease liabilities and no funded debt means no creditor forces the issue while O'Neill resets product; it also means there is no leverage distress discount in the price to harvest.
