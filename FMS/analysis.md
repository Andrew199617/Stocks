## What it is
The world's leading provider of products and services for people with kidney disease (20-F Item 4.B, via profile.json): Care Delivery — about 3,513 dialysis clinics treating about 290,000 patients; Care Enablement — the dialysis machines, dialyzers and products the clinics (and competitors) consume, including the 5008X high-volume hemodiafiltration system rolling out in the U.S.; Value-Based Care — capitated kidney-care contracts. FY2025 segment revenue (segments.json, EUR): Care Delivery €13,238,321,000 (67.5% of total, −2.1% YoY), Care Enablement €4,141,908,000 (21.1%, +2.0%), Value-Based Care €2,247,373,000 (11.5%, +28.2%). FY2025 revenue €19,628M as filed (fundamentals.json), +1.5% in EUR (+5.4% at constant currency per the company's FY2025 release).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: World's leading dialysis provider (about 3,513 clinics, about 290,000 patients, 20-F Item 4.B) with vertical integration into machines/dialyzers (Care Enablement) and unmatched clinic scale — but a reimbursement price-taker in a DaVita duopoly, with ROIC proxy down from 13.6% (2018) to 4.6% (2023/24) recovering to 7.2% (2025) and gross margin down from 33.9% to 25.6%, so the measured franchise earns around its cost of capital, not well above it.
- FOR: world's largest dialysis provider — clinic density and purchasing scale no challenger can replicate quickly; vertically integrated Care Enablement (machines/dialyzers, incl. the 5008X, first FDA-cleared high-volume hemodiafiltration system in the U.S.) feeding its own clinics; life-sustaining, non-discretionary recurring treatments with the global dialysis population growing about 4–5%/yr (20-F Item 4.B); Value-Based Care scaling fast (+28.2% FY2025 segment revenue) on the clinic network and nephrologist relationships.
- AGAINST: reimbursement price-taker — U.S. Medicare/commercial rates are set by payors; measured returns do not show a wide moat (ROIC proxy, fundamentals.json: 9.1% 2016, 10.6% 2017, 13.6% 2018, 8.3% 2019, 8.9% 2020, 6.8% 2021, 4.9% 2022, 4.6% 2023, 4.6% 2024, 7.2% 2025; gross margin 33.9% in 2016 → 24.6% in 2024 → 25.6% in 2025); U.S. same-market treatment growth was −0.9% in Q2 2026 — growth is rate/mix and Value-Based Care, not treatments; DaVita duopoly competition and clinic labor-cost pressure; recurring special items (FME25+ program, portfolio optimization, a China peritoneal-dialysis exit charge of about €110M expected in Q3 2026).
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection. A moat above 5 is not available on this evidence: the rubric's 7+ requires ROIC well above the cost of capital through a cycle, and FMS printed 4.6% in both 2023 and 2024.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the range, not the point estimate: defensible bases run from the zero-fill $4.95/ADS (fake — it charges no capex at all for six years) through this run's conservative override base $3.2328/ADS, with a year-by-year filing-true capex base above $3.2328 because the override overcharges the median year. The weighted IRR moves with it: 26.26% zero-fill (Tier A, fake) → 18.14% on the conservative base (Tier B), and the bear case on any of these bases is a C. Quote the B with its method attached.
- Quality is middling, not elite: this is a scale franchise earning around its cost of capital (ROIC proxy 4.6–7.2% over 2023–2025) with a 25.6% gross margin that has compressed about 8 points in a decade, flat U.S. treatment volumes, and 7.10x leverage on a borrowings-only basis. What the price gives you is a 14.58% OE yield on the conservative base and a real, already-printing margin recovery (FY2025 operating income +31.2%; Q2 2026 excl. special items +20%) — the bull case is an execution story (FME25+ savings, 5008X rollout, Value-Based Care scaling), not a volume story.
- Capital return is real support: a €1.0B buyback (24.8M shares, 8.5% of share capital) was completed on April 30, 2026, ahead of schedule, and a new tranche program followed in May–June 2026 — but note the engine's share series is flattened by the override, so no buyback tailwind is credited in the growth blend; that is conservative, not an omission to fix by hand.
- Watch items: the CEO transition (Korangy succeeding Giza, effective October 12, 2026), the China peritoneal-dialysis exit charge (about €110M expected Q3 2026), U.S. reimbursement/TDAPA durability, and whether U.S. same-market treatment growth (−0.9% in Q2 2026) turns positive.
