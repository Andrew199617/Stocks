## What it is
Canada's third national carrier: TTech connectivity (wireless, fibre Internet, TV, enterprise), TELUS Health (161.2M lives covered at Dec 31, 2025), TELUS Digital (customer-experience / IT services, privatized back to 100% ownership on Oct 31, 2025), and TELUS Agriculture & Consumer Goods. A regulated three-carrier oligopoly business with utility-like cash flows, flat CAD revenue, falling returns on capital, and a heavy balance sheet that forced a 55% dividend reset in Q2 2026.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-moderate, regulator-capped and not converting to returns
Justification: Canadian telecom oligopoly structure (spectrum, fibre/5G scale, sticky base, ~88% gross margins) is real, but the measured franchise economics are weak and deteriorating — roic_proxy fell every year on the recent trend, operating margin compressed, CAD revenue is flat, and the TELUS Digital round-trip ended in a C$2.1B impairment. A moat that cannot earn its cost of capital through the cycle scores weak-moderate.
- FOR: regulated three-carrier oligopoly — spectrum licences and national fibre/5G networks are effectively irreplicable and foreign-ownership limits keep global carriers out; sticky installed base (21,160k total telecom subscriber connections at Dec 31, 2025, FY2025 results release; continued adds in Q2 2026 of +17,000 mobile phone and +20,000 internet subscribers, Q2 2026 release) despite price competition; margin stability through the cycle — gross margin 87.1–88.3% across FY2020–25 (fundamentals.json annual) with no down year; cash engines beyond connectivity (TELUS Health covered 161.2M lives at Dec 31, 2025; TELUS Digital wholly owned again after the Oct 31, 2025 privatization).
- AGAINST: returns prove the moat is not converting — roic_proxy fell from 5.69% (FY2020) to 3.36% (FY2025), below any plausible cost of capital, and operating margin compressed from 16.1% to 11.5% over the same span (fundamentals.json); no pricing power in the core (FY2025 CAD revenue +0.59%; Q2 2026 service revenue −1% YoY and 2026 guidance cut to flat to −2% service revenue with adjusted EBITDA down 2–4%, Q2 2026 release); capital allocation destroyed value (TELUS Digital spun out in 2021, privatized again on Oct 31, 2025 for ~US$539M, then written down by a C$2.1B non-cash impairment in Q2 2026); leverage drives policy (net debt 14.46x normalized OE; the quarterly dividend was cut 55% to C$0.1875 in Q2 2026 to fund deleveraging toward ~3.0x net debt/EBITDA by end-2028); CRTC wholesale/MVNO mandates plus Quebecor/Freedom price competition and immigration cuts removing the population tailwind; structural dilution (+3.0%/yr share count, DRIP-driven — see DILUTION).
- **ROIC evidence (required):** fundamentals.json roic_proxy series (after-tax operating income over equity + debt − cash, CAD as filed): 5.69% (FY2020), 6.31% (FY2021), 5.23% (FY2022), 4.28% (FY2023), 4.69% (FY2024), 3.36% (FY2025) — low and falling, never near a 7+ moat standard. Gross margin level and trend for the last five fiscal years: 87.3% / 87.5% / 88.1% / 87.7% / 88.3% (FY2021–25) — the margin holds; the return on the asset base does not, because the network asset base is enormous and still growing (FY2025 capex C$2,728,000,000 plus C$1,025,000,000 of intangible capex in fundamentals.json).

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range here is narrow: the normalized base equals the latest-FY base ($0.9488 both), the maintenance OE definition lands in the same ballpark ($1,584,980,830 vs $1,456,337,840 conservative), and the recalibrated scenario spread is 2.69x. The honest headline is the level, not a range: an 11.24% OE yield on a flat, levered oligopoly whose returns on capital are falling, priced to return ~13.0% — under Andrew's 15% hurdle.
- The FY2025 base predates the 2026 reset, and that cuts both ways: the C$2.1B Q2 2026 Digital impairment is non-cash and does not reduce OCF-based OE, but the guidance cut (service revenue flat to −2%, EBITDA −2 to −4%) is a real OE headwind — it is priced into the recalibrated scenario starts and the bear-shifted probabilities rather than into the filed base.
- What the model does not pay for: the reset dividend (C$0.75 annualized after the cut; fundamentals.json shows FY2025 paid DPS of C$1.6533 including pre-cut quarters, 5y DPS CAGR +6.89% before the reset) and the deleveraging path to ~3.0x by 2028. Those are balance-sheet stories, not OE growth, and with net debt at 14.46x OE they are also the risk.
- Recurring "one-offs" are a pattern: the Digital round-trip (2021 spin-out, 2025 buy-back at ~US$539M, 2026 C$2.1B impairment) means capital allocation, not the network, is the swing factor for this name; any future pass should stay on the OCF-based definition and watch whether the DRIP-discount removal actually bends the +3.0%/yr share-count trend.
