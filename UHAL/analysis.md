## What it is
The parent of U-Haul International (DIY truck/trailer rental and self-storage, the Moving & Storage segment), Amerco Real Estate, Repwest (property & casualty insurance) and Oxford Life Insurance. SIC 7510 (fundamentals.json profile). FY2026 total revenues $6,037,819,000 (fundamentals.json, Revenues tag — see the BASIS_BREAK flag below for why the engine's revenue series differs), of which the 10-K consolidating schedule attributes $3,811,921k to self-moving equipment rentals and $972,427k to self-storage. Founded 1945; Shoen-family controlled, with two share classes — Voting Common Stock (UHAL) and Series N Non-Voting Common Stock (UHAL.B) — sharing earnings under the two-class method (10-K Note 4).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate; a real network earning shrinking returns
Justification: dominant DIY moving network — 24,000+ locations, >50% DIY share, a fleet larger than the top three rivals combined and the #3 self-storage operator in North America — but a capital-intensive, price-compared rental business whose measured returns have eroded (ROIC proxy 24.5% → 2.2%, operating margin 21.7% → 7.2%, FY2017 → FY2026, fundamentals.json), so the moat is real but no longer earning wide-moat returns.
- FOR: location density no rival matches (24,000+ company + independent-dealer locations vs roughly 2,500 for Penske — convenience is the purchase criterion in DIY moving); scale (~207,600 trucks, larger than the top three competitors combined, >50% DIY share); brand default since 1945 feeding direct/app demand (Truck Share 24/7); the self-storage flywheel (#3 in North America, ~100.3M sq ft, attached to the moving funnel; storage revenue +8.3% in FY2026 with revenue per occupied foot rising); dealer network expanding reach on other people's capital and vertical integration (fleet manufacturing/repair, propane, hitches) supporting availability and cost.
- AGAINST: measured returns contradict a wide moat (below); commodity, price-compared purchase with zero switching cost (Penske, Avis Budget, Enterprise); capital intensity — FY2026 capex $3,154,325,000 against depreciation $1,183,133,000 (financials_history.csv), the network must be continuously re-bought; post-COVID over-expansion (FY2026 fleet depreciation up $186.6M and disposal losses $117.6M per the FY2026 results release; management conceded in Q1 FY2027 that new storage units are completing faster than they fill); Shoen-family control, $114.1M of FY2026 related-party costs (10-K), and the $7.005B net debt load.
- **ROIC / margin evidence (fundamentals.json, roic_proxy series):** 24.5% (FY2017), 9.8%, 6.7%, 4.7%, 8.7%, 13.5% (FY2022), 10.4%, 6.1%, 4.0%, **2.2% (FY2026)** — a decade-long slide from well above the cost of capital to below it, with only the pandemic moving boom interrupting it. Operating margin over the same span: 21.7% → 7.2%, including 17.4% → 12.3% → 7.2% across FY2024–FY2026. The fundamentals.json "gross margin" (~95.9%) is **not** a usable margin for this filer: its COGS leg captures only product-sales cost while rental operating costs sit in operating expenses, so operating margin is the margin line quoted here. A moat above 5 would need ROIC evidence this history does not provide.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection. (Academic here — see DCF.)

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *method range*, and unusually it does not span the hurdle: conservative OE is negative on the median (−$7.85/share), the latest FY (−$6.93/share) and the TTM (−$6.30/share); the maintenance definition is positive but only $83,128,000 in FY2026 against an $11.180B market cap. Every defensible base sits below every hurdle — the range runs F to, at the most generous reading, a deep D. Tier F by numbers stands without an override.
- Why the model is this harsh, and why it is not obviously wrong: U-Haul's capex line is mostly rental-fleet purchases — the product itself. The engine charges gross purchases ($3,154,325,000 in FY2026); the 10-K also reports proceeds from sales of property, plant and equipment of $710,286k in FY2026, an order smaller than the purchase program, so even a net-of-disposals view leaves fleet investment far above the $1,794,584,000 of operating cash flow. Management's own Q1 FY2027 guidance — a reduction of more than $500M in net fleet investment over the remaining quarters — is an admission that the fleet was over-bought; if that cut lands and depreciation rolls off, OE can inflect. That is a forecast, not a filing, and pass 1 scores filings.
- What would change the verdict: two consecutive fiscal years of capex inside OCF (the FY2027 fleet-investment cut, delivered), storage occupancy recovering as the development pipeline (11.7M NRSF in development or pending at FY2026, per the FY2026 release) leases up instead of diluting, and ROIC climbing back above the cost of capital. Until then the market is pricing the asset base ($16,380,445,000 of net PP&E, much of it land and storage real estate) and a normalized-earnings hope — neither of which this owner's-earnings screen credits.
- Governance/ownership note for any future position: family control, the two-class structure (the voting line is the thin one — 19,607,788 shares), and related-party arrangements are permanent features, not catalysts.
