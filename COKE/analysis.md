## What it is
The largest Coca-Cola bottler in the United States: it manufactures, markets and distributes nonalcoholic beverages — primarily products of The Coca-Cola Company (~85% of total bottle/can sales volume to retail customers, FY2025 10-K) plus Monster Energy and Dr Pepper — across an exclusive franchise territory spanning 14 states and the District of Columbia (~60 million consumers). Sparkling is the core; still beverages (energy, water, ready-to-drink tea/coffee, sports drinks) are the growth leg (Q2 2026 still volume +9.4% YoY, sparkling +7.0%, Q2 2026 10-Q MD&A). Controlled company: Class B Common Stock (Harrison family) carries 20 votes/share and ~78% of total voting power (FY2025 10-K, Note 1).

## SHARE BASIS — the trap in this name (read before quoting any per-share figure)
- COKE reports EPS under the **two-class method**: Common Stock and Class B Common Stock are filed as separate, class-dimensioned facts, so no undimensioned diluted share total survives into EDGAR companyfacts after FY2019 — hence `DATA_GAP:shares`.
- In the FY2025 10-K Consolidated Statements of Operations, the **Common Stock diluted line (83,807 thousand for FY2025) is already the company-wide diluted denominator**: it equals Common basic 73,658 thousand + Class B diluted 10,149 thousand, and FY2025 net income $570,582 thousand divided by it gives exactly the reported diluted EPS of $6.81. Summing the Common and Class B diluted lines (93,956 thousand) **double-counts Class B** — that wrong total is the first figure a web search surfaces for this name.
- A **10-for-1 forward stock split** was effected May 27, 2025; all 10-K share/EPS figures are retroactively split-adjusted. Pre-split filed counts in the early history (e.g. FY2019 diluted 9,417,000 in financials_history.csv) are on the old basis and are not comparable to the $210.33 post-split price.
- The count is still moving down: on **November 7, 2025** COKE repurchased all 18,835,460 shares held by The Coca-Cola Company for $2,392,103,420 ($127/share), and H1 2026 diluted WASO was **66,649 thousand** (Q2 2026 10-Q: Common basic 56,517 thousand + Class B basic 10,047 thousand + dilutive securities). The scored bases above use the FY2025 count (83,807,000) because that is the filing total the override mechanism carries; on the current count the same TTM OE total is roughly a quarter higher per share. See Thoughts for why the verdict survives that.

## OVERRIDE — SBC (filing, $0)
- **The gap:** `DATA_GAP:sbc` — no ShareBasedCompensation fact for FY2023–FY2025 under the engine's tags (preliminary run, exit 4).
- **The decision:** judgment.json carries `sbc_override = {value: 0, kind: "filing"}`. COKE has no equity-settled SBC to subtract: the cash-flow statement in the FY2025 10-K shows **no share-based/stock compensation adjustment** among the operating reconciling items for FY2023–FY2025, the equity statement shows **no SBC credit to APIC** in FY2023–FY2025 (APIC flat at $23,764 thousand), and companyfacts' last filed ShareBasedCompensation values are **$0 for FY2020 and FY2021** — so the override equals the last filed value and is in-band (dcf_numbers.json override_checks: sbc out_of_band false).
- **Why the CEO plan is not SBC here:** the only equity-linked plan, the Long-Term Performance Equity Plan for J. Frank Harrison, III, expensed $10.7M (FY2025) / $10.5M (FY2024) / $10.3M (FY2023) in SD&A (FY2025 10-K, Note 2), but it was never tagged ShareBasedCompensation — in FY2020/FY2021 LTPEP expense was $9.2M/$9.8M while filed SBC was $0 — because awards are liability-accrued and may be settled in cash at the holder's election. The cost already sits inside net income and OCF with no cash-flow addback; subtracting it again as SBC would double-count it (same treatment as SQM/TS in this sweep). Its dilutive performance shares are already inside the diluted share count.
- **Measured impact (dcf_numbers.json override_impact + registry):** binary — without the two overrides there is no tier at all. On the engine-native base the zero-fill comparison is distorted by the pre-split share history (zero-fill base $12.4191/share is the FY2019 pre-split figure leaking into the median); the with-override normalized base is $6.3048/share and the TTM base actually used is $7.6848/share.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong territorial franchise, but the brands are rented
Justification: exclusive Coca-Cola franchise territory (14 states + DC) as the largest US Coca-Cola bottler, with returns to prove it — fundamentals.json roic_proxy 25.2% (FY2021), 31.6% (FY2022), 43.7% (FY2023), 32.9% (FY2024), 39.8% (FY2025), five straight years well above the cost of capital — and gross margin expanded from 35.1% (FY2021) to 36.7%, 39.1%, 39.9%, 39.7% (FY2022–FY2025) on annual pricing actions. Capped at 7: the brands and concentrate belong to The Coca-Cola Company, the asset base needs ~$300M/yr of capex, and FY2017–19 roic_proxy of 3–7% shows the pre-scale version of this model.
- FOR: exclusive territory — no rival bottler can sell Coca-Cola to its ~60M consumers; ROIC history above; proven pricing power (gross-margin path above; Q2 2026 net sales +10.6% on volume +7.6% plus pricing, 10-Q MD&A); century-built manufacturing and route-to-market density; the bottling agreements were affirmed intact when KO exited its equity stake in Nov 2025.
- AGAINST: ~85% of bottle/can volume is KO product — COKE buys concentrate and KO controls brand strategy; secular sugary-drink headwinds (health trends, sugar taxes, GLP-1s); capital intensity (FY2025 capex $312,315,000); the Nov 2025 leverage step-up (below); controlled-company governance (Class B ~78% of votes; the only equity-linked pay plan covers the CEO alone).
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended ~10 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, legs quoted above.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023: net income $408,375,000 vs OCF $810,690,000): the FY2023 cash-flow statement (FY2025 10-K) explains it — non-cash addbacks of $159,354,000 fair-value adjustment on acquisition-related contingent consideration, $112,796,000 pension plan settlement expense and $153,472,000 depreciation & amortization. These are refranchising-era charges, not cash generation quality problems; the OCF-based conservative OE used throughout is not flattered by them. Caveat in Thoughts: the contingent-consideration liability is later paid in cash through **financing** activities, which OCF-based OE never sees.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc / OVERRIDE_APPLIED:shares**: see the two OVERRIDE sections — gap, decision, source and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (5.18x): real — the spread is mostly the super-bear's negative growth start against the super-bull's 18.4%. Confidence is capped at medium, and the verdict needs no sizing judgment: every scenario tier is D.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (3.89x normalized OE): balance sheet from the FY2025 10-K — total debt $2,786,009,000 (current $100,000,000 + noncurrent $2,686,009,000, the engine's net_debt basis) less cash $281,918,000 = net debt $2,504,091,000, plus operating lease liabilities $119,488,000; stockholders' equity is **negative $739,723,000** after $2,633,353,000 of FY2025 share repurchases (the KO buyout plus program buybacks). This is a deliberate recapitalization, not distress: H1 2026 shows $275,000,000 of term-loan payments already made (Q2 2026 10-Q cash flows), though net interest expense stepped up to $62,544,000 in H1 2026 vs $12,822,000 in H1 2025. Levered OE can still shrink fast if volume turns — the bear cases carry that.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (preliminary run only; dropped once the overrides filled the history): the 5-year OE/share range mixes pre-split share counts and the refranchising ramp, so the history is not one comparable series — handled by using the TTM base and reporting the normalized median alongside, not by trusting either blindly.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **The verdict is robust to the share-basis fight.** Every defensible denominator leaves this stock far above its DCF value at $210.33: the scored TTM base ($7.6848 on the FY2025 count) gives weighted IV@15% $82.72; crediting the post-buyout count (66,649 thousand, Q2 2026 10-Q) lifts the same TTM OE total roughly a quarter per share, and since the engine's IVs scale with the base, weighted IV@15% would still sit near half the price. On the normalized median base ($6.3048) the values fall further. There is no share-count reading on which COKE clears 15% at $210.33.
- **What the market is paying for:** KO sold its entire stake at $127/share in November 2025; eleven months later the stock is $210.33. The re-rating prices float scarcity (66.6M shares out, ~78% of votes with the family, KO's ~19M-share overhang gone), buyback accretion, and a genuinely strong 2026 volume print (+7.6% cases in Q2). Those are real facts — they are just not owner's-earnings facts, and even the super-bull scenario (18.4% growth start, 1.25x OE) only reaches a 12.86% IRR.
- **OE quality caveat:** cash payments on the acquisition-related contingent consideration run through financing activities — $68,884,000 in FY2025, $37,126,000 in H1 2026 (10-K / Q2 2026 10-Q cash flows) — a real, recurring owner cost of the refranchising deals that OCF-based owner's earnings does not deduct. True owner cash is somewhat below the scored OE.
- Quality itself is not the debate: ROIC proxy 33–44% over the last three fiscal years, gross margin ~40%, OE positive 5/5 years, dividend $0.50/share paid in H1 2026 (Q2 2026 10-Q), and deleveraging under way. The debate is only price: at 27x the TTM OE base ($210.33 vs $7.68/share), the buyer needs the super-bull path and then some.
