All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json, or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed. Filing figures quoted for the overrides are the ones cited in judgment.json; company prints are from the Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 earnings releases.

## What it is
Pure-play Appalachian natural gas and NGL producer: Marcellus shale in southwest Pennsylvania, ~69% gas / rest NGLs and oil, production 2.24 Bcfe/d in FY2025 (company Q4 2025 release), proved reserves 18.1 Tcfe at YE2025. It sells a commodity at other people's prices; what it owns is low-cost rock, 30+ years of undrilled inventory (company), and a transport/marketing book that gets its gas and NGLs to premium markets instead of stranding them at Appalachia basis.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, a cost position, not a franchise
Justification: A real but narrow cost-and-resource moat, not a franchise: Range sits on low-cost Marcellus rock with 30+ years of inventory and a transport/marketing book that earns premium realizations over NYMEX and Mont Belvieu, which is worth something through a cycle. Against that it is a pure price-taker in a single basin whose reserves deplete and whose returns are the commodity cycle itself — filed ROE swung from −43% (FY2020) to +41% (FY2022), and fundamentals.json carries no roic_proxy series (operating income is untagged), so there is no evidence of through-cycle returns well above the cost of capital. That is a 4/10: better rock and costs than the average E&P, no pricing power. CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 4 = 7.2 years.
- FOR: low-cost Marcellus core in the lowest-cost U.S. gas basin — all-in capital spending of $0.83 per mcfe in FY2025 (company) and record drilling/completion efficiency in Q2 2026; 30+ years of undrilled inventory and 18.1 Tcfe of proved reserves; marketing/transport premiums — realized price including hedges was a $0.64 per mcfe premium to NYMEX in Q2 2026 and pre-hedge NGL realizations a $3.49/bbl premium over Mont Belvieu (Q2 2026 release); repaired balance sheet (net debt $1.198B at YE2025, ~$881M by Q2 2026) funding buybacks and a rising dividend from free cash flow.
- AGAINST: pure commodity price-taker — OE/share has swung more than 3x in five years with the strip; single-basin Appalachia concentration with takeaway and permitting limits; a depleting asset — the FY2026 capital budget of $650–700M buys only modest production growth (guided 2.35–2.40 Bcfe/d vs 2.24 in FY2025); returns are the cycle, not a franchise (ROE −43% FY2020 to +41% FY2022 in fundamentals.json; roic_proxy null because operating income is untagged, and the 85–94% "gross margin" series in fundamentals.json is a revenue-less-DD&A artifact for an E&P, not margin evidence).
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection. Peer calibration in this sweep: EQT moat 4, Antero 3, APA 3, all low confidence.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: D (9.03% on the scored run; 9.58% on the preliminary median-base run) to C (11.67% if the engine's price-artifact growth blend is taken at face value). No defensible method clears the 15% hurdle at $43.65 — the price needs the super-bull scenario to be the base case.
- What the market is paying for is visible in the Q2 2026 print: production 2.30 Bcfe/d rising toward 2.6 in 2027, a $0.64/mcfe realized premium to NYMEX, record NGL premiums, and the Appalachia-supplies-AI-power demand story. Those are real operating facts. They are also exactly the facts a cyclical producer shows at the good part of the cycle, and the TTM base capitalizes them at the moment they print.
- Quality is not the debate: this is one of the better-run U.S. gas producers — lowest-cost basin, long inventory, disciplined capex ($650–700M budget against ~$1.36B of TTM OCF), falling debt, buybacks ($231M in FY2025 per the Q4 release) and a rising dividend. The debate is price: at 15.0x the TTM OE print ($43.65 / $2.91) and 34x the normalized median ($1.28), the buyer is underwriting the strip, not the rock.
- Data caveat for the next pass: the engine cannot read this filer's capex or SBC tags at all (the E&P capex tag `PaymentsToAcquireOilAndGasPropertyAndEquipment` is missing from its candidate list), so every future re-score of RRC — and likely of any E&P filing on that tag — needs these overrides re-derived from the then-current 10-K, and its TTM legs will again be prorations rather than filed 10-Q sums.
