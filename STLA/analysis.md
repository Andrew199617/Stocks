## What it is
Multinational automaker formed in January 2021 by the FCA–PSA merger: 14 brands (Jeep, Ram, Dodge, Chrysler, Fiat, Peugeot, Citroën, Opel/Vauxhall, Alfa Romeo, Maserati, DS, Lancia, Abarth, Fiat Professional), ~5.5 million vehicles shipped in 2025. Reports in EUR on Form 20-F (IFRS); the NYSE security is the common share itself. Price $4.37 (nasdaq_quote_api); market cap (est) $12.615B — in scope under $40B. Net debt (latest) $18.566B, plus lease liabilities of $2,883.45M in dcf_numbers.json.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: scale and a few genuine brand franchises are real, but they have not produced durable pricing power, share defense, or through-cycle returns (see ROIC below).
- FOR: scale — one of the world's largest automakers (~5.5M units shipped in 2025), shared platforms and purchasing leverage across 14 brands; Jeep and Ram franchises in North America (47% and 34% of 2025 U.S. sales respectively — the group's historic profit pool); leading regional positions — #2 OEM in EU30 (~16% share in 2025), #1 in EU light commercial vehicles (~28.6%), market leader in South America (~19% share; #1 in Brazil and Argentina).
- AGAINST: share erosion on both sides of the Atlantic (Europe 20.2% in 2021 to ~14-16% in 2025; U.S. 12.0% to 7.7%); no pricing power in the downcycle (2024-2025 revenue pressure from price cuts and high incentives); commodity mass-market product facing Chinese EV entrants in Europe; 14-brand sprawl, and an EV strategy over-investment that forced the ~€25.4B of 2025 reset charges.
- **ROIC evidence (fundamentals.json roic_proxy series):** 15.68% (2019), 16.96% (2020), 34.42% (2021), 32.84% (2022), 27.37% (2023), 3.42% (2024), −29.71% (2025). Gross margin (fundamentals.json): 20.97% / 19.74% / 19.73% / 19.64% / 20.12% over 2019-2023, then 13.08% (2024) and −1.38% (2025). The 2021-2023 returns were shortage-cycle pricing, not a structural moat — they vanished the moment volumes and pricing normalized. A moat above 3 is not supportable on this record.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years (key_numbers.md) — moot here, since no DCF runs on a negative base.

The one-offs are the 2025 strategic reset: ~€25.4B of charges in H2 2025 alone per the FY2025 results release — platform impairments (€6,583M in the full-year fact sheet), product-plan realignments and program cancellations (€9,072M), battery-JV charges (€2,054M), hydrogen fuel-cell discontinuation (€1,094M), a warranty-estimate change (€4,130M), Takata recall (€622M) and restructuring (€913M) — largely non-cash impairments, which is why OCF is far less negative than net income; but OCF is still negative, so the divergence explains the gap without rescuing the base.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the collapse of the cash machine, not the price: OE/share went $3.81 (2022) → $2.72 (2023) → −$3.34 (2024) → −$5.16 (2025) while capex stayed at ~$9-11B/yr. Even excluding every 2025 reset charge, adjusted operating income for FY2025 was a loss (−€842M per the FY2025 fact sheet), so this is not purely an accounting-write-down story — the underlying business lost money in 2025 before the charges.
- Bull case, stated fairly: the price ($4.37, ~$12.6B market cap) already prices deep distress; H1 2026 shows a return to small profits, North America recovering, and management guiding to improvement with positive industrial FCF in 2027. If OE merely returned to the 2023 level ($2.72/share), the stock would clear 15% easily at this price. But that is a turnaround bet on a normalized base that is currently negative — the sweep's Tier F is a statement about the filed base, not a claim the equity is worthless.
- Balance sheet: net debt $18.566B (key_numbers.md) exceeds the $12.615B market cap; the dividend was suspended for 2026 per the FY2025 results. Leverage plus negative OE is the risk pairing, and credit sits one notch above junk per contemporaneous reporting — no position sizing argument survives a second reset year.
- Method range: any defensible method today lands on F for the scored base (median and latest bases are both negative). The range that matters is forward-looking and unscored: 2023-style OE ($2.72/share) would tier high; a repeat of 2024-2025 tiers F at any price. Pass 2 / a future re-score after FY2026 filings is where this name gets re-decided.
