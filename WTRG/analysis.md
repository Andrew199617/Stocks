## What it is
Regulated water, wastewater and natural gas utility (Aqua and Peoples brands), serving approximately 5.5 million people across nine states. Revenue is set by state utility commissions: the company spends on infrastructure, the spend enters rate base, and rates are set to recover it plus an allowed return. Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-05): revenue $530.9M, +3.1% YoY, with regulated water revenue +7.6% on regulatory recoveries and volume; GAAP EPS $0.37, adjusted EPS $0.38, with merger-related costs weighing on GAAP. Management reaffirmed a 5%–7% EPS growth outlook and a $1.7B 2026 infrastructure-spending plan, and the quarterly dividend was raised 5.25% to $0.3606/share (payable 2026-09-01).

**The dominant fact is the pending merger.** On 2025-10-27 American Water Works (AWK) and Essential Utilities announced an all-stock merger: WTRG shareholders receive 0.305 AWK shares per WTRG share (≈10% premium at announcement), creating a combined company with ~$40B pro forma market value serving 4.7M+ water/wastewater and 740,000+ gas connections. Shareholders of both companies approved in February 2026; Kentucky (2026-04-21), Ohio (2026-05-14) and Virginia (2026-06-22) commissions have approved; a non-unanimous Pennsylvania settlement was filed 2026-09-15 and remains pending, as does HSR clearance. Expected close: by end of Q1 2027. WTRG's price is therefore substantially a merger-consideration price (0.305 × AWK, discounted for approval risk and time), not a standalone-DCF price.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate; durable franchise, capped economics
Justification: exclusive regulated territories and essential-service demand make the franchise durable, but regulators cap the return on it: roic_proxy (fundamentals.json) ran 4.3% / 4.4% / 4.3% / 4.4% / 6.1% over FY2021–FY2025 — at or below a normal cost of capital — and gross margin is not filed (the company reports no COGS split, so no gross-margin series exists to quote). The moat protects volume and cost recovery, not price.
- FOR: exclusive regulated service territories — legal monopoly on water/wastewater for ~5.5 million people across nine states; essential, non-discretionary service with inelastic demand and a proven regulatory-recovery mechanism for infrastructure spend; rate-base model — the $1.7B 2026 infrastructure plan enters rate base and earns an allowed return, compounding regulated earnings; scale as one of the largest U.S. water/wastewater utilities, with a pipeline of municipal-system acquisitions.
- AGAINST: returns are set by regulators, not the company — allowed ROE caps the economics and ROIC has run only ~4–6%; capex structurally exceeds operating cash flow — growth is funded with debt and ATM equity issuance, diluting per-share economics; the gas segment faces long-run electrification/decarbonization transition risk; PFAS and water-quality mandates force capex whose recovery depends on rate-case outcomes; the pending American Water merger adds approval and integration risk, and the standalone moat is being absorbed into AWK's.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years (key_numbers.md): growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years. Moot for the verdict — there is no positive OE path to fade.

No impairment, tax deposit, or other one-off is hiding in the gap, and the OCF-based conservative OE is the series used throughout, so the divergence does not contaminate the base.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH:** stands, and it is the correct lens for the whole result. An OCF-minus-all-capex DCF structurally fails regulated utilities: capex is not discretionary maintenance, it is rate-base investment recovered through future rates, and the allowed return arrives over decades, funded meanwhile by debt and equity issuance. Confidence is **low** and the F should be read as "this model cannot value this business," not as "this business is worthless" — the maintenance-definition OE above (+$307,321,000 FY2025) and TTM net income of $554,870,000 show an accrual-profitable, dividend-paying regulated franchise. What the F does establish on the sweep's own terms: WTRG generates no conservative owner's earnings, so it cannot clear Andrew's 15% hurdle on this model, and no price fixes a negative base.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The standalone DCF question is about to be moot: if the AWK merger closes (expected by end of Q1 2027), WTRG holders will own 0.305 AWK shares per share and the analysis that matters is AWK's valuation and the remaining approval path (Pennsylvania, HSR), not WTRG's standalone cash flows. The $40.83 price should be benchmarked against 0.305 × AWK's price, not against any standalone IV — this model produces none.
- The structural cash story is real even if the tier overstates it: a decade of capex above OCF, net debt of $8.097B against $6.857B of equity, ~2.2%/yr share issuance, and a dividend paid from financing. Regulated utilities run this way by design — the return of and on that capital comes through rate cases, which is precisely the cash-flow timing an owner's-earnings DCF refuses to credit.
- Method range: conservative OE definition → F (this run). Maintenance definition (capex capped at D&A) → positive FY2025 OE of $307,321,000, which would support a conventional utility valuation — but that definition would flatter every capital-intensive grower in the sweep, so it is reported, not scored.
