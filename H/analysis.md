## What it is
Global lodging company skewed to luxury, lifestyle, and all-inclusive: managed/franchised fee business plus a shrinking owned/leased estate, World of Hyatt loyalty, and distribution (ALG Vacations, Mr & Mrs Smith). The defining 2025 event was the Playa Hotels acquisition (June 2025) followed by the sale of the entire Playa real estate portfolio for $2.0B, closed December 30, 2025, with 50-year management agreements retained on the sold resorts — i.e., Hyatt bought the fee contracts and briefly owned the real estate in between. Q2 2026 prints (via search of the Q2 2026 release): comparable system-wide RevPAR +5.9%, gross fees $324M (+7.8%), Adjusted EBITDA $297M, net rooms growth 3.9% (4.4% excluding Playa rooms removed in H2 2025), pipeline ~154,000 rooms (+10.0%), FY2026 RevPAR guidance 3.5–4.5% and net rooms growth ~6%.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: Strong luxury/lifestyle brands and World of Hyatt — the highest-value major hotel loyalty currency — plus long-dated management contracts (50-year terms on the sold Playa resorts) and a 154,000-room pipeline, but subscale at ~1,400-1,500 properties vs Marriott/Hilton at 8,000-9,000+, no filed ROIC/gross-margin series in fundamentals.json (operating income untagged, roic_proxy null), and a volatile, thin OE record (negative in 2020, $0.89/share in 2025) that does not evidence returns well above cost of capital through a cycle.
- FOR: World of Hyatt loyalty — highest-value major hotel points currency, high-end guest base; luxury/lifestyle brand portfolio (Park Hyatt, Alila, Inclusive Collection) skewed to high-value guests; long-dated fee contracts — 50-year management agreements on the Playa resorts sold in Dec 2025; pipeline ~154,000 rooms (+10% YoY) and net rooms growth ~4-6% supporting fee growth.
- AGAINST: Subscale network (~1,400-1,500 properties) vs Marriott/Hilton at 8,000-9,000+ — weaker owner distribution and loyalty network effects; no evidenced high ROIC (roic_proxy and gross margin are null in fundamentals.json — operating income is not tagged, so neither series exists in the computed outputs); cyclical lodging demand — revenue fell from $5,020M (2019) to $2,066M (2020); leverage — net debt $3,491M is 9.4x normalized OE (flag basis; diagnostics in key_numbers.md: 9.38x).
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.
- On the required ROIC/margin evidence: fundamentals.json carries no roic_proxy series and no gross-margin series for Hyatt (both null, because no operating-income tag is filed in the engine's candidate set). There is therefore no computed ROIC history to quote, and a moat above 6 on this record would be assertion, not evidence — the OE record above is the observable return history, and it is thin and volatile.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, and here it does not save the stock: on the TTM base ($1.85/share) the value would be roughly half the headline; on the median base actually used, weighted IV@15% is $36.99; even the super-bull scenario (13.0% growth start, 1.25x OE) yields only a 7.40% IRR and an IV@15% of $61.56 — still 64% below the $169.13 price. Every defensible method lands in Tier D.
- Quality is real but priced: the asset-light direction is genuine (Playa real estate exited, 50-year fee contracts kept, pipeline +10%), World of Hyatt is the most valuable major loyalty currency, and Q2 2026 fee growth of +7.8% is a good print. None of that bridges a 2.30% OE yield at this price — the buyer at $169.13 is paying for a fee-compounder outcome the filed OE history has not yet produced.
- What would change the verdict: several years of post-Playa filed OE/share at or above the $3.90 median with the leverage paid down, or a price anywhere near the hurdle levels. On this model the stock clears 15% only below ~$36.99.
- Risks to this (short-side) verdict: a sustained RevPAR upcycle plus faster buybacks at lower prices, further asset sales at large gains recycled into buybacks, or the market continuing to price Hyatt on Adjusted EBITDA (~$1.1–1.2B guided) rather than owner's earnings — the gap between those two measures (D&A, SBC, and the owned-estate capex) is exactly what this sweep refuses to ignore.
