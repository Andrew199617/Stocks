# DHI — D.R. Horton, Inc.
Run 2026-10-13, engine v4. In scope (universe market cap $37.7B; ~$38.4B at the current 283M share count).

## What it is
The largest U.S. homebuilder by volume every year since 2002 — roughly 15% of new U.S. homes, a share it has doubled in a decade. Homebuilding is the core (brands: D.R. Horton, Express, Emerald, Freedom; average closing price $362,000 in Q3 FY2026, ~30% below the U.S. average new-home price — an explicit affordability/first-time-buyer focus), plus a Rental segment (single- and multi-family built to sell to investors), Forestar (majority-owned lot development; 67% of Q2 FY2026 closings were on lots developed by Forestar or third parties), and Financial Services (mortgage/title). Q3 FY2026 (ended 2026-06-30): revenue $9.23B (flat YoY), net income $904.9M (-11.7%), EPS $3.20 (-4.8%), closings 23,983 (+4%), home-sales gross margin 20.7% (down from 21.8%), cancellations 20% (up from 17%). Management cut FY2026 guidance in July to revenue $32.5–$33.0B and 83,800–84,300 closings (from $33.5–$34.5B); FY2026 results are due 2026-10-29.

## Owner's earnings

Maintenance version (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC) prints $3,585.2M = $11.57/share — it equals net income exactly here because capex > D&A (the two cancel) and the engine captures no ΔNWC; for a homebuilder that is a real omission, since inventory *is* the working capital (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE below).

The conservative series is inventory-timing noise, not earning power, on a single year: OE/share was $0.96 (FY2021) and $0.87 (FY2022) while net income/share was $11.42 and $16.51 — OCF collapsed because the company was funding a massive inventory build — then snapped to $11.78 in FY2023 as homes closed. ## MOAT — score 5/10, moderate/narrow. CAP = 8.5 years
**FOR:** Scale that has actually compounded — #1 by volume since 2002, ~15% national share, doubled in 10 years — converts to procurement buying power, subcontractor priority, and local density no sub-scale rival can match. Lot discipline: 570,000 lots with only 22% owned / 78% controlled through purchase contracts, plus captive Forestar supply, caps the land blow-up risk that kills builders in downturns. Balance sheet ($6.0B liquidity at Q2 FY2026, total debt $6.6B, debt/capital 21.7%) lets it keep building and buying back stock through a trough while small builders starve — share is structurally gained in downturns. Affordability positioning ($362k ASP, smaller Express floorplans) targets the deepest, most undersupplied buyer pool.
**AGAINST:** The product is a commodity — no switching costs, no network effects, no lock-in; buyers choose on price, location, and the rate buydown. There is no visible pricing power: incentives ran ~10% of revenue, ~90% of mortgage customers used buydowns, and home-sales gross margin still fell 21.8% → 20.7%. Land and lots are bought in a competitive open market (lot costs +5% YoY in Q3 even as stick-and-brick fell 5%). Lennar, PulteGroup, and NVR are capable, well-capitalized rivals; NVR's asset-light model earns structurally higher ROE. Returns here are cycle timing plus land timing, not a durable per-unit advantage. That is a real but narrow moat — 5/10, CAP 8.5 years (growth holds 2 years, then converges to 2.5% terminal over 8.5 years, 15-yr projection).

- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (super-bull IV 4.4× super-bear):** Real, not a modeling error — this is a cyclical at a possible trough, and even the scenario *sign* of growth is in dispute. Consequence applied: confidence capped at medium (never high with this flag), and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND (share count -4.4%/yr):** Credited twice, both disclosed: the growth blend carries the +4.41pp share-count adjustment, and the TTM base already divides by the shrunken 283.0M count. The treatment is the standardized one used across the sweep; if anything it flatters DHI's per-share growth, which makes the below-hurdle verdict more robust, not less. The buyback is real cash: $2.2B in 9M FY2026, ≥$3.25B guided for FY2026, new $5B authorization.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs):** Resolved as a measurement artifact of the conservative definition, not earnings-power volatility: OE/share ranged $0.87–$11.78 across FY2021–25 while net income/share ranged $11.42–$16.51, because OCF absorbs the inventory build/liquidation cycle. Mitigations applied: normalized/TTM base (not a single year) and bear weight widened — super bear + bear = 45% vs the 35% default, per the flag's own instruction.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2024: NI $4,756M vs OCF $2,190M):** Found: working capital — inventory investment. In FY2024 revenue still grew (+3.9% to $36.80B) and the company spent heavily on homes under construction and lot development ahead of closings, so cash went into inventory instead of out as OCF; the same pattern, more extreme, explains FY2021–22 (OCF ~$0.5B vs NI $4.2–5.9B). No tax deposit, impairment, or one-off gain is involved — capex is trivial (~$0.15B/yr) and SBC is small. Implication: single-year conservative OE *understates* earning power in build years and overstates it in liquidation years; the TTM window (which spans both) is the right antidote, and on TTM the two definitions converge.
- **Not fired, but checked — balance sheet:** the engine extracted no debt (net_debt_latest null; DHI's debt tags don't match the engine's candidates), so neither HIGH_NET_DEBT nor BIG_NET_CASH could fire. From company reporting at Q2 FY2026: total debt $6.6B, cash $1.9B, liquidity $6.0B, debt/capital 21.7% — net debt roughly $4.7B, ~1.5× TTM OE. Modest for a builder holding a large inventory balance; it neither rescues nor sinks the verdict, and book value of $84.85/share (growing ~5%/yr despite the earnings decline) is a soft floor well below price.

## Thoughts / overlay
- The verdict is robust to the base fight that usually moves homebuilders: prelim (median base, default probs) gave 10.4% weighted IRR; the final TTM/bear-skewed run gives 10.3%. Even crediting the higher *median net income/share* of $13.82 (FY2023–25) as earning power, the DCF scales linearly to a weighted IV@15% of ~$108 — still 20% below the $135.57 price and still short of a 15% IRR. This is a Tier C on any defensible base, not a definition artifact.
- Only the super bull clears the current price at a 15% discount ($173.55), and even the plain bull does not ($114.50). At $135.57 the market is already paying for a housing recovery; the buyer is not paid to wait for one.
- The honest bull case is cyclical, not DCF-visible at trough inputs: chronic U.S. housing undersupply, DHI gaining share, and EPS that has snapped back violently in past recoveries. That case lives in the 10% super-bull weight — sized accordingly.
- Red flags / watch items: margin compression is incentive-driven and incentives get *more* expensive as rates rise (buydowns to 4.9% against a ~6.5–7% market rate); cancellations at 20%; lot-cost inflation +5% YoY; the Rental segment faces a proposed ban on institutional single-family purchases (small today — $31M pretax in Q3 — but it removes a diversification vector); Q4/FY2026 print on 2026-10-29 is the next hard data point.
- **Hurdle math:** the price that clears 15% on the weighted path is the weighted IV@15% itself, **~$84**; with a 25% margin of safety, **~$63**. Book value ($84.85) coincidentally sits right at the hurdle price — a useful cross-check, not a coincidence to trade on.
