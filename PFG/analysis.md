## What it is
Des Moines-based retirement, workplace-benefits and asset-management group. Three earnings engines: Retirement and Income Solutions (a leading defined-contribution, defined-benefit, pension-risk-transfer and ESOP provider, focused on small and mid-sized employers), Benefits and Protection (group dental, disability, life and supplemental health, plus business-market life products), and Principal Asset Management (global asset management). It exited U.S. retail fixed annuities and individual life in 2022 (reserves reinsured/sold to Talcott Resolution) and redeployed toward the workplace/fee businesses. Latest quarter Q2 2026 (10-Q filed 2026-07-29): revenue $3,905.8 million, up 6.4% year over year, net income $403.4 million, essentially flat year over year (search results summarizing the 10-Q); segment pre-tax operating earnings grew 12% to $668.8 million, with the GAAP result held back by a $166.5 million pre-tax loss from exited business, driven by a funds-withheld reinsurance embedded derivative that swung to a $104.4 million loss in the quarter (same source). Q3 2026 reports October 26, 2026.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: a sticky, scaled U.S. retirement franchise for small and mid-sized employers (defined contribution, defined benefit, pension risk transfer and ESOP services, bundled with group benefits and protection) plus a diversified global asset-management arm — workplace distribution and long-duration plan relationships are hard to replicate quickly and OE was positive in 5 of the last 5 fiscal years — against commodity, price-competed products with no structural pricing power: asset-management fees face passive/fee compression and active-equity outflows from much larger rivals (Vanguard, Fidelity, BlackRock), spread and premium earnings are hostage to rates, credit and markets rather than Principal's own pricing, legacy exited annuity/life blocks still inject reinsurance embedded-derivative volatility into GAAP results, and insurance regulation/capital intensity caps returns.
- FOR: Sticky SMB retirement franchise — leading defined-contribution, pension risk transfer and ESOP provider; workplace plan relationships are long-duration and bundled with other products.
- FOR: Diversified earnings mix — retirement fees, benefits & protection premiums, asset-management fees and spread income dampen any single product cycle.
- FOR: Scale and distribution — established advisor/employer channels for mid-market employers that larger, scale-focused rivals underserve.
- FOR: Through-cycle profitability and capital return — OE positive 5 of last 5 FY; diluted shares shrinking 4.0%/yr on buybacks.
- AGAINST: No structural pricing power — retirement, benefits and asset-management products are price-competed; fees face passive compression and active-equity outflows.
- AGAINST: Market and rate hostage — AUM fees, net investment income and spreads ride equity markets and the yield curve, outside management control.
- AGAINST: Legacy exited-block volatility — funds-withheld reinsurance embedded derivative swung to a large loss in Q2 2026 and drove the exited-business pre-tax loss well above the prior year.
- AGAINST: Capital intensity and regulation — insurance capital rules cap growth and capital return; larger rivals carry bigger technology/compliance budgets.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the headline, as with APP:** the engine's raw OCF-median base produces Tier A (weighted IRR 21.713982267367415%). Every earnings-based way of looking at this insurer lands lower, because the OCF base capitalizes float/reserve growth that belongs to future policyholders and plan participants, and GAAP earnings are themselves trough-distorted by revaluations (FY2023 net income $623,200,000 against FY2022's $4,756,900,000). The defensible method range here is therefore B-or-lower on earnings lenses to A on the raw engine, with the A end resting entirely on the rejected-for-an-insurer cash-flow definition; the verdict is sized to the conservative end, B — the same one-notch softening applied to the other v5 insurer runs (HIG, ACGL, WRB).
- Quality is not the debate: a sticky SMB retirement franchise, diversified fee/premium/spread mix, OE positive in all of the last 5 fiscal years on the conservative definition, and a buyback shrinking the count 4.023630602458916%/yr. The debate is the entry multiple for a commodity-product, market-sensitive insurer whose GAAP earnings carry legacy exited-block volatility.
- Red flags to monitor: the funds-withheld reinsurance embedded derivative on the exited blocks (a $166.5 million pre-tax exited-business loss in Q2 2026 alone), active-equity outflows and fee compression in asset management, credit losses in the investment portfolio in a downturn, mortality/longevity assumption updates, and buyback pace if markets fall.
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine's numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at $167.93843645478313 (the weighted IV@15%) and trades 55.77982139491036% below that figure at $107.805 — but that clearing price inherits the float-inflated base, so it overstates the true margin of safety; a margin-of-safety buy price would need to sit materially below the weighted IV@15%, in words, and no hand-computed figure is quoted for it. On the conservative (final-tier B) reading, the honest claim is "clears 15% with room, sized like a B", not "56% upside".
