## What it is
The dominant core-systems vendor for property & casualty insurers: InsuranceSuite (PolicyCenter, ClaimCenter, BillingCenter) and InsuranceNow on Guidewire Cloud Platform, plus newer pricing/AI products (PricingCenter, ProNavigator). Fiscal year ends July 31. FY2026: revenue $1,475,363,000 (+22.70% latest rev YoY per key_numbers.md), ARR $1.237B (+19% constant currency, per company Q4 FY2026 reporting), cloud ARR +35% to 84% of total ARR, subscription revenue rising as the on-prem license base migrates to cloud. FY2027 guidance (company): revenue $1.707B–$1.727B, ARR $1.45B–$1.46B (~18% constant-currency growth).

## Moat: 6/10 — strong niche franchise, unproven through-cycle economics
Justification: Dominant P&C core platform (InsuranceSuite/Guidewire Cloud, 570+ insurers) with extreme switching costs — multi-year, mission-critical core replacements — and gross ARR attrition under 1.5% (under 1% for core customers) in FY2026. Capped at 6, not 7+: GAAP ROIC was negative through FY2024 and only inflected to +4.27% in FY2025 and +15.5% in FY2026 (fundamentals.json roic_proxy), so there is no through-cycle record of ROIC well above the cost of capital; the market is a narrow P&C-core niche contested by Duck Creek, Sapiens and Majesco, and long implementations carry execution risk.
- FOR: InsuranceSuite is the enterprise benchmark for large North American P&C carriers — PolicyCenter/ClaimCenter/BillingCenter on Guidewire Cloud; extreme switching costs (core-system replacements are multi-year, mission-critical programs); gross ARR attrition under 1.5% in FY2026, under 1% for core-system customers, 105 customers above $5M fully-ramped ARR; cloud ARR +35% in FY2026 to 84% of total ARR — the installed base is migrating onto its own cloud, deepening lock-in; expanding wallet share inside the base (PricingCenter — Nationwide as first US tier-1 customer; ProNavigator — 28 wins in FY2026).
- AGAINST: ROIC history is weak — roic_proxy (fundamentals.json) ran −5.55% (FY2021), −13.09% (FY2022), −9.88% (FY2023), −3.48% (FY2024), then +4.27% (FY2025) and +15.5% (FY2026), with no through-cycle proof; gross margin only just recovered to 64.2% in FY2026 from the 46.4% FY2022 cloud-transition trough (fundamentals.json annual gross margins: 52.4% FY2021, 46.4% FY2022, 50.6% FY2023, 59.5% FY2024, 62.5% FY2025, 64.2% FY2026); narrow TAM (P&C core only, finite pool of large-carrier replacements/migrations); credible challengers (Duck Creek modular cloud, Sapiens unified suite, Majesco, SI-led modernization); long services-heavy implementations; SBC of $181,799,000 in FY2026 (12.3% of revenue) caps owner's earnings.
- The written reason the measured ROIC history understates the franchise, in part: FY2021–FY2024 GAAP results carried the double cost of running on-prem and cloud estates through the transition plus heavy SBC, while gross margin and operating margin (−24.5% FY2022 to +10.2% FY2026, fundamentals.json) were mechanically depressed. That argues the forward franchise is better than the backward ROIC — but one year at +15.5% is an inflection print, not a cycle, so the score stays at 6.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real and structural, not noise — it is the cloud transition: OE/share went from −$2.21 (FY2022) and −$1.34 (FY2023) to +$2.29 (FY2026) on the filed history as gross margin recovered from 46.4% to 64.2%. Handled by the normalized 3-yr median base plus bear-widened probabilities (15/30 on super-bear/bear).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024, 2025, 2026): explained — it is SBC, not a one-off tax deposit or impairment. Net income vs OCF on the filed history: FY2024 −$6,103,000 vs $195,748,000 with SBC of $146,460,000; FY2025 $69,804,000 vs $300,867,000 with SBC of $161,556,000; FY2026 $139,283,000 vs $389,716,000 with SBC of $181,799,000. In each year the non-cash SBC add-back accounts for most of the gap, with D&A and deferred-revenue/working-capital moves making up the rest. Consequence: the divergence does not flatter the base — the conservative OE definition deducts SBC in full, which is exactly why OE is so far below OCF.
- **DEBT_TAGS_BLANK**: resolved from the filing. The engine found no LongTermDebt tag for FY2025–FY2026 because Guidewire's funded debt is tagged as convertible senior notes, not LongTermDebt. FY2026 10-K balance sheet: convertible senior notes, net $678,094,000 (long-term), against cash $372,887,000 + short-term investments $380,175,000 + long-term investments $462,223,000, with lease liabilities of $36,893,000. Balance-sheet verdict: net cash in substance; no leverage risk in the verdict. Net debt in the engine/score stays null (unknown to the engine), and no net-debt figure is quoted as computed.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $2.29 vs 3-yr median $1.55): both are "real"; they measure different things. The median is dragged by the FY2024 trough year ($0.52), which sits inside the 3-yr window, so the median understates the current run-rate; the latest year is the first clean post-transition year and may overstate the through-cycle run-rate if FY2026 margins prove peak. The median was kept as the conservative base. Method range: re-running the same scenarios on the latest-FY base would scale IVs by roughly the base ratio — the weighted IV@15% would still sit in the tens of dollars against a $168.86 price, so the tier is D on either base. (Stated in words, not as a computed figure — no engine run was made on the alternate base.)
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (base OE yield 0.92%): agreed — at $168.86 the valuation rests almost entirely on future growth and margin expansion, not on current owner's earnings. This is the core of the verdict: even the super-bull path (30.9% growth start, 1.25x OE) yields only a 7.52% IRR.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.78x super-bear IV): real — the outcome range is wide because the base is small and the growth path is contested. Confidence is capped at medium (never high with this flag), and the verdict is sized to the conservative end; the saving grace is that every scenario, super bull included, lands in Tier D at this price.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Quality is not the debate. This is a genuinely strong niche franchise — dominant P&C core platform, <1% core attrition, cloud migration largely self-cannibalizing onto its own platform, net-cash balance sheet, $606,309,000 of TTM buybacks shrinking the count. The debate is purely price: $168.86 capitalizes decades of flawless compounding that the owner's-earnings math cannot produce even in the super-bull case.
- The market is pricing the non-GAAP story (non-GAAP operating income and FCF before SBC). On the sweep's rules SBC is a real cost — $181,799,000 in FY2026 — and once it is charged, OE/share is $1.55 normalized / $2.29 latest against a $168.86 price. Anyone quoting GWRE on FCF/share ($4.42 latest FY on the filed history) without the SBC deduction is using a different definition than this sweep's.
- Risks to the short/avoid stance (i.e., what would make this verdict wrong): sustained 20%+ ARR growth with SBC falling fast as a share of revenue would lift OE/share far faster than the modeled paths; a takeout at a strategic premium is also possible in a consolidating vendor landscape. Neither is in the weighted case at meaningful probability.
- Method range: D on the median base, D on the latest-FY base (in words, above), D in every individual scenario from super bear to super bull. This is one of the least method-sensitive verdicts in the sweep — the gap between price and any defensible IV is roughly 3–6x.
