## What it is
National hospital and ambulatory-care operator. Three pieces: 50 acute-care hospitals (Hospital Operations), USPI — the largest US ambulatory surgery center operator, with ownership interests in 538 ASCs and 26 surgical hospitals in 37 states as of June 30, 2026 (next-largest operator SCA Health has 320 facilities, per industry reporting) — and Conifer, a revenue-cycle services business. The decade-long pivot is away from hospitals toward USPI: in 2024 Tenet sold 14 hospitals for more than $4.8B. Q2 2026: net operating revenues $5.63B, up 6.8% YoY; ambulatory (USPI) revenue up 9.3% with revenue per case up 6.3% while same-facility surgical cases fell 1.2%; same-hospital adjusted admissions up 2.6% and net revenue per adjusted admission up 3.3%. Management raised 2026 guidance: net operating revenues $21.9–$22.5B, adjusted EBITDA $4.83–$5.03B, adjusted diluted EPS $20.30–$21.69, adjusted free cash flow $2.725–$3.025B (company Q2 2026 reporting, via search results).

That cash sits inside the TTM OCF leg, and the client revenue behind it is gone going forward. Management's own adjusted EBITDA excludes the termination revenue. Rather than pin a hand-built ex-windfall base (not permitted from web figures), the final run keeps the filing-derived TTM base and scales every scenario's OE multiplier down by 0.20 versus the defaults (0.60/0.70/**0.80**/0.90/1.00 instead of 0.75/0.90/1.00/1.10/1.25), so the base case runs off an effective level centred on the FY2025 print of $26.71/share instead of the windfall-flattered $33.70. The super-bull multiplier of 1.00 says: even the bull case only gets the raw TTM level, no more.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: USPI's scale as the largest ASC platform is a real local-market and payer-contracting advantage, and hospitals are licensed, regulated local assets — but pricing is set in negotiation with government and commercial payers, not by Tenet, the hospital side is labor- and capex-heavy, and the ASC model is contestable by Optum/SCA, HCA and physician-led de novos.
- FOR: USPI is the largest ASC operator by a wide margin (538 facilities vs SCA Health's 320), with 11,000+ affiliated physicians — referral and case-flow gravity competitors can't quickly replicate; health-system joint-venture partnerships (e.g. Baylor Scott & White, Memorial Hermann) that bring clinical credibility and a built-in acquisition pipeline; scale in payer contracting, supply chain and scheduling across both hospitals and ASCs; hospitals are licensed local assets with high-acuity service lines (orthopedics, cardiology, robotics) that are hard to enter against; Conifer's revenue-cycle scale, now wholly owned again after the CommonSpirit buyback.
- AGAINST: no true pricing power — Medicare/Medicaid rates are administered and commercial rates are negotiated against large payers; payer-mix/policy hostage: exchange and Medicaid enrollment shifts already hurt Q2 2026 (lower exchange admissions, unfavorable mix) and uncompensated-care risk rises if coverage falls; labor is the largest cost line and the post-pandemic normalization can reverse; HCA is a larger, stronger hospital operator and Optum/SCA competes for the same ASC deals; ASCs can be built de novo by physicians and rivals — the moat is density, not exclusivity; levered balance sheet (see above) limits flexibility in a downturn.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *range* across defensible methods: this same run's preliminary pass on the engine's median-of-3-FY base ($14.86/share) with default scenarios gave weighted IRR 11.04% and weighted IV@15% $168.66 — Tier C. The final TTM-based, windfall-normalized run gives 15.16% / $257.38 — a marginal Tier B. Method range: **C → B**. Anyone quoting one number for THC without its method is hiding the ball.
- Quality is decent and improving: the portfolio mix shift to USPI (39%-margin ambulatory EBITDA per Q2 segment reporting, via search results) is structural, hospital margins are genuinely expanding (salaries/wages fell as a share of revenue in Q2 2026), and cash conversion funds both >$300M/yr of ASC M&A and heavy buybacks. Against that: the growth is substantially bought (M&A and buybacks) rather than organic volume — Q2 USPI same-facility cases actually fell 1.2% — and the payer backdrop (exchanges, Medicaid) is a live political variable.
- The Conifer termination is two-sided: ~$1.9B of installment consideration is real future cash, but a marquee revenue-cycle client is gone and the recognition is front-loaded relative to the lost recurring revenue. The 0.80 base multiplier is an estimate of that normalization, not a filing-measured figure — treat the B as soft.
- Balance sheet: the engine could not read debt tags (net_debt_latest null), so no net-debt adjustment is embedded in these numbers beyond what OCF already carries (interest is an operating outflow for a hospital operator's OCF presentation here in the sense that OCF is after interest paid). The September 2026 refinancing pushes maturities to 2034 at 6.250% — no near-term wall, but leverage is a standing reason not to size this like a clean B.
- Margin of safety: the weighted IV@15% of $257.38 is only 1.3% above the $253.97 price — i.e. $257.38 is effectively the price that clears exactly 15% on the weighted case, with no cushion. The bear-case IV@15% ($187.25) sits well below the price. A buyer at $253.97 is paid only if the normalized-TTM method is right; on the median method they are overpaying. Any real margin of safety requires a price below the weighted IV, and sized to the method spread.
