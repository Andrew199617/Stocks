## What it is
Parent of Oklahoma Gas and Electric (OG&E), a regulated electric utility serving ~913,305 customers in Oklahoma and western Arkansas (FY2025 10-K, via search of the filing). Pure-play regulated utility since the December 2021 exit from Enable Midstream (Energy Transfer acquired Enable). Earnings are rate-base recovery: revenue is set in Oklahoma Corporation Commission / Arkansas PSC / FERC proceedings, fuel is largely pass-through, and growth is a $7.3B capital plan for 2026–2030 guided to 5–7% EPS growth through 2030 off a 2026 consolidated midpoint of $2.43/share. Latest prints: Q2 2026 diluted EPS $0.56 vs $0.53 in Q2 2025 on revenue of $711.9M, down 4.0% year over year (company Q2 2026 release, via search); Q1 2026 EPS was $0.24 vs $0.31 a year earlier on milder weather.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q1 2026, legs quoted above. Note the data limit: fundamentals.json warns the latest 10-Q (period 2026-06-30, filed 2026-07-29) is not yet in EDGAR companyfacts, so TTM flows stop at 2026-03-31; the Q2 2026 release itself (EPS $0.56 vs $0.53) shows no break in the regulated trajectory.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 years): the break is FY2021 — revenue $3,588,700,000 in FY2021 vs $2,069,800,000 in FY2020 (financials_history.csv), the Winter Storm Uri year, when fuel and purchased-power costs spiked and were passed through revenue, in a period that still carried the legacy Enable Midstream investment exited in December 2021; revenue then normalized to $2,607,300,000 by FY2023 as fuel prices fell. No CAGR straddling 2021 is trusted: scenario starts were set from the 5–7% EPS guidance and recent prints, not from the 9.04% 5-yr revenue CAGR or the 28.91% OE/share CAGR.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income vs OCF differ by >40% in 2023, 2025): structural, not a one-off. FY2023 net income $416,800,000 vs OCF $1,232,300,000 and FY2025 net income $470,700,000 vs OCF $1,137,100,000 (financials_history.csv) — the gap is dominated by the non-cash D&A add-back ($506,600,000 in FY2023, $559,800,000 in FY2025) at a capital-intensive utility, with the remainder in working-capital and other non-cash items. The OCF-based conservative OE series already charges capex and SBC in full, so the divergence does not inflate the base; if anything the conservative definition is the harsh one for this sector (see SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH).
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Utilities): accepted — confidence is low and model_fit is misfit in score.json. The OE/capex DCF charges all growth capex (~2x D&A) against current owner's earnings, while a regulated utility recovers that capex in future rate base with an allowed return; the screen therefore understates regulated earnings power (GAAP EPS was $2.32 in FY2025 vs conservative OE of $0.34/share). The tier is reported as the model prints it, sized to the conservative end, and not overridden — even the maintenance-OE definition and the TTM base leave the name far below a 15% IRR at $46.81.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $0.34 vs 3-yr median $0.20): both annual figures are distorted by the capex cycle, in the same direction. The median is the FY2023 value ($0.20/share) because FY2024 was negative (OCF $812,800,000 < capex $1,090,900,000) and FY2025 was only $0.34 — a median of a heavy-capex window, not a normalized earning power. The TTM base ($1.01/share) is used instead, with its own caveat stated plainly: it is flattered by the Q1 leg swap (prior-YTD OCF of only $15,900,000 in Q1 2025 replaced by $175,500,000 in Q1 2026, ttm.json). That flattery does not drive the verdict — the weighted IRR is 5.29% even on the TTM base, and the preliminary median-base run also printed Tier D.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 27.6x normalized OE): the ratio uses the depressed normalized OE denominator ($0.20/share); on the balance sheet itself, net debt is $5,661,000,000 against equity of $4,977,300,000 and PP&E net of $12,730,400,000 (FY2025, financials_history.csv) — normal leverage for a regulated utility, serviced by rate recovery, not a distress signal. It still caps the equity's claim: interest and the debt-funded share of the $7.3B plan are paid before owner's earnings, which is why the bear cases (IV@15% $7.32, IRR 4.44%) are the sizing anchor.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, and here it does not change the answer: median base $0.20/share, latest FY $0.34/share, TTM $1.01/share and even the maintenance definition all leave the weighted IRR in single digits at $46.81. The stock is priced as a regulated compounder on GAAP EPS ($2.32 in FY2025, guided $2.43 midpoint for 2026), not on distributable owner's earnings after growth capex — on this screen those two valuations are ~5x apart (weighted IV@15% $9.98 vs $46.81).
- Quality is not the debate: this is a durable franchise with real load growth (+6.6% weather-normalized in 2025), a visible capital plan and a long dividend-growth record. The debate is price and definition — a buyer at $46.81 is paid the regulated ~5–7% EPS growth plus the dividend (DPS TTM $1.69625 in fundamentals.json), not a 15% owner's-earnings return.
- Risks: Oklahoma rate-case outcome (a rate review filing was planned for mid-2026, with new rates expected in 2027), large-load contract finalization and cost-allocation politics, weather, fuel-price pass-through timing, and equity dilution to fund the plan (forward equity for ~4.6M shares).
