## Moat: 5/10 — moderate, narrow
Justification: scarce premier gateway locations, the largest premier-workplace platform in the US and a proven development engine are real advantages, and the current leasing cycle shows tenants paying up for the best buildings. But the filed evidence caps the score: gross margin has fallen four straight years, measurable ROIC was only high-single-digit, GAAP ROE ran 0.3–5.4% in FY2023–25, and the model is capital-hungry and heavily levered. That is not the sustained well-above-cost-of-capital record a 7+ requires.
- FOR: Irreplaceable premier-workplace locations in six gateway markets — scarcity, zoning and replacement cost keep competing new supply slow; scale and integration (largest US premier-workplace owner/developer/manager; 290 Binney, 570,000 sq ft fully leased to AstraZeneca, delivered ahead of schedule and under budget); leasing momentum and visibility (~1.8M sq ft leased in Q2 2026, 29% above the 10-year quarterly average; occupancy up three straight quarters to 88.4%; leased rate 91.3% with most commencements due before year-end 2026); pricing power in the tightest submarkets (asking rents rising sharply at trophy assets such as 510 Madison and 680 Folsom, with AI-driven demand building).
- AGAINST: Hybrid work is a secular demand headwind — occupancy was still only 88.4% in Q2 2026, six years after the pandemic; gross margin eroded from 62.8% (FY2021) to 59.1% (FY2025 and TTM, fundamentals.json) — no demonstrated pricing power at portfolio level; returns are thin (roic_proxy measurable only in FY2016–17 at 8.7% / 8.5% because the filer's operating-income tag stops after FY2017; GAAP ROE 3.2% / 0.3% / 5.4% in FY2023–25); capital intensity ($953,249,000 TTM capex; management expects 2026 leasing capital alone nearer $500M); leverage ($15,633,220,000 of debt on the FY2025 10-K balance sheet against a $9,391,540,935 market cap — refinancing at higher rates is a standing drag, with interest expense up from $579,572,000 in FY2023 to $653,138,000 in FY2025, fundamentals.json).
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the base choice, and here it does not matter: preliminary run on the normalized median base ($2.22/sh) printed weighted IRR 6.72% and IV@15% $19.59; the final TTM-base run prints 5.76% and $15.10. Both are Tier D by a wide margin — the method range is D to D.
- What the OE lens is really saying: at $59.115 the market is paying for the buildings (asset value, FFO multiple, occupancy recovery), not for the residual cash flow after the capex those buildings continuously demand. FY2023–25 capex of $697M → $900M against flat OCF is the whole story; if deliveries (290 Binney done; 343 Madison under construction) let capex fall while the 91.3% leased rate converts to occupancy, OE/share can rebound fast — that is the bull case, and it still only earns 6.83%.
- Balance sheet is the risk the OE paths understate: $15,633,220,000 of debt, a dividend of $643,107,000 paid in FY2025 against $300,386,000 of conservative OE, and an asset-sale program ($1.2B+ of proceeds since the 2025 investor day, per Q2 2026 reporting) explicitly aimed at deleveraging. Management also cut the quarterly dividend to $0.70 ($2.80/yr, from $3.92/yr) — capital discipline, but an admission that the prior payout outran cash generation.
- Quality is not the debate: premier assets, occupancy inflecting up three straight quarters, AI-driven tenant demand in exactly BXP's markets. Price versus owner's earnings is the debate, and it is not close.
