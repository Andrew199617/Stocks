## What it is
Allentown-based regulated utility holding company: PPL Electric Utilities in Pennsylvania (electric distribution and transmission), LG&E and KU in Kentucky (integrated electric and gas, including coal and non-coal generation), and Rhode Island Energy (electric and gas, acquired from National Grid). Essentially all earnings are regulated. Management's plan is rate-base compounding: a $23 billion infrastructure investment plan for 2026–2029 guided to ~10.3% average annual rate-base growth, EPS growth targeted at 6–8% through at least 2029 with compound growth expected near the top end of that range, and dividend growth targeted at 4–6% through at least 2029 (search results — company Q2 2026 release and 2025 business-plan update). Data-center / large-load demand in Pennsylvania and Kentucky is the upside pipeline management cites, with potential generation investment upside it estimates at $10–12 billion through 2032, not in the base plan.

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Exclusive regulated electric and natural-gas franchises in Pennsylvania, Kentucky and Rhode Island, with essential inelastic demand, FERC-regulated transmission, and a $23B capital plan guided to ~10.3% rate-base growth through 2029 — a durable, visible compounding machine. Capped at 6 because regulators (Pennsylvania PUC, Kentucky PSC, Rhode Island PUC, FERC) set the allowed return so there is no pricing power beyond rate cases, the plan is funded with debt (net debt $17,823M) plus about $3B of planned equity issuance that dilutes per-share compounding, Kentucky retains coal-fired generation with transition/recovery risk, and affordability politics rise as data-center load costs are recovered from existing customers.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated electric and gas franchises in Pennsylvania, Kentucky and Rhode Island — captive, essential-service customers; $23B regulated infrastructure plan 2026–2029 guided to ~10.3% average annual rate-base growth, with over 60% of the plan eligible for contemporaneous recovery (search results); FERC-regulated transmission earns formula-rate returns and Pennsylvania/Kentucky large-load (data-center) demand adds a visible growth pipeline; constructive recent regulatory outcomes — a Pennsylvania settlement providing a $275M annual revenue increase effective July 1, 2026 and a $233M Kentucky revenue increase (search results).
- AGAINST: Regulators cap returns — the PUC/PSC/FERC set allowed ROE and recovery; no pricing power beyond rate cases; growth is debt- plus equity-funded: net debt $17,823M and about $3B of equity needs projected for 2026–2029 dilute rate-base growth per share; Kentucky coal-fired generation carries retirement / environmental recovery risk; affordability and large-load cost-allocation pushback as data-center spending is recovered in rates, plus refinancing the debt stack at higher rates.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade (key_numbers.md records CAP as None on this F run).
