# PSO — Pearson plc
Engine 5.5 run, 2026-10-19. In scope (market cap $12.011B at $18.19, nasdaq_quote_api). All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed.

## What it is
The world's largest education company (London-listed, NYSE ADSs 1:1 with ordinary shares): assessments and qualifications, virtual schools, higher-education courseware, English language learning and enterprise skills. FY2025 sales £3,577m by segment (Annual Report 2025): Assessment & Qualifications £1,604m, Higher Education £775m, Virtual Learning £511m, English Language Learning £405m, Enterprise Learning & Skills £282m; ~67% of sales in the US. Underlying sales growth +4% in FY2025 and +4% in H1 2026, adjusted operating margin 17.2% in FY2025.

## OVERRIDE — capex (filing, bounded authority step 8a)
- **The gap:** the preliminary run exited with `DATA_GAP:capex` — no tier. EDGAR companyfacts carries `PurchaseOfPropertyPlantAndEquipmentClassifiedAsInvestingActivities` only through FY2021; from FY2022 Pearson files PPE purchases inside the combined line "Purchase of property, plant and equipment and investment property", which no engine candidate tag reads. Intangible purchases stay tagged every year (£90m FY2022, £96m FY2023, £91m FY2024, £105m FY2025) but are not added on an assumed-zero row.
- **The decision:** judgment.json carries `capex_override = {value: 134000000, kind: "filing", currency basis: declared in GBP, engine-converted}` — the filer's TOTAL FY2025 capex from the FY2025 Form 20-F (filed 2026-03-13) consolidated cash flow statement: PP&E and investment property £29m + intangible assets £105m = £134m. Because the override is the total line, the filed intangible purchases are correctly not stacked on top (OVERRIDE_APPLIED:capex names them). Per the system's single-value convention the latest filed year fills every missing period: exact for FY2025, and it overstates FY2024 (true £124m = £33m + £91m) by ~8% and FY2023 (true £126m = £30m + £96m) by ~6% — conservative in direction. Band check (override_checks): £134m is 2.09× the last value filed under the PPE-only tag (£64m, FY2021) and 0.76× the last filed total capex (£176m, FY2021) — in band either way; the engine records out_of_band: false.
- **Measured impact (dcf_numbers.json override_impact, final scenario inputs):** zero-fill base $1.07/share → **$0.82/share** with the override; zero-fill weighted IRR 10.07% → **8.39%**. Without the override the run's OE was overstated by ~30% on a capex line that is only ~4% of sales — the override is the whole difference between the fake Tier C the zero-fill arithmetic implied and this Tier D.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Evidence first (fundamentals.json, GBP): roic_proxy FY2019–FY2025 ran 3.9% / 7.6% / 3.0% / 4.0% / 7.9% / 9.3% / 7.7% — improving, but never durably above ~10%, i.e. around the cost of capital, not well above it through a cycle. Gross margin 47–52% over the same years, 52.0% in FY2025. A moat above 6 is not supported by those returns.
- FOR: regulated assessment franchise — the Pearson VUE global test-centre network and government/professional certification contracts are hard to replicate; accredited qualifications with institutional lock-in — UK GCSE/A-levels, BTEC and the PTE English test are embedded in school, university and immigration systems; content and assessment IP plus data scale across five segments; proven pricing — underlying sales +4% in FY2025 and H1 2026 with the adjusted operating margin expanding to 17.2%.
- AGAINST: ROIC history is mediocre (above); Higher Education courseware is in structural decline — print erosion, Inclusive Access price pressure, AI tools substituting for paid courseware and study help; contract re-bid risk — US Student Assessment lost the New Jersey contract and government contracts re-tender; English Language Learning is exposed to migration policy (H1 2026 ELL guidance was cut on softer migration-driven demand) and consumer apps pressure the category.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the price, not the business. Pearson is a decent, improving franchise — sales growing mid-single digits underlying, margins expanding, £527m of FY2025 free cash flow, disciplined buybacks — priced at $18.19 for an owner's-earnings stream whose normalized base is $0.82/share, a 4.5% OE yield with 2.85× net debt on top. At that yield the market is already paying for the bull case.
- Method range: the zero-fill preliminary arithmetic (capex silently $0) implied a $1.07 base and a ~10.1% weighted IRR on these same scenario inputs (Tier C) — every step of that gap is the missing capex line, now filed, cited and measured. There is no defensible method on the current filings that gets this name to a 15% hurdle at $18.19; even the super-bull scenario tops out at a 13.89% IRR.
- What would change the answer: a price near the weighted IV@15% ($8.27), or evidence that the enterprise/AI assessment opportunity lifts underlying growth durably above the mid-single-digit guide. The H1 2026 print (+4% sales, +14% adjusted operating profit) is progress, not that evidence yet.
- Watch items: FY2026 tax normalization (FY2025's £2m cash tax will not repeat — the base already uses the pre-recovery median year), US Student Assessment contract renewals after the New Jersey loss, ELL demand under tighter migration policy, and whether Higher Education stabilizes at its FY2025 +2% underlying growth.
