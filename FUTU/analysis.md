# FUTU — Futu Holdings Ltd
Engine 5.4.0 run, 2026-10-16 (first pass). Price $105.64 (nasdaq_quote_api). Cayman-incorporated, Hong Kong-based online broker (Futubull in Hong Kong, moomoo internationally), SIC 6211; files Form 20-F, reports in HKD with its own USD convenience translations.

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, or fundamentals.json — none are recomputed. Interim and regulatory figures attributed to the company's 6-K earnings releases are research citations, marked as such.

**ADS basis (read first).** Each Futu ADS represents **8 Class A ordinary shares** — stated in the company's own Form 20-F (FY2024, filed 2025-04-14), Notes to Consolidated Financial Statements, Note 1, and in the FY2025 20-F (filed 2026-04-15). EDGAR's diluted share count (1,130,846,701 latest) is in *ordinary* shares, so judgment.json carries a filing-sourced `ads_ratio` of 8.0 and the engine scaled every per-share figure ×8: all OE/share and IV figures below are **per ADS**, directly comparable to the $105.64 price. One artifact to know: the engine's `market_cap_est` ($119.463B) multiplies the per-ADS price by the ordinary-share count and so overstates the cap 8×; the universe build that admitted FUTU to this sweep carried a market cap of $15.938B (universe.csv), under the $40B cutoff. `in_scope_under_40b` is set to false in judgment.json only because make_score.py computes scope from the engine's overstated figure — economically FUTU entered the sweep in scope.

## What it is
Tech-driven online brokerage and wealth platform: securities/derivatives brokerage, margin financing and securities lending, fund distribution, FX and IPO financing, plus an in-app investor community. Q2 2026 (6-K release, research): 3.84M funded accounts (+33.6% YoY), 31.3M registered users, client assets HK$1.40T (+43.6% YoY), record quarterly trading volume HK$6.42T (+78.8% YoY). Revenue mix is commissions plus a large interest-income line (margin lending and client cash), which is why the engine's contract-revenue tag captures only part of total revenue — fundamentals.json, on the filer's own total-revenue basis, shows FY2025 total revenue of $2,935,374,000 vs the engine history's contract-revenue line of $1,358,388,000.

## Owner's earnings — the engine's definitions are both unusable, and why
**FY2025 (20-F via EDGAR, USD translations, from financials_history.csv):** operating cash flow $5,240,468,000; net income $1,456,671,000; SBC $44,072,000; capex $7,033,000; D&A $9,291,000.
- **Conservative owner's earnings FY2025 (OCF − capex − SBC): $5,189,363,000 = $36.71/share per ADS** (key_numbers.md: latest FY OE/share $36.71; normalized 3-yr median $28.03). This is client-balance plumbing, not distributable earnings: for a broker-dealer, OCF runs through cash held on behalf of clients, amounts payable to clients and margin receivables. It swung from −$811,352,000 in FY2023 (against net income of $548,140,000) to $3,990,410,000 in FY2024 (against $700,734,000) to $5,240,468,000 in FY2025 (against $1,456,671,000) with no relation to earning power.
- **Maintenance version FY2025: −$66,315,000** (financials_history.csv) — also meaningless here: its ΔNWC term is the same client-balance flow, and it turns a year of $1,456,671,000 net income negative. Both engine definitions run through the broker's balance sheet; neither is used.
- **Base used = FY2025 net income per ADS, declared as a `base_override` in judgment.json (bounded authority (c) — built exclusively from this run's financials_history.csv):** net income $1,456,671,000 ÷ diluted shares 1,130,846,701 = $1.2881 per ordinary share, ×8 (ads_ratio) = **$10.30 per ADS** (key_numbers.md: base OE/share $10.30). For a broker, net income is the honest owner's-earnings proxy (the IBKR precedent in this sweep): SBC is already expensed inside it, and capex ($7,033,000) and D&A ($9,291,000) are trivial and roughly offset. The real reinvestment — regulatory capital against a growing client balance sheet — is handled through the scenario multipliers and low confidence, not a capex line. Noncontrolling interest is immaterial for FUTU, so net income ≈ attributable earnings (unlike IBKR's Up-C).
- Why latest FY, not the 3-yr median NI per ADS ($5.00 — the FY2024 figure, from the same history: FY2023 $3.92, FY2024 $5.00, FY2025 $10.30): the median averages in the pre-inflection client base. Client assets and funded accounts have since grown by roughly two-thirds and one-third respectively in a single year on the company's reported figures; the FY2024 earnings level no longer describes the platform's earning power. The latest FY is also *not* the flattering end of the range: the company's 6-K releases report Q1 2026 net income of approximately HK$2,922.0M before the CSRC penalty accrual (see Flags) and Q2 2026 net income up 41.6% YoY with diluted net income per ADS of US$3.33 in that quarter alone — an ex-penalty interim run-rate above the FY2025 base. No TTM pin was fabricated from those releases: ttm.py was run and exits non-zero for this filer (it cannot compute a TTM from the 20-F filer's companyfacts path), and no ttm.json exists.
- OE positive 4 of the last 5 fiscal years on the engine's OCF definition (the negative year is FY2023's plumbing swing, not an earnings loss — net income was positive every year FY2021–FY2025).
- Balance sheet (FY2025, financials_history.csv): total assets $29,349,617,000 against total liabilities $24,169,154,000 and equity of $5,139,358,000; the company's own cash is $1,344,659,000 and lease liabilities are $76,310,000. The balance sheet is mostly client-related balances (client cash held, payables to clients, margin receivables) — see DEBT_TAGS_BLANK below. Capital return (research, 6-K releases): an up-to-US$800M ADS repurchase program authorized November 2025, with roughly US$418M repurchased by late May 2026; the engine's share count CAGR is −0.59%/yr (key_numbers.md).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate, a franchise moat with an impaired home channel
Justification: Hong Kong's leading retail broker with a proprietary tech platform, 82–87% gross margins and ROE rising to 28.3% in FY2025, scaling internationally — but retail switching costs are low, the historic mainland-China growth channel has been closed and penalized by the CSRC, and earnings ride trading volumes and rates.
- FOR: dominant Hong Kong retail brokerage franchise (Futubull) plus a scaling moomoo network (Singapore, Malaysia, US, Australia, Japan, Canada; Thailand licence secured) — 3.84M funded accounts and HK$1.40T client assets at Q2 2026 (research, above); tech-platform cost structure — gross margin 84.7% / 82.0% / 87.1% over FY2023–2025 and FY2025 operating margin 61.6% (fundamentals.json, filer's total-revenue basis); diversifying revenue (commissions, margin/securities-lending interest, FX and IPO financing, fund distribution, US prediction markets, Hong Kong virtual-asset margin financing); 31.3M registered users and an in-app community that lowers organic acquisition cost at home.
- AGAINST: retail switching costs are low — Webull, Tiger/UP Fintech, Robinhood and local brokers/banks contest every market; the China regulatory franchise is impaired, not protected (see the CSRC flag below); earnings are geared to trading volumes and policy rates; founder dual-class control (Leaf Hua Li), VIE structure, and a US securities class action following the CSRC disclosure; international growth is bought with rising customer-acquisition cost (~HK$2,600 blended in Q2 2026, within the company's HK$2.5k–3.0k guidance, per the Q2 call summary).
- Returns evidence (fundamentals.json): ROE 17.4% (FY2023) → 19.4% (FY2024) → 28.3% (FY2025); the `roic_proxy` series runs 21.7% / 34.4% / 39.5% over the same years and gross margin held 82.0–87.1%. Two caveats keep this at 5, not 7+: the roic_proxy denominator (equity + tagged debt − cash) excludes the client balances that fund this balance sheet, so it overstates capital intensity-adjusted returns for a broker; and the FY2025 returns were earned in a volume boom (total trading volume +89.4% in 2025, per the FY2025 release). A moat of 7+ needs returns proven through a full volume/rate cycle on a clean denominator — FUTU has the margin record, not yet the through-cycle proof.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (the controlling flag):** Finance sector, SIC 6211 broker-dealer. Resolved the IBKR way: both engine OE definitions were discarded as balance-sheet plumbing (see Owner's earnings), the base was rebuilt on net income and declared as a base_override, and confidence is graded **low**. score.json records model_fit "misfit" and the Tier A alert is suppressed for exactly this reason (alert_eligible: false, alert_suppressed_reason: "sector model misfit") — the tier is a misfit-model number and is reported as such. Cross-check in the model's own blind spot: the franchise earned a 28.3% ROE in FY2025 (fundamentals.json), so an ROE-vs-cost-of-equity framing points the same direction as the corrected DCF, but neither framing prices the regulatory tail.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2023, 2024, 2025):** Not a one-off tax deposit or impairment — it is structural for a broker-dealer, in all three years, and it is the reason the OCF base was removed rather than adjusted (figures under Owner's earnings). The one genuine earnings one-off in this story sits *after* the base year, in the NI series itself: on May 22, 2026 the CSRC issued an Administrative Penalty Pre-Notification Letter proposing confiscation of approximately RMB470M plus fines of approximately RMB1.38B (approximately RMB1.85B in aggregate) against Futu entities for unlicensed mainland securities/fund/futures business, plus a personal fine on the founder; the company reflected the amount in its Q1 2026 statements (Q1 net income approximately HK$2,922.0M before the accrual, per the Q1 2026 6-K release), the ADS fell roughly 27.5% on the disclosure, US class actions followed, and the proposal remains subject to further proceedings. This is handled in the scenario design (45% bear-or-worse weight, super-bear OE ×0.75) and in the moat, not by adjusting the FY2025 base, which predates it.
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py was run and exits non-zero for this 20-F filer — no filing-direct TTM exists and no ttm.json was produced, so no TTM pin was used. The base is the freshest filing-direct annual figure (FY2025 net income). Directional corroboration from the 6-K interim releases (research): ex-penalty Q1 2026 net income of approximately HK$2,922.0M and Q2 2026 net income +41.6% YoY both run above the FY2025 quarterly average, so the stale base is not flattering the result on current evidence.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (a >60% revenue jump in the last 5 years): the jump is FY2025's +74.56% on the engine's brokerage-commission line. It is not a divestiture, acquisition or restatement — it is a trading-volume break (total trading volume +89.4% in 2025 and client assets +65.9%, per the FY2025 release) on a growing client base. Consequence taken: no scenario start was allowed to straddle it — growth was recalibrated from earnings prints with the volume-boom reason written (see DCF), not taken from the 39–40% revenue CAGRs the break inflates.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range >3x over 5 years): on the engine definition this is mostly the OCF plumbing swing (FY2023 negative, FY2025 $36.71/share). On the corrected NI basis the series is $2.36 / $2.61 / $3.92 / $5.00 / $10.30 per ADS (FY2021–FY2025, history basis) — still a >3x range, and it is real cyclicality plus a genuine inflection, not an artifact. - **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED** (x8.0): each ADS represents eight Class A ordinary shares (filer's own 20-F, Note 1 — see the ADS basis note at the top). All per-share figures are per ADS. The one unscaled artifact is market_cap_est ($119.463B), addressed at the top.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *method range*, not the point estimate. On the same calibrated scenarios: median-NI base → Tier C (12.03%, IV@15% $77.25); latest-FY-NI base → Tier A (20.20%, $159.12); the engine's OCF base → a Tier S artifact (61.88%, $904.35) that is rejected outright. The scored A sits on the most current filing-direct earnings definition, with every haircut (45% bear weight, growth starts at half the recent prints or less, ×0.75 super-bear OE) applied in the scenarios rather than in a second, unmeasured tier adjustment — so no tier_override is taken. But a reader who believes FY2025 was peak-cycle earnings should read this as a C, and the analysis gives them the exact run that says so.
- Quality is not the debate: 87.1% gross margin, 61.6% operating margin, 28.3% ROE, an US$800M buyback underway, and client assets compounding at +43.6% YoY into Q2 2026. The debate is (a) how much of FY2025's earnings level survives volume normalization, and (b) the China regulatory tail — the CSRC proposal (approximately RMB1.85B) is absorbable financially; the structural question is the mainland-linked client base (~13% of Futu Securities' funded accounts, per the company's disclosure reporting) and whether rectification stops at the penalty.
- What would change the answer: a final CSRC determination materially beyond the pre-notification, evidence that mainland-linked outflows are larger or still accelerating (management said mid-single-digit percent of client assets, mostly absorbed in Q2), or a volume drawdown that reprices the ADS toward the bear IV@15% of $95.54 and below.
- Margin of safety on the scored numbers: the weighted IV@15% sits 50.6% above the price — equivalently the price is roughly a third below the weighted IV@15%. That is a margin against the weighted case only; against the bear case there is none.
