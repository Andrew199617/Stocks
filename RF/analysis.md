## What it is
Large US regional bank holding company headquartered in Birmingham, Alabama: retail and commercial banking, mortgage, wealth management, treasury management and capital markets across a 15-state footprint in the Southeast, Midwest and Texas. Earnings are overwhelmingly net interest income (loans funded by deposits) plus fees — wealth management was a record contributor in Q2 2026, and the bank is adding specialized groups (a Materials & Infrastructure group launched October 2026) and planning 130–150 new branches over 3–4 years.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, a deposit franchise not a pricing-power moat
Justification: Regions has a durable, sticky low-cost deposit base in its footprint and real commercial-relationship switching costs, and it converts that into top-quartile returns on tangible equity — but banking is a commodity business priced off the rate curve, with no proprietary product and returns capped by regulatory capital.
- FOR: Sticky low-cost deposit franchise — interest-bearing deposit costs of 1.69% in Q2 2026, described in the earnings release as peer-leading, with noninterest-bearing consumer/small-business deposit growth outpacing peers; scale across 15 Southeast/Midwest/Texas states with long-standing local relationships; treasury-management and commercial relationships carry real switching friction (record treasury management year in FY2025, record wealth revenue Q2 2026); best-in-class hedging program supporting a top-quartile NIM of 3.66% in Q2 2026 and a mostly neutral short-term rate position; reported Q2 2026 ROATCE of 19% (20% adjusted).
- AGAINST: No proprietary product — loans and deposits are commodities, pricing power is the yield curve, not the franchise; money-center banks and nonbanks compete for the same deposits and loans in its footprint; geographic concentration in the Southeast/Texas means a regional downturn hits the whole book; earnings are hostage to the credit cycle — Q2 2026 net charge-offs were a benign 42 bps annualized and criticized loans fell, which is the good part of the cycle, not the through-cycle rate; regulatory capital requirements cap leverage, payouts and therefore achievable ROE growth; multi-year record of muted net interest income growth versus peers (flagged in analyst coverage) — the franchise defends share more than it takes it.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py was run and exited 3 with "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for RF (CIK 1281761): sbc: no post-FY YTD fact (no 10-Q since the 2025-12-31 fiscal year end)" — so no filing-direct TTM base exists from ttm.py and the engine's median-of-3-FY base was used, per the standing rule. The interim evidence that does exist points the same way as the median base, not above it: Q1 2026 net income $559M with diluted EPS $0.62, Q2 2026 net income $570M ($549M available to common) with diluted EPS $0.64, +8.5% YoY — a run-rate consistent with the FY2025 base, helped by buybacks. Q3 2026 results are scheduled for October 16, 2026, the day after this run.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance): real and binding on confidence. Owner's earnings as OCF − capex − SBC fits a bank poorly: capex ($155M of branch/IT spend) is not the bank's real reinvestment cost — loan growth is, and credit losses run through provisions inside net income/OCF rather than appearing as a clean line. Bank OCF also swings with balance-sheet flows (see the FY2022–25 OE range above), so both OE definitions are noisier here than for an industrial. Confidence graded **low**, tier treated as soft, and the verdict below leans on the earnings/capital facts as a cross-check rather than on the DCF alone.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (preliminary run only, 4.43x): an artifact of the unusable 14.63% default growth blend — the super-bull IV was inflated by a growth start the company has never printed. The recalibrated final run's spread is 2.42x and the flag no longer fires. Confidence is still low, on the sector flag, not the spread.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range is the story: on the engine's untouched defaults (14.63% growth start) RF prints Tier B at a 16.73% weighted IRR — a number built on a revenue tag that excludes the bank's main revenue line. On growth rates the company actually prints and guides to, it is Tier C at 11.20%. The defensible range is C to (an indefensible) B; the verdict takes the C.
- Cross-check outside the DCF: at $27.205 the stock trades around 10–11x trailing earnings with a dividend the bank raised 13% for Q2 2026 ($0.30/share quarterly per the Q2 release) and a $3B buyback authorization running through 2027 — that is a reasonable income-and-buyback package, but it is a ~11% expected-return package on this model, not a 15% one. Even the super-bull case only reaches a 15.41% IRR.
- Credit is currently a tailwind, not a risk: net charge-offs fell to 42 bps annualized in Q2 2026, criticized business-services loans and NPLs declined, and the allowance covered NPLs 241%. The bear cases are about that reversing, plus NIM compression if deposit repricing stalls.
- **Price that clears 15%:** the weighted IV@15% is $18.83 — the price would need to fall roughly to that level (about 31% below $27.205) before the weighted case clears Andrew's hurdle, and a margin of safety against the bear case ($15.14) argues for lower still. There is no margin of safety at the current price on these inputs.
