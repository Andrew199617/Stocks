v5 run, 2026-10-15. In scope (market cap $26.254B at $37.885, nasdaq_quote_api).

## What it is
The largest integrated-resort operator in Asia, with no US properties left: Marina Bay Sands (MBS) in Singapore — one half of a government-licensed duopoly — and, through majority-owned Sands China, five Macau properties (Venetian, Londoner, Parisian, Plaza/Four Seasons, Sands Macao) on Cotai and the Macau peninsula. Revenue is gaming-led (premium mass and rolling/VIP) with hotels, malls, and conventions attached. It sold its Las Vegas properties in February 2022 for $6.25B and has been redeploying into Macau renovations, the ~$8.0B MBS expansion, buybacks, and a rising stake in Sands China.

## Moat: 6/10 — strong assets, government-granted moat
Justification: MBS's duopoly position and Macau scale are real and hard to replicate, but the moat is a licence, not property — concessions are time-limited, repriced at renewal with mandated non-gaming spend, and the Macau customer has no switching cost, so premium-mass share is defended with reinvestment that is visibly compressing margins.
- FOR: Singapore duopoly — only two integrated resorts are licensed, and MBS dominates the weaker competitor, earning ~50%+ property EBITDA margins (Q4 2025 50.3%, Q1 2026 53.0%, Q2 2026 49.9%, per releases via search); largest Macau footprint — five Cotai/peninsula properties with the scale, malls, arena, and convention space to serve every segment; iconic, effectively irreplicable assets (MBS's location and the Venetian/Londoner cluster) built for sums no new entrant can casually match; demonstrated post-renovation pricing/volume power — the Londoner conversion drove outsized revenue growth in 2025–2026 (search).
- AGAINST: the moat is granted by two governments — Macau's 10-year concession (to 2032) already came with ~$4.44B of mandated, mostly non-gaming investment, and Singapore terms are likewise negotiated, so the state captures part of the economics at each renewal; zero customer switching costs in Macau — six concessionaires chase the same premium-mass customer with comps and reinvestment, and Macau EBITDA margin compressed (Q4 2025 margin down 390bp year over year; Q1 2026 Macau margin 29.9%, down year over year, per search); total geographic concentration — two jurisdictions, both ultimately exposed to Chinese consumer health, capital-flow controls, and Beijing/Singapore policy; hold luck — Q2 2026 Macau rolling hold of 1.35% cut quarterly EBITDA by ~$87M versus normalized hold (search), so reported earnings are partly a coin flip each quarter.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, not the B. On the prescribed TTM base the stock is a B (weighted IRR 17.68%, IV@15% $47.00). On the engine's normalized median base ($2.60/share) with identical scenarios, the same run produced a weighted IRR of 12.82% and IV@15% of $30.71 — Tier C. The entire difference is whether H1 2026's doubled operating cash flow is durable earnings power or timing. H1 property EBITDA rising only ~2.7% while the TTM OCF leg ($3,732M) sits well above the FY2025 leg ($3,023M) says a large part is timing until proven otherwise.
- Quality is not the debate: MBS is arguably the most profitable casino building in the world (~50% EBITDA margins at record levels in Q4 2025–Q1 2026), Macau scale is unmatched, SBC is trivial, and the buyback is large. The debate is price paid for a levered, two-jurisdiction, licence-based cash flow whose Macau half is in a reinvestment war — Macau margin fell even as volumes and share recovered in 2026, and management's own $700M quarterly Macau EBITDA target is still unmet.
- Forward capex is the quiet drag: the OE definition already deducts all capex, and the committed programs (~$8.0B MBS expansion plus ~$4.44B Macau plan) mean capex stays at or above the TTM $1,029M level for years before the expansion contributes anything (completion targeted for the end of the decade). The scenarios' OE multipliers, not heroic growth, carry that risk.
- Hurdle math: the price that clears 15% is $47.00 on the TTM method but only $30.71 on the median-base method. At $37.885 the buyer is paid if the TTM base is real and is overpaying on the conservative base — there is no margin of safety against the conservative method. A price at or below $30.71 would clear 15% on both methods.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: B** (weighted IRR 17.68% on the v5 final TTM inputs) — with a documented method range of C to B, which is itself the finding; size it like a C until TTM operating cash flow proves durable.
- Confidence: **Low** (WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD, a tier-spanning base-method range, leverage, and two government concessions as the entire asset base)
- One-liner: Premier Asia casino cash machine (Singapore duopoly + Macau scale), moat 6/10 — clears 15% only on the TTM base (17.7% weighted, IV $47.00 vs $37.89); on the median base it is a C (12.8%, IV $30.71), which is also the price that clears 15% with a margin of safety.
