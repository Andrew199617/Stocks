# BCE — BCE Inc.

## What it is
Canada's largest communications company: Bell CTS Canada (wireless, fibre Internet, TV, enterprise ICT), Bell CTS U.S. (Ziply Fiber, acquired August 1, 2025), and Bell Media (CTV, Crave). A regulated three-carrier oligopoly business with utility-like cash flows, flat revenue, and a heavy balance sheet.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, regulator-capped
Justification: regulated Canadian telecom oligopoly with spectrum and fibre/5G network scale and sticky subscribers, but the regulator caps the moat, pricing power is unproven, revenue has been flat for five years, and returns sit on a huge, repeatedly impaired, highly levered asset base.
- FOR: three-carrier oligopoly — spectrum licences and national fibre/5G networks are effectively irreplicable, and BCE is Canada's largest communications company by revenue and connections; sticky installed base (4.91M Bell CTS high-speed Internet subscribers at Q2 2026; postpaid wireless churn 1.0% in Q2 2026, the lowest comparable level in three years — Q2 2026 results release, cited search result); margin stability through a cycle — gross margin 67.9–68.9% across FY2021–25 (fundamentals.json annual) and FY2025 adjusted EBITDA margin 43.6%, the highest in over 30 years (FY2025 results release, cited search result); Bell Media / Crave (5.1M subscribers, +23% YoY at Q2 2026, cited search result) bundled with connectivity raises switching friction.
- AGAINST: the CRTC wholesale fibre-access decision forced Bell to open its fibre to rivals and BCE curtailed its own fibre build in response; no demonstrated pricing power (Bell CTS Canada revenue −4.0% YoY in Q2 2026, blended ARPU +0.7% only, cited search result) and legacy voice/linear TV decline structurally; value-destroying capital allocation history — impairments of C$2,190M (FY2024) and C$1,027M (FY2025) plus recurring severance programs; leverage drives policy (net debt $29,987,851,240 against equity of C$23,310M at Dec 31, 2025 — F-10 capitalization table, cited search result — and the dividend was cut from C$3.99 to C$1.75 annualized in May 2025); US growth is bought, not moated (Ziply competes in open US fibre markets).
- **ROIC evidence (required):** fundamentals.json carries **no roic_proxy series for BCE** — operating income is untagged under its IFRS tags (operating_income and roic_proxy are null for every annual year), so a filed ROIC history cannot be quoted. The moat therefore rests on the margin evidence above, not on a demonstrated high-ROIC history; a 5/10 (not 7+) is the honest score for an oligopoly whose returns are capped by regulation and diluted by a massive asset base.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py cannot resolve it for BCE — it exits non-zero for this IFRS filer (it reads us-gaap-style facts and refused, reporting no annual fact for ocf/sbc/shares under its tags). The median-of-3 base is used instead, and its median year is FY2025 itself, so the base is the freshest filed year, not a stale one. Post-FY prints show no break: Q2 2026 cash flows from operating activities C$2,162M vs C$1,947M in Q2 2025 (+11.0%) and revenue +1.5% (Q2 2026 results release, cited search result).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024): two different stories, neither touches the OCF-based base. **2024:** net income ($260,628,750 USD) was crushed by a C$2,190M impairment of assets (FY2025 annual report cash-flow reconciliation, cited search result; same figure under the EDGAR ImpairmentLoss tag) plus C$454M of severance, acquisition and other costs — non-cash charges added back in operating cash flow ($4,856,729,880). **2023:** the gap is the structural telecom D&A wedge — D&A of $3,711,516,240 against net income of $1,756,140,360 (financials_history.csv) — plus severance/acquisition costs; the 2023 impairment was small (C$143M). The mirror image sits in 2025: net income is *flattered* by C$5,217M of net gains on investments (the MLSE stake sale) while adjusted net earnings fell 6.2% (FY2025 results release, cited search result). Conclusion: NI is unusable for BCE in all three years; the OCF-based OE base is the clean series, which is why it is the base.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: CAD converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (FY2025: 0.73036; FY2024: 0.69501 — dcf_numbers.json), flow facts at period-end rather than average rates. Practical consequence, handled in the growth check above: USD-basis revenue "growth" overstates CAD reality when the loonie moves; the scenario starts were anchored to CAD prints.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: ratio 1.0, direct-listed common shares — see OVERRIDE. No scaling applied or needed.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc**: filing override, see OVERRIDE — gap, source, out-of-band acknowledgment, and measured impact (base $2.5886 → $2.5179; weighted IRR 14.61% → 14.27%) are all recorded in judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json (overrides_applied / override_impact) and the central registry.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 12.8x normalized OE): the balance sheet from the filing — total debt C$41,059M and total equity C$23,310M at December 31, 2025 (F-10 consolidated capitalization table, cited search result); engine net debt $29,987,851,240 USD on the long-term + current borrowings basis (lease liabilities are untagged and excluded, so the engine figure understates total obligations). Management cut the dividend from C$3.99 to C$1.75 annualized in May 2025 and targets 3.5x net debt leverage by end-2027 (cited search result). The bear cases are the stress: bear IRR 10.30% and super-bear 7.56% assume OE compression against this debt stack, and the verdict is sized to that end (TIER_BAND).
- **TIER_BAND** (bear tier C at 10.3% vs bull tier B at 17.5%): the verdict spans tiers and is sized to the bear end — the final tier is C, the weighted case itself does not clear 15%, and no position logic should be built on the bull leg.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range here is unusually narrow for this sweep: zero-fill vs filing override moves the weighted IRR by 0.34pp, the normalized base equals the latest-FY base, and the tier is C on the weighted and bear cases and B only on the bull. The honest headline is the level, not a range: a 12.36% OE yield on a flat, levered oligopoly, priced to return ~14.3% — just under Andrew's 15% hurdle.
- FY2025 contains only five months of Ziply Fiber (acquired August 1, 2025; Q3 2025 contribution $160M revenue / $71M adjusted EBITDA for two months, cited search result). FY2026 gets a mechanical consolidation tailwind that is not organic growth — the bull case partly prices that arithmetic, which is why it is capped at +4.34%.
- What the model does not pay for: the reset dividend (C$1.75 annualized; fundamentals.json shows FY2025 declared DPS of C$2.31 including pre-cut quarters and a last cut in FY2025) and the deleveraging path to the 3.5x target. Those are balance-sheet stories, not OE growth, and with net debt at 12.82x OE they are also the risk.
- Recurring "one-offs" are a pattern, not noise: impairments in 2024 (C$2,190M) and 2025 (C$1,027M) and annual severance programs mean reported NI will keep diverging from cash earnings; any future pass should stay on the OCF-based definition.
