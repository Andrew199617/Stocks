## What it is
Technology-led health insurer selling ACA individual and small-group plans (2,042,449 members at 2025-12-31; 2.96M at 2026-06-30, +46% YoY), plus ICHRA offerings and the +Oscar platform. ## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: administrative-cost edge and exchange scale, but no pricing power, no switching costs, a structural risk-adjustment tax on its member mix, and no through-cycle earnings record.
- FOR: proprietary tech/AI platform driving the SG&A ratio from 19.1% (FY2024) to 17.5% (FY2025), guided 15.6–16.1% FY2026, record-low 14.2% in Q2 2026; scale — 2.96M members, one of the largest pure-play ACA carriers, gaining share as larger rivals retreat; member brand/engagement plus ICHRA and +Oscar optionality.
- AGAINST: premiums are filed a year in advance and the ACA 80% minimum MLR caps underwriting margin by law — FY2025's mispricing could not be corrected in-year (MLR 87.4%); risk adjustment transfers ~18.5% of direct premiums away from its younger/healthier pool (FY2025, 10-K) and CMS true-ups arrive late; annual open enrollment means no switching costs; single-market policy exposure (enhanced-subsidy expiry, CMS program-integrity disenrollment of up to ~300k members flagged by management); much larger diversified rivals.
- Evidence check (WORKER_SPEC step 5): fundamentals.json `roic_proxy` is not meaningful for OSCR — its denominator (equity + debt − cash) is negative at a net-cash insurer, so the series swings from −1.519 to +1.259 on sign flips rather than economics. The usable history is operating margin: negative every year 2019–2023, +$57,265,000 on $9,177,564,000 in FY2024, −$396,357,000 on $11,701,427,000 in FY2025, +$629,723,000 TTM. A 3/10 sits on that record; nothing in it supports 7+.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection.
