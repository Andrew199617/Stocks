# IRM — Iron Mountain Incorporated
Run date: 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: market cap ~$34.3B < $40B.

## What it is
A specialised REIT built on physical records storage, now pivoting hard to digital infrastructure. Core Global RIM (records information management): storage rental and services (retrieval, shredding, scanning) for 240,000+ customers in 61 countries, ~748M cubic feet stored, retention ~93–94%, average box storage duration ~14.5 years. Growth businesses — data centres, Asset Lifecycle Management (IT asset decommissioning / remarketing, much of it hyperscaler kit), and Digital Solutions — were ~35% of revenue in Q2 2026 after growing >50% YoY, vs ~65% for legacy RIM. FY2025 revenue $6,902M (+12.2%); TTM revenue through Q2 2026 $7,562M (FY25 $6,902M − H1'25 $3,304M + H1'26 $3,965M). Q2 2026: revenue $2,029M (+18.5% YoY, +16.8% organic), adjusted EBITDA $727M, AFFO $433M / $1.44 per share (+17%). FY2026 guidance was raised in August: revenue $7,940–8,010M (~+16%), adjusted EBITDA $2,945–2,975M, AFFO $1,760–1,780M = $5.87–5.93/share. Data centres are the swing factor: revenue +39% in Q2, 110 MW leased year-to-date through July (13 MW in Q2, 75 MW in July alone), ~325 MW scheduled to energise over the next 24 months, backlog supporting ~$370M of revenue growth beyond 2026.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong, two different moats averaged
FOR — (1) Records storage is a genuine switching-cost business: ~93–94% retention, ~14.5-year average storage duration, chain-of-custody / regulatory compliance requirements, and the physical pain of retrieving and moving hundreds of millions of boxes; management has compounded storage revenue for decades largely through annual revenue-management (pricing) actions, i.e. demonstrated pricing power. (2) Scale and network: the largest global records footprint (1,300+ facilities, 61 countries) plus a logistics fleet no sub-scale rival replicates. (3) Cross-sell: the same 240,000+ regulated-enterprise relationships seed Digital, ALM and data-centre sales — ALM in particular monetises hyperscaler decommissioning, a flow created by other firms' capex, not IRM's. (4) Data-centre pre-leasing: ~97% leased capacity, renewal pricing +12% cash in Q2, and a signed backlog — demand is contracted before capacity energises, not speculative.
AGAINST — (1) The legacy core is ex-growth in volume terms: paper records face secular digital substitution, storage volume growth is low-single-digit at best, and reported RIM growth leans heavily on price — a lever customers and litigants are increasingly contesting (disputes over retrieval / destruction fees; see red flags). (2) The growth moat is much thinner than the legacy moat: data centres are a scale-and-power game against Digital Realty, Equinix and hyperscaler self-builds, where IRM is a challenger, not the incumbent, and returns depend on power availability, construction cost and leasing lumpiness. (3) The asset base is mostly leased — only roughly a quarter of facility square footage is owned — so lease obligations stack on top of funded debt. (4) Returns on capital do not yet corroborate a wide moat through the build cycle: GAAP net income was only $152M on $6.9B of revenue in FY2025 and book equity is negative (−$981M).
CAP from moat 6: clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** — moot for the headline DCF (no positive conservative OE to fade), but it is the CAP used in the recurring-capex sensitivity below.

## FLAGS — resolving every flag
The engine's Tier-F branch emits no `fragility_flags` list (dcf_numbers.json carries the F note instead; the sensitivity run's flag list is also resolved here), so each v4 flag was checked manually:
- **STALE_FY** — Fires: FY2025 ended ~9.5 months before this run (also in the sensitivity run's list). Resolved with the filing-derived TTM base above: TTM OE −$2.10/share, an improvement on the latest FY (−$3.60) because H1'26 OCF was a record $888M and TTM capex eased — but still negative. Verdict unchanged.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** — Fires in substance: latest FY −$3.60 vs 3-yr median −$2.41 is a ~1.5× gap. Resolution: neither is an error. The series is deteriorating monotonically because total capex rose every year 2020–2025 (above); the median is flattered by 2023, before the data-centre build peaked. The TTM (−$2.10) is the most current and the least negative — and all three are negative, so the tier is identical under any choice.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** — Fires (also in the sensitivity run, naming 2023, 2024, 2025): net income $152M vs OCF $1,340M in FY2025. Resolution: structural for a REIT, not a one-off. The bridge is principally non-cash depreciation & amortisation of $1,024M (real-estate and data-centre assets) plus $140M SBC add-back, partly offset by working-capital absorption. No KO-style tax-deposit distortion was found. The direction matters: GAAP net income *understates* cash generation here (the opposite of the usual warning), which is precisely why the industry uses FFO/AFFO — and why the OCF-based conservative OE, not net income, is the right series for this screen.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** — **Fires, and it is the central caveat of this run.** Universe sector is Real Estate / REIT, and the OE/capex DCF fits REITs poorly for two documented reasons: (1) it charges all capex, while $2,068M of FY2025's $2,215M total (supplemental split) was growth capex and only $147M recurring — see the quantified sensitivity above, which moves the answer from F to D, not to a pass; (2) real-estate depreciation depresses the maintenance definition's net-income anchor. Per the sweep rule: **confidence low, tier soft.** The F should be read as "fails the standardised conservative OE screen, and still fails (Tier D, 7.7%) on a REIT-friendly recurring-capex base at $115.10" — not as "the rental business loses cash," which it does not (AFFO +$1.54B in FY2025).
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** — Fires (15.5× in the sensitivity run against the recurring base; not computable against the negative conservative base, so in absolutes): net debt $16,273M at FY2025 (cash $159M; debt $216M current + $16,216M long-term), ~10.6× FY2025 AFFO and roughly 6× FY2025 adjusted EBITDA. The company's preferred net lease-adjusted leverage was 4.8× at Q2 2026 (target 4.5–5.5×, lowest since before the 2014 REIT conversion); weighted-average debt maturity 4.6 years at 5.6%. Contested: a November 2025 short report by Gotham City Research / General Industrial Partners argued leverage on its own methodology is roughly double the company's figure, that adjusted EBITDA is materially overstated by recurring add-backs, and that AFFO / dividend coverage look far weaker on a free-cash-flow basis (its value range was $22–41/share); securities-fraud investigations by plaintiff firms followed. Those are allegations, not findings — no regulator finding is in the record reviewed — but the leverage-definition dispute is unresolved and is a reason in itself to size any IRM thesis to the conservative end. Book equity is negative (−$981M), normal for a mature distributing REIT but leaving no book cushion.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD / HIGH_TV_SHARE / WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** — Conservative OE yield is −1.8% (−$2.10 / $115.10); on the recurring base it is +3.1% with terminal value only 20.9% of IV — the shortfall there is the base yield, not terminal dependence. The sensitivity scenario spread is 4.8× (in its flag list), which independently caps confidence. On the headline base, 100% of the $115.10 price rests on future growth-business cash flows that have not yet appeared as owner's earnings.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** — Does not fire on the last 5 years (revenue growth range +7.7% to +12.2%, no >60% year). Two things checked and cleared: (1) the financials_history.csv revenue figures for 2018 ($1,603M) and 2019 ($1,582M) are an EDGAR tag-coverage artifact in the early years, not a real collapse — the 5-yr/3-yr CAGR window (2020 onward: $4,147M → $6,902M) is unaffected and ties to reported revenue; (2) the mix shift to data centre / ALM / Digital is organic growth plus small tuck-ins, not a divestiture or restatement that breaks the OE series — the OE series break in 2023–25 is the capex build, resolved above.
- **OE_VOLATILE** — Fires in substance: OE/share ran +$1.77 (2020) → +$0.30 → −$0.02 → −$1.02 → −$2.41 → −$3.60 (2025). This is not business cyclicality; it tracks the data-centre capex calendar almost dollar-for-dollar. The normalised median exists for exactly this and is still negative.

- **THIN_HISTORY / BIG_NET_CASH** — Neither applies: 10 fiscal years of clean EDGAR history, and the balance sheet is net debt, not net cash (above).

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is the most "explainable" F in the sweep so far, and the explanation still does not produce a pass. Unlike the SBC-driven Fs (AXON, FIG, SNAP), Iron Mountain's owners are not being diluted away — they are being *built* away: every dollar of operating cash flow, plus ~$630M/yr more, is being poured into data-centre capacity whose revenue is largely pre-leased but whose owner's-earnings payoff sits after the build wave. That can be good capital allocation (the sensitivity base shows the existing estate throws off ~$3.53/share after recurring capex) and still be a bad price: at $115.10 you pay 19.5× *next year's guided* AFFO for a levered REIT whose growth must be continuously debt-funded at ~5.6%.
- **Method range: F (conservative, headline) to D (recurring-capex sensitivity, weighted IRR 7.7%, IV@15% $43.89).** That is the honest spread, and both ends are far below the 15% hurdle — unusually for an F, there is no defensible input set found in this run that clears even 10% on a weighted basis (the sensitivity run's super bull alone is 12.3%).
- **Hurdle-clearing price:** on the conservative base there is none (negative OE at any price). On the generous recurring-capex base, the weighted IV@15% is $43.89 — i.e. roughly 60% below the current price — and a margin of safety against that sensitivity case would require a price in the mid-$30s. Anyone buying at $115 is not buying a 15% owner's-earnings return under either definition; they are buying AFFO-multiple stability plus a data-centre growth narrative, with the Gotham leverage dispute as the live tail risk.
- What would change the tier: the build wave actually ending — two consecutive filings in which total capex falls back toward recurring levels while OCF holds (TTM conservative OE > 0), or AFFO/share compounding at a rate that, with the 3% dividend, clears 15% at the then-price without assuming multiple expansion. The 325 MW energisation schedule makes 2027–28 the first window where that could file.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: F** (= tier by numbers; no override — the REIT/growth-capex caveat is quantified in the sensitivity run and it reaches only Tier D, so there is no basis to lift the final tier)
- Confidence: **Low** (per the SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH rule for REITs, reinforced by the 4.8× sensitivity scenario spread and the unresolved leverage-definition dispute — low on the tier's precision, high on the narrower claim that $115.10 does not clear 15% under either OE definition tried)
- One-liner: Durable records franchise pivoting to data centres, moat 6/10 — but conservative owner's earnings are negative in 4 of the last 5 years (−$2.10/share TTM) because ~$2.1B/yr of growth capex exceeds operating cash flow on a $16.3B net-debt balance sheet, so it cannot clear the 15% hurdle at any price on an OE DCF; even charging only recurring capex gives just 7.7% weighted IRR (IV@15% $44 vs $115.10), so at $115 you pay 19.5× guided AFFO for a build wave, not for current owner's earnings.
