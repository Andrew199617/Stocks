## What it is
A regulated utility holding company serving electric and natural gas customers in Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, and Minnesota (We Energies / Wisconsin Public Service, Peoples Gas / North Shore Gas, other-states gas distribution, plus a stake in American Transmission Company). Earnings are what state commissions allow on a growing rate base. The growth story in current coverage (search results): a $37.5B all-regulated capital plan for 2026–2030 driven partly by very-large-customer / data-center load in Wisconsin (Microsoft, Vantage/Oracle), a long-term EPS growth target of 7–8% through 2030, FY2026 EPS guidance reaffirmed at $5.51–$5.61 after Q2 2026, and a quarterly dividend of $0.9525/share ($3.81 annualized) — the 23rd consecutive annual increase (all per search results).

## Moat: 6/10 — durable legal monopoly, capped economics
Justification: exclusive regulated franchises and a constructive home regulator give WEC durable, visible rate-base compounding, but the prize is a regulator-set return — there is no pricing power beyond filed rate cases, Illinois has already imposed disallowance/settlement costs, and the capital plan is financed with debt plus dilutive equity issuance.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated electric/gas service territories in WI/IL/MI/MN — captive customers for an essential service, no competing wires or pipes.
- FOR: Constructive Wisconsin regulation — search coverage puts ~75% of rate base under the PSCW with historically high allowed returns on equity.
- FOR: $37.5B all-regulated 2026–2030 capital plan (search) converts spending into rate base at allowed returns; the Wisconsin Very Large Customer tariff framework addresses data-center credit risk (search).
- FOR: Execution record — long streak of meeting guidance and 23 consecutive annual dividend increases (search).
- AGAINST: Returns are capped by regulators — allowed ROE is the ceiling, and rate-case outcomes, lag, and disallowances are the main earnings risk (Illinois dockets are the proven example, per search coverage of the Rider QIP / bad-debt settlements).
- AGAINST: The plan must be financed — heavy debt (net debt $19,989,900,000) plus planned equity issuance; share count already dilutes at +0.81%/yr (share_cagr in dcf_numbers.json).
- AGAINST: Long-run electrification/decarbonization pressure on the gas distribution network, and affordability politics as rates rise to recover the capital plan.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 9.43 months ago): ttm.py was run and exited non-zero with the message "sbc: no post-FY YTD fact (no 10-Q since the 2025-12-31 fiscal year end)" — no ttm.json was produced, so no TTM pin exists. Per the v5 fallback, the engine's median-of-last-3-FY base ($1.2367/share) is used and the reason is stated here. Interim search-reported prints (Q2/H1 2026 EPS growth, reaffirmed guidance) show no deterioration the stale FY would be hiding.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share −$3.29 vs 3-yr median $1.24): the latest year is the distorted one on this definition — FY2025 capex spiked to $4,398,100,000 from $2,781,100,000 in FY2024 and $2,492,900,000 in FY2023 (financials_history.csv) as the capital plan ramped, while revenue rose +13.96% YoY and net income rose to $1,555,500,000. That is a capex-timing trough, not an earnings collapse. The median is retained as the normalized screening base; the maintenance definition ($2,161,400,000 for FY2025) shows how much of the gap is the all-capex charge.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (NI vs OCF differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): structural utility accounting, not a one-off or an earnings-quality flag. From financials_history.csv: 2023 NI $1,331,700,000 vs OCF $3,018,400,000 with D&A $1,264,200,000; 2024 NI $1,524,300,000 vs OCF $3,211,800,000 with D&A $1,354,500,000; 2025 NI $1,555,500,000 vs OCF $3,379,400,000 with D&A $1,478,500,000. Non-cash depreciation on the large plant base explains the bulk of the gap each year; the remainder is the usual regulated-utility non-cash and working-capital timing, in words — no tax-deposit or impairment one-off of the kind this flag hunts was identified in the filing history.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Utilities): real and central. The OE/capex DCF fits regulated utilities poorly — growth capex enters rate base and earns an allowed return, so charging it all against OE understates earning power. Confidence is graded low and the tier is soft; the cross-method check under Thoughts is what makes the D verdict robust despite the mismatch.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (base OE yield 1.17%): real. At $106.08 the price is carried almost entirely by assumed OE growth, and regulated growth is capped by rate-case mechanics — there is no plausible growth path on this base that clears 15%.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV 4.84x super-bear IV): the IV levels span widely because the OE base is small and capex-volatile, but every scenario's IRR sits between 3.00% and 5.87% — all Tier D. The spread creates no tier ambiguity; confidence is low in any case (sector mismatch), never high.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 49.92x normalized OE): folded into the verdict. The multiple is inflated by the depressed OE denominator — the debt is secured by $38,278,100,000 of net plant and serviced from regulated cash flows — but the leverage is still why the bear cases carry OE haircuts and why rising financing costs on the debt-funded plan are a direct headwind. It does not rescue the valuation: even the maintenance-definition base would leave the shares far from a 15% hurdle at this price, per the method range below.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range (the honest headline):** the v4 run (2026-10-13, score.json on disk) used a hand-built TTM adjusted-EPS base of $5.61/share and produced IV@15% $55.02 at a 9.2% weighted IRR — also Tier D. This v5 run uses the standardized OE base ($1.2367/share) and produces IV@15% $12.48 at 4.09% — Tier D. The two methods disagree sharply on value (the model mismatch is real) but agree on the only question the sweep asks: at ~$106, WEC does not clear a 15% hurdle, or even a 10% hurdle, on either basis. v5 prohibits the hand-built TTM base the v4 run used, so the v5 figures are the recorded result.
- Quality is not the problem: this is a well-run regulated monopoly with visible rate-base growth and a data-center load kicker. The problem is price: the market already pays a full growth-utility multiple for the guided 7–8% EPS path (search coverage: TTM EPS $5.15, guidance $5.51–$5.61, dividend yield ~3.6%), leaving nothing for a 15%-hurdle buyer.
- Red flags sized: Illinois regulatory outcomes are the proven downside channel; the equity-issuance treadmill dilutes per-share compounding; residential volumes were soft in Q2 2026 per search coverage (residential use −1.1%, weather-normal retail deliveries +1.2% excluding very large customers), so growth is concentrated in a few very large customers whose projects can slip. SBC is immaterial ($48,200,000 in FY2025). No going-concern, concentration-beyond-regulatory, or litigation flags beyond the regulatory dockets noted.
- Hurdle math: the price that clears 15% on the weighted v5 case is the weighted IV@15% itself, $12.48; a margin of safety would require a price materially below that. On the friendlier v4 EPS basis the clearing price was $55.02 — still roughly half the current price. There is no defensible input set under which $106.08 clears.

## Final verdict
