# WEC — WEC Energy Group, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Utilities / regulated electric & gas (Wisconsin, Illinois, Michigan, Minnesota). Price $104.91 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap ~$34.0B (engine, 323.8M diluted shares) — in scope under $40B.

## What it is
A regulated utility holding company serving ~4.8M customers: Wisconsin segment (We Energies / Wisconsin Public Service — electric generation, electric and gas distribution in WI and Michigan's Upper Peninsula), Illinois segment (Peoples Gas / North Shore Gas distribution in Chicago and suburbs), other-states gas distribution (Minnesota, Michigan), a stake in American Transmission Company, and non-utility energy infrastructure (We Power generation, Bluewater gas storage, contracted renewables). Earnings are whatever the PSCW / ICC / state commissions allow on a growing rate base. The growth engine is explicit: a $37.5B all-regulated capital plan for 2026–2030 ($12.6B renewables, $7.4B thermal/LNG, distribution and ATC transmission), targeting 7–8% long-term EPS growth, with very-large-customer (data center) load — Microsoft's Wisconsin campus, the Vantage/OpenAI-Oracle Port Washington project — as the accelerant; management expects ~15% of the asset base to serve data centers / very large customers by 2030 and Wisconsin electric sales growth of 6–8%/yr in 2028–2030. Dividend $3.81/yr (raised Jan 2026, 23rd consecutive increase; target growth 6.5–7% on a 65–70% payout).

## Owner's earnings — conservative OE is structural nonsense for this company; the base is TTM adjusted EPS
- **Conservative OE (OCF − total capex − SBC; SBC has no EDGAR tag for WEC, cash comp): negative or near-zero in most years** — FY2023 +$525.5M, FY2024 +$430.7M, FY2025 **−$1,018.7M (−$3.15/share)**; positive in only 2 of the last 5 fiscal years. That is not a slump: a utility growing rate base at this pace spends ~1.5–3× depreciation on plant, and FY2025 capex spiked to $4,398M (from $2,781M in FY2024) as the capital plan ramped. Most of that capex is growth capex that enters rate base and earns the authorized return — exactly the case the maintenance definition exists for, and exactly why the engine's normalized base (3-yr median conservative OE/share, $1.36) is rejected, same as ED/PEG/PCG before it.
- **TTM conservative OE through Q2 2026 is also negative, same story:** OCF TTM $3,574.2M (FY25 $3,379.4M − H1'25 $2,015.9M + H1'26 $2,210.7M, EDGAR 10-Q YTD) − capex TTM $4,947.5M (FY25 $4,398.1M − H1'25 $1,530.5M + H1'26 $2,079.9M) = **−$1,373.3M**. Structural, not earning power.
- **Maintenance OE (engine formula, FY2025): $2,161.4M = $6.67/share** — not used as the base either: it is flattered by a −$605.9M ΔNWC swing in FY2025, so it overstates run-rate earning power vs. reported earnings.
- **Base used — TTM adjusted EPS through Q2 2026 = $5.61/share:** FY2025 adjusted EPS $5.27 − H1 2025 EPS $3.02 + H1 2026 EPS $3.36 = **$5.61**. Cross-checks: TTM GAAP EPS = $4.81 − $3.02 + $3.36 = $5.15 (TTM GAAP net income to common $1,557.5M − $969.6M + $1,103.6M = $1,691.5M); the entire $0.46 gap to adjusted is the Q4 2025 Illinois QIP/UEA rider settlement charge (see FLAGS) — a one-off regulatory settlement, excluded from adjusted earnings, that sits inside the TTM GAAP window. Q2 2026 itself: EPS $0.91 vs $0.76 a year ago, net income $299.2M, revenue $2.06B (+2.6%); H1 2026 EPS $3.36 vs $3.02. FY2026 guidance reaffirmed at $5.51–$5.61 — the TTM adjusted base already sits at the top of that range, which is a reason not to push the base higher. Price = **18.7× TTM base OE**; OE yield 5.3%.

## Moat score: 6/10 — durable legal monopoly, capped economics
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Legal monopoly franchises — no competitor can deliver electrons or gas in its WI/IL/MI/MN territories; demand is essential and weather-driven, not discretionary. (2) Visibility: an all-regulated $37.5B 2026–2030 plan, multi-year Wisconsin rate cases for 2027/2028 test years already in process, and a PSCW-approved Very Large Customer tariff that puts financial-security requirements on data-center customers instead of socializing that credit risk across ratepayers. (3) Execution record: 22 consecutive years of meeting/beating original EPS guidance midpoints (adjusted basis), 23 consecutive dividend increases. (4) A genuine load-growth runway most utilities don't have — the I-94 Milwaukee–Chicago corridor data-center build-out (Microsoft, Vantage) underpins guided 6–8% Wisconsin sales growth in 2028–2030.
- **AGAINST:** (1) Returns are capped by regulators — the moat's prize is an authorized ROE, not pricing power, and Illinois has already shown the downside: ICC disallowances in 2024 and the 2025 QIP/UEA settlement ($205M pre-tax charge, ~$130M rate-base reduction and ~$125M of customer credits in the approved settlement resolving 12 dockets). (2) The plan must be financed: $14.2–14.8B of incremental debt plus $5.3–5.7B of new common equity planned for 2026–2030 (~$1.1B of equity in 2026 alone) — dilution is structural (share count already +0.8%/yr). (3) Gas distribution faces long-run electrification/decarbonization risk, and the coal exit (backup-only by end-2030, out by end-2032) is a cost-recovery negotiation, not a free transition. (4) Affordability politics: rates must rise to recover $37.5B of plant from the same customers regulators answer to.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a good utility priced like a growth stock. The business case is unusually visible — monopoly franchise, $37.5B of pre-identified regulated spend, data-center load, a 22-year guidance record — and the market is paying 18.7× TTM adjusted earnings, a full utility multiple, for it. At that price the DCF cannot clear even a 10% hurdle in the weighted case (9.2%), and **even the super-bull case ($75.20 IV@15%, 11.7% IRR) sits 28% below today's price**. The gap is not a close call and not a base-year artifact.
- Red flags, honestly sized: Illinois regulatory risk is proven, not hypothetical (disallowances, then a settlement costing $0.46/share plus rate-base reductions and customer credits); the equity-issuance treadmill ($5.3–5.7B by 2030) permanently dilutes the per-share compounding the multiple is paying for; residential volumes are soft (Q2 residential use −1.1%, weather-normal retail deliveries only +1.2% ex-VLC) — the growth story is concentrated in a handful of very large customers whose projects can slip. SBC is a non-issue (no SBC tag, cash comp) and there is no customer-concentration, litigation or going-concern issue beyond the regulatory dockets noted.
- **Hurdle math:** the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is **$55.02**; with a 20% margin of safety, **~$44**. Even at a 12% discount the weighted value is only $73.38.
