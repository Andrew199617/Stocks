## What it is
A founding mega-fund alternative asset manager (founded 1992 as Texas Pacific Group), IPO'd in 2022 in an Up-C structure: the public company owns a minority stake in the TPG Operating Group, and the engine's share count (374,125,608 diluted) is the total unit count across all holders, which is the right denominator for group economics. Total AUM $327B at Q2 2026 (+25% YoY), fee-earning AUM $181B (+24% YoY) with a further $39B of AUM not yet earning fees. Platforms: Capital (buyout/growth), Credit (Angelo Gordon, Twin Brook middle-market lending), Real Estate, Market Solutions and Multi-Asset/Other. Q2 2026: fee-related revenue $628M (+27% YoY), fee-related earnings (FRE) $315M (+43% YoY) at a 50% FRE margin, after-tax distributable earnings (DE) $0.69 per Class A share, dividend declared $0.59/share. H1 2026 fundraising $26B toward a >$50B full-year goal; the Jackson Financial insurance partnership closed in January 2026 ($12B of fee-earning AUM over five years). The stock is down from ~$70 at the start of 2026 to $42.20 on a sector-wide selloff (private-credit fears, AI exposure of PE software holdings, redemption gates at peers) — not on TPG's own prints, which set records in H1 2026. The earnings question is the same one as ARES: what the engine's cash-flow definition means for this structure (below), and it cuts the opposite way here.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate-strong, not wide
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **Evidence note (required):** the `roic_proxy` series and gross-margin series in fundamentals.json are **null for every year of this filer** — TPG files no operating-income or COGS tags, and its consolidated balance sheet mixes consolidated-fund assets with the manager's, so neither series is computable from the filings. The moat therefore rests on the observable franchise margins instead: FRE margin 38% (Q1 2025) → 44% (Q1 2026) → 50% (Q2 2026), Fitch TTM fee-related EBITDA margin 47.9% at Q2 2026, and fee-earning AUM inflows of $50.7B TTM (23.2% of beginning fee-earning AUM). A 6, not a 7+: there is no through-cycle ROIC series to point to, and the margin evidence is a fee margin on other people's capital, not a return on TPG's own invested capital.
- FOR: Scale and brand in buyout — a founding mega-fund franchise (1992) with $327B AUM (+25% YoY at Q2 2026) and LP relationships re-upped through many cycles
- FOR: Durable, growing fee core — fee-earning AUM $181B plus $39B of AUM not yet earning fees as a visible pipeline; perpetual capital rose to 14% of fee-earning AUM
- FOR: FRE margin expansion proves operating leverage — fee-related earnings +43% YoY in Q2 2026 at a 50% quarterly margin; Fitch TTM fee-related EBITDA margin 47.9%
- FOR: Diversification and distribution — Capital, Credit (Angelo Gordon / Twin Brook), Real Estate and Market Solutions, plus wealth and insurance channels (Jackson Financial partnership: $12B of fee-earning AUM over five years)
- AGAINST: No structural pricing power — LPs re-bid every vintage against Blackstone/KKR/Apollo/Ares/Brookfield, and the mix shift toward credit compresses fee rates
- AGAINST: Carry and realization cyclicality — PE-heavy earnings depend on exit windows; the 2026 sector selloff took the stock from ~$70 to $42.20 on exit/credit/AI-software fears, not on TPG's own prints
- AGAINST: Growth partly bought, not only earned — Angelo Gordon (closed Nov 2023) and Peppertree (closed Jul 2025) stepped AUM and revenue up inorganically; integration and contingent-consideration risk
- AGAINST: Partner economics dilute the per-share claim — diluted units +6.6%/yr (308,908,052 in FY2022 to 374,125,608 in FY2025, 384,594,589 TTM) and equity comp of $813,741,000 in FY2025

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range (the real headline):** on the engine's standardized consolidated-OCF-minus-full-SBC base, this run prints Tier D (weighted IRR 3.23%, IV@15% $1.93). On the manager's own cash-earnings definition — TTM after-tax distributable earnings of ≈$2.78 per total diluted unit through Q1 2026 — the identical scenario structure prints Tier B (measure-mode: weighted IRR 16.05%, IV@15% $46.48 at the $42.20 price). The truth is between the definitions and closer to DE than to the engine: DE's add-back of equity comp is too generous (the cost is real; it is paid in units at +6.6%/yr, which the scenario growth only partly claws back), while the engine's charge is doubly punitive (full SBC against an OCF line that is itself consolidated-fund plumbing, on a perimeter where the public holder's claim is a minority of the group). Every defensible reading says the same thing about price: at $42.20 the buyer is paying roughly the DE definition's fair value with no margin of safety — the 2026 de-rating from ~$70 removed the bubble, it did not create a bargain.
- Quality is not the debate: FRE is recurring, growing +43% YoY, at a 50% margin, with $39B of AUM not yet earning fees and perpetual capital rising; the dividend was $0.59 for Q2 2026 (DPS TTM $2.24 per fundamentals.json). The debate is definition and price: on the definition that flatters the manager the stock is fairly priced; on the standardized definition it is unownable. Pass 2's forensic pass, not this screen, is where a DE-vs-SBC reconciliation belongs.
- Red flags to monitor: realization/exit markets staying shut (average PE holding periods stretching past 6 years sector-wide); private-credit marks and any redemption pressure in wealth-channel products; fee-rate compression as credit mix grows; SBC and unit count continuing to compound at +6.6%/yr; Angelo Gordon / Peppertree contingent consideration; regulatory scrutiny of retail private-market products; key-person concentration in the founding generation.
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine base the stock clears a 15% IRR only at ≈ **$1.93** (weighted IV@15%) — a meaningless figure that measures the definition, not the company. On the DE definition it clears 15% at ≈ **$46.48**, and with a 20% margin of safety the buy price on that definition is ≈ **$37**.
