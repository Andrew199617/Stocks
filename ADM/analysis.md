## What it is
Global agricultural merchandiser and processor: Ag Services & Oilseeds (origination, storage/transport, crushing, refining), Carbohydrate Solutions (starches & sweeteners, ethanol via Vantage Corn Processors), and Nutrition (human and animal). FY2025 reported totals (earnings release, via search): revenue $80,269M, net earnings attributable to ADM $1,078M, GAAP EPS $2.23, adjusted EPS $3.43. A low-margin commodity toll-taker: FY2025 gross profit was $5,033M on that $80,269M of revenue.

Latest earnings: Q2 2026 (reported 2026-08-04) — revenue $22,681M (+7.2% YoY), GAAP EPS $1.87, adjusted EPS $1.84 (+98% YoY), total segment operating profit up 75% YoY; FY2026 adjusted EPS guidance was raised to $5.15–5.60 from $4.15–4.70 (search results). The driver is explicit in ADM's own release: the EPA finalized 2026/2027 renewable volume obligations in March 2026, lifting crush and ethanol margins, helped by elevated energy prices. That is a policy-and-cycle rebound off a trough — real, but not a durable growth rate. Adjusted ROIC on the trailing four quarters was only 7.8% per ADM's Q2 release (via search).

Red flags: (1) the 2024 Nutrition intersegment-accounting scandal — sales between Nutrition and the other segments were not recorded at market rates, inflating Nutrition's segment profit; ADM restated segment reporting in November 2024 and settled the SEC investigation in January 2026 for $40M (prior v4 analysis of this folder; segment-only, no impact on consolidated earnings or cash flows, so the consolidated owner's-earnings history used here stands). (2) Earnings are hostage to US biofuel policy, crush margins, crop prices and trade flows — all outside management's control. (3) Owner's earnings are extremely lumpy from working-capital / readily-marketable-inventory swings (see flags).

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate; scale without pricing power
Justification: ADM's origination, storage, transportation and processing network is genuinely hard to replicate and has survived a century of cycles, but it earns commodity returns: crush and ethanol margins are set by markets and by US biofuel policy, not by ADM, and through-cycle returns on capital are high-single-digit at best (7.8% adjusted ROIC on the trailing four quarters, at a cyclical *upswing* print). That is scale, not a wide moat.
- FOR: global origination / grain-elevator / rail-barge / export-terminal network at a scale rivals cannot quickly replicate, with real logistics optionality when trade flows shift; diversified processing footprint across oilseeds, corn and Nutrition, so no single spread carries the company; equity earnings from unconsolidated affiliates ($648M in FY2025, earnings release) including the Wilmar relationship; balance-sheet staying power through cycles (378 consecutive quarters of dividends, per the Q2 2026 call via search).
- AGAINST: commodity price-taker on both sides — ADM prices neither what it buys (corn, soy) nor most of what it sells; crush and ethanol margins are market-set, and the entire 2026 earnings recovery is explicitly policy-driven (EPA renewable volume obligations), i.e. the tailwind is a regulation, not an advantage; equally scaled, undifferentiated rivals (Bunge, Cargill, COFCO); Nutrition — the one segment that could have earned a moat premium — is where the intersegment-accounting fraud lived, and it is too small to move consolidated returns; working-capital-heavy model absorbs cash exactly when commodity prices rise; thin structural margins (FY2025 gross profit $5,033M on $80,269M revenue).
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.43 months ago): ttm.py was run and its full inputs are quoted above. The TTM base was *not* adopted — see BASE_DIVERGENCE; the median base is used, with the TTM figure disclosed as the bear-side evidence. The Q2 2026 earnings recovery is reflected in the scenario growth starts and in rejecting any claim that FY2025's trough earnings are the run rate.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $8.51 vs 3-yr median $5.27): both single-point bases are distorted — the latest FY by a working-capital release (maintenance OE is only $1,799,000,000 vs conservative $4,121,000,000), the TTM ($3.15) by an H1 2026 WC build during an earnings upswing. The median is the least-distorted base; resolved by using it and by weighting bear outcomes at 45% combined.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in the engine series): an EDGAR tagging break, not a divestiture. The engine's revenue tag (RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax) matches ADM's reported total revenue through 2021 ($85,249M) and then drops to $27,606,000,000 in 2022, while ADM's reported total revenue was $101,556M in 2022, $93,935M in 2023, $85,530M in 2024 and $80,269M in 2025 (earnings release / Barchart, via search). So the engine's revenue CAGRs (−17.26% 5y, −3.31% 3y, +2.39% latest YoY) are not total-revenue growth and were not trusted as such; the real top-line trend is a mid-single-digit decline from the 2022 commodity-price peak. The OE series is unaffected — it is built from OCF/capex/SBC tags, which show no such break. The genuine basis item — the November 2024 Nutrition restatement — was segment-only with no consolidated impact, so it does not break the consolidated OE history either.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2025: net income $1,078,000,000 vs OCF $5,452,000,000): working capital and non-cash charges, identified in the FY2025 release (via search): $473M of asset impairment, exit and restructuring costs depressing net income, plus non-cash D&A of $1,181,000,000 (financials_history.csv), while OCF was boosted by drawing down inventories/receivables into falling revenue — the mirror image of H1 2026. Neither the NI-based maintenance OE nor the OCF-based conservative OE is a clean single-year run rate for ADM; this is the core reason the base is a 3-yr median.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real and structural — crush/ethanol spread cyclicality plus inventory-driven OCF (conservative OE totals from $1,153,000,000 to $5,265,000,000 across the last 5 years). Handled by the normalized median base, wider bear/super-bear weight (45% combined), and a super-bear OE multiplier of 0.75.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count −4.91%/yr, credited +4.91pp in the blend): stale. Diluted shares fell from 566,000,000 (2021) to 484,000,000 (2025), but the count has stopped shrinking — 493,000,000 in FY2024, 484,000,000 in FY2025, and 485,000,000 on the TTM current-YTD basis (ttm.py) — with management only "evaluating a return to opportunistic share repurchases later this year" (Q2 2026 call, via search). The forward tailwind is ~0, which is one reason scenario starts were offset below the engine's g_base rather than taken from it. Not double-counted in the verdict.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (fired on the preliminary run at 4.51x): after the growth recalibration the final spread is 2.97x ($71.79 / $24.19). Still wide — commodity spreads are genuinely bimodal around policy — so confidence is capped at medium even though the flag no longer fires on the final run.
