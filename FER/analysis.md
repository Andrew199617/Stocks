# FER — Ferrovial N.V.
v5 run, 2026-10-14. In scope (market cap $36.180B at $50.34, nasdaq_quote_api). IFRS filer reporting in EUR, Form 20-F; the Nasdaq security is the ordinary share itself (ads_ratio 1.0 — see FLAGS).

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, or the filer's own 20-F statements where explicitly cited — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Netherlands-domiciled infrastructure group built around toll-road concessions: 48.29% of Toronto's 407 ETR and majority stakes in US managed lanes (NTE, LBJ, NTE 35W, I-77, I-66), airports (49% of JFK New Terminal One, 60% of Dalaman), and a large construction arm (incl. Budimex) that supplies most consolidated revenue at thin margins. H1 2026 (company release, cited from search): revenue €4,701M, up 11.3% like-for-like; adjusted EBITDA €746M, up 21.6% like-for-like; net profit €258M vs €540M in H1 2025 because the prior year included asset-rotation capital gains; construction order book a record €18,046M; JFK NTO construction 92% complete with the company's USD 1.1B committed equity injections completed.

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate, carried by the toll roads
Justification: the highways portfolio is genuinely scarce — 407 ETR and the US congestion-priced lanes sit in corridors that cannot be replicated, with proven toll pricing power — but the crown jewels are minority/JV stakes, toll-setting is politically exposed, and most consolidated revenue is low-margin construction.
- FOR: 407 ETR (48.29% stake): irreplaceable Toronto corridor under a long concession, proven toll pricing power and large recurring dividends; US managed lanes (NTE, LBJ, NTE 35W, I-77, I-66): congestion-based dynamic tolling in corridors that cannot be duplicated; H1 2026 revenue/transaction grew +8.7% to +18.9%, well above inflation; inflation-linked concession cash flows with decades-long remaining lives and extreme permitting/replication barriers; scarce airport capacity positions: 49% of JFK New Terminal One and 60% of Dalaman Airport; record €18B construction order book (North America 47.9%) gives forward revenue visibility.
- AGAINST: crown jewels are minority/JV stakes (407 ETR 48.29%, JFK NTO 49%) — dividend and strategy decisions are shared, not controlled; political/regulatory exposure on tolls (Ontario pressure on 407 ETR pricing; US managed-lane backlash) and finite concession lives; most consolidated revenue is construction: competitive bidding, ~3.5% adjusted EBIT margin target, no pricing power; capital-intensive concession model: value arrives as equity-accounted dividends, and consolidated net debt was €6,283M at H1 2026 (mostly non-recourse project debt); reported profit is flattered by asset-rotation gains (a €2,208M disposal-gain line in FY2024 net income), so headline earnings overstate recurring economics.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **DATA_GAP:sbc / DATA_GAP:capex** (preliminary run): resolved by the filing-sourced overrides above; data_gap_uncovered is empty in the final run and the measured impact is recorded.
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.4 months ago): ttm.py cannot run on this IFRS filer (us-gaap-only; its exit-3 message is quoted under Owner's earnings), so the engine's median base is used, as the spec prescribes for IFRS names. Substantively checked against H1 2026: revenue €4,701M (+11.3% LfL), adjusted EBITDA €746M (+21.6% LfL), highways revenue +15.8% LfL — no post-FY deterioration that would invalidate the median base.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): resolved from the 20-F Consolidated cash flow statements (EUR millions, parent-company net profit 341 / 3,239 / 888 for 2023/2024/2025). 2024 is the distorted year: net income contains an "Impairment and disposal of non-current assets" gain line of €2,208M (the Heathrow-stake asset rotation) that the cash-flow reconciliation strips out of OCF — the OCF-based conservative OE never contained it. 2023 and 2025 run the other way (OCF above net income) on non-cash depreciation (€401M / €490M), equity-accounted profits (€215M / €258M) that arrive in OCF only as dividends received (€324M / €502M, classified as operating), tax payments and working-capital timing. The OE base is OCF-based throughout, so none of this contaminates it; it does mean reported net income is unusable as an earnings proxy for this filer.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: EUR reporting converted to USD at period-end ECB rates (1.105 for 2023, 1.0389 for 2024, 1.175 for 2025, per dcf_numbers.json fx_conversion); flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average — a documented simplification. The two overrides were declared in EUR and converted by the engine on the same basis (overrides_converted: capex, sbc).
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: ratio 1.0 — ordinary shares trade directly on Nasdaq (see OVERRIDE); no scaling applied or needed.
- **OVERRIDE_APPLIED:sbc / OVERRIDE_APPLIED:capex**: see OVERRIDE — gap, filing source, band/ack reasoning and measured impact are all recorded in fields (judgment.json, dcf_numbers.json overrides_applied/override_impact, overrides_registry.json), not just here.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $2.82 vs median $1.61): the median is the conservative one, and it is kept deliberately. FY2025 OCF stepped up (€1,926M vs €1,293M/€1,263M in the two prior years, per the 20-F statement) on highways dividend receipts and +19.02% revenue growth, while 2023–24 OCF was held down by tax payments and working-capital outflows in the same statement. The step-up looks real but is one year; capitalizing it as the base would flatter the DCF. Sizing note: even the super-bull scenario on the median base produces IV@15% $50.23 — essentially the current price — so the D verdict does not hinge on choosing the median over the latest year.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.80x): real — outcomes range from toll-politics damage to a long OE compounding path. Confidence is set to low, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **BIG_NET_CASH** (net cash 3.2x normalized OE): an artifact, not a cash pile. The engine's net debt (−$3,760,000,000) counts cash of $5,018,425,000 against only current borrowings of $1,258,425,000 because long-term borrowings came back null in used_tags (debt_lt) — the filer's project debt is not in the engine's debt figure. The company's own H1 2026 release reports consolidated net debt of €6,283M and net debt excluding infrastructure projects of −€1,307M (i.e. net cash ex-projects). No net-cash credit is taken in the verdict; the balance sheet is project-levered, with the leverage mostly non-recourse inside the concessions.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The standardized OE-DCF is a rough lens for Ferrovial in both directions: it misses concession equity value that arrives as dividends from equity-accounted assets (407 ETR is not consolidated), and it charges only PP&E capex while the group's real investment (infrastructure projects, NTO equity) sits outside OE. Those two errors partly offset, but neither is small — treat the point estimate as a screen, not a valuation. Confidence: low.
- What the screen does say cleanly: at $50.34 (≈$36.2B market cap) the buyer needs the super-bull OE path just to earn ~15% — the weighted IV@15% is $25.63, roughly half the price, and even the bull case (25% weight) returns only 11.15%. The market is pricing Ferrovial as a scarce-asset compounder (analyst sum-of-the-parts stories put most equity value in the highways), not on consolidated owner's earnings.
- Quality of the underlying assets is not the problem: 407 ETR and the US lanes are printing revenue-per-transaction growth far above inflation, the backlog is at a record, and NTO is 92% built with equity fully injected. The problem is purely price vs. consolidated cash generation.
- Hurdle math: the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is the weighted IV@15% itself, $25.63. A margin-of-safety buyer would need materially below that, given the 4.80x scenario spread and the model-fit caveats.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (weighted IRR 9.45% on the v5 final inputs; no tier override)
- Confidence: **Low** (WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD, plus a standardized model that fits concession economics poorly in both directions and an engine net-debt figure that does not capture project debt)
- One-liner: High-quality toll-road concessions (moat 6/10) inside a mostly-construction consolidator — at $50.34 the weighted IRR is only 9.5% (IV@15% $25.63), so it does not clear the 15% hurdle; it would need a price at or below ~$25.63 to clear 15% on these numbers.
