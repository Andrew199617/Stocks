## What it is
Kansas City bank holding company (UMB Bank, N.A.; SIC 6021 National Commercial Banks per fundamentals.json profile): Commercial Banking, Institutional Banking (fund administration and accounting, custody, corporate trust, and HSA/healthcare payment services) and Personal Banking. The balance sheet stepped up on January 31, 2025, when the all-stock acquisition of Heartland Financial USA, Inc. (HTLF) closed — the largest acquisition in the company's history, adding $16.1B in assets and $9.7B in loans and extending the footprint from 8 to 13 states (Q2 2026 earnings materials, via search results); systems and brand conversion completed in October 2025. Q2 2026 (release, via search results): revenue $778.0M (+12.9% YoY), net interest income $532.5M (+14.0% YoY), noninterest income $245.5M (+10.5% YoY), net income available to common shareholders $271,758,000 or $3.56 diluted GAAP EPS (operating EPS $3.57, +21.1% YoY vs $2.96 in Q2 2025 — a clean combined-vs-combined comparison, both quarters post-close), NIM (FTE) 3.32%, return on average common equity 14.16%, operating ROTCE 20.3%, end-of-period loans $41.1B, deposits $59.8B at June 30, 2026, net charge-offs 16bp of average loans and nonperforming loans 31bp of total loans. The board declared a $0.50 quarterly dividend (+16.3%) payable October 1, 2026.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate, high quality for a regional bank
Justification: operating ROTCE 20.3% in Q2 2026 with a 48.14% operating efficiency ratio and return on average common equity of 14.16% (Q2 2026 release, via search results), and a filed ROE history (fundamentals.json) of 11.2% / 16.2% / 11.3% / 12.7% in FY2021–FY2024 (FY2025's 9.1% divides by a year-end equity balance swollen by the Heartland stock issuance). fundamentals.json carries no roic_proxy or gross margin for a bank presentation (both null), so ROE/ROTCE are the return evidence. Capped at moderate because the core product is commodity banking with no structural pricing power, the enlarged loan book is untested through a credit cycle, and returns now depend on holding Heartland's deposits after the October 2025 conversion.
- FOR:
  - Institutional Banking fee franchise — fund administration/accounting, custody and HSA/healthcare payment services are sticky, fee-rich and less capital-intensive than lending; noninterest income was $245.5M in Q2 2026, +10.5% YoY (Q2 2026 release)
  - Deposit franchise at scale — deposits $59.8B at June 30, 2026 against loans of $41.1B (a conservative loan-to-deposit posture), funding the balance sheet cheaply enough for a 3.32% FTE NIM
  - Best-in-class efficiency for a regional — operating efficiency ratio 48.14% in Q2 2026 (GAAP 48.35%), well ahead of large-regional peers, with operating ROTCE of 20.3% in the quarter
  - Asset quality and underwriting record — net charge-offs 16bp of average loans and nonperforming loans 31bp of total loans in Q2 2026; OE positive 5 of the last 5 fiscal years
  - Heartland step-up delivered so far — acquisition closed January 31, 2025, systems/brand conversion completed October 2025, and Q2 2026 operating EPS of $3.57 was +21.1% YoY on a clean combined-vs-combined comparison
- AGAINST:
  - Commodity product, no structural pricing power — deposits and loans are fungible; NIM and deposit pricing are set by money-center banks, regionals and money-market alternatives
  - Credit-cycle exposure on an enlarged, unseasoned book — loans $41.1B at June 30, 2026 after the Heartland step-up, assembled in a benign stretch (charge-offs 16bp are a trough print, not a through-cycle level); FY2025 provision for credit losses was $154.5M even in a good year
  - Purchase-accounting accretion is a fading tailwind inside current earnings — the FY2025 cash-flow reconciliation shows net accretion of premiums/discounts from acquisition of $139,775,000 boosting FY2025 results
  - Capital-constrained growth — loan growth, the raised $0.50 quarterly dividend (+16.3%, declared for October 1, 2026) and any buybacks compete for the same regulatory capital
  - Rate sensitivity — earnings lean on NIM (net interest income $532.5M in Q2 2026, +14.0% YoY) and on lumpy private-investment gains ($27.1M in Q2 2026, including SpaceX-related fund gains, per the release)
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, with its legs quoted in the Owner's earnings section above.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 14.8%/yr): the growth is merger math, not ongoing dilution — diluted shares were 49,056,956 in FY2024 and 73,670,643 in FY2025 because Heartland was paid for in stock (closed January 31, 2025); the cover-page count since is 75,950,030 as of 2026-07-27 (dcf_numbers.json). SBC is rising with the combined company's scale ($18,694,000 in FY2023, $22,579,000 in FY2024, $37,745,000 in FY2025, financials_history.csv) and is deducted in full in every OE figure. The engine's −5.00% share adjustment applies only to the (null) revenue components and the OE/share CAGR enters unadjusted — neither is used: the scenario starts were set independently, above.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share range over the last 5 years spans >3x): real in the OCF series ($3.72 to $14.28) but it is plumbing volatility, not earnings volatility — diluted EPS (fundamentals.json) ranged only $7.18–$9.29 over FY2023–FY2025. Handled anyway as the flag asks: bear weight widened to 30% and both bear starts are negative.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024: net income $441,243,000 vs OCF $225,286,000): resolved from the FY2025 10-K's cash-flow reconciliation (via search results) — the FY2024 gap is the "Other assets and liabilities, net" line of −$317,217,000 dragging OCF, partly offset by the non-cash addbacks that recur every year (provision for credit losses $61,050,000 and depreciation/amortization $52,771,000 in FY2024). No one-off gain was found in net income; the OCF leg is the distorted one in FY2024, which is what makes FY2024 OE/share of $3.72 a trough artifact rather than an earnings collapse. The structural point stands for the verdict: the provision is a real through-cycle cost that OCF adds back every year ($154,500,000 in FY2025).
- **CAPEX_COVERAGE_LOW** (capex below 50% of D&A in each of the last 3 FY: FY2023 39%, FY2024 38%, FY2025 34%): reconciled — the engine read the whole filed capex. Capex is the single rollup line PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment in every year (capex_provenance in dcf_numbers.json: $23,104,000 in FY2023, $20,009,000 in FY2024, $48,584,000 in FY2025), no intangible purchases are filed in any year (capex_intangibles $0.00 in key_numbers.md for all three years), and ttm.py's three legs carry the same single tag. The low coverage is the denominator: D&A jumped to $141,770,000 in FY2025 from $52,771,000 in FY2024 (financials_history.csv) on amortization of Heartland-acquired intangibles — non-cash amortization that needs no replacement capex — on top of an asset-light bank model whose real investment (loans and securities) is an investing outflow, not capex. The base is not overstated on this leg; capex is ~5% of FY2025 OCF either way.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance): accepted in full. A bank's OCF-based "owner's earnings" are not its distributable cash — loan growth consumes regulatory capital, provisions are a through-cycle cost the OCF line adds back, and bank value is normally judged on ROTCE vs cost of equity and tangible book, none of which this model computes. Confidence is low, and the final tier is softened one notch under the universal rule (see Final verdict).
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY OE/share $12.76 vs 3-yr median $8.83): both are distorted, in opposite directions. The median is the distorted anchor: two of its three years are the pre-Heartland standalone bank, its set contains FY2024's OCF-trough $3.72, and the median value is simply FY2023's $8.83. The latest FY is a clean combined year but its OCF leg is flattered by the provision addback and acquisition accretion. The TTM base ($13.10) is used because its window is fully combined and its total ($1,000,871,000) cross-checks against TTM net income of $942,679,000 (fundamentals.json); the median variant is recorded in the method range below.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.51x): real — a bank's outcome range across a credit cycle IS wide (super-bear IV@15% $51.88 vs super-bull $234.05). Confidence is low (also mandated by the sector flag), and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **BIG_NET_CASH** (net cash is 7.6x normalized OE): an artifact of applying an industrial leverage lens to a bank. The engine's net debt is LongTermDebt of $474,229,000 minus cash of $7,771,973,000 (financials_history.csv); it excludes the deposit base that actually funds the bank (deposits $59.8B at June 30, 2026) and the loan book that backs it. The balance sheet from the filing is: assets $73,094,090,000, liabilities $65,400,522,000, equity $7,693,568,000. The "cash pile" is balance-sheet liquidity a bank must hold, not distributable surplus — it is noted here and given no credit in the verdict.
- **TIER_BAND** (bear-case tier C at 10.05% vs bull-case tier B at 18.35%): the verdict is sized to the bear end — the final tier (D, after the misfit softening) sits below even the bear-case tier.
