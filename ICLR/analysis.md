## What it is
Dublin-based contract research organisation (CRO): one of the two largest global providers of outsourced clinical development to pharma, biotech, medical-device and government sponsors — trial design and management, labs, site networks, data/biostatistics, pharmacovigilance and functional-service-provider (FSP) embedding. Revenue FY2025 $8,251,340,000, nearly flat on FY2024 ($8,188,990,000, +0.8% latest YoY in dcf_numbers.json). Backlog $21.8B at December 31, 2025 under the new backlog policy and $23.4B at June 30, 2026 (company releases). Market cap $13.44B at the $170.18 price (nasdaq_quote_api) — in scope under $40B.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, scale-based
Justification: one of the few CROs that can run a global late-phase programme, mid-trial switching costs, and a $21.8B backlog — but measured returns since the PRA deal sit below the cost of capital, gross margin is thin, sponsors hold the pricing power, and the 2025–26 revenue-recognition restatement damaged trust.
- FOR: scale (top-two global CRO, ~$8.25B revenue, $21.8B backlog at Dec 2025 rising to $23.4B at Jun 2026); mid-trial switching costs are real — replacing a CRO mid-study risks timelines, data continuity and regulatory filings; strategic partnerships and FSP embedding inside large pharma; structurally strong cash conversion (OE positive 5/5 years; FY2025 OCF $1,036M vs capex of only $174M).
- AGAINST: return history — roic_proxy (fundamentals.json) was 25.2% in FY2020 pre-deal, then 2.4% (FY2021), 5.5% (FY2022), 7.0% (FY2023), 7.7% (FY2024) and 3.5% (FY2025) after the $12B PRA Health acquisition; gross margin ~29% for FY2020–24 (29.2 / 27.5 / 28.6 / 29.0 / 28.6 in fundamentals.json) with FY2025 direct costs of $6,075,746,000 rising against nearly flat revenue; cancellations are structural (FY2025 cancellations $2,834M under the new policy; backlog itself was cut $3.9B by a policy change to $21.1B at October 1, 2025); contested oligopoly (IQVIA, Thermo Fisher/PPD, Medpace); the Audit Committee investigation found improper revenue-timing adjustments overstating FY2023 revenue by $65M and FY2024 by $93M, with material weaknesses in internal controls still being remediated through 2026.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection. A moat of 7+ would need ROIC sustained well above the cost of capital through a cycle; ICON's post-2021 history shows the opposite.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: the preliminary default run (median base, default multipliers and probabilities) printed weighted IRR 10.24% and IV@15% $104.13 (Tier C); the recalibrated final run prints 8.03% (Tier D). Both sit far below the $170.18 price — the disagreement is over how overvalued it is, not whether it is.
- What the market is paying for is the adjusted story (FY2025 adjusted EPS $12.53, adjusted EBITDA $1,530.7M at 18.6% of revenue). On filed owner's earnings the cash yield is 6.85% at a business whose revenue is guided down, whose largest recent profit year is two years old, and whose controls are under remediation. Even the super-bull case — 7.9% growth start, OE at 1.15x the median base — returns only 12.71%: at this price there is no scenario in the model that clears Andrew's 15% hurdle.
- Cash generation is genuinely not the problem: TTM OCF of $1,070,040,000 (fundamentals.json) through Q2 2026, buybacks running, backlog rebuilding ($22.7B at Q1 2026, $23.4B at Q2 2026) on book-to-bill above 1.4. If the price fell toward the weighted IV the quality question would be worth re-arguing; at $170 it is a price question, and the price is wrong for a 15% hurdle.
- Balance-sheet risk is modest, not negligible: net debt 2.42x normalised OE, and the PRA goodwill pile is still being written down ($364M in FY2025) — further impairments would hit reported earnings, not the OE base.
