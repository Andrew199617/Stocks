# STZ — Constellation Brands, Inc.
Run 2026-10-12 (engine v4). In scope: ~$21.7B at the engine price ($126.40 × 171.5M H1 FY27 diluted shares; universe screener $20.75B at $122.64).

## What it is
The largest U.S. high-end beer supplier, now essentially a Mexican-import beer company with a small premium wine & spirits rump. Beer (Modelo Especial — the #1 U.S. beer by dollars — Corona Extra, Pacifico, Victoria, Modelo Chelada) is ~94% of H1 FY27 net sales ($4.76B of $5.07B) and effectively all of the profit (beer operating margin ~39%). Constellation owns the U.S. rights to the Modelo/Corona brands and brews them in Mexico (Nava; Veracruz plant starting up early FY28). Wine & Spirits was cut to a premium stub (Kim Crawford, The Prisoner, High West, Mi CAMPO) by the June 2, 2025 sale of the mainstream wine brands (Woodbridge, Meiomi, Cook's, Robert Mondavi Private Selection, SIMI, J. Rogét, plus wineries/vineyards) to The Wine Group for ~$846M cash proceeds, and the January 2025 SVEDKA sale to Sazerac. Post-quarter it bought the SpikedAde spirits-RTD brand for $75M upfront plus up to $278M contingent over five years — small, but its first M&A after a long divestiture run.

Latest earnings — Q2 FY27 (ended Aug 31, 2026; released Oct 6, 2026), read from the release/10-Q, not the headline:
- Reported net sales $2.633B (+6% YoY), reported EPS $3.32 (+25%), comparable EPS $3.74 (+3%) — a beat vs ~$3.55-3.61 consensus, driven by shipments, not end demand.
- Beer net sales +5% on shipments +5.5%, but **depletions (distributor→retailer, the real demand signal) fell 0.6%**: Modelo Especial ~-2%, Corona Extra ~-5%, partly offset by Pacifico ~+19%, Victoria ~+15%, Chelada ~+5%. H1 shipments ran ~8M cases ahead of depletions rebuilding distributor inventories that exited FY26 unusually low — so H1 shipment growth overstates consumption, and H2 loses that tailwind.
- Beer operating margin fell 160bp to 39.0%: lower tariff costs and fixed-cost absorption were more than offset by stepped-up marketing and SG&A. Wine & Spirits sales +17%, depletions +10.2% (Kim Crawford ~+11%, Mi CAMPO ~+51%), swinging to a small operating profit.
- Guidance distinction that matters: **comparable** FY27 EPS was only *reaffirmed* at $11.20-11.90 — flat at the midpoint vs FY26 comparable EPS of $11.82 — while **reported** EPS was *raised* to $11.85-12.55, entirely because fewer comparability items (impairments/restructuring) are expected, not because the operating outlook improved. Organic net sales guide is -1% to +1% (beer -1% to +1%); OCF $2.4-2.5B; capex ~$800M; FCF $1.6-1.7B. Management withdrew its FY28 outlook in April 2026 — visibility is explicitly short.
- Demand context: management has flagged for over a year that high-end beer buy rates decelerated, with Hispanic consumers (an outsized share of Modelo/Corona demand — roughly 40% of beer spend per company commentary vs ~15% for the category) pulling back more than the general market on macro/immigration-enforcement anxiety. Q1 FY27 showed the same pattern (beer sales +2%, depletions -0.3%, Modelo ~-2%, Corona >-5%) as Q2 — this is a trend, not a quarter.

## Owner's earnings
History is only usable from the filing text for share counts — see the data-gap flag below. Conservative OE totals (OCF − capex − SBC, EDGAR 10-K): FY24 $1,447.3M, FY25 $1,865.9M, FY26 $1,726.3M; positive 5 of the last 5 years.
Per-share, using the diluted share counts printed in the 10-K EPS notes (FY24 183.959M, FY25 181.476M, FY26 175.568M):
- FY24: $7.87/share; FY25: $10.28/share; FY26: $9.83/share
- **Normalized base (3-yr median) = $9.83/share** (the median is the FY26 figure itself)
- Maintenance version (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): FY26 $1,984.1M = $11.30/share; FY25 only $295.7M and FY24 $739.0M — those two years are useless as printed because net income absorbs the wine & spirits impairments (below) while the formula only adds back D&A. The conservative OCF-based figure is the cleaner one in both directions: impairments are non-cash and never touch it.
- Capex judgment: a large share of recent capex is *growth*, not maintenance — ~$800M/yr guided, ~$2B of Mexico beer capacity over FY26-28 including Veracruz, vs D&A of ~$420M/yr. Charging all of it (the conservative definition) understates steady-state OE; on a maintenance-capex = D&A basis TTM OE would be ~$12.64/share. I keep the conservative base for the headline (screening consistency) and carry this as a quantified sensitivity in the overlay, not as the base.

**Base used = TTM owner's earnings through Q2 FY27 (Aug 31, 2026), built from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (STALE_FY resolution):**

- TTM revenue on the same math: $9,139.0M − $4,996.0M + $5,065.7M = $9,208.7M (+0.8% vs FY26) — the reported FY26 revenue decline of -10.5% is a divestiture artifact, not demand (see BASIS_BREAK below).
- The TTM base ($10.31) sits only +4.9% above the 3-yr median ($9.83), so the stale-FY fix does not flatter the result. Note the forward tension, honestly: FY27 guidance (OCF $2.4-2.5B − capex ~$800M − SBC ~$70M) implies FY27 OE of only ~$9.2-9.6/share — the TTM base is a peak the guide says will dip before it recovers. That is priced into the bear weighting and the low base-case growth start, not by overriding the filing-derived base.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong brands, concentrated and category-capped
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 6 = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection).
- FOR: (1) Brand equity with proven pricing power — Modelo Especial is the #1 U.S. beer by dollars and the portfolio has taken price/mix for a decade while gaining share (it was again the #1 dollar and volume share gainer in U.S. tracked channels in Q2 FY27, +0.8 share points in beer, even in a down depletion quarter). (2) Exclusive, perpetual U.S. rights to the Modelo/Corona family plus owned Mexican breweries — no competitor can sell the same brands into its market. (3) Structural margin/cost position: ~39% beer operating margins and a focused, premium-only portfolio after the wine exit. (4) Smaller brands are compounding off the same distribution (Pacifico now a top-10 U.S. brand, depletions ~+19-21%; Victoria ~+14-15%).
- AGAINST: (1) Extreme concentration — one category (import beer), one supply country (Mexico: tariff and peso exposure; aluminum-can and potential Mexico tariffs are a recurring cost line, and tariff *recoveries* flattered Q2 wine costs), one demand country (U.S.). (2) Demographic concentration: Hispanic consumers are disproportionately its drinkers, and that cohort's pullback has driven two-plus years of soft depletions — a headwind competitors with broader portfolios dilute. (3) The U.S. beer category itself is in secular volume decline, and younger drinkers are shifting to spirits RTDs, non-alcoholic and other categories — the SpikedAde purchase is an admission the core can't supply growth alone. (4) No owned consumer surface and three-tier distribution dependence; brand ownership outside the U.S. sits with AB InBev. (5) Capital-allocation history (Canopy Growth, mainstream wine bought high and impaired/sold low — ~$3.3B of wine & spirits write-downs ahead of the 2025 sale) argues against paying for management's next growth story in advance.
A 6 respects a real brand/distribution moat with a decade of pricing proof; it withholds 7+ because the moat surrounds a shrinking-volume category and a single demographic engine.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
Final engine flags (2), plus three the engine *could not* fire because its share series is empty — all resolved here:
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended Feb 28, 2026; 7.5 months ago): Resolved with the TTM base built line-by-line from 10-Q YTD figures above ($10.31/share through Aug 31, 2026). The TTM is only +4.9% above the 3-yr median, so staleness was not flattering the FY base; if anything the FY27 OCF guide warns the next two quarters dip below the TTM run rate.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2025)**: Found and explained. FY25 net income was -$81.4M against OCF of $3,152.2M because FY25 absorbed ~$2.8B of non-cash wine & spirits goodwill/trademark impairments plus a ~$478M held-for-sale write-down ahead of the 2025 Wine Divestitures (operating income fell 89% to $354.9M while cash flow rose). The gap is an impairment artifact, not a cash-quality problem — which is exactly why the OCF-based conservative OE is the right base and the NI-based maintenance OE is unusable for FY24-25. No tax-deposit or working-capital one-off of consequence was found in the flagged year.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (manual — engine ratio was null because shares were null)**: Net debt $10,194.1M at FY26 end = **5.8× TTM conservative OE** ($1,768.8M). On the right denominator for a brewer — operating cash flow / EBITDA-scale earnings — it is roughly 3×, investment-grade and being paid down with divestiture proceeds, but the OE-based figure is the honest stress lens for this screen: at bear-case OE (~$1.59B) the load is ~6.4×, which caps buyback flexibility precisely when the bear case would need it. This is a reason the bear book carries 45% combined weight; it does not change the tier, which is already set by the weighted IRR.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT (manual — engine's >60% revenue-jump test does not catch a divestiture shrink)**: The break is the 2025 Wine Divestitures (closed June 2, 2025; ~$142M of divested-brand sales sat in the Mar-Jun 2025 stub alone) plus the SVEDKA sale. Reported revenue fell -10.5% in FY26 and -3% in Q1 FY27 while *organic* sales were roughly flat to +3%. Every growth input in this run is therefore either organic-guided or TTM-computed across the break with the divested stub explicitly noted in guidance — no CAGR that straddles the break is used as printed.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND / share-data gap (manual)**: EDGAR companyfacts returns no WeightedAverageNumberOfDilutedSharesOutstanding series for CIK 16918 (the filer reports weighted shares by class in the filing text; the aggregated facts drop them), so the engine produced no per-share base at all on the preliminary run and would have printed a spurious Tier F. Share counts were taken from the 10-K/10-Q EPS notes instead (FY24 183.959M → H1 FY27 171.503M diluted, ~-2.5%/yr). The tailwind is real, filing-sourced, and is embedded in the per-share scenario starts above rather than double-counted through the engine's (empty) share adjustment.
- Trial-run **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.2× under default offsets) did not survive recalibration: the final spread is 3.19×. Confidence is still capped at medium — by the leverage, the two-quarter depletion trend, and the guided FY27 OE dip — not by the spread.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline: at $126.40 the market is already paying for the recovery. An 8.2% OE yield on a TTM base sounds cheap for a staples compounder, but with depletions negative, organic growth guided flat, and comparable EPS guided flat, the per-share growth on offer is roughly the buyback (~2.5%) plus pricing — mid-single digits at best in the bull case. OE yield + realistic growth = ~10-11% expected return. That is the weighted IRR the model prints, and every defensible input change lands in the same neighborhood: median base ($9.83) instead of TTM ⇒ IRR ~9.7%, IV@15% ~$74; FY27 guided OE (~$9.4) as base ⇒ ~9%; un-recalibrated default growth on the TTM base ⇒ 10.2%. **Method range: high-D to low-C, centered on low-C.** No defensible run gets near 15%.
- The one real upside lever the conservative screen suppresses: growth capex. If Veracruz/Mexico capex rolls off toward D&A (~$420M) after FY28, steady-state OE/share is ~$12.64 on today's TTM cash flows, and the weighted IV@15% scales to ~$95 with an IRR of ~12-13% — still short of the 15% hurdle at $126.40. The capex argument makes STZ *less* overvalued, not cheap.
- What would change the answer: depletions back to sustained positive territory (watch Pacifico/Victoria mix vs Modelo/Corona — the two flagships are still ~-2%/-5%), evidence the Hispanic-consumer pullback is transitory, and the FY28 outlook (currently withdrawn) reinstated with growth in it. Until then the shipment beats are inventory, not demand.
- Margin-of-safety math: the weighted case clears 15% only at **$77.67**; a buyer who also wants protection against the bear case needs the bear IV, **~$62**. At $126.40 you are paying above even the bull-case IV@15% ($103.86) — only the 5%-weighted super bull ($138.52) justifies the price at a 15% discount rate.
