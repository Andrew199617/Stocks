# ROP — Roper Technologies, Inc.
Sweep run, 2026-10-13. In scope (universe market cap $36.0B; ~$36.7B at the run price on the current share count — see balance-sheet note below).

## What it is
A diversified vertical-market software and technology compounder — the "Berkshire for software" model. ~29 niche, category-leading businesses run decentrally in three segments (Q2 2026 revenue mix): **Application Software** ($1.18B/qtr — Deltek government-contractor accounting, Vertafore insurance agency systems, Aderant legal, CentralReach autism-therapy SaaS), **Network Software** ($431M/qtr — DAT freight load board, TransCore tolling heritage via Network segment, MHAudio etc.), and **Technology Enabled Products** ($497M/qtr — Neptune smart water meters, Verathon medical devices). Growth is a flywheel: high-margin recurring cash flow funds acquisitions of mission-critical niche leaders (Procare $1.75B in 2024; CentralReach ~$1.65B and Subsplash ~$800M in 2025), whose margins are then lifted under Roper's operating model. Organic growth is mid-single digits; the rest is bought.

## Moat score: 7/10 — strong, portfolio-built
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 7) = **11.1 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).

**FOR:**
- A portfolio of ~29 niche category leaders selling mission-critical systems of record — Deltek (gold standard for government-contractor project accounting), Vertafore (backbone of the independent insurance agency market), Aderant (legal), DAT (freight marketplace with genuine two-sided network effects). Switching means re-platforming core workflows and retraining staff; retention is cited above 95% and annual price increases stick (adjusted EBITDA margin ~41-42% of revenue).
- Proprietary vertical data assets (DAT load-board data, Neptune's installed metering base) that compound with scale and now feed vertical-specific AI features that raise stickiness further.
- A capital-allocation moat on top of the product moats: three decades of disciplined M&A, a decentralized model that has repeatedly lifted acquired units' margins from the low-20s toward 40%+, and mid-teens FCF/share compounding as the explicit long-term target.
- Diversification: no single vertical, customer, or product drives the result — Application/Network Software plus meters/medical devices across uncorrelated end markets.

**AGAINST:**
- The moats are narrow and per-business, not one wide portfolio moat. Each niche is small enough for a focused entrant, a horizontal platform (Tyler Technologies in public sector, Oracle/SAP at the edges), or an AI-native startup to attack one unit at a time.
- Growth is bought, not just built: organic growth is only ~5-6%, so the compounding story requires a perpetual supply of fairly priced targets. Deal multiples have risen (CentralReach at a high-teens EBITDA multiple), and goodwill/intangibles dominate a balance sheet carrying $19.9B of book equity against a much larger asset base of acquired intangibles — a bad vintage of deals impairs for years.
- AI cuts both ways — management created a centralized "AI strike team" precisely because AI-native tools could lower switching costs or let customers insource workflows that Roper's software currently owns.
- Technology Enabled Products is real hardware (meters, medical devices) with cyclical, capex-bearing economics unlike the software core.
- Leverage (below) is part of the model, not an accident — it narrows the room for a deal mistake.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY2025 ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override above — full filing arithmetic shown in the OE section. The TTM base ($24.63) is *higher* than both the latest-FY ($21.94) and the 3-yr median ($20.81), because H1 2026 OCF grew and the share count fell; using it is the generous, current choice, and the stock still lands in Tier C.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT:** Engine basis (Dec 31, 2025): debt $705.2M current + $8,595.8M long-term − cash $297.4M = **net debt $9,003.6M = 4.0× FY25 OE**. It has since risen: at June 30, 2026 total debt was $11,319.4M ($718.3M + $10,601.1M) against $364.9M cash = **net debt $10,954.5M = 4.4× TTM OE**, because H1 2026 buybacks ($2,724.1M for 7.865M shares) were substantially debt-funded. Mitigants, stated plainly: OE here is a *levered* flow (US-GAAP operating cash flow is already after interest paid), revenue is predominantly recurring, and Roper expects ~$1.3B pre-tax (~$1.1B after tax) of cash proceeds from Indicor's pending instrumentation-business sale, which would cut net debt by ~10%. Resolution: leverage is folded into the verdict by keeping default bear weights (35% combined bear/super-bear), capping confidence at medium, and noting that in the super-bear case the equity sits behind ~$11.3B of debt against a stalled OE stream — that is exactly the $134 IV scenario.
- **OE_VOLATILE (OE/share range >3× over 5 yrs):** Resolved — the volatility is a divestiture artifact, not operating cyclicality. The trough is 2022: OE/share printed $5.77 because operating cash flow collapsed to $734.6M in the year Roper closed the Indicor majority sale (Nov 22, 2022: 51% to CD&R for ~$2,604M cash; pretax gain $2,046.0M, which is also why 2022 net income printed $4,544.7M against operating income of $1,524.5M) and the TransCore discontinued-operations disposition (net gain on disposition of discontinued operations of $1,717.5M in 2022). Cash taxes on those gains ran through operating cash flow (the company later disclosed $435M of divestiture-related taxes paid in the twelve months through Q3 2023 alone), crushing 2022 OE while the underlying businesses grew. Ex-2022, OE/share is a steady staircase: $17.74 (2021) → $17.80 (2023) → $20.81 (2024) → $21.94 (2025). The normalized/TTM bases handle the artifact; no extra bear weight was added for "cyclicality" that isn't there. (2019's net income of $1,767.9M exceeding operating income of $1,328.3M is the same signature — divestiture gains during the industrial-portfolio exit that culminated in Indicor.)
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (preliminary run only, 4.4×):** Narrowed to 3.9× in the final run by the recalibrated growth starts, so the flag no longer fires — but 3.9× is still wide in substance (it reflects genuine uncertainty about acquisition-dependent growth, not a modeling artifact). Confidence is therefore **medium**, not high.

## Thoughts / overlay
- Quality is not the debate. This is one of the best capital-allocation machines in the mid-cap universe: two OE definitions agree within 1%, OE/share has compounded double digits, retention and margins are elite, and management just demonstrated conviction by repurchasing 9.0M shares (~8% of the count) for $3.2B over three quarters at an average price in the mid-$300s — rolling the share count back to 2013 levels (shares outstanding 98.90M at July 28, 2026 vs 108.2M FY25 diluted average). Note the engine's market-cap estimate ($40.2B) uses the stale FY share count; on the current count the market cap is ~$36.7B, inside the sweep's <$40B scope.
- The debate is price and the growth the price assumes. At $371 the market is paying 15.1× TTM OE for a business whose *organic* growth is 5-6% — the rest of the per-share compounding must be bought, year after year, at today's deal multiples, now with 4.4× leverage on the balance sheet. The DCF says that package is worth ~13%, not 15%+.
- Method range: every defensible input set lands in Tier C — the preliminary run (median base $20.81, engine growth, moat 5) gave 12.1%; this final run (TTM base, recalibrated growth, moat 7) gives 12.8%; haircutting the base for capitalized software gives ~12.5%; crediting the engine's unadjusted growth starts would push toward ~14% but requires believing a 24.6% super-bull start that no print supports. Range ≈ 12-14%, all C. Unlike the APP pilot, the tier is not assumption-fragile.
- Hurdle math: the stock clears a 15% IRR at or below the weighted IV@15% of **~$299**. With a margin of safety against the bear case (IV $204), a buyer wanting insurance rather than just the weighted expectation needs **~$240-250**. Management's own buyback average ($346.34 in H1 2026) sits between the two — inside the "fair" zone, above the "clears 15%" zone.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: C** (tier by numbers C; no adjustment — weighted IRR 12.8%, and even the super-bull scenario only reaches 19.1%)
- Confidence: **Medium** (high on OE quality and the TTM base — both OE definitions agree and the TTM flow is free of the Indicor gain; capped at medium by 4.4× leverage and a still-wide 3.9× scenario spread)
- One-liner: Elite vertical-software compounder, moat 7/10 — but at $371 it is priced for the bull case: weighted IRR 12.8% (Tier C) on TTM OE of $24.63/share; it clears the 15% hurdle only below ~$299, and with a real margin of safety nearer ~$240-250.
