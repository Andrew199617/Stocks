# FIS — Fidelity National Information Services, Inc.
Engine 5.4.0 run, 2026-10-16. All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json or fundamentals.json — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Core banking, payments and capital-markets processing for financial institutions — mission-critical, multi-year, mostly recurring revenue. The company is mid portfolio-reset: it sold a 55% stake in Worldpay Merchant Solutions in February 2024, then on January 9, 2026 closed a linked swap — acquiring Global Payments' Issuer Solutions business (formerly TSYS, now FIS Total Issuing Solutions; $13.5B enterprise value, ~$7.7B cash plus its Worldpay stake) and selling its remaining 45% Worldpay stake to Global Payments (Worldpay at a $24.25B enterprise valuation). FIS is now the world's largest issuer processor (40B+ transactions/yr, clients in 75+ countries per the closing release) with no merchant-acquiring exposure. In scope: market cap $18.866B at $35.94 (key_numbers.md).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow, not wide
Justification: real switching costs in mission-critical core banking and issuer processing, but a decade of ROIC proxy below the cost of capital proves the stickiness has not converted into excess returns. ROIC proxy (fundamentals.json, roic_proxy series): 4.10% (2016), 6.01% (2017), 6.40% (2018), 1.07% (2019), 0.52% (2020), 1.25% (2021), 1.98% (2022), 2.93% (2023), 4.71% (2024), 5.11% (2025) — improving, still not above a plausible cost of capital in any of the last seven years. Gross margin: 33.2% (2016), 35.9% (2021), 37.2% (2023), 37.6% (2024), 36.9% (2025) — mid-30s and roughly flat, not the expanding margin of a pricing-power compounder.
- FOR: mission-critical core banking and issuer processing with high switching costs and multi-year recurring contracts; scale after the Issuer Solutions deal (largest global issuer processor, 40B+ transactions/yr, 75+ countries); recurring-revenue base (pro forma recurring revenue growth 4.8% in Q1 2026); integrated banking + issuing stack for large and super-regional banks smaller rivals cannot match.
- AGAINST: ROIC proxy below cost of capital for a decade (0.5–6.4%, 5.11% in 2025); Fiserv and Jack Henry competition (Jack Henry holds the community-bank niche at ~98% retention); bank consolidation shrinks the client base and cloud-native cores lower switching barriers; serial large M&A (Worldpay 2019, largely written down and exited 2024–26) destroyed capital and left high leverage.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: on the normalized (median) base of $4.33/share with the preliminary default scenarios, the engine printed a weighted IRR of 16.81% and IV@15% of $41.42 — also Tier B. On the TTM base with recalibrated scenarios it prints 18.62% / $46.72. Both defensible runs land in B; the disagreement is about how much headroom, not the tier. The bear end (C in both framings) is the sizing anchor.
- Quality is the debate, and the ROIC record settles it downward: this is a sticky, cash-generative processor (OE yield 14.19% at this price) that has earned below its cost of capital for a decade because it keeps overpaying for acquisitions. The Issuer deal is the third act of the same play — strategically coherent (issuing fits the banking base better than merchant acquiring did), but financed with debt at exactly the moment guidance was cut.
- The price already reflects a lot of failure: down ~46% YTD per market data, ~6x guided adjusted EPS of $6.15–6.24 — but adjusted EPS strips out the amortization and integration costs of the deal that produced the growth, and enterprise value is roughly double the market cap once the post-deal debt is counted. Cheap on equity multiples, not cheap on the whole capital structure.
- What would change the verdict: two clean post-deal years of OCF on the combined base (kills the hybrid-TTM problem), visible debt paydown from the claimed incremental free cash flow ($500M in 2026, $700M by 2028 per the closing release), and ROIC proxy finally clearing ~8%.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: B** (weighted IRR 18.62% on the TTM base; bear C 14.56% → bull A 22.10%; method range across the preliminary and final runs stays inside B)
- Confidence: **Low** (hybrid pre/post-deal TTM base, post-deal leverage not in the engine's net-debt figure, tier band spans C to A, 2026 guidance already cut once)
- One-liner: Sticky but low-return processor, moat 5/10 — clears 15% on the TTM base (18.6% weighted, IV $46.72 vs $35.94) but the band runs C to A, leverage stepped up again for Issuer Solutions, and 2026 pro forma guidance was already cut; size it like the bear end (C, 14.6%), not the weighted B.
