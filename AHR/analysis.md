## What it is
Self-managed healthcare REIT (NYSE-listed Feb 2024; formerly Griffin-American Healthcare REIT IV) operating 337 buildings / ~22,162,000 sq ft at Dec 31, 2025 through four segments: integrated senior health campuses (ISHC, operated via Trilogy under RIDEA), senior housing operating properties (SHOP, also RIDEA-operated), outpatient medical (OM) buildings, and triple-net leased properties. Because ISHC/SHOP are operated, not just leased, AHR captures resident-fee operating upside directly — and carries the labor and reimbursement exposure that comes with it. Total revenue FY2025 was $2,260,123,000 (fundamentals.json, filer-total basis; the engine's revenue line is the resident-fees component only, $2,094,506,000). Quarterly distribution $0.25/share (DPS TTM $1.00; dividends paid TTM $178,528,000, fundamentals.json).

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-moderate
Justification: the RIDEA operating platform, the demographic tailwind and record-low senior-housing new supply give real current pricing power, but the assets are replicable with capital, there is no durable lock-in beyond leases, measured ROIC is falling, and growth must be continuously bought with dilutive equity issuance.
- **Evidence (fundamentals.json):** roic_proxy — FY2023 19.8% → FY2024 13.2% → FY2025 10.2%, a falling series, and flattering at that: its denominator (equity + debt − cash) omits the debt the engine could not tag (see DEBT_TAGS_BLANK), so true returns on invested capital are lower. Gross margin on the filer-total revenue basis: 19.5% (FY2023) → 20.1% (FY2024) → 20.6% (FY2025) — thin and only modestly rising, as expected for operated senior housing where property operating expenses sit in COGS; operating margin 18.4% in FY2025. A 4 does not need the written exception a 7+ would: the measured record does not show returns durably well above the cost of capital.
- FOR: RIDEA operating platform (ISHC + SHOP) captures operating upside directly, not just rent — Q2 2026 same-store NOI +16.1% ISHC / +20.5% SHOP, tenth straight double-digit quarter; structural supply/demand — senior-housing new supply at generational lows while the 80+ cohort grows; scale and operator network built over ~20 years (Trilogy platform), 337 buildings / ~22.2M sq ft, sourcing off-market deals; deep access to equity capital at premium prices keeps leverage low — net debt / annualized adjusted EBITDA 2.5x at Q2 2026, total consolidated indebtedness $1.4B.
- AGAINST: real estate is replicable with capital — no network effects, no lock-in beyond leases; measured returns falling (ROIC proxy above); growth is bought with continuous equity issuance — weighted diluted shares +27.7% in FY2025, 217,998,775 shares outstanding by Aug 2026, ~12.2M forward-sale shares still unsettled, so per-share economics lag portfolio growth; RIDEA operating exposure — labor costs, operator concentration (Trilogy in ISHC), Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement risk in skilled nursing; short listed history (NYSE Feb 2024), no through-cycle public record, and CEO Danny Prosky on medical leave during 2026 per the FY2025 10-K (Jeff Hanson acting as Chairman and CEO in the Q2 2026 release).
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range does not save the price. On the normalized median base ($0.51/share) the model would value AHR lower still; on the latest FY ($1.08) lower than the TTM base; the TTM base ($1.27, 2.39% OE yield at $53.23) is the most generous defensible base, and it still produces a 5.78% weighted IRR with a super-bull ceiling of 9.09%. The verdict is not base-sensitive: at $53.23 the market is paying for the NFFO story (guided FY2026 NFFO/share $2.15–$2.19, Q2 2026 release), not for owner's earnings after real capex.
- What the screen cannot see, honestly stated: REIT value here is an asset/NAV and FFO-multiple question. The portfolio is genuinely well-positioned for the senior-housing cycle (tenth straight double-digit same-store NOI quarter; SHOP +20.5% in Q2 2026; supply at generational lows), leverage is low (2.5x), and management has repeatedly raised guidance in 2026. None of that is in dispute — it is a quality growth story. The DCF's narrow point is that after ~$100–129M/yr of developments and capital expenditures and a share count that has roughly tripled since FY2023 and is still rising (12,246,596 forward shares unsettled at 2026-08-06), the owner's-earnings stream per share supports a value near $13, not $53.
- Risks to the *short* side of this verdict (i.e., what would make the D too harsh): developments capex is partly growth spend — if development deliveries earn high yields on cost, charging it all against current OE understates run-rate OE; equity issued at premium prices to buy assets below replacement cost can be per-share accretive in NAV terms even while OE/share lags; and same-store NOI compounding at 11–13% for several more years would push OE/share toward the bull path. All three are bull-case arguments, and the bull case still prints 6.96%.
- Hurdle-clearing prices (dcf_numbers.json hurdle_prices): the shares clear 15% at ~$13.23, 20% at ~$9.24, 25% at ~$7.06 — i.e., the price would have to fall ~75% to clear Andrew's 15% hurdle on these inputs.
