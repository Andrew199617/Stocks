## What it is
Operator of 14 airports under long-term concessions: 12 in Mexico's Pacific region (Guadalajara, Tijuana, Puerto Vallarta, San José del Cabo/Los Cabos, Bajío, Hermosillo, Mexicali, Los Mochis, La Paz, Manzanillo, Morelia, Aguascalientes) under 50-year concessions to 2048, and 2 in Jamaica (Montego Bay since 2015, Kingston since 2019). Revenue is aeronautical (regulated passenger/workload fees), non-aeronautical (retail, parking, cargo — including the Guadalajara World Trade Center bonded-warehouse business consolidated July 2024), plus IFRIC-12 construction revenue on concession improvements (a zero-margin pass-through: Ps.6,832,541 thousand of FY2024's Ps.33,614,374 thousand total revenue in the FY2025 20-F's selected-data table). Maximum tariffs and the mandated investment program are set in five-year Master Development Program (MDP) reviews; tariffs are adjusted for Mexican PPI inside each period. The 2025–2029 review raised weighted average maximum tariffs 18.1% in 2025 and committed Ps.43,185 million of investment at the Mexican airports over the period (company reporting / FY2025 20-F).

## MOAT: 7/10 — strong regulated concession monopoly, finite life
- FOR: sole-operator concessions — 12 Mexican airports to 2048 and 2 Jamaican airports, no competing airport in any catchment; regulated pricing power proven in the 2025–2029 reset (maximum tariffs +18.1% in 2025, PPI indexation inside the period); ROIC well above the cost of capital through a cycle — fundamentals.json roic_proxy 13.5% (FY2015), 26.4% (FY2018), 30.1% (FY2019), 17.8% in the COVID trough (FY2020), 42.1% (FY2021), 64.8% (FY2022), 49.5% (FY2023), 37.7% (FY2024), ROE 36–46% FY2022–24; EBITDA margin ex-IFRIC-12 67.1% in 2Q25 company reporting (66.8% 2Q24); non-aeronautical revenue growing faster than aeronautical and less traffic-sensitive.
- AGAINST: finite concessions — Mexican concessions expire 2048 and assets revert, so the model's perpetuity terminal value assumes a renewal no filing grants; the regulator sets both the tariff ceiling and the mandated capex (Ps.43,185m committed 2025–2029; the new bases add a claw-back on excess workload units); traffic cyclicality (Mexican workload units +10.5% in 2023, −1.7% in 2024, +3.7% in 2025 per the FY2025 20-F); MXN earnings against a USD price (the 2024 period-end ECB rate was ~18% below 2023's, mechanically cutting USD OE); gross margin is not presented in the filing (no cost-of-sales split), so the moat case rests on ROIC/ROE and the EBITDA margin, not on a gross-margin series.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## Thoughts / verdict
This is a genuinely good asset — a regulated monopoly with 37–65% recent ROIC, a 67% EBITDA margin and a just-reset tariff — priced as if the capex that sustains it were optional. It is not: the MDP program is mandatory, the 2025–2029 cycle is bigger than the last (Ps.43,185m committed; FY2025 already spent Ps.12,397m), and FY2025 filing-basis OE/share fell ~34% in MXN on exactly that. At $203.90 the ADS offers a 2.0% normalized OE yield and a 5.56% weighted IRR; even the model's un-recalibrated, blend-driven run (weighted IRR 7.44%) and the maintenance-OE method range stay far below the 15% hurdle. The finite 2048 concession life cuts against the perpetuity terminal value the model grants it, a further reason not to stretch for the price.

**Verdict: Tier D (by numbers D, band D→D, no override). Clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only below ~$45.00 (the weighted IV@15%); the Tier A / S prices are $30.98 / $23.40.** Confidence low — data staleness (companyfacts has not ingested FY2025), FX conversion, and an override-built (but filing-sourced) base — but the verdict's direction is robust: every scenario, every base definition, and the un-recalibrated run all land in Tier D.
