## What it is
Third-largest US convenience-store chain and fifth-largest pizza chain: 2,944 stores across 19 states at FY end (April 30, 2026), concentrated in small Midwestern/Southern towns — most stores sit in communities too small to support competing national chains or QSRs. Revenue is mostly fuel (a low-margin traffic driver); the profit engine is inside sales — prepared food and dispensed beverages (pizza, sandwiches, wings) plus grocery/general merchandise. Store base grows by construction plus acquisitions (Fikes/CEFCO, closed late 2024, still integrating); the June 2026 three-year plan targets at least 400 more stores through FY2029.

## MOAT: 6/10 — moderate, real but location-based
Justification: rural store density and the prepared-food program are genuine, compounding advantages with proven inside margins, but there are no switching costs, fuel is a commodity priced off the street, and the model must be re-earned store by store against dollar stores, QSRs, and larger c-store consolidators.
- FOR: rural/small-town density that national rivals can't economically replicate one town at a time (often the only pizza/food option locally); prepared-food brand (made-from-scratch pizza, 59.3% Q1 FY27 segment margin) that turns a commodity fuel stop into a food destination; scale — ~2,950 stores, own distribution centers, private brand, and a 10M+ member rewards program; long record of compounding through cycles, OE positive 5/5 years; owns much of its real estate, supporting the balance sheet.
- AGAINST: zero customer switching costs — loyalty is habit and location, not lock-in; fuel is ~60% of revenue at commodity margins set by competitors' street prices, and fuel margin (the recent earnings driver) is cyclical and outside management's control; long-run EV adoption erodes the fuel-traffic hook; labor and credit-card fee inflation pressures store opex (Q1 FY27 opex +8%); acquisition-led growth (Fikes/CEFCO) carries integration and overpayment risk and added the current debt load; larger consolidators and dollar/QSR chains contest the same small-town wallet.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income vs OCF differ by >40% in 2024, 2025, 2026): structural, not a one-off. This is a depreciation-heavy store base: D&A was $349,797,000 (FY2024), $403,647,000 (FY2025), $449,958,000 (FY2026) against net income of $501,972,000 / $546,520,000 / $714,448,000 and OCF of $892,953,000 / $1,090,854,000 / $1,377,540,000 (all from financials_history.csv). The dominant NI→OCF bridge is the non-cash D&A add-back, with SBC ($41,379,000 / $47,732,000 / $63,407,000) a smaller add-back — no tax-deposit, impairment, or working-capital one-off is needed to explain the gap, and the pattern is stable across all three years. The conservative OE series starts from OCF and charges full capex, so the divergence does not inflate the base; if anything the maintenance definition ($802,099,000 in FY2026) shows the same picture from the NI side.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 3.56x normalized OE): net debt $1,908,603,000, taken on largely to fund the Fikes/CEFCO acquisition. Folded into the verdict two ways: (1) the bear/super-bear OE multipliers (0.90/0.75) are where leverage does its damage — a fuel-margin normalization cuts OE while the debt and the ~120-store/yr capex program remain, which is why those cases sit at 3.8-4.6% IRR; (2) it caps the tier interpretation — this is not distress (OCF $1,377,540,000 covers the load, and the debt is backed by owned real estate), but there is no balance-sheet cushion to argue the multiple is safe. No tier adjustment beyond the numbers; the leverage is a reason the D is a hard D, not a near-miss.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (preliminary run, 4.44x): the final recalibrated run's spread is 3.71x (super-bull IV@15% $268.31 vs super-bear $72.37) — still wide, driven by genuine fuel-margin uncertainty rather than model noise. Confidence is therefore medium, not high, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a quality-versus-price result, not a quality problem. Casey's is a well-run rural compounder with a real food-led moat — and at $664.19 the market is already paying for the fuel-margin windfall as if it were permanent, plus the full 400-store plan, plus margin expansion. The OE yield on the normalized base is 2.16%; no scenario in the grid, including super-bull at a 16% growth start and a 1.25 OE multiplier, produces an IRR above 8.08%.
- The honest range across defensible inputs is narrow in tier terms: default-offset run 6.51% weighted IRR, recalibrated run 5.66% — Tier D both ways. The debate is only about how far below the hurdle it sits.
- What would change the answer: fuel margins structurally resetting higher for years (possible — industry capacity discipline is real — but that is a commodity bet, not an underwriting), or price. The price that clears the 15% hurdle on the weighted case is the weighted IV@15% itself, $155.81; even the super-bull IV@15% is $268.31. A margin-of-safety entry would need to sit below $155.81 — roughly three-quarters below the current price.
- Watch items: fuel margin per gallon (the whole thesis), inside SSS trajectory (decelerating: +4.3% → +3.2%), Fikes integration, and whether the expanded $1.0B buyback authorization gets used aggressively at prices this far above IV (which would destroy, not create, per-share value).
