## What it is
Autonomous trucking: the Aurora Driver (hardware + software stack) sold mainly as Driver-as-a-Service per mile, integrated factory-side by PACCAR (Peterbilt/Kenworth) and Volvo, with an asset-light model where customers own the trucks. First commercial driverless operations on U.S. public roads began on Dallas–Houston in spring 2025; by the September 23, 2026 Investor Day the company reported more than 500,000 driverless miles, targets exiting 2026 with 200 driverless trucks and more than 1,000 at end-2027, guides FY2026 revenue of $14–16M (~400% growth at the midpoint, back-loaded to Q4), and frames a 2030 vision of 30,000+ trucks, more than $5B revenue and ~60% gross margin, with positive free cash flow targeted in 2028. Today it is pre-commercial in every financial sense: Q2 2026 revenue was $2M against a $266M operating loss.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak, a first-mover lead that has not yet become a franchise
Justification: first-mover commercial lead, a clean safety record (500,000+ driverless miles, zero Aurora-attributed collisions), and factory integration with PACCAR and Volvo are real assets, but the fleet is ~200 trucks targeted by end-2026 so no scale/network effect exists, funded rivals are deploying (Volvo also partners with Waabi), Aurora owns neither trucks nor freight demand, and the measured economics are deeply negative — roic_proxy from fundamentals.json was −33.3% (2021), −96.1% (2022), −44.5% (2023), −37.3% (2024) and −37.1% (2025), with gross margin unfiled in most pre-revenue years and −466.7% in FY2025 (gross profit −$14,000,000 on $3,000,000 revenue). A moat of 7+ needs ROIC well above the cost of capital through a cycle; Aurora has never printed a positive year.
- FOR: First commercial driverless trucking on US public roads (Dallas–Houston, 2025); 500,000+ driverless miles with zero Aurora-attributed collisions; factory OEM integration with PACCAR (Peterbilt/Kenworth) and Volvo — together roughly half the US Class 8 market; Aurora Driver data flywheel — every driverless mile trains a system later rivals lack; customer/partner web: Hirschbach (500-truck Driver-as-a-Service plan from 2027), McLane, Schneider, Uber Freight, FedEx.
- AGAINST: Fleet still tiny (200 trucks targeted by end-2026) — no scale or network effect yet; funded rivals deploying: Kodiak, Waabi (also a Volvo partner), PlusAI, Tesla; owns neither the trucks nor the freight demand — customers can switch autonomy suppliers; no pricing power or returns proven (ROIC proxy negative every year since 2021, FY2025 gross margin −466.7%); continuous dilution funds the buildout — share count +17.2%/yr, SBC $205M TTM and rising.
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years — moot here, since there is no positive base OE to project.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a venture-stage autonomy bet trading at $8.928B. The OE-DCF has nothing to grip: five straight years of negative owner's earnings on both definitions, every base at −$0.44 to −$0.49/share, and TTM operating cash burn of $679,000,000 against TTM revenue of $5,000,000.
- The bull case is not a DCF case: if the fleet scales 200 → 1,000+ → 30,000 trucks and per-mile Driver-as-a-Service economics reach the guided ~60% gross margin, today's financials are irrelevant. That outcome is binary on second-generation hardware cost, OEM production cadence, regulatory breadth, safety record survival — and on capital markets staying open for the ~5–6 quarters of runway left before the 2027–2028 revenue ramp.
- There is no hurdle-clearing price to quote: with negative owner's earnings at every base, no positive price produces a 15% OE-implied return. The 15% question only becomes askable after the company prints its first positive-OE year; until then any entry price is paying for the option, not the cash flows.
- Sizing: the F is a statement about the screen, not a short thesis — pre-revenue compounders fail this screen by construction. But on Andrew's hurdles there is nothing here to underwrite today.
