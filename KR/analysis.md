# KR — The Kroger Co.
Run 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: $36.40B at universe build ($61.64, CIK 56873); engine price $62.56 × 655M latest-FY diluted shares = ~$41.0B market cap — it has drifted over the $40B line since the universe was built, noted, not re-scoped (same treatment as CMG/YUM).

## What it is
The largest pure-play U.S. supermarket operator: ~2,700 stores under banners including Kroger, Ralphs, Fry's, Harris Teeter, Fred Meyer and King Soopers, ~$148B of annual sales, plus fuel centers, pharmacies, private-label "Our Brands," and the Kroger Precision Marketing retail-media business. It is the #2 U.S. grocer behind Walmart — a combined Kroger+Albertsons would have held only ~11% U.S. grocery share vs Walmart's ~17%, and that merger was blocked by two courts in Dec 2024 and terminated. Q2 FY2026 prints (quarter ended Aug 15, 2026): sales +2.0% to $34.62B, identical sales ex-fuel +0.2% (vs +3.4% a year ago), adjusted EPS $1.09 (+4.8%), e-commerce +20%, Precision Marketing profit +24%. Management cut FY2026 identical-sales guidance to +0.2–0.8% (from +1–2%) while holding adjusted EPS guidance at $5.10–5.30, FIFO operating profit $5.0–5.2B, FCF $2.7–2.9B and capex $3.8–4.0B.

## Owner's earnings
History (SEC EDGAR 10-Ks, conservative = OCF − capex − SBC): OE positive 5 of the last 5 years. OE/share by fiscal year (ending Jan/Feb): $3.74 (FY2023, ended 2024-02-03) → $2.22 (FY2024) → $5.04 (FY2025, ended 2026-01-31). The series is volatile because OCF swings with working capital and non-cash charges are added back (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE below) — exactly why v4 normalizes the base.
- FY2025 conservative OE: OCF $7,311M − capex $3,855M − SBC $157M = **$3,299M = $5.04/share**. SBC is small (2.1% of OCF) and charged in full.
- Maintenance version FY2025 (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): $2,065M = $3.15/share — materially *below* the conservative figure, and the maintenance series has fallen three years running ($2,702M → $2,219M → $2,065M). That is a caution against treating the FY2025 conservative spike as run-rate earning power, and it supports the lower TTM base below.
- **Base used = TTM owner's earnings through Q2 FY2026, built from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures (STALE_FY resolution):**
  - OCF TTM = FY2025 $7,311M − H1 FY2025 $3,688M + H1 FY2026 $3,085M = $6,708M
  - Capex TTM = $3,855M − $1,968M + $2,437M = $4,324M
  - SBC TTM = $157M − $83M + $101M = $175M
  - **Conservative OE TTM = $6,708M − $4,324M − $175M = $2,209M = $3.61/share** on 612M H1 FY2026 diluted shares (10-Q YTD weighted average). Used via `--base-oe-ps 3.6095`.
- The TTM base is −3.5% below the 3-yr median ($3.74) and −28% below the latest FY ($5.04). I use the TTM: it is filing-derived and current, the FY2025 figure is flattered by the OCF spike in the impairment year, and the maintenance definition ($3.15/share for FY2025) brackets the TTM from below. The verdict is D on every base (see method range), so the choice is not load-bearing. Note the TTM deterioration is real, not a seasonal artifact: H1 capex ran $2,437M vs $1,968M a year ago while H1 OCF fell $3,688M → $3,085M, and full-year FCF guidance ($2.7–2.9B) implies no second-half OE surge.

## Moat score: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, local scale without pricing power
CAP = 2 + 1.3 × 4 = **7.2 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Local density and share: #1 or #2 share in most of its major markets, ~2,700 stores plus distribution built for fresh/food — procurement and logistics scale no regional chain matches. (2) Private label: Our Brands gives margin and a value alternative that keeps price-sensitive shoppers in-store. (3) Loyalty/first-party data at national scale feeding Kroger Precision Marketing — retail-media profit grew +24% in Q2 FY2026, a genuinely higher-margin, growing profit pool. (4) Pharmacy + fuel drive trip frequency and basket attachment. (5) Defensive demand: food-at-home sales do not collapse in recessions; OE was positive 5/5 years including the merger/impairment turmoil.
- **AGAINST:** (1) No structural pricing power: Walmart is larger, is the price leader, and is still gaining; Costco, Aldi/Lidl hard discount, Dollar General and Amazon all attack the same basket — shoppers face zero switching costs and routinely split trips. Q2 identical sales of +0.2% and a guidance cut to +0.2–0.8% are what that looks like in print. (2) Razor-thin economics: ~1–3% operating margins leave no room for a price war or union labor step-ups; shrink and transport costs already pressured gross margin in Q2. (3) The scale step-change was denied: the Albertsons merger — the one move that would have narrowed the Walmart gap — was blocked and terminated in Dec 2024. (4) The owned digital moat failed: the Ocado automated-fulfillment network took a $2.5B impairment (below) and Kroger pivoted to a hybrid model leaning on Instacart/DoorDash/Uber Eats — partners available to every rival. (5) Leverage and lease intensity (below) limit how hard it can invest in price.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY (FY2025 ended 2026-01-31, 8.4 months before the run):** Resolved with the TTM override above — OCF/capex/SBC rebuilt line-by-line from 10-Q YTD figures through Aug 15, 2026. TTM OE/share $3.61 vs the $3.74 median / $5.04 latest FY. Staleness was flattering the stock, not hiding value: the current-year business is earning *less* per the filings (OCF down, capex up in H1).
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE (not flagged by the engine, resolved anyway because latest $5.04 vs median $3.74 is a 35% gap):** The latest year is the distorted one — FY2025 OCF of $7,311M is the high of the decade and coincides with the non-cash impairment add-back and favorable working capital, while the maintenance OE definition for the same year is only $3.15/share and falling. The TTM ($3.61) sits between the median and the maintenance figure and is the defensible base.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2024, 2026 in the engine's fiscal labels):** Both gaps are identified one-offs, and both resolve *in favor of trusting OCF-based OE over GAAP net income*. FY ended 2024-02-03: NI $2,164M vs OCF $6,788M — D&A $3,125M plus the $1,413M opioid-settlement charge recognized in Q2 FY2023 (non-cash at recognition; up to $1.2B + $36M abatement + ~$177M fees payable over 11/6 years) depress NI while OCF adds them back. FY ended 2026-01-31: NI $1,016M vs OCF $7,311M — D&A $3,332M plus the $2,497M automated-fulfillment impairment and related charges ($1,908M net of tax, incl. a ~$350M cash termination payment to Ocado, split $246M financing / $104M operating) explain essentially the whole gap. GAAP EPS of $1.54 for FY2025 vs adjusted $4.85 is the same story. OE is clean of both charges except the small cash pieces that actually left through operating cash flow.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (net debt $12,541M = 5.1× normalized OE on the preliminary run, 5.3× on final diagnostics; 5.7× TTM OE of $2,209M):** Debt $1,366M current + $14,509M long-term − cash $3,334M, before operating/finance leases, which for a ~2,700-store grocer are an additional large fixed obligation the engine's net-debt figure does not capture. Mitigants, honestly stated: OE here is post-interest (OCF-based), so the DCF does not double-count the debt; grocery cash flows are defensive; adjusted FIFO operating profit is ~$5B. But at >5× the (thin, post-capex) OE stream, a price war or margin squeeze hits equity first, and the aggressive buyback (below) is being run *on top of* this leverage rather than to reduce it. This is a core reason the bear book carries 40% combined weight.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND (share count shrinking 3.4%/yr on the engine's window):** Real and accelerating — diluted shares 720M (FY2024) → 655M (FY2025) → 612M H1 FY2026 weighted (608M in Q2 alone). The engine already credits +3.41pp to per-share growth for it, and my scenario starts are per-share, so no double-count is added. Two cautions: (1) the recent pace is far above the 3.4%/yr credited — if it slows, the base +4.7% start is too high, since underlying revenue growth is ~0–2%; (2) buybacks at $62+ for a stock the model values at ~$31 destroy per-share value even as they lift per-share OE growth optics.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (fired on the preliminary run at 4.5×; final recalibrated spread 3.3×, flag no longer fires):** Still wide in absolute terms — it reflects genuine forks (price war/leverage vs media-led margin mix + buyback compounding). Confidence is capped at medium regardless, per the v4 rule.
- Red flags checked: dilution/SBC — none (SBC $175M TTM, 2.6% of OCF; count shrinking). Customer concentration — none (mass-market grocery). Going concern — none. Litigation — two live items, neither in the OE base: Albertsons is suing over the terminated merger, seeking the $600M termination fee plus billions in damages (Kroger calls the claims baseless; outcome binary and unpriced in the scenarios — a loss would be a bear-case accelerant, not a base-case input); the opioid settlement is quantified above and is a real ~11-year cash drag already flowing through OCF. One-off gains — none in TTM OE (the Vitacost sale and fulfillment-center exits noted in Q2 disclosures are small vs the $2,209M base). Basis breaks — none in the revenue/OE history used; the Ocado pivot is forward-looking and is carried in the moat AGAINST section.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is narrow and entirely in D.** Preliminary run (3-yr-median base $3.74, engine defaults): weighted IRR 9.7%, IV@15% $35.96. Final run (TTM base $3.61, recalibrated tails, bear-skewed probabilities): weighted IRR 8.8%, IV@15% $31.04. Even cherry-picking the distorted latest-FY base ($5.04) at the final scenario settings would scale IV@15% only to roughly the low-$40s — still Tier D and still below price. Every defensible input set lands at **~9–10% weighted IRR — Tier D on all of them.**
- The debate is price vs a genuinely thin cash stream, not solvency: Kroger is a durable, defensive operator, but at $62.56 the buyer pays 17.3× TTM owner's earnings (5.8% OE yield) for a business growing revenue ~0–2%, guiding identical sales to +0.2–0.8%, and carrying >5× net debt/OE plus leases. OE yield (5.8%) + realistic per-share growth (~4–5%, mostly buyback) ≈ 10–11% gross before the fade and leverage haircuts — the model's 8.8% is what survives them. Even the super-bull case (12% growth start, OE ×1.25) only reaches a 13.2% IRR / $53.01 IV — below today's price at a 15% discount rate. There is no honest version of this model where $62.56 clears 15%.
- What could change the picture: identical sales actually re-accelerating (the Oct 20, 2026 investor update is the next checkpoint), Precision Marketing/Our Brands mix lifting OE margin structurally, buybacks executed at lower prices, or deleveraging. None is in the current prints.
- **Hurdle math:** the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only at the weighted IV@15% of **~$31**; with a 25% margin of safety, **~$23**. That is 50–63% below the current price — a watchlist-at-a-very-different-price name, not a near-miss.
