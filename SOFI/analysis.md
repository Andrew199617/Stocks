## What it is
A digital one-stop financial-services company built around a national bank charter (SoFi Bank, charter acquired in 2022). Three segments: **Lending** (personal, student and home loans, increasingly funded by its own deposit base), **Financial Services** (checking/savings, invest, credit card, protect — the cross-sell "productivity loop"), and **Technology Platform** (Galileo + Technisys, now branded SoFi Tech Solutions — payment processing, card issuing and core-banking infrastructure sold to other fintechs and banks). Q2 2026 (latest quarter, per earnings coverage): GAAP total net revenue $1.219B, +43% YoY; net income $156.6M, +61% YoY — the 11th consecutive profitable quarter; adjusted EBITDA $357.8M; 15.8M members (+35% YoY), 24.4M products (+42%); deposits $45.5B; record total loan originations of $14.8B in the quarter; FY2026 adjusted net revenue guidance raised to $4.75–4.85B. The operating business is genuinely inflecting. The screen's question is narrower: what does the owner's-earnings definition do with a growing lender? Answer below — it breaks, in a specific and explainable way.

## MOAT: 5/10 — moderate / narrow
Justification: SoFi has real, compounding advantages — a bank charter with a $45.5B deposit base, a 15.8M-member cross-sell loop where existing members took 51% of new products in Q2 2026, and a B2B infrastructure arm — but the consumer products themselves are commodities sold against JPMorgan, Capital One, Chime, Robinhood and every large-bank app, deposits must be bought with a competitive APY, and the core asset is unsecured consumer credit whose returns are set by the credit cycle, not by SoFi. Nothing here has yet demonstrated pricing power through a downturn.
- FOR: National bank charter (2022) and deposit funding — $45.5B of deposits at Q2 2026 funding the loan book, replacing costlier warehouse funding and saving interest expense vs that alternative (per Q2 2026 coverage)
- FOR: Member ecosystem / cross-sell loop — 15.8M members (+35% YoY), 24.4M products (+42%), products per member at a record level and existing members opening 51% of new products in Q2 2026; acquisition cost per incremental product falls as the loop compounds
- FOR: Technology Platform (Galileo + Technisys / SoFi Tech Solutions) — payment processing, card issuing and digital core infrastructure sold to third-party fintechs and banks, a fee-based B2B revenue stream on top of the consumer bank
- FOR: Scale and brand in digital consumer finance — record $14.8B of quarterly originations in Q2 2026 across personal, student and home loans, with an affluent, high-income member base that supports credit quality
- AGAINST: Commodity products, no proven pricing power — personal loans, high-yield savings, investing and cards are contested by the largest banks and fintechs; SoFi wins on experience and marketing spend, it does not set price
- AGAINST: The deposit advantage must be bought with rate — the funding edge narrows whenever competitors match its savings APY, and deposit betas reprice against it when rates fall
- AGAINST: Earnings are geared to unsecured consumer credit — personal-loan charge-offs are the swing factor and rise fast in a downturn; the loan book has not been tested by a real recession at current scale
- AGAINST: Technology Platform momentum has stalled — segment revenue declined year over year in Q2 2026 per earnings coverage, and Galileo competes with Marqeta and in-house bank builds
- AGAINST: Dilution and capital retention — share count growing 11.6%/yr (engine), and a growing bank must retain capital against its loan book, so even GAAP earnings are not fully distributable owner's earnings
- A moat of 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years — moot for the valuation here, since there is no positive OE base to fade.

## FLAGS — every fragility flag resolved
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH (controlling flag):** Finance / lender — the OE/capex DCF fits poorly by construction: for a bank, "capex" ($242,444,000 in FY2025) is not the reinvestment, growing the loan book is, and that reinvestment runs through operating cash flow, so the conservative OE definition mechanically prints negative for any fast-growing lender regardless of GAAP profitability. Resolution per the standing rule: the standardized engine result is kept for comparability, confidence is graded **low**, and the tier is treated as soft — the F says "this screen cannot underwrite the stock," not "the business earns nothing" (FY2025 GAAP net income was +$481,320,000). No tier_override is taken: every filing basis is either negative (OCF-based) or, on the net-income basis, far too small against the market cap to support any hurdle tier, so there is no defensible higher tier to override to. Same treatment as RKT (finance F, no override).
- **STALE_FY** (latest FY ended 2025-12-31, ~9 months ago): resolved with ttm.py — TTM OE through Q2 2026 is −$6.65/share (inputs quoted above). Staleness does not rescue the name; the TTM figure is more negative than the FY figure because origination volume (and therefore OCF consumption) accelerated in H1 2026. No `--ttm` pin was used because the pin would be negative and the F branch keys on the normalized base regardless. Q2 2026 is the latest quarter.
- **DILUTION** (share count growing 11.6%/yr): real and multi-causal. Diluted shares rose from 945,024,000 (FY2023) to 1,101,390,000 (FY2024) to 1,251,767,000 (FY2025), and the TTM current-YTD count is 1,364,978,000. SBC is one leg ($262,058,000 in FY2025; $283,923,000 TTM) but not the largest: coverage reports a ~$1.5B equity raise in December 2025 (~5% dilution on its own), and a growing bank issues/retains capital as a matter of course. Already deducted via the −5pp share-count adjustment in the growth blend (share_cagr 11.59%, share_count_adj −0.05 in dcf_numbers.json); the deeper point for the verdict is that per-share economics lag the headline growth by roughly this rate every year it persists.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs): identified, two parts. (1) The engine's revenue tag is a fee/contract-revenue subset (RevenueFromContractWithCustomerExcludingAssessedTax: $4,520,000 in FY2019 → $103,331,000 in FY2020 → $247,718,000 in FY2021 → $619,353,000 in FY2025) that excludes net interest income entirely, so the series' early explosive growth is partly a tag/scale artifact of a pre-charter company, not the growth of the current bank — Q2 2026 total net revenue alone was $1.219B, roughly double the engine's full-year FY2025 "revenue." (2) The business itself was remade inside the window: SPAC listing (2021), Galileo (2020) and Technisys (2022) acquisitions, and the 2022 bank charter that converted SoFi from a marketplace lender into a deposit-funded balance-sheet lender. Consequence: no CAGR from this tag (rev_cagr_5y 43.07%, rev_cagr_3y 17.98%, g_blend 23.10%) was trusted for anything; growth evidence was taken from the company's reported total net revenue instead, and it does not affect the tier in any case because the F branch fires before growth is used.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025, gaps >40%): structural for a growing lender, not a one-off — no tax deposit, impairment or single working-capital swing explains it. Net income vs OCF (financials_history.csv): 2023 −$300,742,000 vs −$7,227,139,000; 2024 +$498,665,000 vs −$1,119,807,000; 2025 +$481,320,000 vs −$3,742,458,000. Net income accrues interest income and non-cash fair-value marks on loans; operating cash flow carries the cash deployed into loan originations and loan-sale timing (the AFRM lender pattern: originations/sales and provisions drive OCF far from NI in both directions). In every year the loan book grew, OCF printed far below NI. Consequence: the divergence IS the base story — it is why the conservative OE is negative while GAAP earnings are positive — and it is handled by keeping both definitions in view (above) rather than trusting either one alone.
- No BASE_DIVERGENCE flag fired (latest-FY OE/share and the 3-yr median are the same −$3.39) and no HIGH_NET_DEBT / BIG_NET_CASH flag fired (engine net debt is null for this filer; see the balance-sheet note).

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** on the standardized OCF definition the screen prints F (normalized OE −$3.39/share, TTM −$6.65/share). On the most generous filing basis — GAAP net income / maintenance OE of $481,320,000 in FY2025, growing (Q2 2026 net income $156.6M, +61% YoY) — the earnings are real but small against an $18,889,164,030 market cap: that basis supports a deep-D conversation, not a hurdle-clearing one. The defensible range is **F to D**, and no point in it clears 15% at $15.09. That range, not the single F, is the honest headline — but the F is the screen's standardized answer and it stands.
- Quality vs. score: unlike a typical Tier F name, the underlying business is not broken — members +35%, products +42%, deposits $45.5B, 11 consecutive profitable quarters, guidance raised. What the screen is actually measuring is that a balance-sheet lender compounding originations at this rate converts all of its earnings (and more) into loan-book growth, and pays its owners in future, not current, cash. Whether that trade is worth $15.09 is a ROTCE-vs-cost-of-equity and credit-cycle question this model does not compute.
- Red flags to monitor: unsecured personal-loan charge-offs through any employment downturn (the book is untested at scale in a recession); deposit-cost repricing if rate competition intensifies; Technology Platform revenue decline continuing; the dilution pace (11.6%/yr share growth taxes every per-share claim); regulatory/capital demands as the balance sheet scales; and headline/legal overhang noted in Q2 2026 coverage.
- What would change the verdict on a re-run: OCF turning sustainably positive as origination growth normalizes or the capital-light Loan Platform Business takes a larger share of originations, with SBC and share count no longer compounding at double-digit rates.
