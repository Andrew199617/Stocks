# LPLA — LPL Financial Holdings Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-15. Sector: Finance — wealth management / independent broker-dealer platform. Price $323.46 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap $25,573,071,060 (engine estimate, ~$25.6B — in scope under $40B). Diluted shares (latest FY) 79,061,000. Net debt (engine) $6,221,316,000.

## What it is
The largest independent broker-dealer / advisor platform in the US: roughly 32,000 financial advisors run their practices on LPL's custody, clearing, compliance, technology and asset-management infrastructure, and LPL takes a cut of the advisory and brokerage revenue they produce. Per the company's Q2 2026 release: total client assets $2,562.7B (+34% YoY), of which advisory assets $1,548.4B (60.4% of the total); Q2 2026 revenue $5,186,623 thousand (+35.2% YoY), net income $379,261 thousand (+38.8% YoY), diluted EPS $4.74, adjusted EPS $5.84; organic net new assets $23.1B in the quarter (4.0% annualized) and recruited assets $24.9B in the quarter, $89B over the trailing twelve months. Growth is now substantially bought: Atria Wealth Solutions closed in October 2024 and Commonwealth Financial Network closed August 1, 2025 (~$2.7B upfront, ~3,000 advisors, ~$305B of client assets at signing); Commonwealth advisors onboard in Q4 2026 with the company tracking to ~90% asset retention and guided run-rate EBITDA of ~$425M after integration (company investor materials).

## MOAT — score 6/10 (moderate-to-strong)
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-year projection).
- **FOR:** (1) Scale — the largest US independent advisor platform (~32,000 advisors, $2.56T client assets); technology, compliance, custody and clearing at a scale small rivals cannot fund. (2) Advisor switching costs — moving a practice means repapering every client account and changing custodian/clearing; most advisors don't move without a trigger. (3) Recruiting flywheel — $89B recruited assets over the TTM and $23.1B organic net new assets in Q2 2026 alone show the platform keeps winning advisors net of attrition. (4) Recurring mix — advisory assets are 60.4% of client assets and rising; asset-based fees recur through the cycle, and multiple affiliation models widen the recruiting funnel.
- **AGAINST:** (1) The assets can walk — advisors are independent contractors who own the client relationship; Commonwealth advisor teams have already defected to Cetera since the acquisition, and the ~90% retention assumption through the Q4 2026 onboarding is not yet proven. (2) Credible scaled competition on both flanks — Schwab/Fidelity custody economics and Cetera/Osaic/Raymond James recruiting the same advisors with forgivable-loan packages (which is also why LPL's own loan book keeps growing). (3) Exogenous earnings gears — asset-based fees ride equity-market levels, and client cash-sweep economics compress as policy rates fall (Q2 2026 net interest income fell sharply YoY per the company's release coverage). (4) Growth is increasingly acquired and debt-funded, so the moat spend shows up as leverage and integration risk rather than organic compounding.
