## What it is
Diversified holding company. Principal businesses: CNA Financial (commercial property & casualty insurance, >90% owned, the largest earnings contributor), Boardwalk Pipelines (natural gas transportation and storage), Loews Hotels & Co, and an equity stake in Altium Packaging (rigid plastic packaging, reported through Corporate). Q2 2026 release segment net income attributable to Loews: CNA $294M, Boardwalk $100M, Hotels $48M, Corporate $2M (three months); six months: CNA $488M, Boardwalk $259M, Hotels $74M, Corporate −$40M. This is an insurance-led conglomerate, not a single operating business — consolidated DCF inputs need that lens throughout.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak
Justification: a collection of mostly price-competitive businesses. Boardwalk's contracted pipeline network is the one genuinely hard-to-replicate asset; CNA is a mid-tier commercial insurer whose own current combined ratio shows no pricing-power cushion; hotels and packaging are cyclical/commodity exposures. Disciplined capital allocation and buybacks compound per-share value but are not a business moat.
- FOR: Boardwalk Pipelines — regulated/contracted natural gas transportation and storage network, costly to replicate, revenue driven by contracting rates; CNA's established commercial/specialty insurance franchise and investment portfolio scale; Loews Hotels' established luxury/upper-upscale portfolio; diversification across insurance, energy, hospitality and packaging smooths single-segment shocks; financially disciplined parent (parent cash and investments $4.4B vs parent debt $1.8B at June 30, 2026, per the Q2 release) with a long buyback record.
- AGAINST: P&C underwriting is price-competitive and cyclical — CNA's combined ratio worsened to 96.5% in Q2 2026 from 94.1% a year earlier, and to 99.4% for H1 2026 from 96.3% (release figures), i.e. underwriting margin is thin and deteriorating, not pricing-power-led; CNA carries legacy asbestos/environmental exposure (an unfavourable loss-portfolio-transfer charge hit Q4 2025 results per contemporaneous reporting); pipelines face volume, recontracting, regulatory and energy-transition risk; hotels are discretionary-cyclical; packaging is a commodity business; holding-company complexity/discount and no single business with durable share or pricing control.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

The H1/Q2 2026 release cross-check above shows earnings modestly above prior year, so the FY base is not stale in level; what was stale was the engine's growth rate, fixed by recalibration.
- **BUYBACK_TAILWIND** (share count shrinking 4.92%/yr): already credited via the +4.92pp growth adjustment in g_base — and that credit is most of the engine's growth, which is exactly why the scenario starts were recalibrated down rather than accepting a buyback as business growth. Not double-counted in the verdict.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): structural for an insurer, plus named one-offs. From financials_history.csv: 2023 NI $1,434,000,000 vs OCF $3,907,000,000; 2024 NI $1,414,000,000 vs OCF $3,025,000,000; 2025 NI $1,667,000,000 vs OCF $3,279,000,000. The persistent gap reflects insurance cash mechanics (premiums collected ahead of claims, reserve movements) and non-cash charges — CNA took a $265 million pension settlement charge in Q4 2024 (contemporaneous reporting of the Q4 release) that depressed net income without an equivalent operating-cash effect, and an asbestos/environmental loss-portfolio-transfer charge hit Q4 2025. The maintenance OE definition, which starts from net income, prints $1,695,000,000 for FY2025 — close to net income and far below the OCF-based conservative figure. Resolution: the divergence is not a one-year one-off to normalise away; it is evidence the engine's OCF-based base overstates distributable earnings for this filer, which is handled by defensive probabilities, recalibrated growth, low confidence, and sizing the verdict to the conservative end — not by trusting the base.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance sector): confirmed and binding. The dominant business is a P&C insurer; owner's-earnings/capex DCF logic fits insurers poorly (float and reserves dominate cash flow; capex is not the earnings constraint). Confidence is graded **low** and the tier is treated as soft — an upper-bound reading, not a precise one.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (preliminary run: super-bull IV 4.46x super-bear IV): resolved by the recalibration — the spread in the final run is 2.64x and the flag no longer fires. The preliminary spread was itself a symptom of the inflated growth starts. Confidence is still low, on the sector mismatch, not the spread.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range: the preliminary default run printed Tier A (weighted IRR 20.03%, weighted IV@15% $158.31) on the engine's unadjusted inputs; the recalibrated final run prints Tier C (14.82%, $107.23). The entire gap is input credibility — a partial revenue tag inflating the growth blend, a buyback credit counted as growth, and an insurer's OCF treated as owner's earnings. On the maintenance-OE definition the base would be lower still (its FY2025 total is $1,695,000,000 vs the conservative $2,659,000,000 used), so C is the generous end of the defensible range, not the harsh end.
- Quality is real but unexciting: diversified cash generators, a disciplined parent, persistent buybacks, book value per share compounding modestly ($90.71 → $93.52 in H1 2026). What is not present is a growth engine or underwriting pricing power — CNA's H1 combined ratio of 99.4% leaves almost no underwriting profit; earnings lean on investment income, Boardwalk, hotels, and the shrinking share count.
- Price vs hurdle: the weighted IV@15% of $107.23 is the price that clears 15% on the weighted case — the current $108.78 price sits just above it, so the stock does not clear Andrew's 15% hurdle as priced, and there is no margin of safety: the bear-case IV@15% is $88.48. A buyer wanting 15% with a margin of safety against the bear case needs a price well below $107.23, toward the bear-case level.
- Risks: catastrophe losses and adverse reserve development (including asbestos/environmental) at CNA; investment-portfolio marks flowing through earnings; pipeline recontracting; hotel cyclicality; the possibility that buyback pace keeps slowing from the historical rate embedded in the engine's blend.
