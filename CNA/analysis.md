## What it is
Chicago-based commercial property & casualty insurer (SIC 6331, Fire, Marine & Casualty Insurance, per fundamentals.json), the seventh-largest U.S. commercial insurer, ~92% owned by Loews Corporation — a controlled company with a thin public float. Core P&C segments are Specialty, Commercial and International, plus a Life & Group segment carrying long-term-care runoff and a Corporate & Other segment carrying legacy mass-tort / asbestos liabilities. FY2025 (company Q4 2025 release): P&C net written premiums $10,683M (+5%), net earned premiums $10,478M (+7%), underwriting gain $551M, P&C combined ratio 94.7% (underlying 91.8%), full-year net income $4.69/share and core income $4.93/share; a $2.00/share special dividend was declared with Q4 2025 and the regular quarterly dividend raised 4% to $0.48. AM Best upgraded the financial strength rating to A+ (stable) in December 2025. Q2 2026 (company release): net income $321M ($1.18/share), core income $1.19/share, P&C net written premiums +4% to $2,970M with new business +11% to a record $718M and retention 83%, but the P&C combined ratio deteriorated to 96.5% (underlying 94.2%, vs 91.7% a year earlier) and Corporate & Other again took a $77M after-tax charge for unfavorable legacy mass-tort development ($88M in Q2 2025).

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-narrow
Justification: a scaled, A+-rated specialty/commercial franchise with genuine underwriting expertise, but P&C cover is re-bid at renewal through non-exclusive brokers/agents, pricing power belongs to the industry cycle rather than to CNA, and measured returns sit at the cost of capital through the cycle — ROE (fundamentals.json annual) ran 5.43% (2020), 10.66% (2021), 7.98% (2022), 12.18% (2023), 9.12% (2024), 11.00% (2025), i.e. around, not well above, the cost of capital. fundamentals.json reports no gross margin and a null roic_proxy for this insurer (no filed operating-income series), so ROE and the combined ratio are the return evidence: FY2025 combined 94.7% / underlying 91.8% is competent, not elite (elite underwriters run high-80s/low-90s all-in), and the underlying combined ratio deteriorated to 94.2% in Q2 2026. That evidence supports 4, not the 7+ the rubric reserves for franchises earning well above capital costs through a cycle.
- FOR: scale — seventh-largest U.S. commercial insurer with Specialty / Commercial / International breadth (company disclosures); AM Best A+ financial strength (upgraded December 2025) supporting broker and policyholder trust; specialty expertise in professional liability, surety (Western Surety) and healthcare lines where underwriting skill differentiates; expense ratio held below 30% (29.7% FY2025, company release) while investing in technology/AI; retention 83% with new business +11% to a record $718M in Q2 2026; Loews' ~92% ownership as a historically supportive, long-horizon parent.
- AGAINST: commodity cover re-shopped at renewal — policyholder switching costs are low and distribution is intermediated and non-exclusive; far larger rivals (Chubb, Travelers, AIG) set the competitive terms; pricing is cyclical and now softening (renewal rate flat in Q2 2026); social inflation is pressuring loss ratios (underlying loss ratio up across segments in 2026); recurring legacy mass-tort/asbestos charges in Corporate & Other ($77M after-tax in Q2 2026 alone, $88M in Q2 2025) and LTC runoff in Life & Group are permanent-looking drags on group earnings; catastrophe tail (2.3 points of Q2 2026 combined ratio); controlled-company structure — minority holders have no governance say and the float is thin.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): ttm.py was run and failed — exact message quoted in the OE section (SBC YTD leg untagged in the 10-Qs). Per the v5 rules the engine's median base is used instead, with that reason stated; the fundamentals.json TTM through Q2 2026 (OCF $2,332,000,000, below FY2025; net income $1,237,000,000, roughly flat) confirms the median is not stale in a flattering direction.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2023, 2024, 2025): structural for this insurer, not a single one-off. From financials_history.csv: 2023 OCF $2,285,000,000 vs net income $1,205,000,000; 2024 OCF $2,571,000,000 vs net income $959,000,000; 2025 OCF $2,490,000,000 vs net income $1,278,000,000. D&A ($73,000,000 / $67,000,000 / $70,000,000) explains none of it. The cause is insurance mechanics: premiums are collected in cash up front while loss reserves and unearned premiums accrue unpaid (float), and non-cash reserve charges — including the recurring legacy mass-tort development in Corporate & Other — depress net income without consuming operating cash. Consequence: the OCF-based conservative OE ($2,363,000,000 in FY2025) is roughly double the maintenance/N-I-based figure ($1,278,000,000) because it capitalizes float that is owed to future claimants. Neither raw series is clean earning power; the OCF base is an upper bound, which is exactly why the final tier is softened one notch and confidence is low.
- **CASH_INCLUDES_RESTRICTED** (the only cash tag filed is the combined cash + restricted-cash total, $425,000,000 at FY2025 per financials_history.csv, with no restricted-cash tag separating it): net debt of $2,546,000,000 is therefore understated by the unknown restricted amount (escrow/collateral/regulatory balances). Stated balance sheet from the filing (fundamentals.json FY2025): assets $69,443,000,000, liabilities $57,822,000,000, equity $11,621,000,000, long-term debt $2,971,000,000 — the corporate debt slice is small against the insurance balance sheet, and the real leverage is policyholder liabilities, handled via the sector treatment rather than a net-debt adjustment. The absence of a HIGH_NET_DEBT flag is not relied on.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Finance sector — OE/capex DCF fits finance names poorly): controlling flag. Resolved per the rules: confidence graded **low**, and the final tier softened one notch (see verdict). An earnings/book-value lens (core EPS $4.93 in FY2025, book value $41.34/share at Q2 2026 against a $47.90 price) screens materially lower than the float-carrying OCF base — consistent with a soft B, not the A the raw engine printed. This is the standing insurer-float treatment in this sweep (same as PFG/HIG: A by numbers → B final, low confidence).
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (super-bull IV is 4.2× super-bear IV in the final run; 4.19× per diagnostics): real — a levered insurer's 15-year range genuinely runs from a reserve/cat accident (super-bear IV@15% $33.18, below the price) to a sustained hard market ($139.18). Consequence per the rules: confidence is not high (it is low, also forced by the sector flag) and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **TIER_BAND** (bear-case tier B at 16.25% vs bull-case tier S at 26.36%): the verdict spans tiers, so any position is sized to the bear end — the final tier B is exactly the bear-case tier.

## My thoughts / overlay
- **Method range is the headline, as with every insurer in this sweep:** the engine's raw OCF-median base produces Tier A (weighted IRR 21.95%, IV@15% $75.11). Every earnings-based way of looking at CNA lands lower, because roughly half the OCF base is float/reserve accrual rather than distributable earnings (FY2025 conservative OE $2,363,000,000 vs maintenance OE $1,278,000,000). The defensible range is B-or-lower on earnings lenses to A on the raw engine, with the A end resting entirely on the cash-flow definition the sector flag rejects. The verdict is sized to the conservative end: B, low confidence.
- Quality is not the debate: underwriting has been profitable (combined <100% through the cycle, 94.7% in FY2025), the A+ rating was just upgraded, the expense ratio is controlled below 30%, new business is growing double digits, and capital return is real ($2.00 special dividend plus a raised regular dividend in February 2026). The debate is the multiple for a controlled, mid-tier commercial insurer whose pricing tailwind is fading (renewal rate flat, underlying combined +2.5 points YoY in Q2 2026) and whose legacy tail keeps charging.
- Red flags to monitor: further legacy mass-tort / asbestos development in Corporate & Other (a charge in both Q2 2025 and Q2 2026), social-inflation-driven loss-ratio drift in Commercial, LTC runoff in Life & Group, catastrophe severity, investment-portfolio credit in a downturn, and the Loews control overhang (any Loews sell-down changes the float/governance picture entirely).
- **Hurdle math:** on the engine's numbers the stock clears a 15% IRR at $75.11 (the weighted IV@15%) and trades 56.80% below that figure at $47.90 — but that clearing price inherits the float-inflated base and overstates the true margin of safety; the bear-case IV@15% of $52.44 sits only ~9% above the price, which is the honest cushion on the conservative end. On the final-tier B reading, the claim is "clears 15% with room on engine numbers, sized like a B", not "57% upside".
