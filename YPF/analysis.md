## What it is
Argentina's state-controlled (51%) integrated oil and gas company: the largest operator in the Vaca Muerta shale formation, plus the country's dominant refining, fuel-marketing and petrochemicals businesses. It is exiting conventional fields (divestment agreements for conventional assets reported in 2026) to become a near-pure shale producer, with export infrastructure (Vaca Muerta Sur pipeline, Argentina LNG) as the next leg.

## ADS ratio and the 2026 split (judgment call, registered)
judgment.json carries `ads_ratio = {ratio: 1.0, source: "YPF Form 20-F, Item 9 (Shares and ADSs) and the Amended and Restated Deposit Agreement dated March 9, 2022 filed with the 20-F: each ADS represents one Class D share"}`. The wrinkle: a 10-for-1 share split took effect August 4, 2026 (company Form 6-K filed 2026-07-21), moving the depositary ratio to 10 post-split Class D shares per ADS with no change in ADSs outstanding and the local share count going from 393.3M to 3,933.1M. Every share count in this run's filings is on the pre-split basis, where 1 ADS = 1 share; 10 post-split shares per ADS is economically the same claim. Ratio 1.0 is therefore the correct scaling for the filed per-share figures against the $51.11 ADS price — applying 10 would double-count the split and inflate the base tenfold.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak; a real resource under a sovereign ceiling
Justification: YPF holds a real resource-cost advantage — largest operator in Vaca Muerta (the world's second-largest unconventional gas reserve and fourth-largest shale-oil reserve) and dominant in Argentine refining and fuel marketing. But it is a commodity price-taker whose durability is hostage to its controlling shareholder, the Argentine state (51%): the 2012 Repsol expropriation, a history of domestic fuel-price controls, and ARS/sovereign risk have repeatedly overridden shareholder economics. Resource quality earns a weak-moat score, not a moderate one.
- FOR: Largest Vaca Muerta operator — world-class shale resource with falling shale unit costs; dominant Argentine downstream position — refining and fuel-marketing leadership with integrated logistics; scale and infrastructure incumbency (pipelines, refineries, retail network) rivals cannot replicate quickly.
- AGAINST: Argentine state controls 51% — political override risk, expropriation precedent, fuel-price-control history; pure commodity price-taker, no pricing power over its main product; capital intensity consumes the cash engine (FY2025 capex $5,077M vs OCF $4,959M — growth must be bought with capex every year); sovereign/FX exposure between operations and USD shareholders.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.5 months ago): ttm.py was run and cannot compute a TTM base for this IFRS filer (message quoted in Owner's earnings above), so the median base stands per method. Reported post-FY prints (Q2 2026: net profit $1.21B vs $58M a year earlier, revenue $6.57B +42%, record adjusted EBITDA $2.8B; trailing OCF reported around $7.2B) show the business inflecting up — but with 2026 capex guided at $6.2B, a filing-direct TTM OE would still be small next to a $51.11 price; the inflection does not rescue the tier, it only explains why the market disagrees with the screen.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY −$0.34 vs 3-yr median $0.60): the latest year is the distorted one — FY2025 capex of $5,077M exceeded OCF of $4,959M at the peak of the shale build-out, and the conservative definition charges every dollar of that growth capex. The median is also a heavy-capex year (FY2023, OE total $237M), not a "normal" year — there hasn't been one in the window — but it is the less distorted base and the method's standard. The verdict is D on either base; on the latest year alone the OE is negative.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): FY2023 net income −$1,277M vs OCF $5,913M; FY2024 $2,393M vs $5,869M; FY2025 −$799M vs $4,959M (all financials_history.csv). The gap lives below operating income: very large non-cash charges the engine could not tag under IFRS (no D&A series extracted), impairments/write-downs as conventional fields were marked for exit, deferred income tax (FY2025 tax expense $1,709M against operating income of $1,740M), and net financial/FX results. The OE base is built from OCF, not net income, so the divergence does not contaminate the base — it confirms reported earnings are dominated by non-cash and below-the-line items.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real — this is commodity-plus-build-out cyclicality, not a one-off. Handled by the median base and by widening combined bear/super-bear weight to 45%.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: reporting currency ARS converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (frankfurter.app); flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average. Long-history USD figures are approximations (the recorded rates run from 0.1181 USD/ARS at 2014-12-31 down to 0.0167 at 2019-12-31); the recent base years rest largely on the issuer's own filed USD translations (see next flag).
- **USD_TRANSLATIONS_USED**: the engine used YPF's own filed USD convenience translations where the filing provides them — the base rests on the issuer's translations, not just ECB conversion.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED (x1.0)**: see the ADS section above — 1.0 is the split-consistent ratio for the filed (pre-split) share basis; the post-split ratio of 10 post-split shares per ADS is the same economic claim.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (base OE yield 1.18%): the price rests almost entirely on growth and reserve option value that a full-capex OE model does not credit. The tier is "fragile" in construction, but not in outcome: even the super-bull IV@15 ($8.68) is less than a fifth of the price, so no plausible base revision inside this method changes the tier.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (net debt 30.77x normalized OE): the multiple is inflated by the near-zero OE denominator — on reported EBITDA the balance sheet is far less stretched (Q2 2026 reporting put net leverage at about 1.1x, the lowest in over a decade). Stated plainly: $7,293M of net debt is manageable for the operating business, but it still sits ahead of an equity claim whose screen OE is ~zero, so any OE shortfall lands on shareholders first. Folded into the verdict as risk, not as distress.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is a method-mismatch name, and the honest headline is the gap itself: the screen says D at a weighted IV of $4.38; the market pays $51.11 for Vaca Muerta's reserve value, production growth (+47% shale oil YoY in Q2 2026), and record EBITDA — none of which survive a definition that expenses all growth capex in the year it's spent. Both statements are true inside their own frames. Per the sweep's rules the tier follows the numbers: **D, no override** — constructing a maintenance-capex base would require the IFRS D&A series the engine could not extract, and hand-building one is exactly what v5 forbids.
- The method range is narrow at the bottom: latest-year base → negative OE; median base → D (3.55%); super-bull → 4.78% IRR. Nothing inside the owner's-earnings method gets this stock near the 15% hurdle at $51.11. It clears 15% only below the weighted IV@15 of ~$4.38 — i.e., the OE screen would need the ADS to lose over 90% of its value, which tells you the screen and the market are pricing different assets (current OE vs shale reserves).
- Real risks the DCF doesn't need to reach the verdict: state control (51%) with an expropriation precedent, Brent price exposure, ARS translation, a $6.2B 2026 capex plan plus Argentina LNG ambitions that will keep free OE suppressed for years, and legacy litigation/environmental overhangs from the Maxus era.
- Quality note: SBC is a rounding error here ($15M FY2025), the share count has been flat for a decade, and Q2 2026 execution (record EBITDA, deleveraging to ~1.1x) is genuinely strong. None of that changes an OE-based tier at this price.
