## What it is
The dominant technology research and advisory firm: subscription Insights (research + analyst access, the large majority of revenue, mostly multiyear contracts), Conferences (~10% of revenue), and Consulting (~9%). The Magic Quadrant is the standard enterprise tech-buying shortlist; Gartner sells to both the buyers who budget around its advice and the vendors who must be placed in its frameworks. Asset-light people business: PP&E net $214,183,000 on FY2025 revenue of $6,497,226,000, clients prepay (deferred revenue $2,810,056,000 at end-2025).

The story of the last two years is a growth stall: contract value (CV) growth fell from the 12–16% management calls normal to 1% in Q4 2025 and 1.7% in Q2 2026, the February 2026 guidance (2026 revenue of at least $6.46B, below FY2025) knocked ~31% off the stock in a day, and securities class actions now allege the CV/consulting slowdown was not disclosed candidly. Q2 2026 showed early stabilization: CV +1.7% YoY accelerating ~70bp sequentially, ex-U.S.-federal CV +3.3%, new business dollars up ~10% sequentially, and management raised its 2026 adjusted EPS (to at least $14) and free-cash-flow guidance.

## Moat: 7/10 — strong, with an impaired growth engine
Justification: the Magic Quadrant standard and the buyer/vendor two-sided flywheel, multiyear prepaid subscriptions, 98%+ wallet retention even in the current stall, and measured returns on capital far above the cost of capital through the cycle — but contract value growth has been stuck at 1–2% for two years and generative AI is a credible substitution path for the synthesis work Gartner sells.
- FOR: (1) Standard-setting brand — the Magic Quadrant is the enterprise tech-buying shortlist format; vendors must engage Gartner to be placed and buyers budget-hold around its advice, a two-sided flywheel (buyer attention attracts vendors, vendor engagement funds the research). (2) Subscription economics — Insights on mostly multiyear contracts at a 77% contribution margin (Q2 2026), clients prepay: deferred revenue $2,810,056,000 at end-2025. (3) Retention holds even in a stall — wallet retention 98.2% and client retention 85.2% in Q2 2026, contract value per enterprise up ~10% YoY to $414,000: pricing power inside the installed base. (4) Returns on capital — roic_proxy in fundamentals.json: 36.1% (2021), 43.4% (2022), 52.3% (2023), 55.4% (2024), 48.9% (2025), with gross margin stable at 69.5% / 69.7% / 68.3% / 68.3% / 68.8% over the same five years (TTM gross margin 70.1%). Read the roic_proxy level with care — its denominator (equity + debt − cash) is shrunk by years of buybacks, so the level is flattered; the direction is not in doubt, and management cited 31% rolling four-quarter ROIC on the Q2 2026 earnings call. A franchise needing PP&E of $214M to produce $6.5B of revenue earns well above its cost of capital by construction.
- AGAINST: (1) CV growth collapsed from the 12–16% management calls normal to 1% (Q4 2025) and 1.7% (Q2 2026) — two years is erosion evidence, not one bad print. (2) Wallet retention slipped below 100% (98.2% vs 101.3% a year earlier) and client enterprises fell 4.5% YoY to 12,775 — the installed base is shrinking its spend. (3) Generative AI: buyers increasingly start vendor research by asking an LLM; boutiques, peer-review platforms (G2, buyer of the divested Digital Markets properties) and free vendor content attack the same budget. (4) People asset — the product is analyst judgment; analysts can leave and the research must be re-earned every cycle. (5) The growth engine has quietly switched to financial engineering — TTM buybacks of $2,635,746,000 ran at ~2x free cash flow, funded with debt; that pace cannot persist.
- A 7 gives CAP = 11.1 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 11.1 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Method range: this run's two defensible configurations — engine-default growth starts (weighted IRR 14.43%, IV@15% $195.02 in the preliminary pass at the same TTM base and moat) and the recalibrated starts used here (13.19%, $173.63) — are both Tier C. On the normalized FY base ($13.64) instead of the TTM base, every IV scales down by roughly the base ratio and the tier stays C. The finding is robust: at $205.35 the market is paying for the bull case (CV reacceleration) before it has printed.
- Quality is not the debate: 68–70% gross margins for five straight years, a prepaid subscription base, wallet retention above 98% in a trough year, and essentially no tangible capital needs. The debate is purely what growth a stalled CV book can deliver and how long debt-funded buybacks at ~2x FCF can substitute for it.
- Risks the scenarios carry: the securities class actions over CV/consulting disclosures (filed after the August 2025 and February 2026 drops); U.S. federal exposure (~$125M of CV at Q2 2026, per the earnings call) in a federal cost-cutting environment; AI substitution, which is the super-bear story and the one risk that attacks the moat itself rather than the cycle; and a balance sheet that has gone from $526,768,000 net debt to $1,259,153,000 in a year while equity shrank to $319,908,000 — still covered ~1x by one year's OE, but the cushion is being spent on the buyback.
- What would change the verdict: two or three quarters of CV growth back above ~5% with wallet retention back over 100% would make the bull case the base case, and at the 15% hurdle price of $173.63 the current numbers already clear.
