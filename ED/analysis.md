# ED — Consolidated Edison, Inc.
Run date: 2026-10-13. Sector: Utilities / regulated electric, gas & steam delivery (CECONY + Orange & Rockland), New York City metro. Price $107.20 (Nasdaq quote API, engine). Market cap ~$38.5B on the engine's FY2025 diluted count (358.7M); on the 369.8M shares outstanding at July 31, 2026 (Q2 10-Q) it is ~$39.6B — inside the <$40B scope either way, barely.

## What it is
A pure regulated delivery utility: CECONY serves ~3.6M electric, ~1.1M gas and steam customers in NYC/Westchester; O&R serves northern suburbs / NJ. Revenue is whatever the NY PSC allows on a growing rate base — management targets ~8.6–8.8% five-year rate-base CAGR through 2030 and a 6–7% five-year adjusted-EPS CAGR off the 2026 guidance midpoint ($6.00–$6.20, reaffirmed with Q2 2026). Dividend raised 52 consecutive years; H1 2026 dividends were $0.89/share/quarter (~$3.55/yr, ~3.3% yield at $107.20), payout target 55–65% of adjusted EPS. Q2 2026: revenue $4,069M, net income $308M, EPS $0.83 (vs $0.68); H1 2026 adjusted EPS $3.00 vs $2.91.

## Owner's earnings — the engine's base is a capex-tag artifact, and the model fits a utility poorly
- **The preliminary engine run (Tier B, 16.0% weighted IRR) is rejected in full.** From FY2023 Con Edison stopped tagging cash capex as `PaymentsToAcquirePropertyPlantAndEquipment`; its cash-flow statement now reports "Utility capital expenditures" under the EDGAR tag `PaymentsForConstructionInProcess` plus "Cost of removal less salvage" (`PaymentsForProceedsFromRemovalCosts`). The engine read capex = $0 (flagged `capex_assumed_zero` in the CSV) for 2023–2025, so its OE suddenly became OCF alone ($2.16B → $3.61B → $4.80B), its OE/share CAGR printed a fictitious 47%, and its "normalized" base was $10.41/share of money that is in fact spent on plant.
- **Conservative OE with the real capex (utility capex + removal + ~$0 non-utility; SBC is nil — no SBC tag, utility comp is cash): negative every year.** FY2023: OCF $2,156M − ($4,353M + $387M) = **−$2,584M**. FY2024: $3,614M − ($4,770M + $474M) = **−$1,630M**. FY2025: $4,800M − ($4,764M + $481M) = **−$445M**. This is structural, not a slump: a utility whose rate base grows ~9%/yr spends ~2× depreciation on plant, funded by debt and continuous equity issuance. OCF − total capex is the wrong yardstick here — most of that capex is growth capex that enters rate base and earns the authorized ROE, which is exactly why the maintenance definition exists.
- **Maintenance OE (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC, engine formula with real capex; capex > D&A every year, so the min is D&A):** FY2024: $1,820M + $2,155M − $2,155M − (−$230M) = $2,050M = **$5.90/share**. FY2025: $2,023M + $2,321M − $2,321M − (−$150M) = $2,173M = **$6.06/share**. FY2023 is unusable (divestiture gain in NI and a −$2,219M ΔNWC swing from the Clean Energy sale — see FLAGS).
- **Base used — TTM adjusted (maintenance-proxy) OE through Q2 2026 = $5.79/share**, built two ways that agree: (1) reported adjusted EPS: FY2025 $5.70 − H1 2025 $2.91 + H1 2026 $3.00 = **$5.79**; (2) TTM GAAP net income $2,023M − $1,038M + $1,232M = $2,217M, less the after-tax MVP-sale gain of $134M ($0.37/share; the $189M pre-tax gain is in H1 2026 GAAP income and excluded from adjusted EPS — note $2,217M − $134M = $2,083M, and reported H1 adjusted NI $1,098M = $1,232M − $134M exactly) = $2,083M ÷ ~360M diluted shares ≈ $5.79. Price = **18.5× TTM base OE**; OE yield 5.4%.

## Moat score: 6/10 — durable legal monopoly, capped economics
CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP; 15-yr projection).
- **FOR:** (1) A legal monopoly franchise — no competitor can deliver electrons/gas/steam in its territory, customers have zero switching option, demand is essential and weather/inelastic-driven; (2) visibility few businesses have: multi-year rate plans, a ~$38B approved investment program through 2030, rate base compounding ~8.6–8.8%; (3) 52 consecutive years of dividend increases funded through every cycle, earned ROE held ~8–9% for a decade; (4) scale/density in the richest service territory in the US — underground NYC network economics no entrant could replicate.
- **AGAINST:** (1) The regulator is the counterparty and caps the prize: authorized ROE ~9–10% means returns on capital can never be wide-moat high, and earned ROE is only ~8.4% ($2,023M on ~$24.2B equity); (2) political/affordability pressure is rising — NY's Chapter 58 (2026) tightens rate-case rules, caps allowed revenue growth and forces sharing of returns above authorized ROE; (3) the gas network faces structural decline under NY electrification policy (CLCPA) — long-run stranded-asset risk the 2.5% terminal rate does not punish, a deliberate conservatism offset noted here; (4) the model self-funds poorly: ~$5.25B/yr capex vs $4.8B OCF means perpetual debt + equity issuance (shares outstanding 347M at end-2024 → 361M end-2025 → 370M by July 2026), diluting rate-base growth down to mid-single-digit EPS growth.

The engine's preliminary Tier B is an artifact and is rejected in full. This is also a **sector-model mismatch** in substance (regulated utility, like banks/insurers/REITs in the flag list): confidence capped at **low**, tier soft — though the direction is robust, since every defensible earnings definition lands far below the price at a 15% discount.
- **STALE_FY (FY ended 9.4 months ago):** Resolved with the TTM override. Full TTM arithmetic: OCF $4,800M − H1'25 $2,816M + H1'26 $1,971M = $3,955M; utility capex $4,764M − $2,420M + $2,473M = $4,817M; removal $481M − $251M + $208M = $438M; conservative OE TTM = $3,955M − $5,255M ≈ **−$1,300M** (H1 2026 OCF fell $845M YoY on regulatory/pension timing per the 10-Q MD&A — TTM conservative OE is worse than FY2025's, which is the honest picture of the cash strain). The base actually used is the TTM adjusted/maintenance figure, $5.79/share (arithmetic in the OE section).
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT (engine: 6.5× fake normalized OE in the prelim run; 11.6× on the final base):** The engine also *understates* the debt: its $24,172M misses notes payable and the term loan. True net debt at Dec 31, 2025: LTD $25,551M + current LTD $250M + term loan $500M + notes payable $1,575M − cash $1,629M = **$26,247M** (~12.6× TTM adjusted OE of $2,083M); at June 30, 2026: $26,843M + $250M + $721M − $1,470M = $28,344M vs equity $25,719M (~52% debt in the capital structure). Resolution: for a regulated utility this leverage is structural and regulator-sanctioned — debt is serviced out of allowed rate-base returns, not out of free cash flow, and the ~50/50 cap structure is what the PSC sets rates on. It is folded into the verdict via the defensive scenario weights and the low confidence, and it is the reason equity holders sit behind ~$28B of claims while funding a capex program that OCF does not cover — in a disallowance or rate-freeze bear case, the equity, not the debt, absorbs it.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE (2025: NI $2,023M vs OCF $4,800M):** No one-off. It is D&A: $2,023M + $2,321M depreciation = $4,344M, with the remaining ~$456M from deferred income taxes and regulatory-asset/liability deferrals — the normal utility pattern (OCF ≈ NI + D&A). The year that *did* contain a one-off is 2023: NI of $2,519M includes the gain on the March 2023 sale of the Clean Energy Businesses (Q1 2023 EPS printed $4.06 vs a ~$1.6–2.2 normal Q1), the sale proceeds sit in investing, and the engine's ΔNWC swung −$2,219M on the divestiture — which is why neither 2023's NI nor its maintenance OE is used anywhere in the base. 2025 itself is clean: GAAP diluted EPS $5.64 vs adjusted $5.70.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD (preliminary run, 4.5×):** Resolved — it was the product of default ±25% multipliers on the fake $0-capex base. On the corrected base with utility-appropriate multipliers the final spread is 2.06×, the flag no longer fires, and confidence is still capped at low by the model mismatch above.

## Thoughts / overlay
- **Method range:** mechanical engine run: Tier B, 16.0% (reject — capex artifact). Corrected conservative OE: negative in every year and on TTM — a literal reading gives Tier F, which is also the wrong answer for a growing regulated utility (it would score every rate-base compounder F forever). Corrected maintenance/adjusted base with guided growth: **9.0%, Tier D** — the defensible answer, and it agrees with the yield-plus-growth cross-check (~9.8%). The spread between B and D *is* the finding about the engine, not about Con Ed.
- Quality of the business is not the debate: it is a bond-like EPS compounder (6–7% guided) with a 52-year dividend record. The debate is purely price: at $107.20 the market is paying 18.5× for a stream whose regulated ceiling is ~9–10% ROE on rate base. A 15% IRR from here would need either a decade of growth Con Ed has never printed or a multiple re-rating utilities don't get at these rates.
- What would change the answer: a price near the IV, a materially higher authorized ROE, or electrification-driven rate-base growth well above 9% *without* matching equity issuance. Chapter 58 and gas-transition risk point the other way.
- **Hurdle math:** the stock clears a 15% IRR at ≈ **$54** (weighted IV@15%); with a 20% margin of safety, ≈ **$43**.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (= tier by numbers on the corrected base; the preliminary B is rejected as a data artifact, reason written above). Weighted IRR 9.0%.
