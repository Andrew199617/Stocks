## What it is
Roivant is a biopharmaceutical holding company built on the "Vant" model: focused subsidiaries each develop an in-licensed or acquired asset, and the parent allocates capital and eventually monetizes, keeps, or sells the asset. Current Vants that matter (search results): Priovant (brepocitinib, approved by the FDA in August 2026 as Lisraya for dermatomyositis, launch expected by end of September 2026, with Phase 3 programs in non-infectious uveitis and cutaneous sarcoidosis behind it), Immunovant (IMVT-1402, an anti-FcRn antibody in potentially registrational trials across Graves' disease, myasthenia gravis, CIDP, difficult-to-treat rheumatoid arthritis and Sjögren's), Pulmovant (mosliciguat in Phase 2 for PH-ILD, positive Phase 2 data reported September 2026, per search results), and Genevant (lipid-nanoparticle IP, held with Arbutus). The model has been monetized before: Telavant was sold to Roche in December 2023 and Dermavant (VTAMA) to Organon in a deal completed October 2024 for up to $1.2 billion (search results). What it does not yet have is a commercial revenue base: after the Dermavant sale, consolidated revenue is licensing-scale only.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak
Justification: the Vant sourcing/development model and the Genevant LNP patent estate have repeatedly converted into cash (Telavant, Dermavant, the Moderna settlement), which is a real organizational capability — but Roivant owns no commercial product with proven pricing power yet, its revenue base was sold with Dermavant, and the pipeline value is concentrated in three binary assets facing entrenched competition. A proven deal machine is not yet a durable earnings moat.
- FOR: Vant model has repeatedly in-licensed, developed and monetized assets (Telavant to Roche in 2023, Dermavant to Organon in 2024 — search results); Genevant's foundational LNP patent estate produced a $2.25 billion Moderna settlement, $950 million of it paid in July 2026 (search results); three late-stage "pipeline-in-a-product" assets (brepocitinib/Lisraya, IMVT-1402, mosliciguat) each spanning multiple indications; a $4.3 billion cash-and-securities pile at FY2026 (company-reported, search results) that funds the pipeline without capital-market dependence and funded a $208.7 million buyback in Q1 FY2027 (search results).
- AGAINST: No commercial revenue base — FY2026 revenue was $8,260,000 after the Dermavant sale, so there is no proven pricing power, switching cost, or recurring cash stream; value is concentrated in three clinical assets with binary trial/regulatory risk; IMVT-1402 faces entrenched FcRn competition (argenx's Vyvgart, J&J's nipocalimab — search results) and brepocitinib carries the JAK class boxed-warning overhang (search results); heavy SBC load — $346,165,000 in FY2026 and $347,329,000 TTM (ttm.json) — on top of roughly $750–816 million a year of negative operating cash flow; the monetization history is one-off asset sales and litigation, not recurring owner's earnings.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth would hold 2 years, then converge linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (a >60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 years): identified, two divestiture breaks, both verified in search results. (1) Telavant was sold to Roche in December 2023 — a development-stage asset, so its break shows up in earnings, not revenue (see NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE). (2) Dermavant — the VTAMA business that supplied essentially all of Roivant's product revenue ($75.1 million of net product revenue in FY2024, per Reuters via search results) — was sold to Organon in a deal that closed in October 2024. That is why reported revenue falls from $29,053,000 in FY2025 to $8,260,000 in FY2026 (financials_history.csv, the first full post-sale year) and why the latest revenue YoY is -71.57%: the revenue perimeter was sold, not lost to competition. Every revenue CAGR in dcf_numbers.json straddles this break and none of them was used as a growth input to the verdict (there is no positive base for growth to act on).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2024, 2025, 2026): explained for all three years, and in every year the NI side is the distorted one — the conservative OE series is OCF-based throughout, so the divergence does not contaminate the base. **2024:** net income was $4,348,926,000 against OCF of -$765,268,000 (financials_history.csv) — the gap is the gain on sale of Telavant net assets of approximately $5.3 billion recognized in FY2024 earnings on the Roche sale (search results); the cash Roivant received (approximately $5.2 billion pro rata, search results) is sale proceeds, not operating cash flow, while the underlying operations burned cash. **2025:** net income was -$171,981,000 against OCF of -$839,451,000 — earnings were held up by non-operating items the company's own reconciliation labels one-time, including a $110,387,000 Telavant gain/milestone item and a gain on deconsolidation of subsidiaries (search results), while OCF shows the actual burn. **2026:** net income was -$299,771,000 against OCF of -$750,349,000 — FY2026 earnings include a $770,235,000 gain on litigation settlement (the Moderna/Genevant settlement, per the FY2026 results release in search results) whose $950 million cash payment was not received until July 2026, i.e. after the fiscal year ended; the filed history is consistent with a large non-cash build in the year (ΔNWC $1,331,666,000 in financials_history.csv). Strip the settlement gain and FY2026 earnings look like the OCF line: a business burning roughly three-quarters of a billion dollars a year before SBC.

## My thoughts / overlay
- Method range: there is none worth reporting across tiers — every defensible base in this run is negative (normalized median -$1.56/share, latest FY -$1.59/share, TTM -$1.62/share; maintenance OE FY2026 -$1,631,437,000), so every method lands on Tier F. This is not a sector-model artifact: operating cash flow itself is negative on both the FY and TTM windows, in every one of the last five fiscal years.
- What the $33.62 price is actually paying for is not owner's earnings — it is the cash-and-securities pile (company-reported $4.3 billion at FY2026, search results), the remaining $1.3 billion contingent Moderna payment (search results), and risk-adjusted pipeline value in Lisraya, IMVT-1402 and mosliciguat, none of which an OE DCF on a negative base can price. A sum-of-the-parts / risk-adjusted NPV model could in principle support the price; this sweep's screen cannot, and no such figure is stated here because none exists in the computed outputs.
- Red flags: SBC is the quiet second burn — $346,165,000 in FY2026 on top of -$750,349,000 of OCF, and the current-YTD diluted share count in ttm.json (720,776,739) is above the FY2026 count (693,864,530), so per-share cash burn is not being outrun by the buyback; the commercial track record is unproven — the one launched product Roivant owned outright, VTAMA, had a lackluster launch before being sold (search results), and Lisraya's launch is guided "slow and steady" (search results); pipeline concentration means two or three readouts in 2026–2028 decide most of the equity value.
- Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require the SOTP/rNPV model described above, which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained.
