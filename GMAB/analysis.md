# GMAB — Genmab A/S
v5 run, 2026-10-15. Danish biotechnology company (20-F filer, CIK 1434265), in scope under $40B. Price $38.15 per ADS (nasdaq_quote_api, final run). **The headline of this run is a units correction:** the preliminary engine pass printed Tier S / weighted IRR 70.6% because per-share figures were per *ordinary* share while the traded price is per *ADS*, and each ADS represents one-tenth of one ordinary share. With the ADS ratio applied (see ADS RATIO), the same economics print Tier C. Anyone quoting the preliminary number is quoting a units error.

All figures below are copied from dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, or ttm.py output — none are recomputed.

## What it is
International antibody-therapeutics company headquartered in Copenhagen, built on proprietary antibody platforms (DuoBody bispecifics, DuoHexaBody, HexaBody, ADC formats). Eight approved antibody medicines to date. Revenue is mostly high-margin royalties from partners — Darzalex (daratumumab, marketed by Johnson & Johnson) and Kesimpta (Novartis) — plus growing owned product sales (Epkinly, co-promoted with AbbVie). H1 2026 (interim report, Aug 6 2026): revenue $2,051M (+25% YoY), royalty revenue $1,708M (+24%), Darzalex net sales by J&J $8,171M (+21%); full-year 2026 revenue guidance was raised to $4,325–4,525M (vs FY2025 revenue $3,720M). Late-stage owned pipeline: Epkinly expansions, Rina-S (from the 2024 ProfoundBio acquisition), and petosemtamab (from Merus, below).

## ADS RATIO — the correction that decides this run
- Genmab files on Form 20-F; EDGAR share counts are ordinary shares (62,691,630 diluted, FY2025) while the Nasdaq price is per ADS.
- judgment.json declares `ads_ratio = {ratio: 0.1, kind: stated in the filer's own 20-F}` under the bounded worker authority: the FY2023 Form 20-F cover lists the registered class as (brief quote, under 15 words) "American Depositary Shares, each representing one-tenth of one ordinary share" — 0.1 ordinary shares per ADS. The engine scaled per-share figures ×0.1 (flag ADS_RATIO_APPLIED).
- Effect: base OE/share $15.37 per ordinary share → **$1.5374 per ADS**; weighted IV@15% $327.62 (wrong basis) → **$24.49 per ADS**; tier S → C.
- Note on market cap: the engine's market_cap_est ($2,391,685,684.50) multiplies the ADS price by the ordinary share count and so understates the company; a market data profile puts the ADS-basis market capitalization at ~$20.7B (Barchart company profile, searched 2026-10-15). Either way the company is under the $40B scope line, and make_score's scope check runs off the engine figure, consistently.

## Moat: 6/10 — strong platform, concentrated royalty engine
Justification: proven, repeatable antibody platforms that have produced eight approved medicines and a high-margin royalty stream (Darzalex via J&J, Kesimpta via Novartis), plus a growing owned late-stage pipeline (Epkinly, Rina-S, petosemtamab) — but the royalty stream is concentrated in Darzalex, partners control commercialization of the largest earners, exclusivity erodes around the turn of the decade, and the $8.0B debt-funded Merus deal makes the next leg depend on a Phase 3 asset not yet approved.
- FOR: Proprietary antibody platforms (DuoBody bispecifics, DuoHexaBody, HexaBody, ADC formats) with a 25-year record of producing approved medicines — eight approved antibody medicines to date; High-margin royalty engine: Darzalex (J&J) and Kesimpta (Novartis) royalties were $1,708M of $2,051M H1 2026 revenue; Darzalex net sales $8,171M in H1 2026 (+21% YoY); Owned late-stage pipeline diversifying beyond royalties: Epkinly net sales +48% in H1 2026, Rina-S (ProfoundBio), petosemtamab (Merus) in Phase 3 with two FDA Breakthrough Therapy Designations; Asset-light OE conversion: FY2025 capex $37M against OCF $1,186M (financials_history.csv) — royalties and product sales need almost no reinvestment.
- AGAINST: Royalty concentration: Darzalex is the dominant royalty source and its exclusivity erodes around the turn of the decade; multiple myeloma competition (bispecifics, CAR-T) is intensifying; Partner dependence: J&J, Novartis and AbbVie control commercialization of the largest earners — Genmab collects royalties but does not control pricing, promotion or lifecycle strategy; Binary pipeline risk now levered: petosemtamab was bought for $8.0B before its Phase 3 readouts; a failure hits both the pipeline story and a balance sheet carrying ~$5.5B of new deal debt; New leverage: net debt $3,559M at FY2025 (engine) vs a historically net-cash balance sheet; interest drag and a sub-3x deleveraging commitment constrain flexibility.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 9.8 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY2025 ended 9.46 months ago): ttm.py was run and exited 3 — its message: "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for GMAB (CIK 1434265): ocf: no annual (FY) fact; sbc: no annual (FY) fact; shares_diluted: no annual (FY) fact; capex: no capex facts in companyfacts under any candidate tag and no capex_override in judgment.json". That is the expected IFRS limitation (ttm.py reads us-gaap facts only), so no TTM pin exists and the engine's median-of-3-FY base is used, per the v5 spec for IFRS filers. Cross-check from the H1 2026 interim report (revenue +25%, royalties +24%, guidance raised) shows no deterioration against the FY2025 base — if anything the interim prints run ahead of it — so staleness is not flattering the base.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: two breaks, both identified. (1) The filed history jumps 2019 → 2023 with no 2020–2022 rows and changes basis at the seam (DKK-converted vs filed USD translations), which inflates the engine's revenue CAGRs; growth inputs were therefore set from the latest FY YoY, the H1 2026 print and guidance, not from the straddling CAGRs. (2) Merus closed in December 2025: FY2025 carries the deal debt but essentially no Merus operations, so the base OE is pre-Merus while the balance sheet is post-Merus — the bear cases carry that mismatch (petosemtamab spend and interest with no approved-product revenue yet).
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over the last 5 filed years): the span is the 2019 pre-scale year against the 2023–25 royalty-era years plus the basis change — platform scaling, not cyclicality — but the 2023–25 OE line itself is flat, which is its own warning. Handled by the median base and by putting 45% of probability on bear-or-worse.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023: net income $631,000,000 vs OCF $1,071,000,000, financials_history.csv): OCF ran well ahead of net income — the gap sits in non-cash charges and working-capital timing in the filed statements, not in the OE legs themselves; the conservative OE used throughout is OCF-based, so the divergence does not contaminate the base, and the median-of-3 further dampens any single year.
- **FX_CONVERTED**: the 2017–19 DKK facts were converted to USD at period-end ECB reference rates (frankfurter.app); flow facts use the period-end rate, not a period average, and the rates used are recorded in dcf_numbers.json fx_conversion. The base years (2023–25) are not the converted ones — see next flag.
- **USD_TRANSLATIONS_USED**: the base rests on the issuer's own filed USD convenience translations (the round 2023–25 figures in financials_history.csv), used as filed.
- **ADS_RATIO_APPLIED**: resolved by declaration — see ADS RATIO above. Ratio 0.1 ordinary shares per ADS, sourced from the filer's own FY2023 Form 20-F cover under the bounded worker authority for ads_ratio.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (5.23x): real — the outcome genuinely hinges on petosemtamab's Phase 3 readouts and Darzalex's late-decade trajectory. Confidence is low, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end.
- **HIGH_NET_DEBT**: the flag's 36.9x figure is a scaling artifact of the ADS conversion (the final diagnostics divide total net debt by the ADS-scaled normalized OE; the same engine run on the pre-ADS basis printed 3.69x normalized OE). The leverage itself is real either way: net debt $3,559,000,000 at FY2025 after ~$5.5B of new Merus debt, against a company that was net-cash before the deal. It is handled by the bear-skewed probabilities (45% bear-or-worse) and by refusing to let the bull cases carry the verdict.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The quality is not the debate: near-zero capex, SBC charged in full and still modest against OCF, royalties compounding at +24% in H1 2026, guidance raised. The debate is price and duration: at $38.15 the ADS capitalizes the royalty engine well past the point where Darzalex exclusivity erodes, plus a successful petosemtamab launch, on a balance sheet that no longer has the net-cash cushion.
- Method range: defensible bases cluster tightly — the latest-FY base ($1.6286/ADS) sits just above the median base ($1.5374/ADS) actually used — so the tier is not a base-choice artifact: the spread of outcomes is scenario-driven (bear $16.08 to super-bull $53.66 at IV@15%), not method-driven. Even the bull case (14.54% IRR) does not clear 15%; only the 8%-weighted super bull does.
- Margin of safety: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle only below the weighted IV@15% of **$24.49** — about a third below the current $38.15 — and a buyer wanting cover against the bear case ($16.08) needs a far lower entry still. Recent analyst targets ($42–54, searched 2026-10-15) sit above the price; this DCF does not follow them, because they capitalize the pipeline at probabilities the scenario weights here deliberately do not.
- What would change the verdict: petosemtamab Phase 3 interim readouts (expected 2026, launch guided 2027), Epkinly label expansions, and the pace of deleveraging against the sub-3x gross leverage commitment.
