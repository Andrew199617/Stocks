# QXO — QXO, Inc.

Engine 5.4.0 run, 2026-10-18. All figures below are copied from dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json, financials_history.csv, or fundamentals.json — none are recomputed. Web facts are from QXO's Q2 2026 earnings release (8-K filed 2026-08-13) and cited search results, marked as such.

## What it is
Brad Jacobs' building-products distribution rollup, built on the former SilverSun Technologies shell (recapitalized in 2024). Beacon Roofing Supply closed April 29, 2025 ($11B, all cash — Q2 2026 release), Kodiak Building Partners closed April 1, 2026 ($2.25B — search results), and TopBuild closed July 1, 2026 (~$17B, ~45% cash / 55% stock — search results), making QXO the second-largest publicly traded building products distributor in North America with pro-forma revenue above $18B (Q2 2026 release). Residential roofing was 39% of Q2 2026 sales (search result: RBC channel-check note, Oct 2026). Management targets: more than double EBITDA by 2030, $50B revenue within the decade (Q2 2026 release). SIC 5030 — Wholesale, Lumber & Other Construction Materials (fundamentals.json).

This is the single most basis-broken name the sweep has produced so far: the filed history contains three different companies — a ~$50M-revenue software shell (through 2024), a part-year Beacon (FY2025), and a Beacon + one-quarter-of-Kodiak TTM — and none of them is the current company, whose largest acquisition (TopBuild) closed after the last filed period and appears in no filed financial statement yet.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, scale without proven returns
Justification: real and growing procurement/branch scale in a fragmented industry, bought entirely with other people's capital in 15 months; distribution has no structural switching costs, equally scaled rivals exist, and the acquired business has not yet produced a positive-ROIC filed year.
- FOR: Largest roofing distributor in North America via Beacon; pro-forma #2 publicly traded building products distributor (>$18B revenue) after TopBuild — procurement leverage over manufacturers in an $800B fragmented industry (deal reporting, search results); branch density and pro-contractor job-site presence across roofing, waterproofing, insulation and complementary products; Brad Jacobs' repeated rollup playbook (XPO, United Rentals) — disciplined dealmaking and operational lift is a demonstrated skill, even if unproven in this vertical so far.
- AGAINST: Distribution is structurally low-moat — local relationships, no switching costs, customers multi-source; ABC Supply (private) and Home Depot-owned SRS Distribution are equally or better scaled and did not pay rollup premiums; ROIC evidence is negative, not just thin — FY2025 roic_proxy −1.9% and TTM operating margin −3.4% (fundamentals.json), and gross margin stepped down from the shell's ~40% to 23.0% in FY2025 / 24.0% TTM, i.e. commodity distribution economics; the claimed tech/AI playbook is unproven in this vertical; three mega-deals in 15 months is an integration load no operator has carried in this industry; earnings power is hostage to residential roofing volumes (39% of Q2 sales) with housing soft.
- ROIC note per spec: the pre-2025 roic_proxy series (2023: −33.9%, 2024: +250.9% in fundamentals.json) describes the shell and its recap cash, not this business — it is quoted here only to say it cannot be used. The distribution business's only filed year shows negative ROIC, so a moat above 4 would be a claim about the future, not the record. A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (FY ended 9.6 months ago): resolved by construction — the base is the TTM figure from ttm.py through Q2 2026, legs quoted above.
- **DILUTION** (share count +391.2%/yr): this is deal and recap issuance, not ordinary SBC creep — the count went from 6,570,000 diluted (2023, shell) to 204,000,000 (2024 recapitalization that banked $5,068,500,000 of cash) to 613,000,000 (FY2025, Beacon financing) to 1,037,490,709 outstanding (2026-08-07, after TopBuild's ~55% stock consideration) in financials_history.csv / dcf_numbers.json. Consequence: every historical per-share figure is on a different company. SBC itself is separately real and large ($144,500,000 FY2025; $127,500,000 TTM) and is deducted in full in the OE base. The TTM denominator (755,900,000) still predates the TopBuild issuance, which biases the base up, not down.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT**: named — four breaks: (1) the 2024 recapitalization of the SilverSun shell, (2) Beacon closing April 29, 2025, (3) Kodiak closing April 1, 2026, (4) TopBuild closing July 1, 2026 (post-period, in no filing yet). No CAGR in the filed history straddles any of these meaningfully; growth inputs were set from organic evidence (Q2 prints, channel checks), not from the blend.
- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real and structural here — the span is shell-to-rollup, not cyclicality. Handled with the TTM base and 45% of probability on bear-or-worse.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2023, 2024, 2025): 2023 is the tiny shell (NI −$1,070,000 vs OCF $584,000 — immaterial). 2024: NI $28,000,000 vs OCF $84,800,000 on $56,900,000 of revenue — a shell holding a $5,068,500,000 cash pile; neither figure describes distribution operations. 2025: NI −$279,400,000 vs OCF +$261,400,000 — deal/integration charges and non-cash items (D&A $108,400,000) on the loss side, working-capital and add-backs on the cash side; the maintenance OE's −$1,907,900,000 shows the same consolidation running through ΔNWC (+$1,658,700,000). The OCF-based conservative OE is the series used throughout, so the divergence is disclosed, not embedded.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE** (latest FY $0.06 vs 3-yr median $0.25): **both** are distorted — the median is the FY2024 shell year (see Owner's earnings) and the latest FY is eight months of Beacon carrying deal-year SBC. The TTM base ($0.018) supersedes both; the divergence is why confidence is low.
- **IRR_AT_FLOOR**: super-bear, bear, base and weighted IRRs sit within 0.5pp of the 2.5% terminal growth rate — the model cannot express a return below ~2.5-3% at this price. Read the weighted IRR as "at most ~3%", and the IVs ($0.12–$0.61 across all scenarios) as the informative output: at $10.86 the price is 18–90x every scenario's IV@15%.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** (0.2%): confirmed — the filed business currently converts $9,898,600,000 of TTM revenue into $13,700,000 of owner's earnings. The entire $10.86 price rests on earnings power that has not appeared in a QXO filing.

- **HIGH_NET_DEBT** (62.6x normalized OE): the ratio is a tiny-denominator artifact; the balance sheet itself at FY2025 was net debt $695,700,000 plus leases $669,300,000 against equity $9,706,800,000 — not stretched **as filed**. The real leverage risk is unfiled: the TopBuild close (July 1, 2026, ~45% cash on a ~$17B deal) lands on the Q3 balance sheet, and until it files, filed leverage understates actual leverage by an amount this run cannot compute. Bear cases carry the stress.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the gap between the two QXOs: the **filed** QXO (Beacon + a quarter of Kodiak, no TopBuild) earns $0.018/share of owner's earnings and is worth, on this model, well under $1 at any scenario. The **market's** QXO (priced at $10.86 against 1,037,490,709 shares outstanding as filed 2026-08-07) is a claim on pro-forma TopBuild-included earnings, guided synergies ($300M by 2030 in deal reporting), and Jacobs' track record — none of which is in a filing yet. This score grades the first company because the sweep's rule is filed data only; pass 2 should re-run the moment the Q3 2026 10-Q files the first TopBuild-included quarter, because that filing changes the base more than any other pending filing in the sweep.
- What would have to be true: at $10.86 and the filed share count, QXO needs owner's earnings per share roughly 40–60x the TTM figure just to clear a 15% hurdle on these scenario shapes (hurdle price at 15% is $0.29). That is not impossible for a rollup mid-integration — adjusted EBITDA was already $272M in Q2 alone — but adjusted EBITDA is not owner's earnings: SBC at $127,500,000 TTM, capex at $112,200,000 TTM and deal debt service all stand between them, and GAAP operating income is still negative (−$337,600,000 TTM).
- Method range: narrow for once — every base on offer (median $0.25, latest FY $0.06, TTM $0.018) produces a D at this price; even the rejected shell-median base at the preliminary run's capped growth printed Tier C with IV@15% $7.96, still 27% below the price. The verdict does not depend on which distorted base you pick; only its severity does.
- Risks to this (bearish) verdict: Jacobs has compounded through exactly this pattern before; the Q3 print will mechanically multiply the OE base when TopBuild consolidates; and a housing recovery re-rates roofing volumes fast. All three are reasons to revisit on the next filing, none is a filed fact today.
