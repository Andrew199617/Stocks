# RBLX — Roblox Corporation
Run date: 2026-10-13 (engine v4). In scope: market cap ~$34.1B (engine) / ~$35.4B on the H1 2026 diluted count — under $40B either way.

## What it is
A user-generated-content gaming platform, not a game studio: millions of creator-built experiences played inside one client, monetized mainly through Robux (virtual currency bought with cash = "bookings," recognized as revenue over the life of virtual items / user engagement). Q2 2026 scale: 123M daily active users (+10% YoY), 29B hours engaged (+5%), revenue $1.469B (+36%), bookings $1.557B (+8%). The payer base is broadening (monthly unique payers +15%) but monetization per user is falling (bookings per DAU −1.6%, per payer −6.0%). The growth engine is international and older users (Japan and India DAUs up sharply; 18+ cohort the faster-monetizing segment); the U.S./Canada business — 54% of Q2 bookings on 18% of DAUs — was essentially flat (+0.5% bookings).

## Owner's earnings (SEC EDGAR, filing-direct)
- History (conservative OE = OCF − capex − SBC): FY2023 −$730.5M (−$1.18/sh), FY2024 −$373.1M (−$0.58/sh), FY2025 +$226.4M (+$0.33/sh). OE positive in only 2 of the last 5 fiscal years. FY2025 detail: OCF $1,796.4M − capex $441.0M − SBC $1,129.0M = $226.4M.
- Maintenance version (NI + D&A − min(capex, D&A) − ΔNWC): FY2025 −$278.2M — negative even in the first positive conservative year, because GAAP net income (−$1,065.1M) already carries the full SBC expense and the working-capital term is dominated by the deferred-revenue book. The two definitions disagree on the sign in FY2025; the OCF-based conservative figure is the cleaner one for a bookings/deferred-revenue business, and it is the one the engine uses.
- **Normalized base (3-yr median OE/share): −$0.58/share → the preliminary engine run is Tier F by the v4 rule.** That median is a lagging artifact, not current earning power (see BASE_DIVERGENCE below).
- **STALE_FY resolution — TTM base through Q2 2026, from EDGAR 10-Q YTD figures:**
  - TTM OCF = FY25 $1,796.358M − H1'25 $643.176M + H1'26 $947.000M = **$2,100.182M**
  - TTM SBC = FY25 $1,129.004M − H1'25 $543.698M + H1'26 $557.000M = **$1,142.306M**
  - TTM capex = FY25 $440.978M − H1'25 $39.975M + H1'26 $55.000M = **$456.003M** (includes the Q4 2025 infrastructure spike: 9M'25 capex was only $142.6M, so ~$298M landed in Q4 alone)
  - **TTM conservative OE = $2,100.182M − $456.003M − $1,142.306M = $501.873M = $0.7027/share** on H1'26 weighted diluted shares of 714.246M. The final engine pass used `--base-oe-ps 0.7027`.
  - The inflection is filing-real, not a one-off: H1 2026 standalone OE was $335M (OCF $947M − capex $55M − SBC $557M) vs $59.5M in H1 2025, driven by OCF scaling (+118% FY2025, TTM $2.10B) while SBC growth flattened ($868M → $1,016M → $1,129M → $1,142M TTM). SBC is still ~20% of TTM revenue ($5,685.7M) — the single largest cost in the OE bridge.

## Moat score: 6/10 — moderate-strong network, unproven pricing power
FOR — (1) Two-sided network effects at real scale: 123M DAUs attract creators, and the creator base (top 1,000 creators averaged ~$1.3M of earnings in 2025, per company coverage) builds the content library no rival can copy quickly; content concentration is falling (top 10 experiences now ~20% of hours vs ~30% before). (2) Social-graph and identity lock-in: friendships, avatars, inventories, and Robux balances live inside Roblox; a child/teen cohort that grows up on the platform is a durable installed base, and the 18+ cohort is now growing and monetizing better. (3) Infrastructure and safety/ML stack built over a decade — running real-time 3D for 100M+ concurrent-leaning users on cheap devices is a genuine engineering barrier.
AGAINST — (1) No owned IP or content exclusivity: creators multi-home (Fortnite Creative, Minecraft, standalone engines), and hit experiences are not owned by Roblox. (2) Pricing power is unproven and currently going backwards: bookings per DAU and per payer fell in Q2 2026, management deliberately re-tuned discovery away from monetization toward retention, and the DevEx rate for U.S. 18+ spend was *raised* (26.6% → 37.8%) — margin given to creators, not taken. (3) Child-safety is a structural tax and an existential regulatory surface: global age checks in January 2026 cut chat/communication density and contributed to a guidance cut; active child-safety litigation, COPPA 2.0, and EU DSA scrutiny can impose costs and product restrictions competitors aimed at adults don't bear. (4) No proven returns on capital: GAAP losses in every year of history, book equity down to ~$0.15B at Q2 2026; the moat has produced engagement, not yet earnings.
CAP from moat 6: clamp(2 + 1.3×6) = **9.8 years** (growth holds 2 years, then converges to 2.5% terminal over the CAP, 15-year projection).

## DCF — FINAL v4 run (2026-10-13)
Price: **$49.51** (Nasdaq quote API, engine run). Base: TTM OE **$0.7027/share** — a 1.4% OE yield. The price is ~70× TTM owner's earnings; everything depends on growth, which is why the growth inputs below matter more than the base.

**Growth recalibration (written reason, per the standing rule):** the engine's blended growth is 30.8% after the −5pp dilution adjustment (components: rev CAGR 5y 39.6%, 3y 30.0%, latest YoY +35.8%). Those are *revenue* signals, and revenue here is a lagging indicator — it recognizes bookings collected in earlier, viral-hit periods. The forward cash indicator is bookings, and bookings have broken: +8% in Q2 2026 (low end of guidance), Q3 2026 guided to $1.58–1.65B, a **−14% to −18% YoY decline**, full-year guidance (already cut in Q1 to revenue +20–25% / bookings +8–12%) not updated at Q2, with management saying monetization weakness is likely to continue and AI infrastructure spend rising. OE is collected cash minus SBC and capex, so OE growth must be set off bookings, not off the +36% revenue print. Scenario starts were therefore recalibrated down via offsets (−30.8 / −22.8 / −15.8 / −8.8 / −0.8pp vs the engine blend) to 0% / 8% / 15% / 22% / 30%. Even the super-bull start (30%) sits below the latest revenue YoY (+35.8%) — the written reason is the bookings guide above; a super-bull above the bookings trajectory would be inventing a reacceleration no filing or guide shows. Probabilities skewed bearish (15/25/30/20/10 vs default 10/25/30/25/10) for the guidance withdrawal and the safety-litigation overhang. Multipliers left at defaults (0.75/0.90/1.00/1.10/1.25).

- **Tier by numbers: D.** Upside at 15%: −78.5% ($10.66 vs $49.51).
- Diagnostics: OE yield 1.4%, terminal value only 22% of IV@15% (the low IV is a base problem, not a terminal assumption), scenario spread 6.2×, net debt (engine, cash-only) −0.44× normalized OE.
- Sanity check: no scenario — not even super bull at a 30% start and a 1.25× OE multiplier — clears even a 10% IRR at $49.51. The binding constraint is the $0.70 base against a $49.51 price, not the growth debate.

## FLAGS — resolving every flag
Final-run flags (from dcf_numbers.json), plus the manual checks the Tier-F preliminary run requires:
- **STALE_FY** — FY2025 ended ~9.4 months before this run. Resolved with the filing-derived TTM base above ($0.7027/share). It raises the base vs both the median and the latest FY — and the tier is still D.
- **BASE_DIVERGENCE (manual — the engine's flag needs a positive median, so it could not fire)** — Latest FY +$0.33 vs 3-yr median −$0.58 vs TTM +$0.70. The median is the distorted one: two of its three years (2023, 2024) predate the OE inflection, when OCF was $458M/$822M against SBC of $868M/$1,016M. OCF has since more than doubled while SBC flattened, so the median measures a cost structure that no longer exists. The TTM override is used, with the reasoning written here — the same treatment as the APP inflection, and it is the most generous defensible base.
- **LOW_OE_YIELD** — Fires: 1.4% on the TTM base. Resolved by acknowledgment, not adjustment: at ~70× TTM OE the valuation rests almost entirely on future growth, which is exactly what the recalibrated scenarios and the low confidence grade reflect.
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** — Fires: super-bull IV is 6.2× super-bear IV. Real uncertainty (bookings are guided negative next quarter), and it caps confidence below high by rule.
- **DILUTION** — Fires: share count +5.0%/yr (616M in 2023 → 690M FY2025 → 714M H1 2026 YTD). Driver is SBC of ~$1.14B/yr (~20% of TTM revenue). The engine already deducted the maximum −5pp in its blend; the recalibrated scenario starts are OE/*share* growth targets set below revenue growth for the same reason. No buyback offsets this — capital allocation commentary prioritizes organic investment, with buybacks only "to offset dilution" as a possibility.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** — The >60% revenue jumps are 2020 (+82%) and 2021 (+108%) — the pandemic surge, not a divestiture, acquisition, or restatement; there is no M&A/divestiture break in the recent window (2023 +26%, 2024 +29%, 2025 +36%). Consequence: the 5-yr revenue CAGR (39.6%) straddles the pandemic step-up and overstates the forward run-rate — one more reason the scenario starts were set off bookings, not the blend.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** — Fires for 2023, 2024, 2025. Resolved: the gap is SBC, not a one-off cash item. Net income vs OCF: 2023 −$1,152M vs +$458M; 2024 −$935M vs +$822M; 2025 −$1,065M vs +$1,796M. The non-cash SBC added back in the cash-flow statement ($868M / $1,016M / $1,129M) plus D&A ($208M / $226M / $226M) and the deferred-revenue build (bookings collected ahead of recognition) explain the divergence in every named year; no tax deposit, impairment, or WC one-off is distorting the OCF-based OE — and OE subtracts SBC explicitly, so the base is not flattered by the add-back.
- **OE_VOLATILE (manual)** — OE/share spans −$1.18 → −$0.58 → +$0.33 → TTM +$0.70: a sign change, i.e. maximum volatility by construction. The normalized-median machinery exists for exactly this and was overridden only because the direction is a documented cost-structure inflection (OCF scaling, SBC flattening), not a single spike year — H1 2026 standalone OE ($335M) confirms the TTM is not one quarter's artifact.
- **BIG_NET_CASH / HIGH_NET_DEBT (manual)** — Neither fires on the engine's cash-only math (net cash $212M at FY2025). The fuller picture: at Q2 2026, cash $991M + current marketable securities $2,023M = $3,014M of liquid assets vs long-term debt $1,009M → **net cash ≈ $2,005M ≈ $2.81/share, ~5.7% of the price**. A cushion, not a thesis. Note current liabilities of $5,520M are mostly deferred revenue / creator obligations — an obligation to deliver service and DevEx payouts, not financial debt, but it is why the cash pile is not all "excess."
- **HIGH_TV_SHARE** — Does not fire: terminal value is 22% of IV@15%.
- **THIN_HISTORY** — Does not fire: 7 fiscal years (2019–2025) plus 2026 quarters.
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** — Does not fire: a gaming/UGC platform is exactly what the OE model is built for. The one model caveat is timing: OCF is bookings-driven while revenue is recognition-driven, so OE leads the income statement — handled by setting growth off bookings.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The business inflected; the stock price already paid for the next several inflections. Roblox finally prints positive owner's earnings — $0.70/share TTM, growing — and at $49.51 that is a 1.4% yield with bookings guided *down* 14–18% next quarter. The debate bulls are having (does retention-first discovery + aging-up reaccelerate bookings in 2027?) is a real debate, but it is a debate about whether the weighted IRR is ~6% or ~9%, not about clearing 15%.
- Method range, documented: median base → **F** (no DCF at all); TTM base with the engine's unrecalibrated backward-looking growth (base start 30.8%, default probabilities) → weighted IRR 8.9%, IV@15% $21.47, still **D**; TTM base with bookings-calibrated growth (final) → 5.7%, **D**. Every filing-defensible method lands at D or below. There is no defensible route to B/A here without assuming both a much larger OE base than any filing shows and a bookings reacceleration no guide supports.
- What would change the tier: bookings reaccelerating to 20%+ with SBC/revenue falling toward the low teens — that combination roughly triples OE/share in two years and would re-rate this name through the screen. Watch the bookings line and the SBC line in the Q3 2026 print, in that order; DAU growth alone does not move OE.
- Balance sheet and scale are not the problem: ~$2.0B net cash (incl. marketable securities), 123M DAUs, no funding need. The problem is purely price vs current earning power, plus a regulatory/litigation surface (child safety) that no other name at this scale carries in the same form.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: D** (= tier by numbers; no override — the TTM override already gives the company its most generous defensible base, and it still lands in D)
- Confidence: **Low** — the arithmetic is filing-exact, but the base rests on the first two positive OE periods in company history, the scenario spread is 6.2×, and the forward bookings guide contradicts the backward revenue signals the engine blends.
- Hurdle math: the price that clears 15% on the weighted case is the weighted IV@15% itself — **$10.66**, ~78% below $49.51; even the super-bull IV ($24.14) is ~51% below the price, so there is no margin-of-safety price near current levels to quote.
- One-liner: Real platform with a moderate-strong moat (6/10) that has finally inflected to positive owner's earnings ($0.70/share TTM) — but at $49.51 (~70× TTM OE, 1.4% yield) with Q3 bookings guided down 14–18%, it clears only a 5.7% weighted IRR (Tier D, IV@15% $10.66); it would need to trade near $11 to clear the 15% hurdle.
