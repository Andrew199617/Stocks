## What it is
Parent of Southern California Edison (SCE), a regulated electric utility delivering power across Southern, Central and Coastal California under an exclusive franchise. Earnings come from the rate-base loop, not volume: grid, wildfire-mitigation and transmission capital enters rate base, and the California Public Utilities Commission (CPUC) sets rates to recover costs plus an authorized return. Per the company's Q2 2026 release (search results): Q2 2026 GAAP EPS $1.39, core EPS $1.54, net income $534 million, full-year 2026 core EPS guidance of $5.90–6.20 reaffirmed, and continued confidence in 5–7% core EPS growth from 2025 to 2030. SCE forecasts $37.5–$40.6 billion of capital expenditures for 2026–2030 (search summary of the Q2 2026 10-Q).

## Moat: 6/10 — moderate, regulated and durable, but single-state and wildfire-exposed
Justification: SCE's exclusive California franchise, inelastic essential demand, and irreplaceable grid give a real regulated moat, but it is concentrated in one state and one regulator, allowed returns are capped, and California's inverse-condemnation wildfire liability sits outside the franchise's protection — a risk no diversified utility peer carries at this scale.
- FOR: Exclusive regulated franchise — no competing wires provider in SCE's territory; essential, inelastic demand for electricity; irreplaceable long-lived grid infrastructure with prohibitive replacement cost; rate-base model — authorized returns on invested capital underpin management's guided 5–7% core EPS growth from 2025 to 2030; AB 1054 Wildfire Fund and customer-funded self-insurance provide a structured recovery framework for wildfire claims.
- AGAINST: Single-state, single-regulator (CPUC) concentration — no diversification of rate-case or political risk; regulators cap allowed returns and impose rate-case lag — pricing power is granted, not owned, and affordability pressure is acute after a 13% customer rate increase in October 2025 (search results); catastrophic wildfire liability under inverse condemnation — SCE has recorded $1.6 billion of Eaton Fire settlement losses with additional material losses considered probable (search summary of the Q2 2026 10-Q), and a short-seller report has alleged a roughly $20 billion exposure scenario that is not an established liability; capex permanently exceeds operating cash flow — the $37.5–$40.6 billion 2026–2030 program is funded with debt (net debt $37,840,000,000).
- A moat of 6 would give CAP = 9.8 years under the sweep formula (growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate over the CAP, 15-year projection) — but the engine runs no projection here at all, because the normalized base is negative. The moat is recorded for comparability; it does not rescue a negative base.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 9 months ago): TTM was attempted in code as required — `ttm.py EIX` exited non-zero (exit 3) with: "ttm.py: cannot compute TTM for EIX (CIK 827052): sbc: no post-FY YTD fact (no 10-Q since the 2025-12-31 fiscal year end)". No ttm.json exists, so no TTM base could be pinned; the engine's median base was used, with this failure as the stated reason. Web reporting confirms the company did report Q2 2026 results (above), so this is a filing-facts gap in the TTM tool's SBC leg, not evidence the quarters did not happen — but per the mechanics, TTM figures are never hand-computed, so the median base stands. It does not change the verdict: a TTM base on the conservative definition would still subtract a full year of rate-base capex running ahead of OCF.
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income and operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2024): resolved — this is primarily structural depreciation, not a one-off that distorts the base. From financials_history.csv, 2024 net income was $1,284,000,000 vs OCF of $5,014,000,000 with D&A of $2,866,000,000; D&A alone accounts for most of the NI-to-OCF gap, as expected for a capital-intensive regulated utility, with regulated deferral and working-capital timing making up the character of the rest. No tax-deposit or impairment one-off is needed to explain it, and the divergence does not distort the OE base — the base is built from OCF directly. (The genuinely one-off earnings item in this history is on the net-income side in 2025 — the TKM/insurance wildfire recoveries noted above — which the OCF-based base also bypasses.)
- **SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH** (Utilities — OE/capex DCF fits utilities names poorly; grade confidence low): accepted in full. Confidence is low, and the F below is explicitly a method verdict: charging all rate-base capex against one year's OCF treats regulated investment as if it were maintenance spend. The maintenance-definition OE ($6,111,000,000 in FY2025, above) is the in-file evidence that a different, also-standard definition flips the sign. No tier override is taken — the sweep's rules assign the tier from the conservative definition — but no reader should treat this F as comparable to an F for an industrial with the same cash-flow shape.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The method range here is binary, not a spread of tiers: on the conservative definition there is no tier at all (F, no IV, no IRR at any scenario weighting); on the maintenance definition the FY2025 OE total is positive and large. That definitional flip — not growth, not the moat — is the main finding for EIX, and it is exactly what SECTOR_MODEL_MISMATCH warns about.
- Quality on regulated terms is not what the F answers: core EPS grew year over year in Q2 2026, guidance of $5.90–6.20 for 2026 was reaffirmed, and management guides 5–7% core EPS growth to 2030 off rate-base growth. None of that is capturable by an OCF-minus-all-capex screen while SCE is in a regulator-approved grid and wildfire-mitigation investment cycle ($37.5–$40.6 billion planned 2026–2030 per search results).
- The risks that actually decide this stock sit outside the OE screen: the unresolved Eaton Fire liability (recorded settlement losses of $1.6 billion, expected recoveries from self-insurance, the Wildfire Fund and FERC rates per the 10-Q summary, additional material losses probable, and an alleged ~$20 billion short-seller scenario that is not established), whether California's wildfire-liability framework is strengthened or left to lapse, CPUC affordability politics after double-digit rate increases, and a debt-funded capital program (net debt $37,840,000,000) at a consolidated debt-to-capitalization of 0.66 per the Q2 2026 10-Q summary.
- Price that clears 15%: none can be stated. With a negative normalized base the engine produces no IV@15% at any price input, so there is no hurdle-clearing price to quote from the computed outputs — saying so in words rather than calculating one, per the hard rule. At $55.68 the stock plainly does not clear a 15% owner's-earnings hurdle on this method; whether it clears a regulated-utility return hurdle is a question this sweep's model cannot answer.
