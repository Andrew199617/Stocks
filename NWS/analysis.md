## What it is
A diversified media conglomerate with four reporting segments after the Foxtel sale: **Dow Jones** (WSJ, Barron's, MarketWatch plus the Professional Information Business — Risk & Compliance and Energy data), **Digital Real Estate Services** (61%-owned REA Group in Australia; Move, operator of Realtor.com), **Book Publishing** (HarperCollins), and **News Media** (News UK, News Corp Australia, New York Post). FY2026 (ended 2026-06-30): revenue $9.028B (+6.81% YoY), Total Segment EBITDA $1.63B (+15% per the results release), operating cash flow from continuing operations $1.237B (+26%).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate, blended not consolidated
Justification: Two genuine franchises inside a conglomerate — Dow Jones (brand, 6.26M digital-only subs, Risk & Compliance/Energy B2B data with switching costs) and REA Group's Australian portal network effects, plus HarperCollins scale. But News Media (~$2.23B revenue) earns near-zero segment EBITDA, and consolidated ROE ran only 1.8–7.6% in FY22–26 excluding FY25's Foxtel-gain year. Returns below the cost of capital through the cycle cap this at moderate.
- FOR: Dow Jones — WSJ/Barron's brands with 6.26M digital-only subscriptions (Q4 FY26: WSJ digital-only 4.47M, +8%; Barron's Group 1.51M, +15%) and a growing enterprise/B2B recurring base; Professional Information Business — Risk & Compliance revenue $392M in FY26 (+16%) and Dow Jones Energy ($301M, +8%), data products with real switching costs; REA Group — Australia's dominant property portal with network effects (146.4M average monthly visits in FY26); HarperCollins — Big Five scale and a deep backlist, audiobooks/e-books growing double digits; net-cash balance sheet and $641M of FY26 buybacks.
- AGAINST: News Media (~$2.23B FY26 revenue) earns near-zero segment EBITDA (Q4 FY26: $24M, down $4M on California Post investment) — structural print decline drags consolidated returns; consolidated ROE only 1.8–7.6% across FY22–26 excluding the Foxtel-gain year — below the cost of capital through the cycle; Realtor.com is No. 2 to Zillow in the US and housing-cycle exposed; book publishing is hit-driven and platform-dependent (Amazon, Audible/Spotify), and AI content licensing (OpenAI/Meta deals) is opportunistic, not a contracted moat; dual-class Murdoch control and a persistent conglomerate discount — the segments do not reinforce each other.
- **ROIC / gross-margin evidence (required check):** fundamentals.json carries **no** roic_proxy series and **no** gross-margin series for NWS (null in every year) — News Corp tags no operating-income or COGS lines the engine can use, so those two figures are not in the computed outputs and are not quoted here. The return evidence available is ROE from fundamentals.json: FY2022 7.6%, FY2023 1.8%, FY2024 3.3%, FY2025 13.4% (inflated by the Foxtel discontinued-operations gain, see Flags), FY2026 6.7% — low-single-digit through the cycle, which is exactly why a moat above 6 is not justified despite the strong sub-assets.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (2024, 2026): explained by non-cash charges, not cash leakage. FY2024: net income attributable $266M vs OCF $1,098M — the 10-K shows impairment and restructuring charges of $133M and D&A of $440M sitting between them, plus a $206M tax charge on $585M of pretax continuing income. FY2026: net income attributable $573M (total net income $743M) vs OCF from continuing operations $1,237M — the FY2026 cash-flow statement adds back D&A of $485M, impairment charges of $27M and deferred taxes of $41M, partly offset by a $237M receivables build. In both years NI + D&A lands close to OCF. The OCF-based conservative OE series is therefore the right base and is not contaminated by the divergence. (Related, unflagged: FY2025 net income of $1,180M attributable included a $692M net-of-tax discontinued-operations gain on the Foxtel sale — one more reason the NI-based maintenance definition is not used as the base.)
- **WIDE_SCENARIO_SPREAD** (4.49x): real — the range runs from a structurally declining print conglomerate (super-bear IV@15% $4.98) to a digital-data compounder (super-bull $22.39). Confidence is capped at medium per the rule, and the verdict is sized to the conservative end. It does not change the tier: every scenario, including super bull, is Tier D at this price.
- **Basis break — Foxtel (not flagged, resolved here because it drives the growth inputs):** Foxtel was sold (to DAZN, closed April 2025) and is presented as discontinued operations; FY2023–FY2026 in financials_history.csv are on the continuing-operations basis (revenue $8.012B / $8.252B / $8.452B / $9.028B), while FY2022's $10.385B still includes Foxtel. The apparent 2022→2023 revenue drop is the restatement, not an organic collapse. Consequence: the 5-yr revenue CAGR (-0.72%) straddles the break and is not trusted; the blend's weight on the 3-yr CAGR (4.06%), latest YoY (6.81%) and OE/share CAGR (7.70%) — all continuing-basis — is what makes the 6.47% blend usable.
- **Balance sheet:** net debt $-106.0M is on the LongTermDebt basis (cash $2.095B, debt $1.989B); lease liabilities of $926M sit outside that figure and are noted here — even capitalizing them, leverage is ~1.2x latest FY conservative OE, not a fragility. ## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest tension: this is a sum-of-the-parts story priced as a conglomerate, valued here on consolidated owner's earnings. Two caveats cut in opposite directions and roughly offset. (1) The consolidated OE base includes 100% of REA Group's cash flow while ~39% of REA belongs to noncontrolling interests — that flatters the base, so the Tier D is, if anything, generous on an attributable basis. (2) A market SOTP (REA stake at market, Dow Jones at a data-multiple) has historically argued for more than the consolidated cash flows support — but that is a break-up/re-rating thesis, not an owner's-earnings yield, and the dual-class structure gives public holders no lever to force it. On the method this sweep uses, the answer is unambiguous.
- Quality is mid-tier, not poor: OE positive 5/5 years, FCF $811M in FY2026 (OCF $1.237B − capex $426M, per the release), net cash, buybacks stepped up to $641M. The problem is purely price vs cash yield: a 3.45% OE yield on the normalized base cannot clear a 15% hurdle without heroic growth, and even the super-bull path only reaches an 11.43% IRR.
- Method range: using the latest-FY base ($1.31/share) instead of the median would raise every IV by roughly the base ratio and still leave the weighted IV@15% far below $32.83 — the tier does not flip on any defensible base choice. Tier D is robust, not assumption-fragile.
