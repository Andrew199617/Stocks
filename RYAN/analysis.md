# RYAN — Ryan Specialty Holdings, Inc.

NYSE: RYAN · Finance / Insurance brokerage & underwriting management (SIC 6411) · Price $41.91 · Market cap $11,694,105,390 (dcf_numbers.json)

## What it is

A specialty-insurance distribution platform, founded 2010 and IPO'd in 2021 in an Up-C structure: the public company is sole managing member of Ryan Specialty, LLC and owned 49.1% of the LLC Common Units at December 31, 2025 (Form 10-K, Note 1); the rest is held by noncontrolling LLC unitholders whose units are exchangeable 1-for-1 into Class A stock (paired with Class B shares). It operates three channels — Wholesale Brokerage, Binding Authority and Underwriting Management (MGUs/program administrators with delegated authority) — across commercial, industrial, institutional, governmental and personal lines, and, in the 10-K's own words, "with the exception of the Company's equity method investments, the Company does not take on any underwriting risk": carriers keep the risk, Ryan keeps commissions and fees. FY2025 revenue was $2,994,582,000 (+21.9% YoY, financials_history.csv). The growth engine has been acquisitions: business combinations consumed $446,682,000 (FY2023), $1,708,737,000 (FY2024) and $746,485,000 (FY2025) of investing cash (10-K Consolidated Statements of Cash Flows), funded with the Term Loan B, the 2030/2032 senior secured notes and the revolver. The 2026 prints show the model downshifting: Q2 2026 total revenue grew 7.2% YoY to $916.6 million on 6.7% organic growth, Adjusted EBITDAC was $326.9 million at a 35.7% margin and adjusted diluted EPS $0.74 (Q2 2026 earnings release); management guides FY2026 organic growth to mid-single digits (toward the upper end), the CFO said a meaningful acquisition is unlikely to close in 2026, and the multi-year Empower restructuring programme absorbed $39.3 million of costs in H1 2026. Capital returns stepped up instead: ~8.1 million shares repurchased for $260 million in Q2 2026 and the buyback authorization raised by $300 million, with company-reported net leverage ~3.3x at quarter-end.

## OVERRIDE — shares (filing; the Up-C denominator)

- **The gap:** EDGAR companyfacts carries no diluted share fact for this filer in any year (`shares_diluted` blank for FY2019–FY2025), so the preliminary run exited DATA_GAP:shares with no tier.
- **Why the obvious fix is wrong:** the 10-K EPS note's GAAP diluted count — 138,246,414 for FY2025 (Note 11) — divides *attributable* earnings. It excludes the 135,429,254 LLC Common Units the same note lists as antidilutive for FY2025, even though consolidated OCF/capex/SBC cover 100% of the LLC including those unitholders' share. The GAAP denominator also flips with the antidilution test quarter to quarter (Q3 2025 diluted was 273,462k with the units included; the nine-month figure was 138,090k with them excluded). Dividing consolidated OE by 138.2M would roughly double OE/share and print a fake tier — the VNOM/Up-C failure shape, caught here before it shipped.
- **The decision:** `shares_override = 279,029,000, kind "filing"` — the company's own FY2025 *adjusted* diluted share count from its Q4 2025 earnings release (Exhibit 99.1 to Form 8-K, 2026-02-12): Class A diluted 138,246k + impact of all LLC Common Units exchanged 135,429k + dilutive impact of unvested equity awards 5,354k = 279,029k. Cross-checks: the 10-K cover page shows 264,189,701 shares outstanding at February 9, 2026 (Class A 129,680,816 + Class B 134,508,885 — basic total-economic shares, before award dilution), and FY2024's adjusted count in the same release is 276,288k.
- **Known approximations, disclosed:** the engine applies the single FY2025 value to every missing year, so FY2024 OE/share is understated ~1.0% against its own 276,288k count (conservative for the median) and FY2023 by a similar small amount; the TTM denominator likewise uses 279,029,000 where H1 2026's own adjusted count was 270,080k after Q2 2026 buybacks (~3% conservative). The override is flagged OVERRIDE_OUT_OF_BAND by construction — there is no filed value to anchor the 0.1x–10x band (last_filed_value is null) — and judgment.json carries the ack_reason saying exactly that; make_score's measured impact is in the registry (the override unblocks the tier: without it there is no score at all).

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow

Justification: a scaled specialty-insurance distribution platform — wholesale brokerage, binding authority and delegated-authority MGUs — whose carrier appointments, placement volume and E&S product breadth (300+ specialty products, Q2 2026 call) take years to replicate, converting revenue to owner's earnings at an 18–20% margin on almost no physical capex. It is not wider: the producing asset walks out the door, rivals hold comparable scale, and the ROIC record no longer supports a strong-moat claim (below).

- FOR: scale — FY2025 revenue $2,994,582,000 across the three channels, one of the largest US specialty distribution platforms; delegated underwriting authority granted by carriers on loss-ratio track records and programme data, earned over years; structural E&S tailwind as complexity pushes risks out of the admitted market into the channels Ryan serves; asset-light cash conversion (conservative OE margin 20.1% of revenue FY2023, 17.7% FY2024, 19.1% FY2025, fundamentals.json).
- AGAINST: a people business — producers are portable, customers not contractually locked in, Amwins/CRC and carrier-direct channels compete for the same flow; **ROIC falling — roic_proxy 18.0% (FY2022), 17.3% (FY2023), 10.7% (FY2024), 9.4% (FY2025) (fundamentals.json), with operating margin flat in a 16.5–17.7% band and no filed gross-profit line (revenue is net commissions/fees): growth is being bought at rising prices, not earned on cheap capital** — a moat of 7+ would need ROIC sustainably well above the cost of capital, and Ryan's measured history heads the other way; leverage (net debt 7.5x the TTM OE total, plus TRA and contingent-consideration claims); E&S cycle softening (property pricing declines, builders-risk weakness, new capacity) already visible in the prints; Up-C claims ahead of Class A holders ($64,126,000 of tax distributions to noncontrolling unitholders and $25,169,000 of TRA payments in FY2025 alone, 10-K cash-flow statement).
- Fade: CAP = clamp(2 + 1.3 × 5, 2, 15) = **8.5 years**; growth holds 2 years, then converges to the 2.5% terminal rate.

## My thoughts / overlay

- **The method range is C→D, and the swing factor is growth, not the base.** The preliminary run on the normalised base ($1.3937) with the engine's unadjusted 21.2% blend and default probabilities printed a weighted IRR of 10.89% and IV@15% of $26.23 (Tier C). The final run uses a *higher* base (TTM $1.5209) yet prints 7.45% / $15.81 (Tier D), because the scenario starts were recalibrated to what the company is actually printing and guiding (single-digit organic) instead of its acquisition-era CAGR. Anyone quoting a single RYAN number without its growth basis is hiding the ball; on no defensible basis does it clear 15% at $41.91.
- **The OE definition is generous to a roll-up, and the verdict is D anyway.** Owner's earnings charge only $70,967,000 of physical capex; the $746,485,000 of FY2025 business combinations (and the $274,426,000/yr of resulting intangible amortisation) are treated as growth investment outside the base. For a broker whose growth model *is* buying books of business, a maintenance-capex view that charged even part of acquisition spend would cut the base hard — the falling ROIC (18.0% → 9.4%) is that cost showing up in another column. The D here is printed on the kind definition; the unkind one is worse.
- What is genuinely good: a scaled, diversified specialty platform with a structural E&S tailwind, ~19% OE margins, a buyback now retiring stock ($260 million in Q2 2026), and a restructuring programme aimed at the cost base. None of that is the debate — the debate is price: at $41.91 the market capitalises this as if the acquisition-era growth were organic and permanent.
- Hurdle math: the stock clears Andrew's 15% hurdle at the weighted IV@15% of **$15.81** — 62% below the $41.91 price; even the base-case IV@15% ($15.66) and the bull-case IV@15% ($21.16) sit far below it.

## Final verdict

- **Final tier: D** (weighted IRR 7.45% on the TTM base; tier by numbers D, no tier override, bear and bull cases also D)
- Confidence: **low** (sector model misfit, wide scenario spread, high leverage, and a share denominator that rests on a filing-sourced override for a total EDGAR gap)
- One-liner: Scaled specialty-insurance distribution platform, moat 5/10 — but its growth was bought with debt-funded acquisitions and has decelerated to single digits: on the TTM base of $1.52/share the weighted IRR is 7.45% (Tier D) at $41.91 with weighted IV@15% $15.81; it clears the 15% hurdle only below ~$15.81, and even the super-bull case (11.66% IRR) does not clear 15% at this price.
