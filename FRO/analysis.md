# FRO — Frontline plc
Engine 5.4 pass-1 run, 2026-10-18. Cyprus-incorporated crude and product tanker owner, 20-F filer reporting under IFRS in USD; ordinary shares listed directly on the NYSE and the Oslo Stock Exchange under FRO. In scope (market cap est. $11.685B at $52.49, nasdaq_quote_api).

All figures below are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, or financials_history.csv / fundamentals.json — none are recomputed. Figures marked "search-cited" are from company releases and reporting found in web search; they are narrative context only and were NOT used in the scoring, because they are not in this run's code outputs.

## What it is
The largest publicly listed pure-play tanker owner: VLCCs (200,000–320,000 dwt), Suezmax tankers and LR2/Aframax product tankers, run predominantly on spot charter exposure. The fleet was reshaped in October 2023, when Frontline agreed to buy 24 modern VLCCs from Euronav NV, taking the fleet from 65 to 89 vessels (search-cited company release). It sells carrying capacity at a market rate it does not set.

## Moat: 3/10 — weak
Justification: a spot tanker owner is a pure price taker — charterers pay the market time-charter-equivalent rate for a fungible vessel; there are no switching costs, no network effects and no pricing power, and Frontline deliberately runs mostly spot exposure. The return history proves the earnings are the cycle, not a franchise: roic_proxy in fundamentals.json was −5.82% in FY2017 and 0.25% in FY2021, then 10.13% / 13.76% / 13.57% / 11.06% in FY2022–FY2025 as spot rates spiked — above the cost of capital only in upcycle years. Gross margin is not separately tagged in its IFRS filings (fundamentals.json carries it as null), so operating margin is the margin evidence: 1.9% in FY2021, 31.0% in FY2022, 40.9% in FY2023, 36.1% in FY2024, 30.4% in FY2025 — a rate series, not a margin structure.
- FOR: Scale — largest publicly listed pure-play tanker owner after the October 2023 purchase of 24 VLCCs from Euronav, which took the fleet from 65 to 89 vessels (search-cited company release); Modern ECO fleet with a large scrubber-fitted share and low vessel operating costs (Q2 2026 fleetwide opex about $8,700/day excluding dry-docking, search-cited company release) — a low cash breakeven through troughs; Counter-cyclical fleet trading and capital access: anchor shareholder Hemen/Fredriksen, and older VLCCs sold into the strong 2026 market (search-cited)
- AGAINST: Pure spot price taker — TCE rates are set by the market for a fungible vessel; no pricing power, no customer lock-in, deliberate spot exposure; Returns are the cycle, not a franchise: roic_proxy −5.82% FY2017 and 0.25% FY2021 vs 13.76% FY2023 (fundamentals.json); operating margin 1.9% FY2021 vs 40.9% FY2023 on spot rates alone; The fleet is wasting capital: replacement capex is huge and lumpy — $1,631.4M in FY2023 and $915.2M in FY2024 (financials_history.csv) — and nine VLCC newbuilding contracts were agreed in 2026 for $1,224.0M with $601.1M of commitments remaining at Q2 2026 (search-cited); Leverage amplifies the cycle: net debt $2,816.4M at FY2025 (dcf_numbers.json) against a normalized owner's-earnings base that is negative; The dividend is a pass-through of spot earnings, not a through-cycle return — it was zero in FY2021 (fundamentals.json dividend notes)
- A 3 gives CAP = 5.9 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 5.9 years, 15-year projection. Moot here — with no positive base there is nothing to fade.

## DCF — engine 5.4 final run (2026-10-18)
**No scenario table exists, by design.** The engine's data gate refuses to project a DCF off a non-positive normalized base: tier_by_numbers is F, f_subtype `no_cash_generation` (dcf_numbers.json basis: the sum of the last 3 fiscal years — the median window — of OCF minus capex is not positive), implied_irr_weighted and IV are null. Probabilities passed were the defaults (10/25/30/25/10); they change nothing when the base is negative. Inventing scenario IVs off the single low-capex year (FY2025, +$2.98/share) would be exactly the fake-precision failure the gate exists to prevent — FY2025's capex of $12.5M is a gap between delivery programmes, not a run rate, as the $601.1M of remaining newbuilding commitments at Q2 2026 (search-cited) already shows.
- Growth sanity check (mandatory): the engine's growth blend of 9.75% (components: rev CAGR 5y 35.70% · rev CAGR 3y 11.08% · latest rev YoY −8.85%, key_numbers.md) is basis-break and rate-cycle arithmetic, not a growth rate — the 5-yr CAGR starts in the FY2021 rate trough and straddles the Euronav consolidation (see BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT). The blend was not used; under Tier F no growth input can change the verdict.
- The honest quantitative statement the numbers support: at $52.49, the market is paying for the 2026 spot-rate super-cycle (see STALE_FY). On the normalized base there is no price at which a DCF can be run at all; on the single best filed year (FY2025, OE/share $2.98) the earnings power is a fraction of the price, and that year carried almost no fleet capex. A buyer is long tanker spot rates and fleet trading, not long through-cycle owner's earnings.

## Flags — every fragility flag resolved
- **STALE_FY** (latest fiscal year ended 10 months ago): RESOLVED WITH THE CAVEAT STATED. ttm.py was run and failed as the spec predicts for an IFRS filer: "cannot compute TTM for FRO (CIK 913290): ocf: no annual (FY) fact; sbc: no annual (FY) fact; shares_diluted: no annual (FY) fact; capex: no capex facts in companyfacts under any candidate tag" — ttm.py reads us-gaap facts only, so the median base was used, as instructed. Interim context the engine cannot score (search-cited, company Q2 2026 release): Q2 2026 was the company's best quarterly profit ever — net profit $659.2M, or $2.96/share, adjusted profit $580.2M, or $2.61/share, on revenue of $943.3M, with spot TCEs of $152,700/day for VLCCs, $111,500 for Suezmax and $92,400 for LR2/Aframax, and a declared dividend of $2.61/share plus a $0.80 special dividend tied to vessel sales; Q1 2026 was likewise far above the filed window. The F verdict is therefore a verdict on normalized, filed annual numbers through a fleet-replacement window; it is explicitly not a claim that the trailing twelve months are loss-making at 2026 spot rates. A base_override built from interim filings is outside a worker's bounded authority (it could not be built exclusively from this run's code outputs, which contain no interim OE legs) — if a current-base read is wanted, that is a main-agent decision.
- **BASIS_BREAK_SUSPECT** (>60% revenue jump/drop in the last 5 yrs): NAMED — two breaks, neither organic growth. (1) The rate break: revenue in the engine's series rose from $640,327,000 in FY2021 to $1,438,248,000 in FY2022 (financials_history.csv), a jump driven by the spot TCE spike that followed the rerouting of crude flows in 2022, on essentially the same fleet. (2) The consolidation break: the purchase of 24 VLCCs from Euronav NV, agreed in October 2023 and delivered from late 2023 (search-cited company release), took the fleet from 65 to 89 vessels and is the reason capex printed $1,631.4M in FY2023 and $915.2M in FY2024 and net debt rose to $3,148.1M at FY2023 (financials_history.csv). Consequence: every CAGR in the blend straddles one or both breaks, the 9.75% blend is not a usable growth input, and no growth input was relied on (moot under Tier F).
- **NI_OCF_DIVERGENCE** (net income vs operating cash flow differ by >40% in 2025): RESOLVED — the bridge is non-cash vessel depreciation. FY2025 net income was $379,081,000 against operating cash flow of $682,460,000 (financials_history.csv); depreciation and amortisation for FY2025 is $328.5M on a search-cited reading of the company's cash-flow statement — the depreciation load of an ~80-vessel fleet, charged in net income and added back in operating cash flow. The engine's own outputs cannot quote that figure (the `da` column is blank for FY2022–FY2025, see Owner's earnings), so it is stated here in words as the search-cited bridge rather than as a computed number. No tax-deposit, impairment or working-capital one-off is needed to explain the gap, and it does not contaminate the base: the conservative OE starts from OCF, not net income.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The F is the method working as intended on a hard case, with one real distortion named: the median window (FY2023–FY2025) is precisely the window in which Frontline paid for the Euronav fleet, so the normalized base charges the full purchase price of 24 VLCCs against three years of operating cash flow while crediting only part of their earnings life. That is the conservative definition doing its job — a ship bought is cash gone — but it means the F reads as "no through-cycle owner's earnings demonstrated on filed annual numbers", not as "this fleet earns nothing". Operating cash flow was positive in every year of the window; the window's capex was simply larger (the engine's f_subtype basis is exactly that: the sum of the last 3 fiscal years of OCF minus capex is not positive), and over a longer window that included the ships' full earnings life the arithmetic would look different.
- What the F does NOT capture, stated plainly: (1) 2026 spot rates are at generational highs on Strait of Hormuz / Red Sea disruption and longer voyages (search-cited Q2 2026 release and reporting), and Frontline's unhedged spot exposure converts them into record profit and a $2.61/share quarterly dividend plus a $0.80 special — cash returns that are real but entirely rate-dependent; (2) the fleet has been renewed into that strength — eight oldest first-generation ECO VLCCs sold for $831.5M with delivery in Q1 2026 and nine latest-generation scrubber-fitted VLCC newbuilding contracts bought from Hemen for $1,224.0M, deliveries from late 2026 through Q2 2027 (search-cited) — so the capex bill that produced this F is about to repeat; (3) the balance sheet at the filing date (net debt $2.816B) had already improved by Q2 2026, with borrowings reported down to $2,434.8M (search-cited).
- The structural question the DCF cannot settle is the mid-cycle TCE rate. Frontline's own history spans operating margins of 1.9% (FY2021) to 40.9% (FY2023) on the same basic business; a buyer at $52.49 — against FY2025 book equity of $2,510,878,000 (financials_history.csv) and up from a 52-week low of $20.47 (search-cited) — is underwriting something close to current rates persisting, while the orderbook, a Suez/Hormuz normalisation, or a crude-demand downturn would all cut them at once.
- Method range: there isn't one to quote — every defensible base this run can construct (3-yr median −$0.79/share; latest FY +$2.98/share on a near-zero-capex year) fails to support a DCF at $52.49. The verdict is robust to the base choice even though the business's current run-rate is not.

## Final verdict
- **Final tier: F** (no positive normalized owner's earnings — OE DCF not supportable; f_subtype no_cash_generation; no tier override)
- Confidence: **Low** (IFRS filer with no TTM path, a consolidation capex window inside the median base, and 2026 interim prints far above the filed base — the F is solid as a normalized-base statement and explicitly not a current-run-rate statement)
- One-liner: Spot tanker price-taker, moat 3/10 — Tier F: no positive normalized owner's earnings (3-yr median OE/share -$0.79; OE positive in only 2 of the last 5 years) because Euronav-era fleet capex swamps operating cash flow in the median window, so the OE DCF produces no IV and no price clears a 15% hurdle on normalized numbers; the $52.49 price is a bet on the 2026 spot-rate super-cycle (record Q2 2026 profit, search-cited), not on through-cycle owner's earnings.
