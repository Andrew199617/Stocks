## What it is
Detroit-based regulated utility holding company. DTE Electric is an electric utility serving approximately 2.3 million customers in southeastern Michigan (generation, purchase, distribution and sale of electricity; regulated by the MPSC, FERC, NRC and EPA — FY2025 10-K, Electric segment description). DTE Gas is the regulated gas utility; DTE Vantage runs non-utility businesses (renewable natural gas and custom energy solutions); Energy Trading is the power marketing arm (segment operating results in the FY2025 10-K and Q2 2026 reporting, search results). Essentially all earnings are regulated or contract-backed. Management's plan is rate-base compounding: a $36.5B capital investment plan for 2026-2030, 2026 operating EPS guidance of $7.59 to $7.73 (reaffirmed after Q2 2026, with management saying in September 2026 it is positioned for the high end), and a long-term operating EPS growth target of 6% to 8% through 2030 (search results).

## Moat: 6/10 — durable regulated monopoly, capped economics
Justification: Franchised regulated monopolies — DTE Electric (approximately 2.3 million customers in southeastern Michigan, per the FY2025 10-K) and DTE Gas — with captive, essential-service demand, plus DTE Vantage (renewable natural gas / custom energy solutions) and Energy Trading. The rate-base model converts the $36.5B 2026-2030 capital plan into regulated earnings (management guides 6-8% operating EPS growth through 2030, search results), and large-load data-center agreements (1.4 GW Oracle approved and under construction; 1.0 GW Google executed, pending MPSC approval) add visible load growth. Capped at 6 because the MPSC sets the allowed return and has disallowed previously recorded capex (write-offs recorded in the FY2025 10-K), there is no pricing power beyond rate cases, and the plan is funded with debt (net debt $25,098M, dcf_numbers.json) plus planned equity issuance of $500-600M per year through 2028 (search results) that dilutes per-share compounding (share-count CAGR +1.84%/yr, dcf_numbers.json).
- FOR: Franchised regulated electric and gas monopolies in Michigan — DTE Electric serves ~2.3M customers in southeastern Michigan; captive, essential-service demand; the rate-base model turns the $36.5B 2026-2030 capital plan into authorized-return earnings, with 6-8% operating EPS growth guided through 2030; large-load growth is contracted (1.4 GW Oracle approved/under construction, 1.0 GW Google executed pending MPSC approval); recovery mechanisms (including the Infrastructure Recovery Mechanism for distribution spend) shorten the lag between capex and rate recovery.
- AGAINST: The MPSC sets allowed ROE and recovery and has disallowed capex before — the FY2025 10-K records write-offs of previously recorded capital expenditures from the December 2023 rate order (in 2023) and the January 2025 rate order (in 2024); affordability politics as data-center and clean-energy costs are recovered from existing customers; net debt $25,098M plus $500-600M/yr of planned equity issuance through 2028 dilutes the rate-base growth per share; coal retirement (all coal by 2032 per the 2026 Integrated Resource Plan, search results) and clean-energy mandates force transition capex whose full recovery is not guaranteed.
- A 6 gives CAP = 9.8 years by the sweep formula — academic here, since there is no positive OE stream to fade.

## My thoughts / overlay
- This is the model's known blind spot, flagged as such — the same shape as AEE in this sweep: a profitable, dividend-paying regulated monopoly whose conservative owner's earnings are negative in all of the last 5 years, and on TTM, because utility capex runs above operating cash flow every year of a multi-year rate-base build (here supercharged by a $36.5B five-year plan and guided 2026 capex of $6.8B against $3.9B of guided operating cash flow, search results). The dividend and the build are funded the utility way — rate recovery plus continuous debt and equity issuance — which an OE screen reads as negative distributable cash.
- On numbers a utility investor actually uses (from search results, not the engine): 2026 operating EPS guided $7.59-$7.73 at a $128.31 price, long-term operating EPS growth guided 6-8% through 2030, and a declared quarterly dividend of $1.165 per share (annualized $4.66). That is a bond-like high-single-digit total-return profile before any multiple change — nowhere near Andrew's 15% hurdle on any defensible reading, so the F here agrees with the hurdle conclusion even though the model's mechanism (negative OE) is a sector artifact. No method range spanning tiers is claimed: on the v5 method, with the filing's own capex and SBC figures applied, there is one result, F. (The only "range" is the discredited one: zero-filling the missing capex/SBC manufactures a 25.17% weighted IRR on the measured counterfactual — that is the v4 failure mode the data gate exists to prevent, not a defensible input set.)
- Hurdle-clearing price: not computable from this model — with no positive OE base there is no IV@15% at any price, and stating one would require a different model (dividend / rate-base), which is out of scope for this sweep. No tier override: the numbers tier F is retained, with the mismatch documented, rather than inventing a tier the v5 method cannot support.
- Real risks for a holder (outside the model): MPSC disallowances and affordability pushback as data-center and clean-energy costs are recovered, approval risk on the 1.0 GW Google agreement, coal-retirement execution (all coal by 2032), and refinancing a $25,098M net-debt stack at current rates while issuing $500-600M/yr of new equity.
