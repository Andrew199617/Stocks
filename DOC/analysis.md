All figures are copied from key_numbers.md, dcf_numbers.json, financials_history.csv, fundamentals.json or the cited Q2 2026 reporting — none are recomputed.

## What it is
A fully integrated healthcare REIT (S&P 500, SIC 6798): outpatient medical buildings, lab / life-science buildings, and senior housing (the Janus Living platform, IPO'd in 2026 with Healthpeak retaining control). On March 1, 2024 it completed its merger with Physicians Realty Trust, adding 299 outpatient medical buildings — the event that breaks the per-share history (see DILUTION). Q2 2026 (release, Aug 4, 2026): total revenues $771.6M (+11.1% YoY, helped by senior-housing consolidation), FFO as Adjusted $0.46/share — flat YoY; same-store adjusted NOI +1.8% (lab −3.2%); outpatient occupancy 90.7%, lab occupancy 78.5%; full-year 2026 FFO as Adjusted guidance raised twice to $1.73–$1.77/share.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-moderate
Justification: scarce on-/near-campus outpatient locations, health-system relationships and post-merger scale are real but replicable with capital; there is no pricing power beyond contractual escalators. The measured history argues against more: the `roic_proxy` series in fundamentals.json is null for every year (the filer tags no operating-income line the proxy needs, so no ROIC evidence can be quoted — itself a reason not to award a high moat on faith), gross margin ran 58.1% / 58.6% / 60.2% in FY2022–24 (fundamentals.json annual) with no expansion, OE/share fell from $1.57 (FY2016) to $0.49 (FY2025), Q2 2026 FFO as Adjusted was flat YoY, and lab occupancy is 78.5%.
- FOR: scarce healthcare locations (on/near hospital campuses, core lab clusters) that are supply-constrained and slow to replicate; health-system relationships and scale — the Physicians Realty merger made it one of the largest outpatient medical landlords, and Q2 2026 executed 1.6M sq ft of new/renewal leases at 90.7% outpatient occupancy; long leases with contractual escalators and a rent roll diversified across outpatient, lab and senior housing (Janus Living revenue +45% YoY in Q2 2026); investment-grade balance sheet with 4.7x net debt / adjusted EBITDAre and proven capital-recycling access ($1.025B Brookfield outpatient JV recap, July 2026).
- AGAINST: no structural pricing power — rent resets to local market supply/demand and tenants can relocate; lab oversupply (occupancy 78.5%, same-store lab NOI −3.2% YoY) whose recovery depends on biotech funding, not Healthpeak; a weak through-cycle per-share record (OE/share $1.57 → $0.49, FY2021 dividend cut, DPS $2.10 in FY2016 vs $1.22 TTM); leverage/rate exposure — REIT values are hostage to cap rates and refinancing costs.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

The normalized median base is used and the staleness is disclosed; no base override was declared, because a TTM OE built on an invented capex leg would violate the bounded authority (no filing source for the missing leg was located).

- **OE_VOLATILE** (OE/share spans >3x over 5 years): real on the filed series (−$0.16 to $0.49 across FY2022–25, with the merger break and development-capex swings inside it). Handled by the normalized median base and by shifting scenario weight bear-ward (45% on bear-or-worse vs 35% in the defaults).

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the *method range*, and here it is unusually wide because the model is the wrong tool: engine OE basis → weighted IRR 4.90%, IV@15% $3.66. REIT-native basis → FY2025 AFFO ~$1.69/share (external reporting) is an ~8.9% AFFO yield at $18.98, the dividend alone yields 6.4% (DPS TTM $1.22), and per-share FFO is guided essentially flat ($1.73–$1.77 for 2026, FFO as Adjusted flat YoY in Q2 2026). An AFFO-yield-plus-growth total return lands in the high single digits — better than the engine's print, still nowhere near a 15% hurdle. **Both methods agree on the tier; they disagree only on how bad it is.** That agreement is what makes a D verdict safe to record at low confidence.
- What the buyer at $18.98 is actually underwriting: a 6.4% monthly-paid dividend, lab occupancy recovering from 78.5%, Janus Living scaling inside the consolidated group, and capital recycling (Brookfield recap, $1.75B of proceeds year-to-date through Aug 3, 2026 per the Q2 release) funding buybacks below intrinsic value. That is an income-plus-modest-growth proposition, not a 15% compounder, and management's own guidance (flat FFO/share) says so.
- Risks: lab oversupply working off slowly if biotech funding stays tight; senior-housing operating leverage inside Janus; interest expense already rising (Q2 2026 interest $92,280,000 vs $75,063,000 a year earlier in the filed 10-Q); transaction/change-of-control gains flattering 2026 GAAP income (H1 2026 net income includes gains on the SWF senior-housing JV consolidation and an outpatient disposition, per the Q2 2026 release) — none of which changes the cash picture.
- Hurdle math, stated plainly: on engine numbers the stock clears 15% only at $3.66 (20% at $2.62, 25% at $2.04 — key_numbers.md). Those prices are artifacts of charging growth capex to OE and should **not** be quoted as real entry points; on the AFFO basis there is no plausible near-term price-independent path to 15% either, since the starting yield plus guided growth sums to roughly 9–11% even before multiple risk.
