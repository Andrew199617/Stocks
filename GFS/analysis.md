## What it is
The largest US-headquartered pure-play semiconductor foundry: wafer manufacturing on specialty/mature process platforms (RF SOI, silicon germanium, silicon photonics/Fotonix, FD-SOI, power) for automotive, industrial, IoT, smart-mobile and communications/data-center customers, from fabs in Malta NY, Dresden, Singapore and Vermont. It deliberately does not chase leading-edge nodes. Q2 2026 (per the company's reported results): revenue $1.786B, +6% YoY, operating cash flow $405M, cash/equivalents/marketable securities ~$3.3B; communications infrastructure & data center revenue +62% YoY on AI optical networking demand, guided +50–60% for FY2026, partly offset by weaker automotive and smart-mobile demand. Q3 2026 guidance: revenue $1.885B ± $25M.

## Moat: 5/10 — moderate/narrow
Justification: A differentiated specialty foundry, not a scale leader: GF exited the leading-edge race and leads in specialty platforms (RF SOI, silicon germanium, silicon photonics/Fotonix, FD-SOI, BCD power) designed into long-life automotive, industrial, IoT and communications programs, where re-qualification makes switching slow and expensive, and its US/EU/Singapore footprint is a scarce secure-supply asset that governments fund (CHIPS awards and LOIs). Against that: roughly 3% pure-play foundry revenue share against TSMC's ~73% (Q2 2026), share slipping from 4%, no leading-edge offering, mature-node price competition from UMC/SMIC and Chinese capacity, and returns that have not held through a cycle (net losses in 2019-20, a $935M Malta impairment driving the 2024 net loss, owner's earnings negative in the 2022 capex peak). Net: moderate/narrow moat, 5/10.
- FOR: Specialty platform leadership (RF SOI, SiGe, silicon photonics/Fotonix, FD-SOI, power BCD) in nodes the leading-edge players largely vacated; high switching costs — designs are qualified into long-life automotive/industrial/IoT programs and are slow and costly to re-qualify at another fab; scarce geographically diversified secure supply (Malta NY, Dresden, Singapore, Vermont) backed by US/EU government funding — CHIPS awards/LOIs including $375M quantum and a $300M silicon-photonics LOI in 2026; AI data-center optical demand inflecting (communications infrastructure & data center revenue +62% YoY in Q2 2026, guided +50–60% for FY2026, silicon photonics revenue expected to more than double).
- AGAINST: Subscale — ~3% pure-play foundry revenue share vs TSMC ~73% in Q2 2026, slipped from 4% in 2025, no scale or cost leadership; no leading-edge nodes, so the industry's fastest-growing wafer demand is structurally out of reach; mature-node price competition from UMC, SMIC and expanding Chinese capacity caps pricing power; capex intensity eats the cash flow (OE negative in 2022, returns on capital mid-single digits); cyclical end markets — automotive and smart-mobile weakness offset data-center strength in the 2026 prints, and the 2023-24 downturn cut revenue from $8,108M to $6,750M.
- A 5 gives CAP = 8.5 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 8.5 years, 15-year projection.
