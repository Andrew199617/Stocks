# RRX — Regal Rexnord Corporation
Pass-1 run, 2026-10-18, engine 5.4.0. Price $154.01 (nasdaq_quote_api), market cap $10.257B — in scope under $40B. All figures below are copied from dcf_numbers.json, ttm.json, fundamentals.json, or financials_history.csv — none are recomputed.

## What it is
Milwaukee industrial that powers, transmits and controls motion, built by deal-making: Regal Beloit merged with Rexnord's Process & Motion Control business in 2021, then acquired Altra Industrial Motion for ~$4.95B in March 2023, then sold the bulk of its Industrial Systems segment (industrial motors & generators) to WEG in 2024. Three segments remain (FY2025 revenue $5,934.5M total): Industrial Powertrain Solutions (IPS — gear drives, couplings, bearings, brakes, conveying; the largest), Automation & Motion Control (AMC — Kollmorgen/Portescap/Thomson precision motors, drives and linear motion from Altra), and Power Efficiency Solutions (PES — motors and air-moving for HVAC/pool). End markets: discrete automation, data centers (including its ePOD modular power ramp), aerospace & defense, food & beverage, mining, commercial/residential HVAC. Competitors: ABB, Siemens, Emerson, Rockwell, Timken, SKF, Gates. A new CEO, Aamir Paul, took over July 1, 2026.

## Moat: 4/10 — weak-to-moderate, installed-base not pricing-power
Justification: A real installed-base/aftermarket moat in powertrain components where downtime costs dwarf component price, but no essential IP, PES is close to commodity motors, and a decade of deal-making has produced ROIC persistently below the cost of capital — the measured history does not support a higher score.
- Evidence (fundamentals.json, annual): ROIC proxy ran 7.2% (FY2021), 3.6% (FY2022), 7.2% (FY2022 on the Dec year-end basis), 2.5% (FY2023), 4.5% (FY2024), 4.9% (FY2025) — never sustainably above a plausible cost of capital, and the Altra deal's stated aim of raising ROIC has not yet shown up in the series. Gross margin is the bright spot: 27.7% (FY2021) → 29.2% → 32.0% → 33.1% (FY2023) → 36.3% (FY2024) → 37.4% (FY2025), TTM 37.75% — real mix improvement from exiting low-margin motors and adding Altra's precision portfolio, plus pricing. Margin up, returns on the enlarged capital base still low: that is a portfolio still digesting $4.95B of acquired goodwill/intangibles, not a wide moat.
- FOR: Installed base of mission-critical components (gearboxes, couplings, bearings, brakes) where failure cost >> part cost, so spec-in positions and aftermarket demand are sticky; breadth — the ability to sell a complete powertrain/motion subsystem (cross-sell across the Rexnord + Altra portfolios) rather than a part; application engineering, distribution and service network built over decades; AMC precision brands (Kollmorgen, Portescap, Thomson) with genuine design-in positions in medical, robotics, aerospace & defense and data-center power.
- AGAINST: The company itself discloses no individual patent or group of patents is essential to the business — the moat is execution and installed base, not protected IP; PES motors/fans compete on price against global giants (ABB, Siemens) and ride weak residential HVAC/pool demand (PES organic sales −6.6% in Q2 2026); giants with deeper pockets contest every segment (Rockwell/Emerson in automation, Timken/SKF in bearings/powertrain); ROIC of 2.5–4.9% over FY2023–25 proves pricing power has not converted to capital returns through this cycle; leverage (net debt 7.9x normalized OE) leaves no room for a bad cycle.
- A 4 gives CAP = 7.2 years: growth holds 2 years, then converges linearly to the 2.5% terminal rate over 7.2 years, 15-year projection.

## My thoughts / overlay
- The honest headline is the method range, and unusually all three candidate bases tell the same verdict story at $154.01: on the median base the weighted IRR is 8.47% (Tier D); the preliminary engine run (median base, un-recalibrated 10.12% growth) printed 10.71% (Tier C) — that C is bought entirely with acquisition-inflated growth; on the TTM base ($6.56/sh) the result would be lower still. No defensible method gets this price near the 15% hurdle — the hurdle price on the final run is $72.69.
- What the market is paying for is visible in Q2 2026: orders +8.8%, AMC orders +17.1% with data-center strength, adjusted EPS $2.99 (including a $0.39 IEEPA tariff-refund benefit — a one-off; $48M / $0.57 per share of refunds is baked into 2026 guidance), gross margin still expanding, and an ePOD data-center ramp mostly in 2027+. Some of that is real. But adjusted EPS of $10.60 guided for 2026 sits far above GAAP EPS guidance of $5.42–$5.92 — the gap is the amortization/restructuring the OE method refuses to wave away, and cash conversion (TTM OE $6.56/sh) currently agrees with GAAP, not with adjusted.
- Quality check: SBC is genuinely small ($27.9M TTM, <1% of revenue) — no dilution games; share count is flat (66.6M → 66.8M diluted). The problem is not the income statement's honesty, it is the balance sheet and the price: $4.27B of net debt against $438–856M of OE depending on the year's WC weather, and a $10.26B market cap that needs the super-bull decade to clear even ~12%.
- What would change the verdict: two or three quarters of TTM OE recovering toward $8+/share as the AMC working capital converts to shipments, net debt/adjusted EBITDA through 2.5x, and ROIC printing above ~7% — evidence the Altra capital is finally earning its keep. Until then the deleveraging story is a bondholder's thesis, not an equity compounder at this price.
